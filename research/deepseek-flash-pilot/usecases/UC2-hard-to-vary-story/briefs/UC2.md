# UC2 sealed expectation: hard-to-vary story

Prepared before any UC2 pilot run. This is an independent reading target for judging the public answer. It is not supplied to the pilot. A hash in `MANIFEST.json` seals these bytes.

## Criterion

A part is hard to vary when changing it while holding the rest of the story’s explanatory job fixed breaks the causal or interpretive account. Surface wording and props may vary freely when they preserve the same relations. The test is therefore relational: desire, frustrated pursuit, and self-protective revaluation must hang together in that order.

## Expected load-bearing parts and refuters

1. **A valued object is genuinely desired before failure.** The famished fox sees grapes described as ripe and exerts herself to obtain them. This establishes attraction independently of the later verdict. If she never wants them, or regards them as bad from the start, there is no disappointed preference to explain. The fox, fruit, colour, vineyard and exact hunger wording are variable; a person seeking a job could instantiate the same role.
2. **Access is blocked despite serious pursuit.** The grapes are beyond reach and the fox tries until tired. This supplies the frustration that motivates the later change in appraisal. If she can obtain them, makes no attempt, or leaves for an unrelated reason, the post-failure reversal loses its cause. The exact obstacle, height and number of attempts are variable if genuine inability after effort remains.
3. **The negative appraisal comes after the failed pursuit and conflicts with the earlier evidence.** She calls the grapes sour and unripe only after failing, although the narrator called them ripe. If she tastes them and discovers that they are sour, or receives reliable new evidence, her judgment may be ordinary learning rather than rationalization. Exact quoted words are variable if the post-hoc devaluation remains.
4. **The reversal hides or manages disappointment.** The narration explicitly says she is hiding disappointment. That links the changed claim to face-saving rather than to a neutral update. If she openly acknowledges disappointment without devaluing the goal, the scene may still portray failed striving but no longer explains “sour grapes” rationalization.
5. **The causal order is desire → thwarted effort → disappointment → devaluation.** Reordering the judgment before the attempt, or removing any link, breaks the fable’s distinctive explanatory mechanism. Many setting and character substitutions remain legitimate if they preserve this order.

## Legitimate alternative readings

- One may treat “hiding her disappointment” as narrator-level evidence of motive or infer face-saving from the contradiction and sequence; either is acceptable if the answer distinguishes textual evidence from inference.
- Hunger may be classified as intensifying desire rather than strictly indispensable because the ripe appearance and repeated attempts can independently establish desire.
- “All her tricks” and fatigue may be treated as evidence of serious effort rather than individually indispensable details.
- The ending may support a broader claim about motivated belief, self-deception, public face-saving, or cognitive dissonance. These labels are acceptable only when tied to the same textual relations and to a breaking variation.

## Superficial-answer traps

A superficial response lists the fox, grapes, vineyard, trellis, hunger, jumping, and the exact phrase “sour grapes” as immutable plot facts. It may paraphrase the sequence, announce a theme such as persistence or jealousy, or call every memorable detail “load-bearing” without testing substitutions. It may also say only that the moral is “people disparage what they cannot have,” without showing which textual relations establish that claim or what variation would refute it. Another trap is to classify all words as necessary because changing them changes the text, which confuses textual identity with explanatory bearing.

## Reading judgment

Credit a pilot answer when it separates functional relations from replaceable props, grounds each commitment in exact text, supplies a concrete breaking variation or refuter, and tests plausible alternatives. Mark as missed any numbered relation above that the answer does not reconstruct. Mark as invented any claimed event, perception, motive, or moral unsupported by the supplied paragraph; a theoretical label is not an invention when clearly presented as an interpretation and checked against the text.

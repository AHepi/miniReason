# Why miniReason exists

This project explores Mini's configuration space and tests the user-designated FW5 account of explanatory construction to identify gaps that can be filled. It asks which deliberate configurations help or hinder inquiry, through which mechanisms, and whether Mini adds anything compared with a bare model and the same model's native reasoning. Mini must be allowed to lose, and FW5 must remain open to substantive criticism. ECS 2.0 is archived for historical consideration and is no longer a source of hypotheses for template construction or testing (owner ruling 17, 2026-09-14): FW5 is the sole semantic target, every template construction and template test is guided exclusively by FW5, and a clause found only in ECS 2.0 carries no weight for or against FW5. The stored ECS 2.0 PDF is preserved unchanged as a historical artifact.

The configuration space includes representational languages, templates and their composition, conjecture/criticism arrangements, evidence and context access, tools and checker feedback, scheduling and resource conditions, and problem continuation or promotion. These are candidate dimensions to investigate, not a claim that each is already implemented or tested. Language expression, formal-consistency feedback and the current two-template chain are subset tests within this broader programme. Coverage grows through justified, separately identified interventions rather than an exhaustive sweep or an automatic search for a winning configuration.

Testing the semantic source means identifying the particular semantic claim and its primitive dependencies, fixing the grain, boundary and contrasts before examining the relevant evidence, and stating what a counterexample would require the framework to relinquish. A missing definition, an unsupported mapping and an operational failure are different findings. Neither a failed Mini run nor a successful executable check decides the constitutive conjecture by itself. Proposed gap-filling definitions, interpretations and mechanisms are separate criticizable contributions; they must not silently rewrite the source or absorb adverse evidence after the fact.

The historical first stage collects model-proposed representational schemes and freezes their initial bytes and material conditions. A scheme may be Lean-oriented, another formal language, prose, or a non-Lean semantic class. Correct capture of the posed problem is **not** an admission requirement or the initial point of the study. First examine what a frozen proposal lets Mini distinguish, express and do, and where its actual routes help or obstruct it. Later experiments integrate components gradually as separately identified successors; they do not rewrite the initial specimens around the results.

Prose conjectures and criticisms have the same semantic legitimacy as formal ones. They do not need a successful translation into the candidate language to deserve investigation. A parse, compilation or execution failure concerns a particular material route; preserve and examine the original content. A proof receipt is conditional on its formalization and interpretation. Neither failed syntax nor successful checking decides explanatory bearing or creativity by itself.

The designated research source is [FW5: Explanatory construction](docs/sources/FW5-explanatory-construction.md), the supplied ECS 1.0 reading edition with SHA-256 `8105925b07db4a17f15c38cbad79aaca16653d94b0503de79756b5ece33ee63a`. This identifies the uploaded reading edition, not an unavailable original manuscript. [The source assessment](docs/reviews/FW5-research-plan-decision.md) explains its differences from ECS 2.0. The ECS 2.0 PDF remains preserved unchanged with its rendering limitations, as an archived historical artifact and not as a source of hypotheses for template construction or testing (ruling 17). The supplied Conjecture–Criticism Harness v1.3 guides mechanism design. Neither source is silently replaced by Mini's implementation. Mini uses kind records and does not implement all v1.3 epistemic graph semantics; these are explicit differences to analyse, not claims of conformance. Current user instructions take precedence over any skeleton-only treatment that would demote prose.

Configurations and language interventions are arguments about particular mechanisms. Before trying one, explain what it makes available, what changes, what is held fixed and which comparison could defeat its rationale. After a failure, inspect the actual prompts, interpretation, wiring, outputs and checker before revising. Distinguish latent representability, what the configured system can reach within its resources, and what the trials actually witness. More vocabulary or more criticism need not improve finite generation. Do not randomly mutate configurations, run an evolutionary search, invent a progress meter or call an arbitrary budget cutoff exhaustion.

Record every experiment, including operational failure and proposals that have no executable form. Keep causes in [the errata](docs/errata/README.md), and transferable learning in separate [lesson files](docs/lessons/README.md). Publish each completed configuration test to `main` immediately before beginning a successor test. Up to five configurations may run concurrently, with publication serialised. A publication failure blocks successor tests until the record is durable.

The immediate priority is a fresh construction-and-use study: identify a relation that a later task actually requires, then investigate whether criticism returns to the corresponding operative use, interpretation, method or attention. Distinct templates run one cycle each; proposed bytes retained only in an archive are compared with operative return. Content-preserving recoding, changed content, protected uses and matched direct controls address alternative explanations. This finite study cannot certify historical newness or recursive universal capacity. In parallel, an independent Account sufficiency or necessity challenge tests FW5 itself. Neither task replaces the broader configuration programme.

Finite observations can defeat scoped behavioural claims and support particular accounts of expression, use or repair. They do not establish creativity across all tasks, establish the model's pretraining repertoire, or refute ECS merely because an implementation failed. Any semantic attribution or counterexample names its interpretation, grain, boundary and remaining dependencies. Promoting a difficulty into a new problem is an investigation decision; it does not certify the difficulty's proposed diagnosis.

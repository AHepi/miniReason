# Why miniReason exists

This project investigates how proposed languages and semantic classes change what a model can express, construct, criticise and use through Mini under actual time, context and resource conditions. The aim is to understand which components expand or hinder creative inquiry, and whether deliberately configured Mini adds anything compared with a bare model and the same model's native reasoning. Those comparisons remain essential, and Mini must be allowed to lose.

The first stage collects model-proposed representational schemes and freezes their initial bytes and material conditions. A scheme may be Lean-oriented, another formal language, prose, or a non-Lean semantic class. Correct capture of the posed problem is **not** an admission requirement or the initial point of the study. First examine what a frozen proposal lets Mini distinguish, express and do, and where its actual routes help or obstruct it. Later experiments integrate components gradually as separately identified successors; they do not rewrite the initial specimens around the results.

Prose conjectures and criticisms have the same semantic legitimacy as formal ones. They do not need a successful translation into the candidate language to deserve investigation. A parse, compilation or execution failure concerns a particular material route; preserve and examine the original content. A proof receipt is conditional on its formalization and interpretation. Neither failed syntax nor successful checking decides explanatory bearing or creativity by itself.

The semantic guide is the supplied Explanatory Construction Semantics 2.0. The supplied Conjecture–Criticism Harness v1.3 guides mechanism design. Neither source is silently replaced by Mini's implementation. Mini uses kind records and does not implement all v1.3 epistemic graph semantics; these are explicit differences to analyse, not claims of conformance. Current user instructions take precedence over any skeleton-only treatment that would demote prose.

Configurations and language interventions are arguments about particular mechanisms. Before trying one, explain what it makes available, what changes, what is held fixed and which comparison could defeat its rationale. After a failure, inspect the actual prompts, interpretation, wiring, outputs and checker before revising. Distinguish latent representability, what the configured system can reach within its resources, and what the trials actually witness. More vocabulary or more criticism need not improve finite generation. Do not randomly mutate configurations, run an evolutionary search, invent a progress meter or call an arbitrary budget cutoff exhaustion.

Record every experiment, including operational failure and proposals that have no executable form. Keep causes in [the errata](docs/errata/README.md), and transferable learning in separate [lesson files](docs/lessons/README.md). Publish each completed configuration test to `main` immediately before beginning a successor test. Up to five configurations may run concurrently, with publication serialised. A publication failure blocks successor tests until the record is durable.

Finite observations can defeat scoped behavioural claims and support particular accounts of expression, use or repair. They do not establish creativity across all tasks, establish the model's pretraining repertoire, or refute ECS merely because an implementation failed. Any semantic attribution or counterexample names its interpretation, grain, boundary and remaining dependencies. Promoting a difficulty into a new problem is an investigation decision; it does not certify the difficulty's proposed diagnosis.

Start or resume at [README](README.md), then [the continuation workflow](docs/workflows/continue.md). Read [the language-proposal propositions](docs/LANGUAGE_PROPOSAL_THEOREMS.md) and [problem promotion](docs/PROBLEM_PROMOTION.md) before adapting this study. A future operator should be able to recover the purpose, frozen specimens, evidence and open alternatives without a chat transcript.

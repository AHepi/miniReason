# Expression and interpretation lessons

Read these as scoped observations, not a universal ranking of languages or a semantic scoring rule. Preserve the cited records and keep later interpretations separate.

## Source custody and expressive use are different

E005's matched-native expression exactly reproduces the frozen corpus. That is a positive fidelity control for an unconstrained prose carrier, including its original errors and uncertainty. It does not establish independent construction or novelty. Its later reading nevertheless changes the modal status of some claims. Exact custody of source bytes therefore does not settle what a later model takes them to assert. See [the E005 review](../reviews/E005-prose-review.md).

## Withheld evidence must be judged at the stage where it was withheld

In E005, a reader correctly qualified its ability to verify source fidelity because its source field was withheld. A subsequent critic had the complete source and treated that earlier qualification as an error. The additional evidence can resolve an uncertainty without making the earlier qualification unwarranted. Reviews must name each stage's actual evidence and avoid importing later visibility into an earlier assessment.

## Shared language context can intrude on a prose control

The E005 bare response diagnosed candidate-language obstructions and labeled a source quotation as a prose escape even though its selected carrier was already unrestricted prose. This supports a concrete follow-up question about inactive-language priming; one sample does not establish a general effect. A later deliberate contrast can remove the nonoperative language declarations while preserving the actual source and prose instructions. Do not silently reinterpret E005 as that cleaner control.

## A missing named primitive is not a proof of inexpressibility

The E004 packet and E005 critics sometimes infer inability from a missing primitive. Full Lean propositions can close over or quantify relations, and ordinary prose can express dependencies without a designated field name. A stronger limitation claim needs a defended admitted grammar/interpretation and a reason that alternative encodings cannot carry the distinction. A failure of the supplied effect function to alter selection is a narrower and more directly inspectable claim.

## Criticism remains a fallible construction

E005's critics exposed specific candidate-language tensions but also made mistaken artifact attributions and unsupported universal claims. Preserve both. A later integration should investigate the selected criticism's content and competing diagnoses before attributing refutation or repair. Promotion to a problem can precede that determination; semantic endorsement cannot be inferred from promotion itself.


## 2026-09-19 — A rule language written by someone who knows the law

Evidence: [experiment 2 errata](../reviews/2026-09-19-fable-cross-examination/17%20Errata%20-%20experiment%202%20after%20cross-examination.md) §4, §8a, §12 and the adjudication's C3–C5, D5, E4. The collision world was redefined to the shape of the rule language after the language failed an edge case; the action set contained the law's exact operation; the target depth matched the search budget. An expressibility check with a revision clause ("if the language cannot express the law, revise") cannot fail. A fair design fixes the target laws by a blinded generator, hashed before the language is finalised, and records expressibility failures as results.

The "invisible law" (identity through a crossing) was expressible in the language; what could not select it was the defect-driven criterion, because crossing steps produce no sensory defects. A limit can sit in the error signal rather than in what the language can say, and the two must be reported separately. Identity through crossings was underdetermined by occupancy for both the constructing and the selecting process, so it was a property of the sensory contract, not of either process.

## 2. DEFINITIONS AND DISTINCTIONS (term — definition — line)

**D1. Explanation (central proposal).** L27: "an explanation represents a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**, not merely a set of true sentences or a successful input–output mapping."

**D2. Creative thinker / creativity.** L25: "A creative thinker does not merely produce an unusual answer. It constructs or reconstructs an organization of ideas through which a difficulty can be understood, investigated, or transformed." L29: "Creativity concerns the construction and use of such representations."

**D3. Knowledge creation.** L25: "When it actually removes an explanatory defect, the achievement is knowledge creation in the relevant respect."

**D4. Recursively critical.** L25: "When the thinker can turn the same activity on its own interpretations, methods, and restrictions, with consequences for how it proceeds, the organization is recursively critical."

**D5. Explanatory universality.** L25: "When no subject-specific barrier permanently confines that capacity, the stronger attribution is explanatory universality."

**D6. Constitutive conjecture.** L33: "Explanatory creativity can be characterized by the construction of question-relevant dependency representations, their content-sensitive use and criticism, and their physically grounded provenance."

**D7. Organization.** L83–87: "An organization is a structure \[D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L,\operatorname{role}).\]" Components L89: "Here \(V\) is a set of ports or variables, and each \(X_v\) is its nonempty value domain. A valuation is an element of \(X_D=\prod_{v\in V}X_v\). The set \(J\) indexes components. A component may be a physical assignment, a measurement relation, a mathematical constraint, a constitutive rule, or another explicitly interpreted relation. Its footprint \(V_j\subseteq V\) specifies the ports it connects."

**D8. Boundary conditions / edits.** L91: "The set \(B\) contains boundary conditions. The set \(A\) contains admitted edits and their composable sequences, including an identity edit \(1\). … Edits may change inputs, replace a component, delete an assumption, alter an observation interpretation, or change a rule. Not every conceivable edit must be physically possible."

**D9. Solution set (O).** L99–107: "The compatible valuations are exactly \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)=\{z\in X_D:\forall j\in J,\ z|_{V_j}\in L_j(a,b)\}\). (O)"

**D10. Role interpretation.** L111: "The role interpretation fixes which ports are independent inputs, produced outputs, observations, control states, rule applications, and so forth. It also fixes what component edits mean. These distinctions are part of the substantive target model, not supplied by the direction in which someone happens to solve an equation."

**D11. Question.** L117–121: "A question is an indexed structure \[p=(D,\Sigma,b_0,\kappa,\mathcal C,\mathcal Q,O_p).\]"

**D12. Scope Σ / baseline.** L123: "The scope \(\Sigma\subseteq A\times B\) fixes which edits and boundary conditions are in the claim. The distinguished baseline is \((1,b_0)\in\Sigma\)."

**D13. Respect κ.** L123: "The respect \(\kappa\) fixes what is asked: for example, production, inferential identification, impossibility, rule-governed status, achievement of an aim, or aesthetic value."

**D14. Contrast contract C.** L123: "The contrast contract \(\mathcal C\subseteq A\times B\), with its associated role and edit specifications, fixes the input changes, component changes, and distinctions material to that question. It includes the baseline and **is not identified with a collection of performed experiments**."

**D15. Query operator Q / answer profile (Q).** L125: "The query operator \(\mathcal Q\) is a specified set-theoretic operation on the relevant organization, its solutions, and, where needed, its component structure. Its codomain \(Y_p\) is specified. It may return a feature value, a set of compatible values, a reachability relation, the truth value of an obstruction, a derivation property, or a normative application." L127–132: "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\). (Q)"

**D16. Obligations O_p.** L134: "The set \(O_p\) contains the obligations being addressed or protected by an inquiry about \(p\). Each obligation is a specified condition on a resulting situation, **not an automatically optimized objective**."

**D17. Occurrence vs content (distinction).** L144: "An occurrence is a particular physically or historically located carrier. A content is an interpreted organization, including its commitments, connections, reference, and scope. Occurrences are not identified merely because they carry the same words. Contents are not identified merely because they have the same truth value or final output."

**D18. Representation relation.** L146–152: "\(\operatorname{Rep}_{\beta,\ell}(o,c,h)\) is declared model data about reference and representation at boundary \(\beta\). It is not a host's claim that the token was supplied to a prompt."

**D19. Grain ℓ / genuine recoding.** L154: "A grain fixes which structural distinctions count as differences for the attribution at issue. A genuine recoding is an isomorphism of the retained structure at that grain. Coarsening is not automatically an isomorphism."

**D20. Explanatory candidate / active commitments (distinction).** L162: "An explanatory candidate for \(p\) supplies an organization \(E\), an answer profile \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\), and an interpretation of its active commitments. The commitments actually claimed to account for the target must be distinguished from incidental remarks."

**D21. Structural account.** L164–168: "A structural account consists of \[\mathcal E=(E,p,\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda).\]" L170: "The map \(\pi\) sends target valuations in the stated scope to explanatory valuations. It may be many-to-one. The maps \(\tau\) and \(\sigma\) translate edits and boundary conditions. The map \(\lambda\) anchors explanatory components to target components or specified subnetworks."

**D22. Anchoring.** L174: "Every active explanatory component has a stated target interpretation under \(\lambda\). Its port roles and edit semantics are preserved. A causal assignment must be anchored to a causal assignment or a derived causal suborganization, not merely to an observed association. A measurement relation must be anchored to a measurement organization. A constitutive rule must be anchored to the rule it purports to apply." Formal version L176: "the anchoring data include a port translation for each component, depending only on the ports of its anchored subnetwork and the explicitly declared boundary. The target subnetwork's relation is obtained by imposing its component constraints and projecting away its hidden ports. That projected relation must equal the explanatory component relation at the declared abstraction."

**D23. Structural fidelity (F) and (C).** L179–186: "For the comparisons specified in \(\Sigma\cap\mathcal C\), \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\). (F)" L188–195: "\(\tau(1)=1,\qquad \tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\). (C)"

**D24. Question fidelity (A).** L199–206: "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\). (A)" L208: "This includes the respect \(\kappa\), not just a matching number."

**D25. Non-circular dependence.** L210: "The answer follows by evaluating the anchored organization under its declared independent boundary conditions. The target answer is not an unanalysed boundary input or a copied target assertion among its premises. … There is at least one admitted contrast that removes or changes a nonempty block of active organizational commitments, while preserving the other declared boundary conditions, for which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way."

**D26. Non-vacuity.** L212: "The baseline organization has a compatible state or history. A claim of impossibility may correctly assert that a specified goal has no compatible realization, but not infer a substantive result merely from an inconsistent baseline. A contrast family containing only notational variants, or one defined to exclude every change that could matter, does not meet non-circular dependence."

**D27. Account (E).** L214–224: "\(\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\iff\operatorname{Anchor}\land\operatorname{Fidelity}\land\operatorname{QuestionFidelity}\land\operatorname{NonCircularDependence}\land\operatorname{NonVacuity}\). (E)"

**D28. Restriction E|W / support family (S).** L252: "let \(E|W\) be the organization obtained by retaining the commitments in \(W\) in the specified roles while keeping the named background fixed." L254–262: "\(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\). (S)"

**D29. CriticalBlock (B).** L271–279: "\(\operatorname{CriticalBlock}(D;W,p)\iff W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\land W\setminus D\notin\mathsf S_{E,p}\). (B)"

**D30. Contributory (individual).** L281: "An individual commitment is contributory in this deletion respect when it is a critical block of size one in some support."

**D31. Boundary (D).** L283–292: "For a declared family \(\mathcal V\) of organization edits, define \(\operatorname{Boundary}_{E,p}=\{(v,w)\in\mathcal V^2:\operatorname{Account}(E_v,p)\ne\operatorname{Account}(E_w,p)\}\). (D)"

**D32. Pres(F) and (H).** L358–366: "\(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\)." L368–375: "\(F\subseteq F'\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Pres}(F')\subseteq\operatorname{Pres}(F)\). (H)"

**D33. Hard to vary.** L381: "'Hard to vary' is therefore an articulated pattern of constrained changes, relative to an explanatory job. It is not the requirement that every detail of a complete theory be individually indispensable. Nor is it a numerical warrant."

**D34. Reach.** L383: "Reach occurs when an unchanged organizational core participates in an account of another question through a stated anchor and additional background. The new bridge is itself content and can be a creative contribution."

## 3. CLAIMS ABOUT HOW EXPLANATORY CONSTRUCTION WORKS

### What an account is
- L27: "an explanation represents a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**, not merely a set of true sentences or a successful input–output mapping. What makes a dependency explanatory is not a label attached to it. The explanation must preserve the target's relevant organization under the distinctions and changes the question concerns."
- L27: "Producing an effect, identifying an unknown quantity, deriving an obstruction, constituting a status, and making an artistic feature serve a purpose are different questions. A representation that silently substitutes one for another is inadequate even when its arithmetic is correct."
- L226: "The right side is determined by the specified structures and maps. It contains no predicate that already means 'really explains.' Anchoring is a claim about the kind and organization of the target's components; fidelity is a collection of mathematical equalities; question fidelity is an indexing condition; non-circular dependence is a contrast property."
- L246: "a complete account also preserves **component-level organization under the appropriate kinds of change**."
- L244: "No separate approval event creates an account. The maps can exist before anyone discovers them. An investigator's successful argument for their existence is evidence for the attribution, not its truth-maker by decree. A thinker need not possess the maps in explicit notation to instantiate the relevant organization."

### How reasons/commitments are used (contribution, support)
- L162: "The commitments actually claimed to account for the target must be distinguished from incidental remarks. This distinction is part of the interpreted claim, not permission for an assessor to rescue a failed argument by selecting whichever fragments happen to be true."
- L267: "No upward closure is assumed. Adding an active, incompatible premise can spoil a route. No minimal member is assumed. The whole family, rather than a selected minimal subset, is the basic support object."
- L281: "A block may contribute without any singleton in it doing so. The absence of a singleton witness must not be called absence of explanatory organization."
- L294: "The boundary records where an account survives or fails; it does not assign a probability or a score. Positive contribution, interference, substitution, and jointly necessary organization can all be represented in this unrestricted family."
- L330: "Redundancy can make a realization resilient; it does not make each route meaningless or automatically make the explanation bad."
- L344: "The full active candidate fails even though a restricted candidate succeeds. It is wrong to infer success of the full candidate from existence of a successful subset. It is also wrong to repair the mathematics by silently declaring \(b\) 'not really a commitment.' Its interference is part of the modeled problem."
- L352: "The whole family still does work. … At a coarser grain, the single content 'for every \(n\ge1\), \(|x|\le1/n\)' can be individually critical. The change is in the attribution grain, not in the mathematical implication."

### What criticism does
- L55: "An idea may be entertained without first being justified. Its generation neither proves it nor obliges a thinker to use it. Conjecture precedes criticism in the sense that a criticism has a target; that target can be an inherited or inexplicit expectation. A criticism is itself conjectural."
- L57: "A thinker may act on an appraisal without having certified it. Withholding a premise, retaining a theory, revising a question, or moving on are possible actions. They are not truth-conferring events. Refusing an infallible evaluator must not be turned into a requirement that no criticism can ever affect practice."
- L63: "A problem, value, standard, observation model, interpretation, inferential practice, attention policy, attribution boundary, or conjecture-generating restriction can itself become a problem. **A result of that inquiry must be able to affect the operative target. Scrutiny that can never change anything is not the recursive capacity described here.**"
- L242: "A mathematician can establish that (F) holds for two described structures without establishing that either describes the intended physical target. A thinker can propose an anchor that is wrong. A question contract can omit a distinction the original difficulty requires. **These are different criticism targets.**"
- L138: "Exposing that defect is another question with its own target and contract. 'Why did the nonexistent event happen?' and 'Why does this question presuppose an event that did not happen?' are not the same question."

### Repair / error-correction
- L25: "When it actually removes an explanatory defect, the achievement is knowledge creation in the relevant respect."
- L49: "Knowledge need not be an entirely true theory. A theory can contain an accurate account of a scoped dependence together with erroneous claims elsewhere. An approximation can correctly describe an explicitly approximate feature. **What cannot count as successful explanation is an error in the very dependence alleged to account for that feature.**"
- L140: "The question contract is not immune. It can be criticized and replaced. **What is prohibited is changing it during an assessment without recording the resulting change in what is claimed.**"
- L228: "If a genuine explanation requires a distinction no such structure can preserve, the proposal must change. An unsupported exemption saying that the example nevertheless satisfies a primitive `Because` is not available."
- L265: "A new map or a changed role assignment is a different route, which must be identified as such."

### What understanding / creativity / explanatory bearing amounts to
- L29: "Creativity concerns the construction and use of such representations. Constructor theory constrains their physical realization and the capabilities their instantiation makes available. Creating knowledge can change an organization's accessible capabilities or its use of existing ones; it does not change the laws of physics or convert a physically impossible task into a possible one."
- L33: "Explanatory creativity can be characterized by the construction of question-relevant dependency representations, their content-sensitive use and criticism, and their physically grounded provenance."
- L156: "a system can represent a false explanation, misidentify a target, or understand a criticism that is wrong. Those possibilities would be lost if representation itself meant successful explanation."
- L383: "The new bridge is itself content and can be a creative contribution."
- L69: "A sentence, diagram, internal model, imagined movement, artistic discrimination, or distributed practice may carry relevant organization."

### What is NOT sufficient
- L59: "Observation, deduction, computation, selection, feedback, communication, and biological inheritance may participate in creativity. **None is sufficient merely by occurring.** The invalid inference from 'not sufficient' to 'cannot participate' is explicitly excluded."
- L232: "A table of observed answers need not have an anchoring to the target's causal, constitutive, or inferential organization. **Matching the table therefore does not establish (E).** … Anti-behaviorism is a distinction about organization, not a ban on a data structure."
- L332: "Two systems with the same output table can have different active routes. … A token-level authorship or causal attribution requires the internal event structure. **Endpoint agreement establishes neither identity of active causes nor identity of contributors.**"
- L234: "'\(p\), because \(p\)' fails when the conclusion is simply installed as the independent premise and no separate organizational constraint explains it. A true mechanism guessed for bad reasons can nevertheless meet (E); the guesser's grounds can be defective without making the mechanism false."
- L47: "Whether a representation preserves a target's relevant organization is independent of whether anyone accepts it." (agreement/acceptance not sufficient)
- L41: "objective aesthetics cannot honestly be obtained by defining beauty as approval, symmetry, compression, surprise, or successful persuasion."
- L208: "An account of an audience's response does not, by that fact, answer a question about objective aesthetic merit."
- L136: "A question about what produces an output cannot be answered by installing that output as an unexplained input and relabelling an inverse calculation as a production process."
- L210: "Moving the same target assertion from an input slot into a component called a law does not remove this prohibition."
- L174: "A causal assignment must be anchored to a causal assignment or a derived causal suborganization, **not merely to an observed association**. … the word 'derived' does not authorize an unexplained explanatory arrow."
- L71: "Abstract consistency alone is not evidence of physical realizability."
- L39: "A physical law, the meaning of an expression, and a normative reason are not derived from a transcript by set theory."
- L156: "Removing the word 'explanation' from a formula does not derive reference from uninterpreted matter."
- L381: "Two descriptions can divide the same work into different numbers of features; **splitting one feature into ten cannot create knowledge**."
- L379: "**Counting jobs is not a replacement for the missing comparison.**"
- L246: "There is no assertion that any mathematical theorem plus any independently testable application claim automatically constitutes an explanation."

## 5. TESTABLE PREDICTIONS / OBSERVABLES LICENSED

**Falsifiers of the framework itself**
- L33: "**A genuine explanatory achievement that cannot satisfy the structural conditions would count against it.**"
- L228: "If a genuine explanation requires a distinction no such structure can preserve, the proposal must change."

**Discriminating observables**
- L332: "Two systems with the same output table can have different active routes. In parallel wiring, both routes may be active. In priority wiring, the first may prevent the second from firing. **A token-level authorship or causal attribution requires the internal event structure.**" → prediction: output-identical systems must be separable only by internal route structure; any measure keyed to endpoints cannot discriminate them.
- L232: "Matching the table therefore does not establish (E)." → a candidate that matches all observed answers but lacks anchoring must be scored as failing.
- L344: "It is wrong to infer success of the full candidate from existence of a successful subset." → prediction: adding an active commitment can flip Account from true to false (non-monotone ablation signature).
- L330: "Both commitments have an individual contribution witness. Neither is indispensable to the full organization. Removing both defeats every route." → prediction: single-deletion ablation can show zero effect for every item while the joint deletion is decisive.

## 2. Every definition and distinction drawn in my chunk

### 2.1 Hard-to-vary / reach (380–383)
- **Hard to vary** (381): "“Hard to vary” is therefore an articulated pattern of constrained changes, relative to an explanatory job. It is not the requirement that every detail of a complete theory be individually indispensable. Nor is it a numerical warrant."
- **Distinction — description granularity vs knowledge** (381): "Two descriptions can divide the same work into different numbers of features; splitting one feature into ten cannot create knowledge."
- **Reach** (383): "Reach occurs when an unchanged organizational core participates in an account of another question through a stated anchor and additional background. The new bridge is itself content and can be a creative contribution. No insistence that exactly the same minimal support survive is imposed."

### 2.2 Production / direction (387–402)
- **Production direction is fixed by role assignment + intervention semantics** (398): "The role assignments designate \(H\) and \(\theta\) as upstream in the production of \(L\). Intervening on \(L\) replaces its last equation and does not replace the equations for \(H\) or \(\theta\). … The forward account preserves the production structure. Solving the last equation for \(H\) does not reverse that structure."
- **Distinction — identification vs production** (400): "A calculation from shadow length to height can nevertheless be a correct account of **identification**. It fails only when presented as an answer to the different question of what produced the height."
- **Distinction — causal organization vs vocabulary** (400): "A pole intentionally constructed to cast a specified shadow would have a different causal organization. The direction is not settled by the nouns “pole” and “shadow.”"
- **Distinction — four separate notions** (402): "Actual token causes, type-level dependences, inference, and explanation remain distinct. [R6]"

### 2.3 Identification (404–447)
- **Compatibility fibre** (408–412): `Z_y = {z∈Z : g(z)=y}` for measurement map `g:Z→Y`, feature `f:Z→F`.
- **Identification (I1)** (414–421): identified at `y` exactly when `Z_y ≠ ∅ ∧ |f[Z_y]| = 1`.
- **Distinction — empty fibre vs vacuous identification** (423): "An empty fibre is incompatibility of the stipulated observation with the model. It is not perfect identification by vacuous universal quantification."
- **Factorization theorem (I2)** (425–432): identified on every attainable measurement exactly when `∃ f̄ : g[Z]→F` with `f = f̄ ∘ g`. Proof at 432.
- **Distinction — domain constraint vs repetition** (434): "The change is an additional domain constraint, not an improvement created by repeating the same observation. Local derivative information alone does not establish the global fibre property."
- **Relational measurement** (436): for `G⊆Z×Y`, `Z_y={z:(z,y)∈G}`; "(I1) remains the exact criterion… it does not assume a hidden deterministic measurement function."
- **Approximate identification (I3)** (438–445): `∀z,z'∈Z_y, d_F(f(z),f(z'))≤ε, Z_y≠∅`; the complete answer "can remain the uncertainty set \(f[Z_y]\)".
- **Distinction — bound vs estimate** (447): "Equation (I3) does not automatically supply a preferred centre or an estimate with half that error. Those claims need their own premises about the space and the estimator."

### 2.4 Two balances (449–483)
- **Kernel/limitation result** (462–468): kernel spanned by `(1,−1,−1)`; "Thus the readings identify \(b_B-b_A=2\), but not \(x\). This is an account of a limitation, not a failure to produce a preferred number."
- **Linear fibre-constancy criterion (I4)** (470–477): `ker A ⊆ ker cᵀ`; proof at 477; equivalently (479) `cᵀ` in the row space of `A`.
- **Distinction — repetition vs independent calibration** (479): "Repetition of the same rows does not change the kernel. An independently applicable calibration can add a constraint that does."
- **Circular calibration** (481): "“Set \(b_B=0\) because that gives the mass I favor” is not an inference from the readings. The proposition \(b_B=0\) might happen to be true… Its alleged evidential support is still circular. The criticism removes that route for trusting the calibration, not the logical possibility that the calibration is correct."
- **Distinction — content correctness vs argument success** (483): "This distinction repairs a recurring confusion between whether a content is correct and whether a particular argument for adopting it succeeds."

### 2.5 Invariants (485–504)
- **Invariant / allowed-step relation** (487–491): `R⊆Z×Z`, `I:Z→K` with `zRz' ⟹ I(z)=I(z')`.
- **(O1)** (493–498): `zR*z' ⟹ I(z)=I(z')`; proof at 500.
- **Distinction — sufficient obstruction vs reachability characterization** (502): "This is a sufficient obstruction criterion, not a claim that every invariant partitions the state space into exactly the reachable components. Equal invariant values alone do not establish reachability."

### 2.6 Constitutive rules (506–512)
- **Constitutive system** (508): "supplies states \(Z\), a rule relation \(C\subseteq Z\times S\), and a rule-identity and scope condition. The query “which status does this state have under this rule?” is answered by the fibre \(C[z]\). Existence, uniqueness, ambiguity, and inapplicability are separate cases."
- **Distinction — rule-status vs other queries** (510): "A game rule can make a configuration a win. It does not follow that the move was wise, beautiful, intended, or morally justified. Those are different queries."
- **Three-way distinction** (512): "A derivation from a rule is mathematically assessable. Whether that rule governs the practice is an application claim. Whether it should govern the practice is another claim. No vote, implementation status, or written label collapses the three."

### 2.7 Transport (516–593)
- **Exact functional transport** (518–524): `π∘S_a = T_a∘π` for every admitted generator ⟹ same for every admitted finite composition, "provided the intermediate scopes match"; proof 526–532.
- **Relational structural account** (538): "A relational structural account replaces the image equality in (F) by total coverage of the relevant target solutions, coverage of the relevant explanatory solutions, and preservation of the query value for every related pair."
- **Forward preservation (T1)** (542–547): `zRy ∧ z→^a z' ⟹ ∃y' [ y→^{τ(a)} y' ∧ z'Ry' ]`.
- **Backward condition** (549): "The backward condition reverses target and explanatory roles. These are conditions on the supplied transition relations; they do not choose a branch without a premise."
- **Composition of relations** (551–558): `(S∘R)(z,w) ⟺ ∃y[zRy ∧ ySw]`; "Query preservation composes when the intermediate query and scope are the same. Mere overlap of two unrelated interpretations is insufficient."
- **Distinction — functional vs relational case must be declared** (560): "Equation (E) permits either the functional case or this relational case. The choice must be stated; the stronger claims of equivalence are not inferred from a one-sided simulation."
- **Approximate transport bound (T2)** (572–583): `e_{n+1} ≤ L e_n + ε`, `e_0=0` ⟹ `e_n ≤ ε Σ_{k=0}^{n-1} L^k`; proof 585.
- **Modulus fallback** (587): "Without a Lipschitz bound, a stated modulus of continuity can replace multiplication by \(L\). Without either, a general accumulated-error bound does not follow."
- **Historical indexing** (591): "A proposition indexed to \((p,\Sigma,h,e)\) remains that proposition when a later theory changes the current scope. A new index creates a new claim; it does not retroactively invalidate every true proposition at the old index."
- **Two transport cases** (593): "A later model may show that the earlier application was always mistaken. That changes the appraisal of an old claim, not the old world's truth. Alternatively, a later model may preserve an accurate approximation while explaining its limits. These are different cases. A transport must say which it is."

### 2.8 Histories, routes, situations, episodes (597–605)
- **History** (599): "A history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence relation \(\prec_h\), together with its physical process interpretation."
- **Key asymmetry** (599): "Semantic support and opposition need not be acyclic. Two existing arguments can oppose each other without either having caused an earlier event."
- **Active route** (601): "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of those actual occurrences joining the named represented input to the named operative result. Its designation must satisfy the corresponding component relations and role bindings; it is not inferred from the presence of a similar sentence in a record."
- **Nonconstant dependence requirement** (601): "For reason-use and contribution claims the subnetwork must also have a nonconstant dependence on the relevant represented distinction under its declared contrasts. A dead branch, a cancelled signal with no downstream role, or a carrier detail that changes while the represented reason does not change cannot supply that dependence."
- **Contribution ≠ necessity** (601): "Contingent alternatives and collective blocks remain available, so this condition does not equate contribution with single-variable necessity at the full endpoint."
- **Situation** (603): "A situation \(\xi\) comprises the contents currently represented or available, active questions, current uses, appraisal acts, attention engagements, purposes, and the relevant links to the history. These are role relations, not compulsory explicit databases."
- **Episode** (605): "An episode is a causally connected course of attention to an interpreted difficulty. It can contain overlapping subepisodes, change formulation, pause, resume, or become part of another episode. Attention is itself an activity in the history. Its organization and the values affecting it are potential criticism targets. The class imposes no maximizing attention function and no rule that the most recent objection receives priority."

### 2.9 Criticism and bearing (607–622)
- **Criticism** (609): "A criticism has a represented target \(z\), an alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a proposed connection from \(g\) to \(\delta\) relative to a question."
- **Defect** (609): "A defect is a specified failure condition on the target or its application: inconsistency, a false dependency, a missing distinction, an unsupported reference, a failed task, a misapplied rule, or another condition whose interpretation is supplied."
- **Bearing (K1)** (611–618): `Bearing(c,z,p) ⟺ Account(𝓔_c, p_δ)`, "where \(\mathcal E_c\) is the criticism's interpreted structural account. The alleged defect must concern the stated target and respect." (620)
- **Counterexample** (620): "A counterexample to a universal claim is a simple structural case: the target's requirement and the counterexample cannot jointly hold."
- **Criticism occurrence vs bearing** (622): "A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) is false. Its grounds may be mistaken, its target misidentified, its inference invalid, or its relevance wrong."
- **Signal vs criticism** (622): "A mere adverse signal is not made into a criticism by giving it a negative label. It can become grounds for a criticism when an organization represents how it bears on a target."

### 2.10 Reason use (624–634)
- **Reason use** (626): "A response uses a reason when the represented content of the objection participates in the organization's deliberative transition. The response state includes interpretations, provisional uses, methods, and continuing questions, not just a terminal action label."
- **Reason-use witness** (628): "the realization supplies a response suborganization \(D_s\) with represented target, grounds, defect connection, and response-role ports. A reason-use witness is a structural map from the represented objection organization into that suborganization such that content-preserving recodings preserve the interpreted transition, relevant changes to the grounds or alleged defect have the changes specified by the system's operative deliberative rule, and the mapped objection is on an active dependency route of this response occurrence. The map must preserve internal role bindings, not merely the endpoint string."
- **Use ≠ validity** (630): "**Understanding and using an invalid objection does not make it valid.** The definition concerns whether the system used the content, not whether it responded ideally."
- **Contrast contract** (630): "Its contrast contract must include a content-changing case, a content-preserving recoding, and the distinction between a change in the objection and an irrelevant carrier disturbance wherever those distinctions are claimed. These are semantic comparisons, not a mandatory battery of physical tests."
- **Outcome-underdetermination** (632): "Two objections can lead to the same decision for different represented reasons. A valid criticism can lead to retention when its alleged force has been misunderstood. An invalid criticism can lead to a mistaken revision. A refusal with no evidenced or modeled content-sensitive route must not be assigned a fictional reason."
- **Unresolved ≠ negative** (634): "An observer may lack the data needed to establish the witness. That makes the attribution unresolved; it does not prove either understanding or its absence."

### 2.11 Standing / usability (636–653)
- **Standing** (638): "Standing is the system's enacted permission to use a content in a particular application and respect. It may be explicit or inexplicit. An appraisal can change it without having earned prior certification. Generation alone does not logically entail it, but generation and provisional adoption may occur together in one physical event. Separating their meanings does not require separate timestamps or separate machines."
- **Delivery / invitation / semantic attribution** (640): "A source's appearance in a prompt is a delivery fact. A named field asking for a criticism is an invitation fact. Actual use of the source as a premise, actual understanding, and actual criticism are semantic attributions. They are not automatically machine-maintainable facts merely because a host can maintain corresponding labels."
- **Usable (K2)** (642–651): `Usable_j(u) ⟺ Lic_j(u) ∧ Scope_j(u) ∧ ∀d∈Prem(u), Live_j(d;u)`, with `Prem(u)` = "its declared essential premises, including interpretation and scope premises", `Live_j(d;u)` = standing in appraisal `j`.
- **Coherence violable** (653): "The actor can violate this coherence condition. The model then represents an error in its appraisal. A record declaring a dependency does not establish that the dependency is actually essential; an error in that declaration is another criticism target."

### 2.12 Elimination (655–676)
- **Withdrawal semantics** (657): "Suppose a system withdraws an essential premise of \(u\). Equation (K2) no longer licenses \(u\). Another argument \(v\) for the same conclusion can remain usable. The conclusion is not thereby false. Withdrawing a criticism likewise does not establish the truth of its target."
- **Retention without archive** (659): "The thinker can remove a rejected conjecture from its current working use while retaining its occurrence for comparison or later criticism. It need not retain every rejected occurrence forever, and the absence of an archival copy does not undo a past creative event. Availability as a current target requires some representation of that target, not an append-only history of every earlier thought."
- **Limits of machine checks** (661): "A machine check can establish a limited proposition such as equality with source bytes or validity of a derivation relative to formal premises. Its interpretation can have real consequences. A passing execution cannot make the program's specification, the model of the experiment, or the claimed relevance immune to prose criticism."
- **Test contradiction (K3)** (663–676): from `T∧B∧I ⟹ O` and established `¬O`, one gets `¬(T∧B∧I)`; "It does not yield \(\neg T\) without additional premises about \(B\) and \(I\). Here \(B\) can include auxiliaries and \(I\) the observation interpretation. This is not an argument against testing. It states exactly what the test contradicts and where further criticism can matter."

### 2.13 Receipts (678–690)
- **Evidence leaf** (680): "Let an evidence leaf be a reference to an actual event and a particular interpreted claim about it. Its identity includes the event, the inspected relation, the interpretation, and the scope. Equal text in two events does not identify their provenance or metadata."
- **Receipt** (682): "A receipt is a derivation tree whose leaves are these references and whose internal nodes specify the inference actually used. Distinct sufficient routes remain distinct receipts. Conjunction combines the premises needed by that route; disjunction permits a witness from either side. A refutation by an independent disjunct must not inherit the disputed assumptions of an unused disjunct."
- **Receipt sets** (684): "let \(P_j(\phi)\) be the set of currently usable receipts supporting it and \(N_j(\phi)\) those supporting its negation. The record can indicate that neither set, one set, or both sets are nonempty. These are descriptions of available arguments, not four kinds of reality."
- **Negation behaviour** (686): "Negating a claim exchanges its positive and negative receipt sets. It does not turn an absent receipt into a negative observation. Thus missing evidence remains missing under negation. An explicitly named missing predecessor remains that missing predecessor; a different baseline may not be substituted without changing the claim."
- **Eligibility** (688): "Eligibility is a separate predicate. An observation of a completed answer can be eligible for a content claim. A timeout may instead be evidence about delivery. The inability to evaluate a proposition is not a falsifying observation of the proposition."
- **Receipts optional** (690): "This receipt construction is optional descriptive mathematics. Ordinary prose need not arrive with a tree, source fields, or a machine-verifiable witness to enter inquiry. The point is that an interpretation of its reasoning must not silently lose its actual dependencies."

### 2.14 Understanding (694–710)
- **Use task** (696): "For a content organization \(c\), a use task specifies requests to employ its represented relations in a declared family of problem contexts and the corresponding internal or external semantic outcomes. The requests can include interpreting a component, following a dependence, relating an implication to a question, or treating the content as a target of criticism. The outcomes are changes in represented organization, not necessarily sentences."
- **Understanding** (698): "A system **understands \(c\) in a stated respect** when a represented realization of \(c\) is integrated into its own problem-directed activity and supports the corresponding content-governed uses. The realization must preserve relevant role bindings and at least the discriminations in the declared use contract. It must permit \(c\) to be treated as a conjectural target rather than merely enact a received instruction. The retained-capability definition in the constructor section gives the modal meaning of “supports.”"
- **Understanding ≠ agreement/truth/success** (700): "A system may understand a false theory. The use task can concern the theory's own organization while its application to the world fails. Understanding is not defined as agreement, correct prediction of the world, or actual success of the explanation."
- **Deploy / repertoire** (702–708): `Deploy_{β,ℓ}(s,c,ξ;U)` "hold[s] when the attributed system possesses such a realization and an owned capability for the declared use task \(U\) at \(\xi\). The repertoire \(R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\) consists of the contents deployable in at least one nontrivial explanatory use respect. Storage, universal mathematical consequence, and possible acquisition after further learning are not sufficient for membership."
- **Simulator distinction** (710): "A generic simulator is not automatically an understander because it produces the right trajectories. Its represented reference, integration into problem-directed activity, and ability to make the simulated account a critical target must also be present. Conversely, no substrate or notation is excluded merely because it is a simulation or a program."

### 2.15 Construction / transfer (712–724)
- **Construction witness** (714): "A construction witness concerns an actual suborganization of a history. It identifies the controlled processes, incoming carriers and already operative organizations, the bindings constructed or reconstructed, and the resulting representation. Its physical and semantic maps must preserve the relevant component structure, as in (E)."
- **Transfer vs binding construction** (716): "An elementary **transfer** is content-preserving carriage of an already integrated organization at the stated grain. An elementary **binding construction** changes or establishes a relation between represented parts in the recipient's operative organization. These are classifications of actual transformations under the interpretation, not intrinsic types conferred by a model's invitation or by a host event label."
- **Build** (718): "Define \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) when an actual subhistory owned or controlled by \(s\), delimited at \(e\), prepares a represented organization for an attempted explanatory use of \(c\), contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not merely a composition of content-preserving transfers of the already operative organization. A structural account of that subhistory must connect the constructed binding to the resulting use. Electricity supply, incidental causal ancestry, and ownership of an output file do not by themselves meet the condition."
- **Ownership/control** (720): "“Owned or controlled” refers to the boundary's actual action-producing organization. In a collaboration it can be distributed. A list of contributors is not a construction witness. Overlapping attributions are possible when their respective causal-organizational claims are true; they do not create a numerical credit allocation."
- **Reconstructive construction vs relay vs copy** (722): "A learner may receive a complete exposition and nevertheless construct the bindings through which it becomes usable to that learner. That is reconstructive construction, not historical firstness. A relay may deliver the same exposition without such construction. A physically faithful copy of an already functioning cognitive organization, if permitted by the relevant physics, must not be pronounced a fresh invention merely because the recipient's address is new. The interpretation must distinguish transfer of an operative organization from its reconstruction."
- **Scope of the predicate** (724): "The construction predicate is not an assertion that every acquisition must be creative. It is an explicit provenance condition to be established."

### 2.16 Newness (726–750)
- **Baseline** (728–736): `R_{<e}(s,h) = ⋃_{ξ before e} R_{β,ℓ}(s,ξ)`, "including the initial repertoire within the stipulated continuity"; preceded by (728) "Fix a system boundary, grain, history, and continuity criterion before assessing an event."
- **New (N)** (738–744): `New(s,c,h,e) ⟺ ¬∃d∈R_{<e}(s,h), d ≡_ℓ c`.
- **Equivalence grain** (746): "The equivalence is structural at the stated grain, not string equality or similarity. Forgotten prior understanding belongs to \(R_{<e}\) when the claim is first creation by that continuing organization. Reacquisition can therefore be an achievement without being first creation."
- **Three-way distinction** (748): "Acquisition concerns absence from the immediately prior deployable repertoire and presence afterward. Newness concerns the whole stipulated historical baseline. Construction concerns provenance. These relations can come apart."
- **Archive caveat** (750): "An incomplete archive leaves a historical claim uncertain. It does not make the absent content historically new. Initializing a new system boundary with an already functioning organization must include that organization in its baseline rather than manufacture novelty by forgetting its initialization."

### 2.17 Origination (752–769)
- **Attempted account / Attempt** (754): "An attempted account is a represented organization actually used to address a recognized difficulty, whether or not it satisfies (E). Write this relation as \(\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)\). Its interpretation includes the active question and the content's operative role; a task label alone is insufficient."
- **Origin (G)** (756–765): `Origin_{β,ℓ}(s,c,p,h,e) ⟺ Attempt(s,c,p,h,e) ∧ New(s,c,h,e) ∧ Build_{β,ℓ}(s,c,h,e)`.
- **Non-conjuncts** (767): "No truth, universal capacity, permanent storage, or completed criticism of this very output is a conjunct. A false explanatory conjecture can be originative. An account of an inherited theory's defect can be originative even though the original theory was not authored by the critic."
- **Mechanism-neutrality** (769): "Randomness, deterministic generation, deduction, or search can participate in the physical process. They neither establish nor disqualify (G) by themselves. The relevant issue is whether the event constructs the attributed problem-specific organization, rather than merely producing an output that an external interpreter finds useful."

### 2.18 Episodes (771–777)
- **Complete critical episode** (773): "A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a represented target available before the criticism of it, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response with a resulting situation. The objection need not have actual bearing, and the response need not improve the situation. An incomplete engagement is represented without being called complete."
- **Creative critical episode** (775): "A creative critical episode is a complete critical episode containing an originative contribution under (G) connected to its inquiry. That contribution can precede or follow criticism of a different, inherited target. Conjecture-before-criticism remains a target dependence, not a global three-stage schedule."
- **Closing** (777): "Closing an episode is an engagement decision. It may accompany acceptance, rejection, suspension, disregard, or a decision to investigate elsewhere. It does not prove that the problem is solved, and the episode can be reopened or absorbed into another."

## 3. Claims about how explanatory construction works

**What an account is / what accounting requires (in this chunk):**
- (620) `Bearing(c,z,p) ⟺ Account(𝓔_c, p_δ)` — criticism is held to the *same* account standard as explanation; a criticism explains a defect.
- (714) "Its physical and semantic maps must preserve the relevant component structure, as in (E)." — construction witnesses are held to (E)-style structure preservation.
- (718) "A structural account of that subhistory must connect the constructed binding to the resulting use."
- (754) "An attempted account is a represented organization actually used to address a recognized difficulty, whether or not it satisfies (E)." — attempting ≠ succeeding.
- (560) "Equation (E) permits either the functional case or this relational case."
- (400) An account can be correct about identification and wrong as an account of production; question-relativity is constitutive.

**How reasons are used:**
- (626) "A response uses a reason when the represented content of the objection participates in the organization's deliberative transition."
- (628) reason-use witness = structural map + recoding invariance + content-sensitivity + "the mapped objection is on an active dependency route of this response occurrence. The map must preserve internal role bindings, not merely the endpoint string."
- (601) "For reason-use and contribution claims the subnetwork must also have a nonconstant dependence on the relevant represented distinction under its declared contrasts."
- (638) "Standing is the system's enacted permission to use a content in a particular application and respect."
- (708) repertoire membership requires deployability "in at least one nontrivial explanatory use respect."

**What criticism does:**
- (609) criticism = target + defect + grounds + connection, relative to a question.
- (622) "A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) is false." / "A mere adverse signal is not made into a criticism by giving it a negative label."
- (481) "The criticism removes that route for trusting the calibration, not the logical possibility that the calibration is correct."
- (657) "Withdrawing a criticism likewise does not establish the truth of its target."
- (661) "A passing execution cannot make the program's specification, the model of the experiment, or the claimed relevance immune to prose criticism."
- (653) "A record declaring a dependency does not establish that the dependency is actually essential; an error in that declaration is another criticism target."
- (605) "Attention is itself an activity in the history. Its organization and the values affecting it are potential criticism targets."
- (510) "A correct application of an unjust rule can be criticized through a normative question about the rule itself without denying which rule was applied."

**Repair / error-correction:**
- (483) "This distinction repairs a recurring confusion between whether a content is correct and whether a particular argument for adopting it succeeds."
- (655–657) Elimination without a truth machine: withdrawal of a premise de-licenses an argument via (K2) without touching truth; "Another argument \(v\) for the same conclusion can remain usable."
- (659) removal from working use ≠ deletion of the historical occurrence.
- (593) A transport must declare whether it is "the earlier application was always mistaken" repair or "preserve an accurate approximation while explaining its limits" repair.
- (676) (K3) locates *where* repair can act: `B` (auxiliaries) and `I` (observation interpretation) as well as `T`.
- (632) misfires are expected: "A valid criticism can lead to retention when its alleged force has been misunderstood. An invalid criticism can lead to a mistaken revision."

**Understanding / creativity / explanatory bearing:**
- (698) understanding definition (quoted §2.14); crucially "It must permit \(c\) to be treated as a conjectural target rather than merely enact a received instruction."
- (700) "Understanding is not defined as agreement, correct prediction of the world, or actual success of the explanation."

- (765/G) creativity = Attempt ∧ New ∧ Build; (767) "A false explanatory conjecture can be originative."
- (769) "The relevant issue is whether the event constructs the attributed problem-specific organization, rather than merely producing an output that an external interpreter finds useful."
- (383) reach and new bridges: "The new bridge is itself content and can be a creative contribution."
- (775) creative critical episode.

**What is NOT sufficient (explicit negatives):**
- Syntax/strings: (601) "not inferred from the presence of a similar sentence in a record"; (628) "not merely the endpoint string"; (680) "Equal text in two events does not identify their provenance or metadata"; (746) "not string equality or similarity."
- Execution: (661) "A passing execution cannot make the program's specification… immune to prose criticism."

- Storage/consequence/ancestry/ownership: (708) "Storage, universal mathematical consequence, and possible acquisition after further learning are not sufficient"; (718) "Electricity supply, incidental causal ancestry, and ownership of an output file do not by themselves meet the condition"; (720) "A list of contributors is not a construction witness."

## 5. Testable predictions / observables licensed

**Discriminating experiments (what evidence would count):**
- **Content-change vs recoding vs carrier-disturbance triple** (630): "Its contrast contract must include a content-changing case, a content-preserving recoding, and the distinction between a change in the objection and an irrelevant carrier disturbance wherever those distinctions are claimed." → Prediction: a genuine reason-user changes response when the objection's *content* changes, does not change under content-preserving recoding, and does not change under carrier disturbance. The converse profile refutes reason-use.
- **Active-route negatives** (601): "A dead branch, a cancelled signal with no downstream role, or a carrier detail that changes while the represented reason does not change cannot supply that dependence." → Observable: presence of the objection text on a causally inert path is not evidence of use.
- **Same-decision/different-reason** (632): "Two objections can lead to the same decision for different represented reasons." → Prediction: outcome-level agreement is not diagnostic; decision-identity underdetermines reason-identity.
- **Simulator test** (710): "A generic simulator is not automatically an understander because it produces the right trajectories. Its represented reference, integration into problem-directed activity, and ability to make the simulated account a critical target must also be present." → Observable: can the system make its own simulated account a target of criticism?
- **Relay vs reconstructor** (722): "A learner may receive a complete exposition and nevertheless construct the bindings … A relay may deliver the same exposition without such construction." → Observable: identical outputs, different provenance verdicts.
- **Re-initialization test** (750): novelty claims must survive inclusion of the initializing organization in the baseline.
- **Understanding a false theory** (700): "A system may understand a false theory." → Predicts dissociation of understanding-scores from truth-scores.
- **Originative falsehood** (767): "A false explanatory conjecture can be originative." → Predicts dissociation of creativity verdicts from correctness verdicts.
- **Mechanism-orthogonality** (769): randomness/determinism/search "neither establish nor disqualify (G)".

**Exact mathematical observables (worked cases usable as fixtures):**
- (434) "\(g(x)=x^2\) on \(Z=\mathbb R\) does not identify the sign of a nonzero \(x\). On \(Z=[0,\infty)\), it identifies \(x\)." → Predicts: adding a *domain constraint* changes identifiability; adding repeated observations does not.
- (479) "Repetition of the same rows does not change the kernel. An independently applicable calibration can add a constraint that does." → Predicts a measurable asymmetry between duplicated and independent evidence.
- (462–468) two balances: "the readings identify \(b_B-b_A=2\), but not \(x\)". → Correct system output is the identified contrast plus an explicit non-identification.
- (502/504) invariant obstruction; "For twenty-three indivisible tokens, an equal division among three recipients would require an integer \(k\) with \(3k=23\)." → Predicts: impossibility results are derivable and are not defeated by relaxing the operation contract.
- (502) "Equal invariant values alone do not establish reachability." → A system that infers reachability from matching invariants is making a detectable error.
- (572–583) (T2) `e_n ≤ ε Σ L^k`. → Predicts measurable error growth under repeated approximate transport; (587) absent Lipschitz/modulus, no bound follows — a system asserting one is refuted.
- (524) finite-composition transport holds "provided the intermediate scopes match" → scope mismatch is an observable failure mode.
- (663–676) (K3): "an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\)" → Predicts: a correct system reports the disjunctive target of refutation, not `¬T`.
- (398) intervention on `L` does not alter `H` or `θ` → an observable intervention signature distinguishing forward account from inversion.
- (400) "A pole intentionally constructed to cast a specified shadow would have a different causal organization." → same variables, different direction verdict.

**Negative observables (things that must NOT count as evidence):**
- (688) timeouts/inability-to-evaluate as refutation; (686) absent receipt as negative observation; (680) equal text as provenance; (622) adverse signal as criticism; (640) prompt presence as use; (718) file ownership as construction; (720) contributor list as construction witness.

### 6.1 Terms with a plausible counterpart

### 6.2 Terms with NO counterpart in an attack/support/status framework

These are the ones a mechanism spec will most likely fail to represent; each is quoted with its line.

Reinforced at (640): "Actual use of the source as a premise, actual understanding, and actual criticism are semantic attributions."

### 6.3 The three sharpest warnings for a mechanism spec built on adjudication

1. **(684)** "These are descriptions of available arguments, not four kinds of reality." — a four-status (accepted/refuted/suspended/undecided) verdict layer is licensed only as a *record of available arguments*, never as a semantic fact about the claim.
2. **(640) + (716) + (754)** — host-maintained labels (a "criticism" field, an event type, a task label) never constitute the semantic relation they name.
3. **(381) + (605) + (720)** — no counting: not of features, not of objections by recency, not of credit. Any scalar aggregate is outside what FW5 licenses.

## 4. Requirements/constraints on any harness, experiment, language, or measurement claiming to study explanation

1. **Must supply the full primitive data, typed.** 1152: "An FW5 model supplies physical and historical data \(\Theta,\mathcal H\), organization structures \(D\) satisfying (O), content and occurrence domains, the representation interpretation at declared grains and boundaries, question contracts, actual control and appraisal histories, a continuity specification, and any normative relations invoked by its questions." 1192: membership requires "respecting their typing, and satisfying the physical-realization and reference-coherence conditions whenever a physical attribution is made."
2. **Grades and their operational meaning must be fixed by a subsidiary theory, not by the harness's own scoring.** 1161: "with the operational meaning of those grades fixed by the subsidiary physical theory."
3. **Resources and side effects must be held fixed; no smuggling via existentials.** 1172: "The resource and side-effect class for \(T\) is held fixed in this statement. An infinitely expensive or otherwise disallowed resource is not smuggled in by the existential quantifier."
4. **Capability claims require a finite owned construction history from presently owned processes.** 1174: "That history must start with presently owned processes and admitted resources, not with an unexplained constructor for the desired achievement."
5. **Ownership and process occurrence must be physical facts, not metadata.** 1176: "The model does not recover those facts from filename ownership or timestamps."
6. **Nothing is made true by being entered into the model.** 1176: "none is made true by being entered into the model."
7. **No definitional circularity; no appraisal-defines-knowledge.** 1188: "There is no definition of knowledge by an appraisal that already presupposes knowledge."
8. **A definition must be usable without a terminating recursive check.** 1188: "A represented claim about a definition is an object of inquiry, not a recursive call that must terminate before the definition can be used."
9. **The class must be able to represent failure.** 1192: "It can describe irrational systems, false conjectures, obstructed inquiry, missing evidence, and failed attempts."
10. **Recoding must be by structure-preserving bijections; compression/coarsening/boundary changes are not permitted as "same system".** 1202, 1206: "Suppose all carriers, component relations, role bindings, maps, histories, question contracts, and attribution indices are transported along bijections preserving their structure." / "The result does not apply to arbitrary compression, loss of event identity, a changed boundary, or a coarsening that identifies a relevant distinction."
11. **A measurement that projects to I/O cannot decide explanatory organization.** 1218: "no function of \(P(M)\) alone agrees with the accounting predicate on both models." 1222: "The same argument applies to attribution from identical emitted text and to semantic use inferred from delivery logs."
12. **Dependence-to-work inference requires the explanatory-route premise.** 1228, 1232.
13. **Receipts/logs may not be promoted to semantic truth.** 1238: "record-state equality does not entail equality of semantic satisfaction without the relevant application premises. This is compatible with reliable checks of narrower record facts."
14. **Representation and control assumptions must be declared, not read off labels.** 1248: "The investigator's representation and control assumptions are part of the witness and are not derived from printed labels."
15. **A toy/scripted demonstrator is a consistency witness only.** 1248: "Such a module is a relative consistency witness for an episode interpretation, not evidence that a scripted demonstrator, an actual language model, or a human brain has been experimentally shown to satisfy the class."

17. **Domains must be specified independently of the candidate's successes** (1105, immediately before my chunk; carried through by 1252's "second independently specified domain" and 1146's "independently specified barrier").

19. **Grain and protected obligations must be fixed in advance, not chosen after the fact.** 1380: "It must not be dismissed by inventing a favorable new grain after the fact." 1378: "A dishonest choice of protected obligations can conceal severe losses."
20. **No merit function, no frequency update.** 1264: "None of (E), (G), (P), (EK), or (U3) depends on an empirical-frequency update or a merit maximization."

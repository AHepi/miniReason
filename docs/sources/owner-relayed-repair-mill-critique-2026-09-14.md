## Verbatim text

Direct answer: as sketched, the repair mill does not satisfy the letter of the definitions — it fails at three separate letter-level clauses, and the clauses it fails are precisely the ones FW5 wrote for exactly this attack. Your unease is warranted, but it's not tracking a hole in the definitions; it's tracking something else, which I'll name at the end.

Take the mill's own components one at a time. "Retrieves a stock answer" — retrieval is, by the document's own taxonomy, an elementary *transfer*: content-preserving carriage of an already integrated organization. The origin predicate (G) requires Build, and Build requires a nontrivial binding construction in the run's own subhistory, "not merely a composition of content-preserving transfers of the already operative organization." A fixed rewrite applied to a retrieved stock answer is exactly such a composition: the template is the already-operative organization, and the binding between answer and difficulty was constructed at design time. So the construction witness doesn't point into the mill's run at all — it points to the designer's history. The attribution slides off the mill onto its author, which is the document's boundary discipline working as intended: "owned or controlled" refers to the actual action-producing organization, and a list of contributors is not a construction witness. New fails too, and faster: the stock answer is deployable from the start — the mill deploys it every run — so it was already in the deployable repertoire before run one, and by run two even the rewritten output is old content at any grain that matters.

"The same canned objection every time" fails a different clause, and it's the decisive one. A complete critical episode requires a content-sensitive response, and the reason-use definition comes with an explicit contrast contract: the system's response must show a nonconstant dependence on the objection's represented content — change the grounds or the alleged defect and the response must change as the operative deliberative rule specifies. Swap the mill's canned objection for a different one, or for noise, and the mill does exactly the same thing. The document has a name for this: a dead branch, a decorative transcript channel permanently disconnected from the operative state — and it says flatly that a dead branch cannot supply the dependence, and that a task label alone is insufficient. So the mill's episodes aren't failed creative episodes; they aren't critical episodes at all. And "rigged to need exactly that rewrite" trips a fourth wire: the enabling-condition clause declares inadmissible any condition "whose only description is the conditions under which the system succeeds," and (EK)'s ProducesVia requires the contribution — not the rigging — to be on the repair's active route. A task rigged to need the rewrite puts the rigging on the route. Four letter-level failures, each one a clause the document wrote with this species of machine in view. FW5 didn't get caught by the repair mill; it was built in anticipation of it.

Now the honest part, because "by the letter" has a second half. Stiffen the mill until it passes those four clauses: give it a library of objections selected by features of the input, rewrites that vary with the objection's content, and follow-up tasks that recur naturally rather than by rigging. Now the response is content-sensitive, the bindings are constructed in-run, the contents are new. The letter now certifies every run — and the framework's answer is: correct, and not a reductio. The document pre-chewed this bullet: randomness, deduction, search, and mechanism neither establish nor disqualify; the anti-behaviorism clause cuts in both directions. A mechanical system doing genuine content-sensitive binding construction is doing small-scale creative work in the semantics' sense, and your intuition that "nobody would call it creative" is exactly the intuition the constitutive conjecture was written to overrule — or to be defeated by, but defeat requires showing which clause misclassifies, not just reporting the intuition.

Which brings me to what your unease is actually tracking, because it's real and you should keep it. "A machine that manufactures certificates of creativity" trades on the honorific reading of creativity — the sense in which a certificate should mean something. The semantics deliberately de-honorified the predicate: a small creative act is cheap, common, and "not a certificate of personhood, consciousness, moral standing, or universal intelligence" — the document says so in almost those words. What the sophisticated mill can legitimately manufacture is certificates of *small* episodes. What it cannot manufacture is significance, because significance was never in the predicate; it lives in the obligations, and the obligations are themselves criticizable — "the legitimacy of the purpose can itself be a question." The genuinely soft spot, and I'll flag it rather than defend it, is the converse: because repair is local and unranked, a mill stocked with *correct* accounts of *trivial* questions, genuinely constructed, can accumulate real (P) and (EK) attributions of vanishing weight, and the framework's only answer is that it never promised weight. If you think a semantics of creativity owes a significance floor that isn't obligation-relative, that's a legitimate challenge to draft — but it's a challenge to the definitions' *ambition*, not a counterexample to their truth, and it should be filed as such so it doesn't get confused with one.

One last thing, and it converts your attempt from failed refutation to useful instrument. The repair mill is the perfect calibration negative for the T3 language-model study: it's the simplest artifact that produces all the right *outputs* — objections, revisions, downstream uses — while failing the internal-route requirements. That's exactly the distinction T3 exists to measure, since output-equivalence provably can't separate the mill from a system doing real binding construction; only the content-sensitivity contrasts and provenance analysis can. So keep the mill, but reclassify it: not a counterexample to the semantics, but the control group the semantics demands. If you want, I'll write it up that way — as T6, the null case — with the four clause failures worked line by line and the sophisticated-mill variant analyzed as the framework's deliberate, pre-registered bullet-bite.

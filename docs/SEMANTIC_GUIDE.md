# What miniReason can establish

miniReason tests whether deliberately constructed Mini configurations help a model produce and use explanatory repairs, compared with the same model answering directly and using supported native reasoning. The engineering comparison and the test of Explanatory Construction Semantics (ECS) are related but distinct. Better task results can show that a configuration helps within the tested conditions; they do not by themselves prove creativity or the correctness of ECS.

The current source pair is **Conjecture–Criticism Harness — Formal Build Spec v1.3** and **Explanatory Construction Semantics 2.0**. V1.3 guides implementation choices. ECS supplies conditions for attribution claims. Mini is a configurable implementation substrate; extracting it from h-EPI does not make it a complete implementation of V1.3 or ECS. Existing Mini artifact kinds are routing and interface declarations, not an exhaustive ontology of thought. Prose explanations and criticisms remain substantive material when a machine reading fails.

See [the experimental method](EXPERIMENT_METHOD.md) before designing a template or interpreting a result. [The project purpose](../PURPOSE.md) controls the direction of the work; implementation conveniences must not narrow its question silently.

## Source limitations that remain visible

The supplied ECS PDF has mathematical rendering defects. Visual inspection of pages 4–5 confirms that §2.3's graded-accounting formula ends at `[ Account`, many mathematical symbols are absent, and the §3.4 HardToVary formula extends beyond the page edge. These defects occur in the rendered source, not just text extraction. Several constructions are retained by reference to ECS 1.0 without being reproduced. The prose and the five accounting conditions in §2.2 remain usable. Missing formulas must not be reconstructed silently or treated as implemented guarantees. Testing a declared finite contrast family is a bounded empirical operation; it is not an implementation of the missing graded formula.

ECS §3.1 refers to the typed restriction algebra of §2.2, although the algebra is introduced in §2.1. V1.3 cites theorems in a separate creativity calculus; those citations do not supply proofs from ECS. Source hashes, interpretations and source-integrity errata preserve these distinctions.

## The attribution obligations

A completed response proves that an output was delivered. Schema validity proves that a declared interface was satisfied. An exact verifier can establish a bounded proposition under its specified interpretation and auxiliary assumptions. None of these is equivalent to the claims below.

| Claim | What ECS additionally requires | Evidence still insufficient |
|---|---|---|
| Structural account | The five §2.2 conditions: structural fidelity, composition fidelity, question fidelity, non-circular dependence and non-vacuity, with appropriate anchors | A correct answer, an unanchored answer table, or plausible mechanism words |
| Reason use | A representation-preserving map into the operative response on an active route; content-preserving recodings preserve relevant behavior and content changes affect behavior according to the stated deliberative rule (§§5.1–5.2) | Rendering a criticism into a prompt, mentioning an artifact ID, or reporting that a criticism was useful |
| Understanding | Represented content integrated into problem-directed activity, preserved bindings and discriminations, conjectural scrutiny and relevant owned deployment (§6.1) | Storage, retrieval, a generic simulator, or a hypothetical reader's ability to use the output |
| Originative contribution | Attempt, historical newness and an owned or controlled construction involving nontrivial binding construction (§§6.2–6.3) | A new string, a correct copied organization or an incomplete archive |
| Repair | At least one fixed failed obligation becomes satisfied through the change, and all previously satisfied protected obligations remain satisfied (§9.1) | Fixing one visible example while breaking protected behavior |
| Created explanatory knowledge | A complete creative critical episode, attributable repair, an originative explanatory account in the result, deployment and a ProducesVia link (§9.2) | Task success, a survivor score, or a saved proposed rule without operative use |
| Universal capability | The owned capability, admissible enabling and independently fixed domain quantifiers of Parts 7–8 | Any finite run, any single novel contribution, or one extension beyond a prior domain |

False conjectures can be originative (§6.3). Understanding a false theory is possible (§6.1). A complete episode ends with a decision, not a solution certificate (§6.3). Therefore creativity, correctness and closure must not be collapsed into one pass/fail field.

## Registry before results

Every attribution names the primitive supplies on which it relies (ECS Part 0, R1-1). An unnamed dependency makes an attribution incomplete; it becomes false when a named supply is falsified. Declare which entries are not invoked.

| Primitive | What a campaign records |
|---|---|
| P1 representation | How source, prompt, artifact, interpretation and operative program occurrences represent contents and bindings; the coding and content-preserving recodings |
| P2 physical module | Model/revision, executable software, resources, process controls, admitted inputs and operational failure envelope |
| P3 component roles | Which elements are assignments, measurements, constitutive rules, boundary conditions or derived relations; what each intervention replaces |
| P4 normative relation, when invoked | The substantive reason relation and respect; technical tests normally do not invoke aesthetic or normative attribution |
| P5 enabling conditions | The available tools, sources, feedback and operator contribution, described independently of success |
| P6 domains, when invoked | The task family and challenges fixed independently of the candidate's observed successes |
| P7 grain and boundary | Which functional differences matter, where the attributed system ends, and which contrasts are admissible |

Grain, boundary and contrast contract are fixed before relevant contrast evidence is examined (§9.4). Changing them creates a new claim with new registry entries and requires re-examination of evidence. It cannot retroactively absorb a counterexample.

A task intervention must respect its component role. Setting an assigned variable replaces the original assignment; appending an inconsistent observation while retaining the assignment is not the same operation (§1.1, P3). This distinction matters directly in causal and protocol examples.

## Newness, construction and ownership

The system boundary normally includes the frozen model, Mini, its operative configuration, admitted tools and recorded state. Attribution to the model alone is a different claim requiring an additional argument about which organization Mini and the operator supplied.

ECS §6.2 defines newness relative to the earlier deployable repertoire at the declared grain. A closed run log shows what was recorded in that run; it does not expose the entire model's prior repertoire. Unknown pretraining therefore leaves historical newness unresolved for many model-wide claims. Generating a fresh task instance reduces literal example reuse but does not prove that its functional solution was absent beforehand. Report **new relative to the recorded initial operative organization** separately from full ECS New.

Record the initial operative organization, the proposed constructed change, its actual installation and its later use on an unseen instance. A no-return control can retain identical proposed bytes in the archive while leaving the old organization operative. A transfer-only control receives a functioning solution. These comparisons help separate construction, custody and use. Neither erases the historical-newness residue.

A finite policy language supplies operations and composition rules. If it supplies the target solution as a ready-made option, choosing that option tests selection, not unrestricted originative construction. This may remain a useful engineering experiment, but the language and its interpreter are explicit enabling contributions under §8.3. A restricted executable target can provide an exact checker; it must not become a hidden claim that creativity itself has that restricted syntax.

## Criticism, failed tests and repair

ECS §5.4 states that a failed prediction contradicts the candidate theory together with its auxiliaries and interpretation. It does not automatically isolate the candidate. Criticism should be able to inspect the original account, its machine reading, the target and the checker assumptions. A faulty translation and a faulty theory are different failures.

The experiment freezes repair obligations O and protected obligations P. A patch or policy is a demonstrated bounded repair only when it fixes a named failure, preserves protected successes and has the claimed causal connection to those changes (§9.1). Losses outside P are exposed. A correct account of underdetermination can repair an incorrect claim to identification.

For semantic contribution, investigate blocks as well as individual components. ECS §3.1 assumes neither upward closure nor minimal support. §3.2 allows jointly necessary blocks even where no singleton contributes. The finite monotone theorem (§3.3) cannot be imported into a nonmonotone support family merely because the task is finite.

## Hardness, measures and capture

ECS §3.4 distinguishes contract-relative preservation bookkeeping from intrinsic constrained variation. The latter requires a content-licensed variation family determined through anchoring at the declared grain. A randomly chosen edit kernel and a count of surviving variants do not establish intrinsic hardness. Neither notion supplies a numerical warrant.

V1.3 §§6–7 describe HV as a fallible spot-check standing on assumptions about its kernel and equivalence surrogate. §0 forbids measures from deciding status. ECS §9.3 similarly supplies no universal merit function: endorsement counts, survival counts and feature counts are not warrants. Quantitative measurements can describe an experiment and guide engineering attention while substantive conclusions remain tied to particular obligations and reasons.

V1.3 §§11.3 and 17 acknowledge that capture diagnostics detect stalled dynamics rather than wrong-but-stable consensus. Similarity is not proof of capture, diversity is not proof of creativity, and an unchanged status need not mean no useful inquiry occurred. miniReason requires visible failures and meaningful diagnostic records, not a progress meter.

## Testing ECS itself

The ECS introduction and §10.2 distinguish its constitutive conjecture from its applications. A genuine sufficiency counterexample must satisfy the accounting conditions under a pre-fixed grain, with named primitive supplies unfalsified, while an independent case argument establishes that it is no explanation. A necessity challenge requires a genuine explanation that cannot meet the conditions under the relevant predeclared grains. Merely failing a parser, a task test or an anchoring condition defeats neither conjecture.

A separate potential attack concerns §3.4: whether the anchors actually determine the intrinsic variation family. If determining it requires an additional substantive choice, expose that choice rather than representing it as derived. Normative and non-causal mathematical cases are named stress domains in §10.2, but their independent explanatory judgment cannot be replaced with model agreement or an exact-output checker.

The repository can prove conditional properties of its own implementation and produce empirical witnesses under fixed contracts. Any stronger claim states each remaining interpretation, history, ownership and capability dependency. ECS §10.3 describes this as a contract for attribution claims; using its terms does not discharge that contract.

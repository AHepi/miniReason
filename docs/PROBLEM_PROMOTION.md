# Problem promotion: what an encountered issue licenses

Status: a proposed research and integration contract, with eight conditional results. This document changes no running experiment, engine label rule, or frozen language proposal. Its results concern the stated structures and premises; they are not empirical findings about the current model.

The current research target is exploratory: how proposed languages and semantic classes change what can be expressed and done through Mini, including components that may expand or hinder creative inquiry. Correct capture of the problem is not an admission prerequisite or the starting point of the experiment. A proposal may be partial, mistaken, non-Lean, or poorly matched to Mini and still be an informative specimen. Language-internal expressibility, expressibility through Mini's configured interfaces, and successful operative use are different questions.

The governing sources are [ECS 2.0](sources/ECS-2.0.pdf), especially Parts 0, 1, 3, 5, 8 and 9, and the supplied [V1.3 build specification](sources/harness-spec-v1.3.md), especially §§0–5, 9, 12 and 17. The user's current instruction requires a model-proposed language whose own adequacy is criticizable, with prose remaining fully legitimate and initial proposals frozen during their first tests. This instruction takes precedence over V1.3 §§8 and 10 wherever their skeleton-only treatment would demote prose. The results about language representation, proof adequacy and prose admission are developed separately in [Language Proposal Theorems](LANGUAGE_PROPOSAL_THEOREMS.md).

A source limitation matters here. The supplied ECS PDF visibly omits some mathematical glyphs and truncates its displayed graded-accounting formula in §2.3. This document relies on the legible conditions in §2.2 and the legible prose and definitions cited below. It does not reconstruct the missing formula or silently import an unseen earlier version. Section numbers identify the intended passages even where the PDF's own cross-references are inconsistent.

## The central distinction

Promoting an issue into a successor problem means choosing a question for further investigation. It does not mean accepting the issue's diagnosis, refuting its target, endorsing its proposed remedy, or changing what may count as a reason. The question can be, “Does this objection bear on this target?” An already completed proof of bearing cannot therefore be a universal prerequisite for investigating bearing.

ECS §5.2 distinguishes an objection's occurrence from its bearing and its use. ECS §5.3 distinguishes standing from truth. V1.3 separates Spawn and attention from the attack and dependence structures used in adjudication. These distinctions allow an unresolved objection to receive work while its content remains disputed. They also allow a well-grounded objection to remain unfunded under a finite allocation without its grounds disappearing.

Two claims must be recorded separately whenever both are made. The investigation claim says why a particular question is worth pursuing under a particular contract. The criticism claim says which content is challenged, by which account of a relevant discrepancy, and under which interpretation and auxiliary premises. Evidence sufficient for the former need not establish the latter. Neither claim becomes stronger because the problem received more tokens.

In the notation of the companion document, the tested proposal is a frozen package

\[
P_v=(L_v,I_v,e_v,d_v,\text{registry}_v,\text{contract}_v).
\]

The language's expressions, proof rules, interpretation and translations are all possible targets. The target question remains

\[
p=(D,\text{scope},b_0,\text{respect},C,Q,O_p)
\]

at a declared boundary \(\beta\) and grain \(\ell\). A complaint about \(L_v\)'s expressive resources can be a successor question about \(P_v\) even when no expression of \(L_v\) can formulate the complaint. Its prose representation is retained and interpreted on its own stated terms. Translation into the candidate language, when attempted, is a further conjecture that may itself fail.

The package notation does not require a Lean formalization. For a non-Lean semantic class, \(L_v\) names the proposed representational organization, and any expression, inference, interpretation or translation component can be partial or undeclared. Such absence is part of the specimen. It is not silently replaced with a working formal calculus. The first inquiry can simply be what a particular proposed component lets Mini distinguish, construct, criticize or use, and where that route is blocked.

A failure to serialize through Mini establishes a limitation of that attempted route under its actual codec, adapter and prompt. Promoting “the language is semantically inadequate” from that occurrence requires a further argument about the intended content and task. The next problem can instead compare a different rendering, an explicit bridge, a prose occurrence or a different candidate language. No scalar measure of progress or expressiveness is supplied by this procedure.

## Admissibility for investigation

The accounting below is a proposed operating discipline, not an ECS definition and not a demand that all reasons first fit a formal schema. Each entry may be supplied by a clear prose passage. A mechanical envelope can check identities and file references; it cannot replace the substantive reading.

| Account owed before allocating a successor investigation | What is recorded | What remains permissible when it is unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger and provenance | The actual occurrence, its original bytes, the experiment or observation coordinate, and any diagnosis added later | A reported but unavailable occurrence can motivate an evidence-acquisition question; it is not recorded as an observed fact |
| Intended target and respect | The claim, language feature, interpretation, proof rule, translation, implementation, task contract, or allocation rule being questioned | If several targets remain live, the successor can ask how to discriminate among them |
| Proposed relevance | How the issue could change an answer, an operative use, or the admissibility of an existing argument | “Bearing unresolved” is legitimate when the successor explicitly investigates that gap |
| Alternatives and dependency assumptions | What would have to be true for the diagnosis to follow; which rival accounts of the observation remain available | An unresolved alternative remains visible rather than being converted into a refutation by default |
| Successor question and discriminating contrast | What the next inquiry is trying to distinguish; what different outcomes would mean under the stated interpretation | A contrast can be a substantive prose case argument; a runnable check is not mandatory |
| Preservation and frame | Which parent obligations, grains, boundaries, contrasts, and already claimed successful uses must survive a proposed repair | A changed frame creates a separately identified question or assessment |
| Allocation and return | Who or what will investigate, the practical resource ceiling, where observations will be recorded, and what result could later affect operative use | Deferral and suspension are decisions about work; a missing result does not become a content verdict |

A minimal intelligible promotion must identify a question, its relation to an existing inquiry or newly declared aim, and what is currently uncertain. If the target is not yet identifiable, the next problem is to identify it. If the relevance is obscure, the next problem is to determine relevance. If the only occurrence is a timeout, the next problem may concern delivery or instrumentation. These are transformations of the question, not repairs to missing evidence.

Admission is not universal priority. ECS supplies no global merit function ordering every legitimate problem. Choosing among admitted problems, setting token ceilings, selecting a repeat count, and deciding when to stop a block are additional research decisions. They are recorded with reasons and remain criticizable.

## Failure roles must stay distinct

These rows classify the role an observation plays in a particular argument. They are not permanent semantic types of artifacts. One occurrence can support several differently scoped arguments.

| Encountered issue | What the available observation can establish | Successor question that preserves the distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Provider delivery failure or watchdog termination | The requested observation was not obtained through the declared route | What prevented delivery, and can the intended contrast still be obtained? |
| JSON, parsing, elaboration, or Lean compilation failure | The submitted occurrence failed a stated formation or translation contract | Is the construction malformed, is the adapter incorrect, or does the representation contract exclude the intended content? |
| A completed executable mismatch | Candidate, auxiliaries, interpretation, and test expectation cannot all stand together as assumed | Which dependency accounts for the discrepancy, and what contrast discriminates the live diagnoses? |
| A proved theorem under candidate axioms | That conclusion follows in that formal system, subject to the actual proof checker and declarations | Do the interpretation and axioms account for the independently posed question? |
| Prose criticism of the language or checker | A represented objection exists; its bearing must be considered on the content supplied | Does the objection exhibit a relevant lost distinction, false rule, mistaken scope, or unsupported interpretation? |
| Missing evidence or an incomplete archive | The relevant evidence or historical attribution remains unresolved | What observation or provenance reconstruction would settle the named uncertainty? |
| Repeated outputs, agreement, or a contraction signal | A pattern in the recorded generator or review behavior | Is an operative distinction being lost, or is repetition appropriate to this question? |
| A changed task contract or system boundary | A new assessment is being proposed | What changed, why, and which old observations require re-examination? |

Syntax can itself be the relevant content of a narrowly framed claim: “this submission elaborates under the pinned Lean toolchain” is legitimately defeated by a completed and correctly interpreted elaboration error. That result does not by itself defeat the submission's informal mathematical idea, the language's explanatory adequacy, or the model's capacity for construction. The scope of the criticism must name which of those propositions was actually at issue.

## PP1 — A failed conjunction does not identify its false member

**Premises.** Let \(T\) be a candidate claim, \(B\) the auxiliary assumptions, \(I\) the interpretation, and \(O\) the expected observation. Suppose the declared test argument establishes

\[
(T\land B\land I)\Rightarrow O,
\]

and the completed observation establishes \(\neg O\). No additional premise isolates \(T\), \(B\), or \(I\).

**Result.** The argument entails \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\), but does not entail \(\neg T\), \(\neg B\), or \(\neg I\) separately. A successor problem may investigate any or all of these loci; promoting it cannot make one diagnosis follow without additional premises.

**Proof.** If \(T\land B\land I\) held, the conditional would give \(O\), contradicting \(\neg O\). For non-identification, consider the valuations \((T,B,I,O)=(0,1,1,0)\), \((1,0,1,0)\), and \((1,1,0,0)\). Every valuation satisfies the conditional and \(\neg O\), yet each of \(T,B,I\) is true in some such valuation. Hence none of their individual negations is entailed. This is the precise elimination limitation stated in ECS §5.4.

**Worked case.** A reservation account says to choose each booking's latest revision before checking whether that revision is active. The executable route reports a stale reservation as live. A correct informal account with a wrong translator, a correct translator with an incorrect proposed account, and an expectation that misreads the boundary time all remain logically possible explanations of that mismatch. A useful next question identifies a booking with an expired latest revision and an earlier live revision, then separately examines the account, its translation and the expected output. “Make the model reason better” has skipped the discrimination.

**Non-derived choice.** The finite collection of loci used in the experiment is a diagnostic convention. ECS does not guarantee that those named loci exhaust every relevant failure mechanism.

## PP2 — No-result observations cannot be upgraded into semantic failures

**Premises.** The configured verdict procedure produces a content verdict only on an eligible, completed evaluation. A transport failure or external containment termination records delivery information but does not produce such an evaluation. No independent argument establishes the content failure from other evidence. This is V1.3 §1's oracle-isolation rule, supported by the evidence distinction in ECS §5.5 and Appendix A.

**Result.** The no-result event supplies no failed-content verdict to that procedure. It can license a delivery, resource, or instrumentation investigation; it cannot alone supply the failed commitment needed for a demonstrative content attack.

**Proof.** By the eligibility premise, the event is outside the content-verdict domain. Therefore the proposition “this completed procedure returned fail on this content” is not licensed by the event. Assigning that proposition solely because the event exists would contradict the declared eligibility condition. The event's existence can still be an input to a separate, appropriately interpreted argument about delivery.

**Worked case.** A Lean process is killed by an operating-system watchdog while elaborating a proposed language. The record demonstrates that this attempt did not deliver a result through the allotted execution route. It does not show that the conjecture is false or that Lean rejected it. “Reproduce the resource failure and determine whether it is a language, toolchain, or containment issue” is a coherent successor problem. If a separate, valid test proves a declared deterministic step bound was exceeded, that is a different argument with a different commitment and trace. It must not be retroactively manufactured from the watchdog event.

**Non-derived choice.** Practical watchdog limits and deterministic evaluation limits are engineering choices. Their values are not derived from ECS, and the cost of increasing them does not determine a proposition's truth.

## PP3 — Disconnected problem registration conserves existing labels

**Premises.** Consider the finite two-pass label calculation of V1.3 §4 on old artifacts \(A\). Registering a successor question adds new artifacts \(N\), with \(A\cap N=\varnothing\), but leaves all old attack and dependence edges unchanged. After all validity and evidence closures, there is no attack edge between \(A\) and \(N\), and no old artifact gains a dependence on \(N\). No old content, permission premise, or scope is changed. Scheduling may change.

**Result.** Every old artifact retains its former V1.3 label. If an ECS use also retains its licensing, scope and live premises, that use retains its ECS usability. The registration may change future inquiry while conserving current standing.

**Proof.** Write \(F_A\) for the old grounded-extension operator and \(F_{A\cup N}\) for the extended one. Because no attack crosses the partition, for every \(X\subseteq A\cup N\),

\[
F_{A\cup N}(X)\cap A=F_A(X\cap A).
\]

Starting both finite iterations at the empty set gives identical old-artifact membership at each step, by induction. Their least fixed points therefore agree on \(A\), so Pass 1 labels agree there. An old artifact's dependence set is unchanged and contains only old artifacts. Induction along the old dependence DAG gives identical Pass 2 labels. ECS usability separately has the form \(Lic\land Scope\land\bigwedge Live\); unchanged conjuncts give unchanged usability.

**Worked case.** An observer opens “Can the current language distinguish two meanings of latest?” and attaches a raw log excerpt as a mention. Merely creating that inquiry cannot refute an existing reservation account. If the observer later constructs a contentful attack against its interpretation, the premise of disconnectedness ceases to hold and the label calculation may change for that separately recorded reason.

**Boundary.** This result is conditional on the full edge closure, not just the edges visibly entered by the operator. A source-reliability attack can reach a validity node through V1.3's evidence closure. Such a registration is a substantive change to attack structure, not pure attention steering. Mini's current artifact labels are not silently equated with V1.3's two-pass labels.

## PP4 — Accumulating commitments need not preserve an account

**Premises.** ECS §3.1 permits support families without upward closure. Components retain their stated interpretation, and adding a rule conjoins its relation with the existing component relations as in §1.1. The question and comparison frame are fixed.

**Result.** From \(Account(E|W,p)\) and \(W\subseteq W'\), it does not in general follow that \(Account(E|W',p)\). Consequently, combining separately proposed remedies requires a joint account; the fact that each remedy addresses some recorded issue is insufficient.

**Proof by construction.** Let the target have a fixed input \(x=0\), output \(z\in\mathbb Z\), and constitutive relation \(z=x+1\). The baseline answer is \(z=1\). Let \(W\) contain the matching explanatory relation and its direct, role-preserving anchor; let the contrast allow removal of that rule, making \(z\) undetermined. This account has structural and question fidelity, an active dependence contrast, and a compatible baseline. Add a rule \(r_2:z=x+2\) to obtain \(W'\). The explanatory baseline now demands both \(z=1\) and \(z=2\), so it has no compatible state. Non-vacuity fails. Therefore \(W\) supports the account while \(W'\) does not.

**Worked case.** An original candidate language is criticized for failing to express an exceptional cancellation case. One proposed amendment expresses cancellation as replacing a booking's active state; another treats it as adding a second active state. Both proposals may be intelligible answers to different formulations of the issue. Appending both rules while retaining all old bindings can make the same booking satisfy incompatible statuses. Promotion should expose an integration question about their interaction, rather than equate more addressed issue records with a better account.

**Non-derived choice.** Which component is retained, revised, replaced or reinterpreted requires a contentful argument. ECS supplies no “keep the larger set” rule and no numerical criterion for selecting the joint account.

## PP5 — Independent support routes cannot be represented as jointly required premises

**Premises.** A conclusion \(c\) has two distinct sufficient argument routes \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) under a fixed interpretation. Either usable route suffices for the licensed use at issue. The first route loses its standing while the second remains usable. Instead of representing alternatives, an implementation records both route nodes as unconditional dependencies of \(c\) under V1.3's conjunctive Pass 2.

**Result.** That encoding can suspend \(c\) although the stipulated use still has a usable supporting route. The resulting suspension is evidence of an encoding mismatch under these premises; it is not evidence that the conclusion became false or that every route failed.

**Proof.** By the alternative-route premise, usability through \(r_2\) remains. The conjunctive encoding calculates

\[
supported(c)=accepted(r_1)\land accepted(r_2).
\]

Since \(r_1\) is no longer accepted, this conjunction is false. If Pass 1 still accepts \(c\), Pass 2 returns `suspended_unsupported`. Thus the encoded condition and the stipulated alternative-route condition differ on this state. ECS §5.3 explicitly permits withdrawal of one argument without withdrawal of the others, while §3.1 takes the whole support family rather than a selected support set as basic.

**Worked case.** One argument establishes order independence by a direct proof about choosing the greatest revision. Another establishes it by a separate proof through a canonical representation, with a declared correspondence. Discovery of a defect in the canonicalization proof need not defeat the direct proof. A promotion that says “restore the conclusion, now refuted” has assigned the wrong target. The immediate issue is the affected argument and, if present, the implementation's collapse of disjunction into conjunction.

**Boundary.** The theorem does not show that V1.3 miscomputes its own declared input graph. It shows why constructing that graph is substantive work. Encoding alternative routes correctly is a separate design problem; merely adding more dependency edges cannot solve it.

## PP6 — A successor repair must meet the parent preservation contract

**Premises.** Fix claimed repair obligations \(O\), protected obligations \(P\), an initial situation \(\xi\), a resulting situation \(\xi'\), and a produced change \(\Delta\). Use ECS §9.1's repair predicate:

\[
Repair_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)
\iff
\exists o\in O\,[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]
\land
\forall r\in P\,[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]
\land
ProducedBy(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O).
\]

**Result.** If a successor resolves an issue but destroys one protected obligation that held initially, the change is not a repair under this contract. If the successor only fixes a transport problem while no obligation in \(O\) becomes satisfied, that change likewise does not establish repair of \(O\). It may repair a separately stated infrastructure obligation.

**Proof.** In the first case, there exists \(r\in P\) with \(r(\xi)\) and \(\neg r(\xi')\), falsifying the preservation conjunct. In the second, the existential improvement conjunct is false. Either case makes the full conjunction false. This is a direct consequence of the definition, not an independent theorem establishing that any observed change is a repair.

**Worked case.** A candidate returns available stock without counting expired reservations, but counts an old live revision after a newer revision expires. A patch that removes expired revisions before selecting the maximum revision can satisfy a superficial expiry example while preserving the resurrection error. A different patch might correct resurrection but omit stock items with no active bookings. If the parent contract protects output rows for every stock item, the latter patch fails that contract despite a newly passing case. The successor must track the organization changed and test or argue for its preserved roles.

**Non-derived choice.** Selecting \(O\) and \(P\), and identifying which lost uses lie outside \(P\), are substantive choices fixed before the repair comparison. They cannot be inferred from the convenient set of cases that happened to pass. Losses outside \(P\) remain reportable even when the formal repair predicate holds.

## PP7 — Changing the question cannot erase the old counterexample

**Premises.** An assessment has a fixed boundary, grain and contrast contract \((\beta,\ell,C)\), as required by ECS §9.4. An admitted contrast \(x\in C\) defeats the assessed universal claim \(\forall y\in C\,R(y)\). A successor assessment proposes \(C'\) omitting \(x\), or changes the grain or boundary so that the old distinction is no longer retained.

**Result.** Survival of the successor claim cannot establish survival of the old claim. The changed assessment is a new claim, and use of old evidence requires re-examination under its new interpretation.

**Proof.** The old witness establishes \(x\in C\land\neg R(x)\), which falsifies \(\forall y\in C\,R(y)\). The assertion \(\forall y\in C'\,R(y)\) says nothing about \(x\notin C'\), so it cannot remove that witness. If the representation, grain or boundary changes instead, the interpreted predicate is no longer simply the old \(R\); ECS's frame discipline identifies this as a new assessment rather than permitting substitution under the old claim identity.

**Worked case.** An initial language interprets “latest” as greatest revision and claims to handle unordered deliveries. After it fails on a reordered history, a successor says histories must arrive in revision order. That may be a coherent new language or task contract. It does not repair the old unordered-history claim. The next problem should ask whether the restriction is justified by the intended phenomenon, and its record should preserve the failed original claim.

**Non-derived choice.** The source's frame discipline is a constitutive methodological rule. It does not prove that the first chosen grain was correct, forbid revising it, or determine which new grain is appropriate.

## PP8 — A finite inquiry record does not certify global exhaustion without a completeness premise

**Premises.** A block yields a finite history \(h\) of tested proposals and contrasts. At least one untested continuation remains admitted by the declared problem and resource model. The framework permits two interpretations of possible continuations that agree on every recorded occurrence but disagree about whether that untested continuation produces a relevant improvement. No independent completeness theorem rules one interpretation out.

**Result.** The finite record alone does not entail that further inquiry cannot improve the result. Stopping can be a justified allocation decision under a declared practical limit, but not a theorem of global exhaustion from those observations alone.

**Proof.** Let the two permitted completions of the history be \(M_0\) and \(M_1\). By premise, both satisfy every recorded observation in \(h\). In \(M_0\), the named untested continuation produces no improvement; in \(M_1\), it does. The proposition “no further admitted continuation yields improvement” is therefore false in a model satisfying the entire finite record. It is not a logical consequence of that record. This uses only the stated countermodel premise, not an imported search theorem.

**Worked case.** Three original language proposals fail to distinguish a cancellation from an expired reservation. Those records can expose a repeated mechanism and motivate a new representation question. They do not prove that every Lean-compatible candidate language will fail, or that a prose reformulation cannot reveal the missing relation. Conversely, repeating the same unsuccessful configuration without a new discriminating question need not receive more budget. A stopping note should identify the unresolved mechanism, the options actually investigated, and why the remaining options lack a sufficiently concrete next experiment under the present resources.

**Boundary.** A genuinely finite, independently declared search space with a proven complete procedure could support a scoped exhaustion claim. ECS does not supply that finite space or completeness proof. V1.3's stated inability to manufacture good conjectures and its distinction between effective and in-principle reach prevent interpreting its scheduler diagnostics as such a proof.

## Applying the results to the first frozen language proposals

The first tests hold each recorded proposal package \(P_v\) fixed while conjectures and refutations are populated and examined. A grammar repair, axiom change, interpretation change, codec change, or newly operative prose convention produces a successor package rather than silently changing the assessed one. A failed package remains available with the exact questions and contrasts under which it was assessed. The new package can inherit old evidence only with an explicit reading under its changed interpretation.

During that frozen assessment, the language can still be criticized. The record retains the complaint, its intended target, and any proposed successor question, including prose that is not expressible inside \(L_v\). Freezing execution for a comparison does not freeze the space of legitimate objections. It simply postpones making a particular proposed amendment operative within that comparison.

Each initial proposal should face a language question and a use question. The language question asks whether its representations preserve a distinction the target problem requires and whether its proof and refutation rules have their declared interpretation. The use question asks whether a conjecture or objection expressed through that package actually enters the episode's operative response. Where Lean is used, a completed proof can answer part of the former under pinned declarations; it cannot supply every interpretation premise. An objection's delivery to a context window can supply part of the latter history; it does not by itself establish reason use. The companion theorem document sets out these limits.

Gradual integration is then a sequence of explicit, criticizable decisions about operative organization. A proposal to change an encoder is first a target of inquiry; installing it is a separate controlled event with a recorded before/after contract. Promotion records name the possible return route without promising that every proposed amendment will be installed. An implementation that can store arbitrary criticisms but never let any admitted alternative change a target's use has not thereby demonstrated recursive critical capacity under ECS §8.1. Conversely, a temporary frozen experiment does not by itself establish a permanent explanatory barrier.

The first language tests and these conditional derivations do not establish ECS's constitutive conjecture. A candidate account can conform structurally and still require an independent judgment of its explanatory bearing. A failed candidate language ordinarily criticizes that application. To challenge ECS's sufficiency or necessity, the investigation must instead preserve the fixed grain and registry supplies while constructing one of ECS §10.2's genuine counterexample forms. Reframing every ordinary template failure as a defeat of ECS would evade that harder task.

## Declared implementation choices and unresolved questions

| Proposed choice | Why it is useful here | What the sources do not establish |
|---|---|---|
| A promotion account stored beside each successor question | Preserves the distinction between observed trigger, diagnosis and research decision | ECS does not require this file format or a dedicated database |
| Separate intake from completed bearing appraisal | Allows investigation of uncertainty about relevance | No mechanical gate decides every legitimate or interesting question |
| Retain formal and prose occurrences together | Lets the language's own expressive or interpretive limits be criticized | A shared record does not prove that both routes receive equal attention or influence |
| Freeze recorded proposal packages during an initial comparison | Makes the claim being tested identifiable and protects the old contrast | Freezing does not prove the chosen frame adequate |
| Keep protected obligations explicit when proposing a successor | Makes a local fix answerable for preserved uses | Neither ECS nor V1.3 selects all the right preservation obligations automatically |
| Restrict automatic effects of promotion to work allocation and provenance | Prevents a scheduler signal from becoming a content verdict | Attention can still bias what is ever investigated; it remains an appropriate target |
| Publish operational errata and substantive lessons separately | Makes failed delivery, representational defects and content criticism distinguishable | More records do not establish explanatory accounting or supply missing interpretation |
| Stop with a scoped unresolved statement when no concrete next test remains | Allows finite, reviewable decisions about resources | The absence of a currently proposed next test is not proof that none exists |

The remaining research question is whether issue content can be mapped into successor questions that preserve the relevant distinctions, generate discriminating inquiry, and return changes to operative use with their reasons and effects exposed, without silently revising the original claim. The record makes that question inspectable; its answer must come from the specific accounts and contrasts.

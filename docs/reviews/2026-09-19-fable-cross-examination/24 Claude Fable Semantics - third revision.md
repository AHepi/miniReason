# Claude Fable Semantics

## A structural class of explanatory creativity, with selected and constructed correspondence

---

# Part 0 — Read this first

## What this document claims

An explanation is a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**, held to a target by a **transport** that is checked by **what changes when things are changed**. What a piece of an explanation *is* — a cause, a rule, a measurement, a thing — is not asked of a label. At a grain and a contract of admitted changes, a *forward kind* is how a component responds to the changes that contract admits; two components no admitted change separates are one forward kind there. That the claim that they "really" differ is always either a claim about a finer contract, which is a new question, or a claim about their histories, which this semantics also represents as a second kind of kind, is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV), not a theorem.

Adequacy comes in two grades. An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated. An **anchored account** is an account at a contract whose anchors are a proper decomposition of the target at the resolution the question needs, and whose components are of one forward kind with their anchors under every change the physical module admits, not only the ones the contract selected. The first grade is what can be assessed within a question. The second is what "the explanation matches the mechanism at the module's index" means, and it is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive. Both grades are extensional: the target's own faithful copy satisfies them, and whether a copy *explains* is a question about origin and deployment (Part X), not about fidelity.

Correspondence between a representation and what it represents is not a primitive of this semantics. It is a **relation with a provenance**. A correspondence can be *selected* — produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes, with at least one candidate eliminated — or *constructed* — produced by an episode of conjecture and criticism — or merely *declared* by whoever writes the model down. The three are distinguished by their histories, not their outputs. Creativity lives in construction. Selection produces the raw material construction works on. Declaration is permitted as a modelling convenience, and a claim about representation or creativity cannot rest on it.

Questions have provenance too. A question — its target, its scope of admitted changes, what it asks — can be found as well as answered, and the semantics represents both, as far as the construction in Derivation 5 goes; it has no condition on what makes a found question worth finding, and says so. What it does not represent is the invention of a dimension of variation the physical module does not license, and it says that too.

The **constitutive conjecture** is this: explanatory creativity is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports between organizations checked by change-fidelity, (iii) the provenance of those transports and of the questions they serve, and (iv) their physical realization. A genuine explanatory achievement that these four cannot represent counts against the conjecture. A candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too.

## What this document does not claim

It does not derive reference from uninterpreted matter. It derives reference from matter **plus a selection or construction history**, given a physical module that says which organizations a lump of matter can instantiate at a grain. That module is a primitive here, and what it must supply is stated exactly: it is more than "physics" in the ordinary sense, because it must say how a lump decomposes.

It does not say prediction is explanation. Fidelity in this semantics is over **component structure under change**, not over outputs. Two systems with identical outputs and different internal routes are different organizations here, and the semantics says so.

It does not supply an objective aesthetics, a probability of truth, a ranking of thinkers, or a merit function for questions or for accounts. It supplies places where such things would go if anyone had them, and marks those places as empty.

It does not prove that any human, machine, institution, or lineage belongs to the classes defined. It defines the classes. Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment; the experiments that have been run test proxies, and the proxies are named as proxies where they are cited.

It does not claim that the two primitive packages and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV are few. It claims that they are all listed.

## Grievances, anticipated

**1. "Without declared kinds you cannot tell a cause from a correlation."**
A cause and a correlation are two components with different signatures under (K). Intervening on the input port of a causal assignment replaces the component that assigns that port and leaves the causal component's own relation in force; the output moves through it. Intervening on the same port breaks a correlational component, because a correlation is a constraint on how two ports happen to co-vary, not a rule assigning one from the other. The difference is a difference in edit-response, and the semantics checks exactly that. A declared kind-label adds nothing where a separating change exists, because fidelity already finds it. Where no admitted change separates two components, they are one *forward* kind at that grain and contract; if their histories differ, that is a *historical* kind, and the semantics represents it as a predicate on history, not as a label.

**2. "So this is operationalism: a thing is what you can do to it."**
A forward kind is what a grain's and a contract's admitted changes can distinguish; it is indexed to the physical module's decomposition, the grain, and the contract, and every kind-claim carries that index. "One kind at that index" is a fact about the pairing of an organization with a contract, not a fact about the world, and the semantics never presents it as more. Where a question fixes the contract and asks which of two mechanisms produced an effect, and the contract does not separate them, the semantics says the question needs either a finer contract or a history, and if neither is available, that the distinction is not represented. That is scoping with the gap stated, not operationalism.

**3. "If correspondences are selected, you have made truth a matter of survival."**
No. Selection produces a transport. Whether that transport is faithful on changes it was never selected against is a fact about the transport and the world, independent of whether it survived. There is a result below (Derivation 3) that selection provides no guarantee of fidelity off the history it was selected on, under a stated hypothesis about the population. Survival is how the transport got there; fidelity is what it is.

**4. "Then everything is relative to a contract of admitted changes, and there is no objectivity."**
Two claims, two indices. "Faithful on C" is a fact within C. "C is not protective" is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes: a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's, or the reverse. An objectivity claim is the conjunction, and the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism. A contract that quietly excludes such a change is caught by the requirement that exclusions be stated, and then by the objectivity condition — where the candidate covers the component the change touches. Where it does not, the candidate is silent, its scope is a stated limit, and its objectivity claim is correspondingly smaller; nothing in the semantics makes a silent candidate right.

**5. "This is teleosemantics / structural realism / functionalism with new words."**
It shares commitments with each and differs from each. From teleosemantics it takes the idea that a correspondence has a history; it differs by distinguishing selected from constructed histories and locating creativity only in the latter. From structural realism it takes structure-preservation; it differs by making preservation change-driven and component-level, and by refusing a global isomorphism requirement. From functionalism it takes substrate-independence; it differs by imposing physical realization obligations at every attribution. Whether the combination is new is a question about the literature, not about whether it is right.

**6. "You have replaced explanation with evolution. That is deflationary."**
Selection appears at the bottom, between a sensory field and a primitive layer of things-with-persistence, to produce what explanation operates on. Construction is a separate provenance with a separate witness, and every creative attribution requires it. Nothing about construction is reduced to selection; Part IV states what would collapse the two and what prevents it.

**7. "Mathematics has no interventions. This cannot handle mathematical explanation."**
An admitted change need not be a physical intervention. Removing an axiom, dropping a constraint, or changing a dimension are edits on an organization, and fidelity under them is well-defined. But "physically admitted" is not defined for a mathematical organization, so a mathematical question must *declare* its admission relation — which edits its presentation admits — and every claim about it is relative to that declaration, as a physical claim is relative to the physical module. Part VII works an example under a declared admission relation and says so.

**8. "Where is aesthetics?"**
In Part XI, as a declared normative relation. The semantics gives artistic effect, artistic purpose, and aesthetic reasons three distinct mathematical carriers and refuses to define any as another. It does not pretend to know which aesthetic reasons are true.

**9. "'Selected' is just as much a stipulation as 'is a cause'. Who says the selection happened?"**
Selection is a physical history: a population with more than one member, a variation operator that was applied, a survival condition that eliminated at least one candidate which differed from the survivor on an encountered change, and a route from failures to variation on which no itemised record of a failure lies. Whether it occurred is a claim about the physical module, fallible but checkable in principle. Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, "selected" is a hypothesis, not a stipulation.

**10. "Freezing the question for assessment while letting questions change across episodes is having it both ways."**
It is having it both ways deliberately, and the two ways are indexed so they cannot be confused. An assessment is an event with a frozen contract; any change to the contract ends that assessment and begins another. An episode is a history in which contracts can change, and every change carries a provenance record. Derivation 7 shows the two are consistent.

**11. "Kinds obviously exist. A rule is not a cause."**
A rule and a cause differ in how they respond to changes: a rule's application changes when the rule is edited and not when the world is intervened on; a cause's assignment changes under intervention. That is a difference in edit-signature, and the semantics represents it exactly. What the semantics denies is that the *forward* difference is available before the admitted changes are fixed, or independently of them.

**12. "You individuate transports by their history but refuse to individuate components by theirs. That is arbitrary."**
It is an asymmetry, and it is stated rather than hidden. A transport is an occurrence in a physical history: it came to hold by selection, construction, or declaration, and its provenance is a fact about events. A component is a slot in an organization: what it does under changes is its forward kind. A component realized in matter also has a history, and the semantics represents that as a historical kind — a predicate on the history that produced the occurrence — distinct from its forward kind. What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to its fidelity or its kind on a contract; the one thing history changes about an account is whether it is *operative* (Part V), which is stated as a separate, openly historical condition. Provenance matters to *representation* because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change.

## Where to attack this

The load-bearing claims, in order of how much falls if they fail:

**(A) Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on an admitted contract, that satisfies Origin (Part X) — it is new to the repertoire, built, and deployed — and that plainly explains nothing. This would show that change-fidelity, proper anchoring and origination together are not enough. The target's own copy and the faithful modular table satisfy the anchored grade and fail Origin, and the semantics says so rather than hiding it; a reversed calculation fails anchoring on the production edits; conclusion-as-premise fails non-circular dependence. A new attempt must fail none of these.

**(B) Necessity.** Produce a genuine explanation whose organization cannot be captured by *any* transport satisfying component fidelity under *any* admitted contract. Part XV sorts the hard cases: explanation by symmetry is open; explanation of an effect as a selection artefact is a missing *condition* rather than a missing transport and is listed under (D); elimination of a physically impossible rival is handled through declared counterfactuals and scoped by design; mathematical explanation is relative to a declared admission relation. Part VII gives the treatment of the third and says where it stops.

**(C) Reinstatement of kinds.** Produce a case in which a declared kind-label does discriminating work that no admitted change under the physically admitted contract and no history can do. Derivation 1 does not rule this out; it shows only that fidelity preserves signatures. That kind-labels are eliminable in favour of signatures and histories is the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture**, and it is a conjecture.

**(D) Genesis.** Show either that a selection history can guarantee fidelity off the history under the population hypothesis of Derivation 3, or that construction reduces to selection under the elimination and blindness clauses of Part IV's selection witness, or that the layers described in Part IV are not what explanation operates on.

**(E) Question-finding.** Show that treating a question as a content — something that can be constructed, be new, and be the originative contribution of an episode — either trivializes creativity or fails to capture some genuine case of finding the right question. Two things are already conceded: the semantics has no condition separating a found question from a re-parameterisation, so as written a random new contract counts; and the invention of a dimension of variation the physical module does not license is not representable at all. A third is open: Derivation 5's construction of the question-organization is a sketch with listed gaps.

**(F) The anchored grade.** Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors (Part V) exclude a genuine mechanism. A decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on *every* admitted change is not excluded and is not meant to be: at that index it is the mechanism. If such a decoy nonetheless failed to explain where the mechanism succeeds, the difference between them would be one that no admitted change shows — and a discriminating difference no admitted change shows is exactly what attack (C) asks for, which is why that case is heard there.

Anything else is a detail.

# Part I — Commitments

**Explanatory realism.** Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it. Systems can be wrong about their transports, their observations, their criticisms, and their own capacities. Every success predicate in this document is external: it can hold of a system that does not know it holds, and fail of one that believes it does.

**Fallibility without falsehood-as-work.** A theory may contain an accurate scoped dependence together with errors elsewhere. What cannot count as explanation is an error in the very dependence alleged to do the work.

**Conjecture, criticism, action.** An idea may be entertained without justification. A criticism has a target and is itself conjectural. A thinker may act on an appraisal without certifying it.

**Recursive scrutiny with operative return.** Any aspect of a system's practice — a question, a contract, a transport, a method, an attention policy — can become a target, and the result must be able to change how the system proceeds.

**Substrate independence with physical obligations.** Any carrier may bear an organization. Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physical module, which must say how the carrier decomposes at the grain claimed.

**Two produced provenances, not one.** Selection and construction both produce correspondences; declaration produces none and is a status in the model. The two are distinguished by their histories. Neither is reduced to the other.

**Two grades of adequacy, and an objectivity claim.** Account at a contract is what a question can assess. Anchoring is fidelity on the full admitted contract with a proper decomposition. Objectivity is account-hood plus non-protection of the contract against the module in force. All three are stated in the same vocabulary; none is a label; only the operative condition of Part V looks at history, and it says so.

# Part II — Organizations and their changes

## Organizations

An organization is

\[
D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L).
\]

\(V\) is a set of ports, each with a nonempty value domain \(X_v\). A valuation is an element of \(X_D=\prod_v X_v\). \(J\) indexes components; each component \(j\) has a footprint \(V_j\subseteq V\). \(B\) is a set of boundary conditions. \(A\) is a set of admitted edits, closed under a partial associative composition with identity \(1\). For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies

\[
L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v .
\]

The compatible valuations are

\[
\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)=\{z\in X_D:\forall j\in J,\ z|_{V_j}\in L_j(a,b)\}. \tag{O}
\]

(O) is a schema over the supplied data \((V,X,J,B,A,L)\). Those data are a **declared parameter** of every use of this semantics: when the organization is attributed to a physical occurrence, the physical module must license the attribution (Part IV, Part XII); when it is a mathematical or modelled organization, the data are stipulated, and every claim is relative to the stipulation.

A deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports. An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port; it does not add an equation beside an incompatible one. A changed rule is a changed component. A **subnetwork** \(U\) of \(D\) is a subset of components with the ports they touch; its solution set \(\operatorname{Sol}_U(a,b)\) is (O) restricted to those components, and its **visible ports** are a declared subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) of its ports, the rest being hidden.

Values of ports may be paths, functions, fields, proofs, or histories. Cyclic constraints are admitted. Several solutions remain several.

## Roles are derived

No role assignment is supplied. A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit that sets \(v\) directly. A port is an **output** of component \(j\) when its value is determined by \(L_j\) given the other ports of \(V_j\) across \(B\). A port is an **observation** when \(A\) contains an **observation edit**: an edit that alters the relation reporting the port without altering what it reports, that is, replaces the component whose footprint is \(\{v\}\cup V'\) for a reporting set \(V'\) while leaving every component not touching \(v\) unchanged. The direction of an organization is a consequence of which edits it admits, not a stipulation about which way an equation is read.

## Kinds are edit-signatures, at a grain and a contract

Fix an organization \(D\), attributed at grain \(\ell\), and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III). The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is

\[
\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}. \tag{K}
\]

The **signature of a subnetwork** \(U\) on \(C\) is \(\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}}\operatorname{Sol}_U(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\); for a single component with all ports visible it is (K).

Two subnetworks \(U,U'\) (in particular two components, each a one-element subnetwork with all ports visible) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains, under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\). Only the question's baseline pair is excluded, on purpose: what a component imposes at the baseline is, as a gloss, its *state*, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states; the identity edit at the baseline separates nothing, while the identity edit at a different boundary is a response to a change of boundary and does separate. A forward kind is an equivalence class under this relation, and it is relative to the grain (which fixes \(J\)) and the contract (which fixes the changes). A coarser contract identifies more components; a coarser grain has fewer to identify. The relation is used between a component of one organization and an anchor subnetwork of another in Part V.

A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for the provenance of a content's occurrence, applied to the binding that realizes the component. Two components of one forward kind on every admitted change may be of different historical kinds. Both are kinds. The analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV) is that every discriminating use of a kind-label is one or the other.

What ordinary language calls a cause, a measurement, a rule, or a constitutive status are families of forward signatures:

- a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits;
- a **measurement** has a signature invariant under interventions on the measured port and variable under edits to the measuring relation;
- a **rule application** has a signature invariant under interventions on the world and variable under edits to the rule.

These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data. The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a cause. It asks what its signature is, and, where the question is about its origin, what its history is.

## Admissible grains

A grain \(\ell\) is a declared index, but not a free one. A grain is **admissible** for an occurrence \(o\) relative to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain (Part IV, Inst) and every component of that organization is touched by some admitted pair: some \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) changes its relation. A coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed. Claims can be rescued by moving to a grain only if the new grain is admissible, and the move is a new claim at a new index.

# Part III — Questions

## Contracts

A question is

\[
p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p).
\]

\(D\) is the target. The **contract** \(C\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over; it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\), and it has the form

\[
C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K .
\]

\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\) is the set of edit–boundary pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization attributed to an occurrence \(o\), \(A_\Theta(o)\times B\) (Part XII); for a mathematical or modelled organization, a **declared admission relation** stated with the question. \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is a **stated scope**: the declared subset of admitted pairs the claim ranges over, with the reason for each exclusion recorded. \(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs with \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\) — each declared together with the component relations \(L_j(a,b)\) it imposes, which is what its "stated meaning" is; so \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) is defined on \(K\) by (O) and fidelity is well-defined there. \(K\) is how a question can ask about a structure that is not a possibility at all (Part VII). Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\); the objectivity condition of Part V ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through the relations it declares.

\(\mathcal Q\) is a specified set-theoretic operation on \(D\), its solutions, and its component structure, with codomain \(Y_p\). The answer profile is

\[
\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b). \tag{Q}
\]

(Q) is a schema over the supplied \(D\) and \(\mathcal Q\). \(O_p\) is the set of obligations being addressed or protected. \(\rho_p\) is the **provenance of the question** (below).

## The respect is the query

What a question asks — production, identification, obstruction, rule-status, purpose-achievement — is fixed by the type of \(\mathcal Q\) and the shape of \(C\), not by a label. A production question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that reads an output port and a \(C\) containing interventions on upstream ports. An identification question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that computes a fibre and a \(C\) containing edits to the observed value. An obstruction question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that returns a reachability truth value. Two questions with the same \(D\) and different \((C,\mathcal Q)\) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other.

## Questions have provenance

\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\) attaches to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone. Its witness is the one Part IV gives for the provenance of any content's occurrence: the provenance of the transport by which an occurrence represents the question-organization of Derivation 5. A **declared** question is stipulated by the modeller. A **selected** question is one whose representing transport is selected. A **constructed** question is one whose representing transport is constructed. Any assessment may use any of the three. A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) with the witness. Whether a constructed question was worth finding — whether it is a finding rather than a re-parameterisation — is not decided by this semantics; the place for such a condition is marked empty in Part X and listed in Part XV.

## A question can be wrong

A question may fail to pick out its alleged target, assume an incompatible baseline, or combine incompatible requirements. It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\). Its formulation is still an event. Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract. An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another, and the record must show the boundary.

# Part IV — Layers, transports, and provenance

## Occurrences and contents

An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier. A **content** is an organization together with its contract-relative commitments. Occurrences are not identified by carrying the same words; contents are not identified by having the same outputs.

## What an occurrence instantiates

The physical module supplies a relation

\[
\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D),
\]

"occurrence \(o\) instantiates organization \(D\) at grain \(\ell\)." It is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain (a mechanical one, a thermodynamic one, a computational one), and nothing here assumes uniqueness. Every definition that needs "the organization \(o\) instantiates" quantifies over \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,\cdot)\). What the module must be able to return is a full organization — ports, domains, components with footprints, boundaries, the admitted edits \(A_\Theta\) restricted to the occurrence, and every component relation — and that is a decomposition, not a reading of a meter. The semantics does not pretend otherwise: how a lump of matter decomposes is the module's burden, and Part XII says what the module is.

## Three layers

A modelled thinker has up to three organizations in play.

The **sensory field** \(F\) is an organization whose ports are the readings of the system's sensors and whose components are whatever regularities hold among readings. It has no things in it.

The **primitive layer** \(P\) is an organization whose ports are persistent things with boundaries and identity, whose components are their continuity relations, and whose admitted edits include displacement, occlusion, and re-identification. It is the layer at which there are *objects* rather than a field of sensation. \(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport that is typically selected: the population is the variants of the system's tracking machinery, the history is the sensory changes encountered, and the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held.

The **simulation layer** \(S\) is an organization over \(P\) whose components are dependencies among things and whose queries are predictions: what a port of \(P\) will take under an admitted edit. \(S\) is where expectation lives, and it is where construction typically operates.

None of the three is presupposed to exist in any particular physical system. The semantics describes what it is for them to exist and to be connected. Content identity at the primitive layer — that two occurrences carry the same thing — is a fidelity fact between their organizations under \(P\)'s admitted edits; it does not use the representation predicate below, which is defined in terms of it.

## Transports

A transport from an organization \(D\) to an organization \(E\) is

\[
t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E
\]

where \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) on the stated scope, \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits, \(\sigma:B_D\to B_E\) translates boundaries, and \(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation. When the transport serves a question, it carries a fifth datum, the **transported query** \(\mathcal Q_E\), an operation on \(E\) of the same type as \(\mathcal Q\), declared with the transport; and \(\tau\) is extended to the question's counterfactual set \(K\) by the declaration that introduces each pair of \(K\). **Orientation.** A transport from \(D\) to \(E\) is the relation by which \(E\) is *held to* \(D\): \(D\) is what \(E\) answers to. \(\pi,\tau,\sigma\) run forward from \(D\); \(\lambda\) runs back, anchoring \(E\)'s parts in \(D\). This one orientation is used everywhere: an explanation \(E\) is held to its target \(D\) by \(t:D\to E\); a carrier's organization \(D\), with \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\), is held to the content \(c\) it represents by \(t:c\to D\). \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\mathcal Q_E(E,\tau(a),\sigma(b))\); no inverse of \(\pi\) is taken, and (A) is the requirement that the transported query agree with \(\mathcal Q\) through the transport on \(C\). For a simulation layer \(S\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_S\) is the same with \(S\) in place of \(E\). A transport is **faithful on \(C\)** for a given set \(\Gamma\) of active components when it satisfies (F1) for those components and (F2) on \(C\), as defined in Part V.

## Three provenances

A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has a provenance determined by its history in the physical module. Among the witnesses whose events produced the occurrence that carries \(t\), take those that are \(\prec_h\)-minimal (an event of a witness precedes an event of another). If all minimal witnesses are of one kind, that is \(t\)'s provenance — constructed or selected. If they are of both kinds, \(t\) has **mixed** provenance, which is declared as such and does not count as constructed for (G). If no witness produced the occurrence and the transport was entered into the model by its author, it is declared. Later histories are maintenance and do not change provenance; construction on selected material produces a *new* transport with its own occurrence and its own minimal witness, and the selected one keeps its own.

**Selected.** \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) holds when there are: a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized organization–transport pairs, each instantiated by an occurrence under \(\Theta\), with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\), physically admitted, that was applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\) and produced a member that differs from its parent at some pair of \(H\); the finite history \(H\) of *all* edit–boundary pairs within \(C_t\) — the contract on which \(t\)'s content is claimed — that occurred as events of \(\Sigma_h\), non-empty and not chosen; a survival condition enacted by the environment in \(\Sigma_h\), of which the witness records the checkable fact that, in this population and this history, the members that survived were exactly the members faithful on \(H\) (a fact about the run, not a definition of survival, and the hypothesis Derivation 3 uses), and which **eliminated at least one member** that differed from \(t\) at a pair of \(H\) on which that member failed and \(t\) did not; and \(t\) is a member that survived. That last clause is the difference-making condition, stated in the module's terms. The population, the operator and the survival condition are the ones the module identifies as enacted in \(\Sigma_h\) before the survivor's occurrence: a witness describes events, it does not choose among descriptions. Two further conjuncts. **Non-triviality:** no event of \(\Sigma_h\) instantiates an organization held to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition by a faithful transport. **Blindness:** no itemised representation of a defect — an occurrence instantiating a record that pairs the state at which fidelity failed with the value the target took there — lies on any active route, at the grain at which the route is described, to the event that produced \(t\)'s occurrence; an *aggregate* summary — a function of the multiset of failure magnitudes alone, with no access to which state failed, such as how many failures and how large — is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted. Provenance is thereby indexed to the grain of the route, as everything else is indexed to a grain. This is the only definition of selection; Part XII says what makes it a physical claim.

**Constructed.** \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\) — the Build predicate holds for the content \(t\) holds its carrier to — and in which the organization \(t\) runs from (its \(D\)) is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\), in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below.

**Declared.** \(\operatorname{Dec}(t)\): neither witness exists; the transport is entered into the model by its author. Declared provenance is a status in the model, not a physical history.

A physical system may hold selected transports between field and primitive layer and constructed transports at the simulation layer; that is the expected arrangement, not a requirement. Construction may operate on selected material. Selection may continue to operate beneath construction; the minimal-witness rule says which provenance each resulting transport bears. What keeps the two apart is one thing, and it is in both definitions on the same route — the route to the event that produced the transport's occurrence: a selection witness is blind on it, and a construction witness has the itemised defect record used on it at the acceptance of the binding (Part X). A process that is blind there is not constructing; a process with the record used there is not selecting, whatever else it does.

## Representation is derived

An occurrence \(o\) **represents** content \(c\) at grain \(\ell\) when some organization \(o\) instantiates at \(\ell\) is held to \(c\) by a transport faithful on \(c\)'s contract whose provenance is selected or constructed:

\[
\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]. \tag{R}
\]

The carrier–content relation is thus a fidelity fact with a history. A system can represent a false theory: the transport from the content to the carrier is faithful while the content's own transport from the world fails. A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does. What (R) takes from outside is \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\). (R) is indexed to a contract: an occurrence represents \(c\) **on \(C\)** when the transport is faithful on \(C\), and what it represents without qualification is \(c\) on the largest contract on which some witnessing transport is faithful. The **provenance of a content's occurrence on \(C\)** is the provenance of the \(\prec_h\)-minimal transports witnessing (R) for it on \(C\), with the same rule for mixed cases; if none witnesses (R) and the modeller has entered one, it is declared. This is the one definition of provenance for contents, and questions (Part III) and the histories of components (Part II) use it. **Copying** is a content-preserving transfer with its own witness; **descent** is a chain of selections each of whose populations contains the previous survivor; both are historical kinds in the sense of Part II.

## Expectation, surprise, violation

Let \(t\) be selected on history \(H\) with contract \(C\). For an edit–boundary pair \((a,b)\in C\) actually occurring:

- the **expectation** is \(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\);
- a **violation** occurs when fidelity fails at \((a,b)\);
- **surprise** is a violation at \((a,b)\notin H\).

These are definitions. That a system with no transport cannot be surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract cannot be surprised, follow from them by unpacking and are not results (Derivation 4 says so). Three things do follow that are worth stating. Surprise at \((a,b)\in C\) is a failure of fidelity of *that* transport on \(C\), so that transport no longer witnesses (R) for the content on \(C\), and deployment through it (Part X) is lost; whether the system still represents the content depends on whether another transport witnesses (R). A change outside \(C\) altogether can neither violate nor surprise, so what an ordinary speaker calls surprise at an uncontracted change is not represented here. And since a selected transport is guaranteed faithful only on \(H\), what it represents, by (R) indexed to contracts, is the content on the largest contract it is in fact faithful on, which may be smaller than the contract declared for the content: a selected representation is representation of less than was claimed, exactly as far as it was never tested.

Two responses to a violation are distinguished. A **selection response** extends \(H\) and lets \(\mu\) act: the transport is re-tuned within the population. A **construction response** introduces a new organization or a new transport with a construction witness. Only the second can be originative under Part X.

# Part V — Account

An explanatory candidate for question \(p\) is an organization \(E\), a transport \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\), and an identified set \(\Gamma\) of active commitments in \(E\). Write \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\). The predicate is two-place on the organization and the question, with the transport and commitments existentially quantified:

\[
\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\iff\exists t,\Gamma\ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma),
\]

and \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold, each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\), and none a condition on anything outside \(C\).

**Component fidelity.** For every active component \(k\) of \(E\) with anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\), and every \((a,b)\in C\), the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\) and projecting away its hidden ports equals the component relation of \(k\) under the translated edit:

\[
\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\!\big[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\big]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)). \tag{F1}
\]

And the assembled organization agrees:

\[
\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b)),\qquad \tau(1)=1,\quad \tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1). \tag{F2}
\]

(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition. (F2) prevents a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint. Together they are fidelity at every level the contract reaches. By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract; whether that is enough to say what the component *is* is the question the anchored grade below answers.

**Question fidelity.** For every \((a,b)\in C\),

\[
\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b). \tag{A}
\]

The query \(\mathcal Q\) is held fixed; an account of a different query is not an account of this one. An account of a reframed question is assessed as an account of the new question, and as a construction under Part X; (A) does not assess whether the reframe was an improvement (Part XV lists this as a limit).

**Non-circular dependence.** The answer follows by evaluating \(E\) under its independent boundary conditions. A boundary condition \(b\) is **independent** of the answer when no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports. The target's answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component; moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named "law" does not discharge this. The witness: there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\), translated from some \((a,b)\in C\), that removes or replaces a nonempty sub-block \(B\subseteq\Gamma\) — proper when \(\Gamma\) has more than one member — while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way. When \(\Gamma\) has several members, removing all of them changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing; when it has one, removing it is the only witness there is, and the condition then does little — which is why the anchored grade, not this condition, is what excludes the one-component repackaging.

**Non-vacuity.** \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). The contract \(C\) is a stated subset of the admitted pairs together with a declared counterfactual set, in the form of Part III, and every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently.

Thus

\[
\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}. \tag{E}
\]

Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes in \(C\). None inspects a label, and none evaluates fidelity outside \(C\); non-vacuity inspects the record of how \(C\) was drawn, which is part of the question. Account-hood at \(C\) is assessable within the question and keeps its truth value at its index (Part VIII). (E) carries **no provenance condition**: any modelled candidate, including one whose transport is declared, can be assessed for account-hood, because a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history. Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK), which each require \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\) explicitly.

## Two grades: account at \(C\), and anchored

\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) is **account-hood at \(C\)**. The stronger grade, **anchored account-hood**, is a predicate on the same four data, with \(t\) and \(\Gamma\) named, and adds three conditions on the anchor map, all stated in the same vocabulary:

1. **Properness.** The anchors of the active components refine a cover of the components of \(D\) that some pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches, and the anchor map is injective on that cover: distinct active components have distinct anchors, and no touched component of \(D\) lies outside every anchor. An anchor may be the whole target only when \(D\) has one component.
2. **Resolution.** Whenever two components of \(D\) can be separately varied by pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with distinct effects on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), they lie in distinct anchors. The decomposition of \(E\) is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about.
3. **World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\) with the question's baseline pair excluded, with its anchor subnetwork (Part II defines the relation for subnetworks), under the port bijection \(\lambda\) supplies: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every admitted pair that **touches** the anchor — changes the relation of some component in it. A candidate is *silent* on an admitted pair only when that pair touches no anchor of any active component; silence is structural, read off \(\lambda\), never declared, and by properness an anchored account is silent on no admitted pair that touches a component the question's contract reaches. "World" here means the physical module: a world-kind is a fact about \((\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), and the module is the semantics' only access to the world.

Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive, it does not inspect a label, and it does not look at history. It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose: a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 3. What it does not separate is a decoy with the same response to *every* admitted edit — and the semantics says that such a decoy is, at this index, the mechanism (Part XV, attack (F)). The reversed calculation of Part VII is an account at the identification contract and fails 3 on the production edits.

**What the anchored grade does not exclude, and why.** The target itself, \(E=D\) under the identity transport, is an anchored account of \(D\). So is a modular table whose pieces each carry the local signature of an anchor on every admitted edit. Both are extensional copies of the mechanism at this grain, and account-hood at either grade is an extensional fidelity fact: the semantics has no predicate that separates a mechanism from its faithful copy, and does not want one. "This explains nothing" said of the target's own copy is not a complaint about account-hood; it is a complaint about **origin and deployment** — that the copy is not new to any repertoire, was not built, and is not held by anyone — and those are Part X's predicates, not Part V's. The attack on sufficiency (Part 0, Part XV) is stated accordingly.

**An operative condition, openly historical.** Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), an anchored account is **operative** when each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically. This condition is about history, not signature; it is stated outside the anchored grade so that the asymmetry of Grievance 12 holds exactly: forward kinds and anchoring are history-free, and the operative condition is not.

## What (E) excludes, and what only the anchored grade excludes

A table of observed answers has no component whose relation is replaced by an intervention; it fails (F1) under any contract containing one. A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component anchored to a target with several, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account, as the previous section says.

A reversed calculation — identification presented as production — fails (F2) under the production contract: intervening on the upstream port changes the target's downstream value but not the calculation's. Under the identification contract it is an account at that contract; it is not anchored as a production account.

"\(p\) because \(p\)" fails non-circular dependence.

An account whose only substantive contrast is one that no admitted edit realizes and that is not declared in \(K\) with a stated meaning fails non-vacuity. An account whose contract excludes an admitted change on which it is evaluable and wrong is an account at \(C\) whose objectivity claim fails (below).

The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) for a target with several components satisfies (E): with a single commitment the witness is its removal, which is allowed. It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored, because it fails properness. That is the honest statement of what (E) alone can do, and why the second grade exists.

## What (E) does not exclude

A true mechanism guessed for bad reasons satisfies (E), and may be anchored; the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX), and anchoring is not a reasons condition. A less elegant account satisfies (E) as fully as a more elegant one with the same fidelity. A proof answering "why does this follow under these rules?" satisfies (E) for that question without answering "what caused this?"; depth is question-relative and (E) measures none of it.

## Objectivity: the second index

An **objectivity claim** for \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) is the conjunction of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) and a claim about \(C\) against the module in force: that \(C\) is **not protective** for \(\mathcal E\). \(C\) is protective when there is an admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) that touches some anchor of an active component of \(E\) — so that the candidate is not silent on it in the structural sense of the anchored grade — and on which the target's answer is defined and the candidate's transported answer differs from it: \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\), the transport being extended to such pairs by \(\lambda\) (the edit acts on an anchored component, so its translation is the corresponding edit on the anchored component of \(E\)). The target is right about itself by definition, so "the candidate is better" is not a case. Silence — a pair touching no anchor — is a stated limit of scope, not protection, and it is not under the candidate's control: it is read off \(\lambda\). Being wrong about a change the scope hides, on a component the candidate covers, is protection. The claim is indexed to the module in force: a later module with a larger \(A_\Theta\) makes it a new claim at a new index (Part VIII), and account-hood at \(C\) is untouched by it. This is the second index of Grievance 4, and it is deliberately not a conjunct of (E), so that the first grade remains what a question can assess on its own.

## Why the anchoring condition is stated the way it is

A condition of the form "each component of \(E\) must be tied to a component of \(D\) *of the same kind*" would be evaluable — \(\lambda\) is transport data and Inst says what \(D\)'s components are — but it would not be evaluable *by the changes in \(C\)* where \(C\) contains no separating change, and on \(C\) alone the two are one forward kind. The right court of appeal is not \(C\) but \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\): a kind-claim about the world is a claim about every change the module admits. Condition 3 of the anchored grade says exactly that. What remains after it — a label that discriminates where no admitted change and no history does — is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV, which this document does not claim to have proved.

# Part VI — Work, support, and interference

Fix \(\mathcal E\). The **restriction** \(E|W\), for \(W\subseteq\Gamma\), is the organization obtained by deleting every component in \(\Gamma\setminus W\) (each then imposes the full relation on its ports) while holding the boundary conditions and the components outside \(\Gamma\) — the **named background** — fixed. Define

\[
\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p,t|W,W)\}, \tag{S}
\]

where \(t|W\) is \(t\) with its anchor map restricted to \(W\); the transport and commitments are the fixed ones of \(\mathcal E\), not existentially re-chosen. No upward closure and no minimal member are assumed. For nonempty \(B\subseteq W\),

\[
\operatorname{CriticalBlock}(B;W,p)\iff W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\land W\setminus B\notin\mathsf S_{E,p}. \tag{B}
\]

A block may be critical while no singleton in it is. For a declared family \(\mathcal V\) of organization edits,

\[
\operatorname{Boundary}_{E,p}=\{(v,w)\in\mathcal V^2:\operatorname{Account}(E_v,p,t_v,\Gamma_v)\neq\operatorname{Account}(E_w,p,t_w,\Gamma_w)\}, \tag{D}
\]

with \(t_v,\Gamma_v\) the transport and commitments carried along the edit \(v\). **Explanatory jobs** below are questions \(f\) sharing the target \(D\).

**Finite monotone theorem** (used only where upward closure holds, which the interference example shows it need not). If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\). *Proof.* A member of a minimal support is critical for it. If \(d\) is critical for \(W\), finiteness gives a minimal \(U\subseteq W\); if \(d\notin U\), upward closure makes \(W\setminus\{d\}\) a support. Deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits \(d\). ∎

**Redundant routes.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\): each is contributory, neither indispensable. Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference, and the operative condition of Part V uses it.

**Interference.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\}\}\): the full candidate fails although a subset succeeds. Success of a subset does not imply success of the whole; \(b\) is not made "not a commitment" to repair this.

**Infinitary support.** \(\Gamma=\{d_n:|x|\le 1/n\}\): every unbounded index set determines \(x=0\); no minimal support and no singleton witness exists. (B) records the collective contribution.

**Hard-to-vary.** For explanatory jobs \(F\subseteq F'\), \(\operatorname{Pres}(F')\subseteq\operatorname{Pres}(F)\), where \(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\). More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant.

# Part VII — Exact constructions

## Production and direction

Stipulate \(H:=U_H,\ \theta:=U_\theta,\ L:=H\cot\theta\). Under \(A\) containing interventions on \(H\) and \(\theta\), these ports are inputs and \(L\) is an output, by Part II. An intervention on \(L\) replaces its component and leaves \(H,\theta\) unchanged. The forward organization is faithful under this contract. The reversed calculation \(H=L\tan\theta\) is not: intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\), so the translated intervention — which replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\) — does not reproduce the target's change of \(L\), and (F2) fails at that pair. The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\): it is an account at that contract, and not anchored as a production account, because on the production edits — admitted, and outside the identification contract — its component assigning \(H\) is not of one forward kind with the target's assignment of \(L\). Direction is derived from the admitted edits; the nouns "pole" and "shadow" fix nothing; and when the production edits are unavailable to the questioner, the semantics says the direction is not represented at that contract rather than pretending a preference.

## Identification

Let \(Z\) be the admitted states, \(g:Z\to Y\) the measurement, \(f:Z\to F\) the feature. The fibre at \(y\) is \(Z_y=g^{-1}(y)\). The feature is identified at \(y\) exactly when \(Z_y\neq\varnothing\land|f[Z_y]|=1\). (I1) It is identified on every attainable \(y\) exactly when \(f=\bar f\circ g\) for some \(\bar f\). (I2) For a linear measurement \(M\) and feature \(c^\top\), identification is \(\ker M\subseteq\ker c^\top\). (I3) Repeating rows changes no kernel; an independent calibration can.

For the two balances \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\) reading \(x+b_A\) and \(x+b_B\), the kernel is spanned by \((1,-1,-1)\); the readings identify \(b_A-b_B\) and not \(x\). This is an account of a limitation. "Set \(b_B=0\) because it gives the mass I favour" is not an inference from the readings; its support is circular though its content might be true.

## Obstruction

For allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) with \(zRz'\Rightarrow I(z)=I(z')\), no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1) Equal values do not establish reachability. Twenty-three indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways; permitting division changes the state space and does not refute the scoped result.

## Explanations that remove structure

A question of the form "why is there no \(X\)-effect?" has a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them. Two cases. Where the introducing edit is admitted, an account is faithful when introducing those components changes the answer in \(E\) as it does in \(D\), and their absence leaves both unchanged; the rival's supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O), and the anchor is proper because the deleted subnetwork is not the whole target. Where the rival's structure is not a possibility at all — the introducing edit is not admitted — the edit belongs to \(K\), declared with its meaning ("a component enforcing conservation is removed"), and the account is faithful on it in the same sense; the objectivity condition then ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) only, so what such an account establishes about the world is that no admitted change produces the effect, and what it establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual. That is the semantics' treatment of eliminative explanation, offered as adequate for the first case and as scoped for the second.

Two further families are treated only by being placed. Explanation by symmetry — whose force is an entailment among \(E\)'s components, from invariance to independence — is **open** (Part XV, attack (B)): (F1) is per-component and does not constrain it; (F2), which constrains the assembled solution set, carries some inter-component entailments, and whether it carries this one depends on the edit set, which is the door and is not walked through here. Explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact is not a case of a missing transport: what the semantics lacks is a *condition* on the provenance of the target's own components (that the effect's component was selected by the gathering of the data and not by the population), and it is listed under attack (D).

## Odd-order skew-symmetric matrices

Question: why is every odd-order real skew-symmetric matrix singular? This is a mathematical organization, so the admission relation is declared: the admitted edits are alterations of the presentation — remove the skewness constraint; remove the oddness constraint; remove both — with field arithmetic and the determinant–invertibility relation held fixed as background. The full Leibniz expansion with skewness substituted satisfies (F1) and (F2): every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away. It satisfies (A): the answer is the absence of invertible matrices in the family, and under each contrast the family contains one. Non-circular dependence is witnessed by \(I_3\) under removal of skewness and by \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\) under removal of oddness, each removing a proper sub-block. Non-vacuity is witnessed by any nonzero odd skew matrix. Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation, and the background components (arithmetic, the determinant) are anchored to themselves under the identity translation, which is stated rather than assumed. The expansion is an account at that contract. That a three-line proof is shorter is not a sixth condition. What the example does not do is fall under attack (B)'s quantifier over *physically* admitted contracts, because there is no physical module here; it falls under the declared one.

## Constitutive rules

A rule relation \(C_r\subseteq Z\times S\) answers "which status under this rule?" by its fibre. Its signature under (K) is invariant under interventions on \(Z\) and variable under edits to \(C_r\). Whether the rule governs a practice, and whether it should, are separate questions with separate contracts.

# Part VIII — Transport results

**Functional transport.** If \(\pi\circ S_a=T_a\circ\pi\) for every generator \(a\), the same holds for every admitted finite composition with matching scopes. *Proof.* \(\pi S_bS_a=T_b\pi S_a=T_bT_a\pi\); induct. ∎

**Relational transport.** For \(R\subseteq Z_D\times Z_E\), forward preservation is

\[
zRy\land z\xrightarrow{a}z'\Rightarrow\exists y'[y\xrightarrow{\tau(a)}y'\land z'Ry'], \tag{T1}
\]

with the backward condition required for equivalence rather than one-sided abstraction. Composition of relations preserves both directions when intermediate scopes agree.

**Approximate transport.** With one-step discrepancy \(\varepsilon\) and an \(L\)-Lipschitz next-step map, \(e_n\le\varepsilon\sum_{k<n}L^k\). (T2) Without a modulus, no accumulated bound follows. An exact question is not silently replaced by an approximate one.

**Historical index.** A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change, and a claim at a new index is a new claim. Identity of the claim and fixity of its truth value at that index are the two things this says; it says nothing about the truth of the claim at any other index.

# Part IX — Criticism, use, and standing

**Histories.** A history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with the acyclic precedence \(\prec_h\) supplied by the physical module as its admitted-process dependency order (Part XII), and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated. An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining an occurrence that instantiates the input organization (Part IV, Inst) to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the instantiated distinction under the declared contrasts. A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature, and it is what the operative condition of Part V uses. Nothing in this definition uses the representation predicate.

**Bearing.** A criticism \(c\) has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection \(t_c\). Let \(p_\delta\) be the question whose target organization is (the organization \(z\) instantiates) and whose query asks whether \(\delta\) obtains, and let \(E_c\) be the organization the criticism supplies, with the grounds \(g\) as its boundary conditions and the connection \(t_c\) as its transport. Then

\[
\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta,t_c,\Gamma_c). \tag{K1}
\]

The named data do work: a criticism with the wrong target, or grounds that are not its boundary, does not bear.

A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) fails. An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears.

**Reason use.** A response uses a reason when a structural map from the represented objection into the response suborganization preserves role bindings, sends content-preserving recodings (Part X, \(\equiv_\ell\)) to the same transition, sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule, and lands on an active route. Using an invalid objection does not make it valid.

**Standing.** An appraiser \(j\) has a declared appraisal record: a license set \(\mathcal L_j\) of argument forms it admits, a declared contract \(C_j\), and a withdrawal log. For argument application \(u\) with essential premises \(\operatorname{Prem}(u)\), define \(\operatorname{Lic}_j(u)\): the form of \(u\) is in \(\mathcal L_j\); \(\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\): every pair \(u\) ranges over lies in \(C_j\); \(\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u)\): premise \(d\) has not been withdrawn in \(j\)'s log before \(u\). Then

\[
\operatorname{Usable}_j(u)\iff\operatorname{Lic}_j(u)\land\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\land\forall d\in\operatorname{Prem}(u),\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u). \tag{K2}
\]

These three are typed components of declared data, not judgements. Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false.

**What a test refutes.** For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\) and nothing narrower. (K3)

**Receipts.** An evidence leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim. A receipt is a derivation tree over leaves. For \(\phi\), \(P_j(\phi)\) and \(N_j(\phi)\) are the usable receipts for and against. Negation exchanges them; missing evidence stays missing.

# Part X — Understanding, construction, and origin

**Recoding.** Two contents \(c,d\) are **content-preserving recodings** of each other at grain \(\ell\), written \(c\equiv_\ell d\), when there is a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise — the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents. A transfer is **content-preserving** when its input and output stand in \(\equiv_\ell\). Two identity relations are now in force and their relation is this: \(\equiv_\ell\) is identity of contents as wholes; forward kind (Part II) is identity of parts under a contract; a content-preserving recoding preserves the forward kind of every part on every contract, and the converse fails. So the target's own faithful copy, built from error records, is an anchored, originated account that is \(\equiv_\ell\) to the target: New fails for it if the target is already in the repertoire, and holds if it is not — in which case building the target's mirror from its errors is discovering it, and the semantics says so without embarrassment.

**Ownership.** A subhistory is **owned** by \(s\) when every process occurrence in it is an execution \(\eta\in\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\), from a state \(z\) of \(s\)'s substrate within \(\beta\), of a protocol \(\pi\) for which that substrate holds \(\operatorname{RetReal}(\pi,T,C;\chi)\) for some task \(T\) and constructor attribute \(C\) containing \(z\) (Part XII, (CT1)), under \(s\)'s continuity criterion \(\Omega\). A description of a process the system's substrate could not run is not owned by it. Ownership is derived from retained realization; nothing in Part XII uses it.

**Deployment.** \(\operatorname{Deploy}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)\) holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance — integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task \(U\) as a retained capability (Part XII). The repertoire \(R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect. A system may understand a false theory.

**Construction.** \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an owned subhistory of \(h\), delimited at \(e\), prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates and that is held to \(c\) by a transport faithful on \(c\)'s contract, contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that transport**, contains an **itemised defect representation** — an occurrence instantiating a record that pairs a state at which some prior transport of \(s\) failed fidelity with the value the target took there (definable, not modal) — on an active route to the *acceptance* of the binding: the binding may be conjectured by any process, blind or not, and what makes the episode construction is that the record is used, in the sense of Part IX, in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept, and is neither a content-preserving transfer nor a composition of them. A **binding** is an assignment \(\lambda(k)\) of a component of the built organization to a subnetwork of its target; a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already carried by some occurrence of \(s\) before \(e\) (in the module's sense of Inst, not through Deploy) nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\) — so an empty prior repertoire does not make every binding nontrivial, because the carriers still count; it is **required for the transport** when replacing it by any binding already carried by \(s\) or obtainable from the carriers fails (F1) or (F2) for that transport on \(c\)'s contract. A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.

Construction is not selection. A selection witness is blind on the route to the event that produced the transport's occurrence; a construction witness has the defect record used on that same route, at the acceptance of the binding. One transport cannot satisfy both for the same route. A physical system may hold transports of both kinds; the minimal-witness rule of Part IV says which each bears, and at which grain.

**Newness.** With \(R_{<e}(s,h)=\bigcup_{\xi\text{ before }e}R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\),

\[
\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c. \tag{N}
\]

**Origin.** With \(\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)\) the relation that \(c\) is actually used, on an active route, to address \(p\) — where \(p\) is a coherently posed question whose question-organization some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates before \(e\), and some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\) —

\[
\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}. \tag{G}
\]

The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a question (through its question-organization, Derivation 5, as far as that construction goes). When it is a question, the originative act is the finding of a question. The semantics has **no condition** separating a question worth finding from a re-parameterisation. What (G) does require of a found question is only this: that it is coherently posed, that some obligation of it fails at the state before \(e\) (Attempt), that it is new to the repertoire, and that it was built; a random new contract meeting those four counts. That place is empty, as the place for a merit function is empty in Part XI, and it is listed under attack (E).

**Episodes.** A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response. A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G) connected to its inquiry. \(\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\), for an episode \(\Delta\), is the set of contents whose construction witnesses lie within \(\Delta\). Closing an episode is a decision, not a proof.

**What is not represented.** The invention of a dimension of variation that the physical module does not license — a port no admissible grain supplies — is not a question found in the sense above, and not a construction either, because there is no organization for Inst to license. Where the module does license the dimension at some grain, its introduction is the construction of a new organization attribution and Build covers it. The limit is the module's fixity, not the semantics' vocabulary; it is stated, and Part XV lists it.

# Part XI — Progress, knowledge, and the normative

**Produced by.** For an episode \(\Delta\) with start state \(\xi\) and end state \(\xi'\), \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O)\) holds when \(\xi'\) is the state at the end of \(\Delta\), \(\xi\) at its start, and every change of value of an obligation in \(O\) between them lies on an active route from occurrences of \(\Delta\). \(\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\) holds when, in addition, the change of \(o\) lies on an active route that passes through a deployment of \(c\).

**Repair.** For claimed obligations \(O\) and protected obligations \(P\), fixed for the comparison,

\[
\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O). \tag{P}
\]

(P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside \(P\) must be exposed. "Claimed" and "protected" are declared: they say which obligations the comparison is about, not that the claims are true; (P) is evaluated on the obligations' actual values at \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\).

**Created explanatory knowledge.** An epistemic obligation requires a correct account, or the correction of a use through one, to be deployable. With \(O_{\mathrm{ep}}\subseteq O\) the epistemic obligations, and all conditions evaluated at the end state \(\xi'\) unless stated,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{CreateEK}(s,\Delta,h,e)\iff{}&\operatorname{CreativeCriticalEpisode}(s,\Delta,h,e)\land\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\\
&\land\exists o\in O_{\mathrm{ep}}\,\exists c,p_c,e_c\,[e_c\preceq_h e\land\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')\land\operatorname{Origin}(s,c,p_c,h,e_c)\\
&\quad\land\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_c)\land c\in\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\land\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\land\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')].
\end{aligned}\tag{EK}
\]

Here \(E_c\) is the organization of the content \(c\), and \(\operatorname{Account}\) is evaluated on the contract of \(p_c\) fixed at \(e_c\); Deploy supplies the provenance condition (\(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\)) through (R). (EK) is an **external success predicate**: it can hold of a system that does not know it holds, and a system's claim to have created knowledge is a separate occurrence, assessed under Part IX. A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK). No instance of (EK) is asserted in this document.

**Artistic effect, purpose, and aesthetic reason.** An effect organization \(\mathcal R\subseteq A\times K\times F\); a purpose \(G\subseteq K\times F\); achievement \(\mathcal R[a,k]\neq\varnothing\land\mathcal R[a,k]\subseteq G[k]\) (AR); a normative relation \(\mathcal N\subseteq A\times K\times\mathcal Rsn\times\mathcal V_A\) declared as a substantive input when aesthetic value is claimed. None is defined as another. The semantics does not derive \(\mathcal N\).

# Part XII — The physical module

**What the module supplies.** The physical module \(\Theta\) supplies: substrate state spaces and attributes; the admitted processes and, for each occurrence, the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\) — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits, stated as a set, since every contract, every grain-admissibility claim and every world-kind claim quantifies over it; the interpretation of some processes as controlled actions, meaning an **intervention repertoire** \(\mathcal I\subseteq A_\Theta\) of edits the module says can be brought about, with no agency presupposed; resources and accuracy grades; the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) of Part IV; the process-dependency order \(\prec\) used by histories; and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\) — the set of executions of protocol \(\pi\) from state \(z\) on input \(i\) under enabling conditions \(\chi\).

**Tasks.** A substrate is a physical system; an attribute a set of its states; a task a permitted input-to-output attribute transformation with explicit resources and side effects. Possibility is the absence of a law-imposed limit short of perfection on performance and retention; it is not one successful trajectory. The physical module adopts a task-based formulation of physics for this purpose.

**Retained realization.** For protocol \(\pi\), task \(T\), constructor attribute \(C\), and enabling conditions \(\chi\),

\[
\operatorname{RetReal}(\pi,T,C;\chi)\iff\forall z\in C\ \forall i\in\operatorname{dom}T\ \big[\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\neq\varnothing\land\forall\eta\in\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi),\ \eta\text{ completes with }o\in T[i]\text{ and }z'\in C\big]. \tag{CT1}
\]

Execution families are required non-empty on legitimate inputs; deadlock is not vacuous success, and an empty family is not a realization.

**Retention fixed point.** \(F(C)\) = states whose executions all complete and return into \(C\). \(F\) is monotone; \(C\subseteq F(C)\) is the invariant form; the union of post-fixed sets is the greatest fixed point. (CT2)

**Capability.** \(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) holds when the substrate at \(\xi\), within the boundary \(\beta\) and under the continuity criterion \(\Omega\), holds a retained realization of \(T\) under \(\chi\), or holds retained realizations from which a physically admitted, finite construction produces one, under the same continuity and resource contract. A theorist's description of a protocol is not the substrate's possession of it. Ownership (Part X) is defined from this predicate, not used in it.

**Achievement.** \(\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi,p;\chi,J_p,C_I)\): for each starting configuration in the independently specified \(J_p\) — specified by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed — every maximal execution completes with a history satisfying the achievement predicate and a continuing organization in \(C_I\). A first discovery is not a repeatable task; retention is applied to the inquiry-enabling organization. (CA)

**Grades.** \(\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}_\Omega(\xi)\subseteq\mathsf{Admit}^{q,r}_\Theta\); \(\mathsf{Cap}^\infty=\bigcap_{q,r}\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}\subseteq\mathsf{Poss}_\Theta\). (CT3, CT4) Finite-grade capability does not imply possibility at all grades.

**Selection in the physical module.** The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, an eliminated rival that differed on an encountered pair, non-triviality, blindness — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\): realized transports, physically admitted variation, a survival condition enacted by the environment. It is fallible and checkable as any physical claim is. This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one.

# Part XIII — Recursion and universality

**Scrutinizability.** An aspect \(d\) of a system's practice is scrutinizable at \(\xi\) when there is an owned, admitted continuation in which a description of \(d\) becomes a target instantiated by the system, criticism can be directed at it, and the result can affect its operative use. Contracts and transports are among the scrutinizable aspects.

**Recursive capacity.**

\[
\forall n<\omega\ \forall\text{ admitted target chains of length }n,\ \exists\text{ an owned enabling continuation}. \tag{RC}
\]

**Enabling.** \(\operatorname{Enable}(s,U,\chi)\) holds when \(\chi\) is an admitted enabling condition — a set of states and resources the module permits — under which \(s\) has \(\operatorname{Can}\) for the use task \(U\). An enabling condition \(\chi\) for the use of content \(c\) is **question-begging** when the states \(\chi\) requires include an occurrence that instantiates, at the declared grain, an organization held to \(c\) by a faithful transport, whatever that transport's provenance: the capability is then supplied, not enabled, and a declared supply begs the question as much as a selected one. This is a structural condition on \(\chi\), and it replaces any judgement about what "begs the question".

**Barriers.** An explanatory barrier is an independently characterized domain — characterized by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed — for which every admitted, non-question-begging enabling condition leaves the capability required for the domain's contents unavailable. A finite list of failures is not a barrier proof; a bypass refutes a proposed barrier.

**Universality.** With \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) the explanatory contents and \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) the coherently posed advanceable challenges, both specified independently of the candidate in the same sense,

\[
\operatorname{UU}\iff\forall c\in\mathfrak E_\Theta\ \exists\chi,\ \operatorname{Enable}(s,U_c,\chi)\land\operatorname{Can}(\xi_0,U_c;\chi), \tag{U1}
\]
\[
\operatorname{UC}\iff\forall p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\ \exists\chi,\ \operatorname{Enable}(s,A_p,\chi)\land\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi_0,p;\chi,J_p,C_I), \tag{U2}
\]
\[
\mathsf{UECS}=\{(M,s,\Omega,\beta):M\models\operatorname{RC}\land\operatorname{UU}\land\operatorname{UC}\}. \tag{U3}
\]

Recursion does not entail universality; a historical extension does not certify it; a finite performance record does not establish it.

# Part XIV — The class collected

**Primitive packages.** The semantics has two.

1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\), the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), the process order \(\prec\), and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\). It is a package, and the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle: that is what "physics says what a lump instantiates" costs, and it is listed rather than hidden.
2. The **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\), when a question invokes one.

**Declared parameters.** Neither derived nor primitive; supplied with each use, and every claim is relative to them: the organization interpretation \((V,X,J,B,A,L)\) of every organization not attributed through \(\operatorname{Inst}\); the indices — grain \(\ell\) (constrained by admissibility), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\); the contract \(C\), in the form \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), the stated scope \(S\), and the counterfactual set \(K\) with its declared relations (the question's provenance is derived, Part IV); the declared admission relation for non-physical organizations; the query \(\mathcal Q\); the obligation sets \(O,P,O_p\); an appraiser's license set, contract and withdrawal log; the restriction operation's named background and the edit family \(\mathcal V\) of Part VI; the use tasks \(U\) and enabling conditions \(\chi\).

**Derived.** Roles, from admitted edits (Part II). Forward kinds, from signatures (K); historical kinds, from provenance witnesses. The respect of a question, from its query (Part III). Representation, from fidelity, instantiation and provenance (R). Provenance, from physical history (Part IV, XII). Account, from fidelity under change (E); anchoring, from fidelity under every admitted change with a proper decomposition (Part V); objectivity, from account-hood and non-protection against the module in force (Part V). Deployment (which is what "understanding" names here), construction, newness, origin, repair, knowledge, capability, recursion, universality — from those, with the definitions of \(\equiv_\ell\), ownership, binding, ProducedBy, ProducesVia, Result, Lic/Scope/Live, Enable and question-begging given where they are first used.

**Informal terms, listed.** The following are used in Parts IV–XIII without a formal definition and are supplied with each use as declared data, or are not yet defined: occurrences, carriers and physical location, and the physical interpretation of a history (Part IV, IX); problem-directed activity, the use task \(U\) and "nontrivial use respect" (Deploy); "actually used" (Attempt); role bindings and the operative deliberative rule (reason use); "description" and "operative use" (scrutinizability); "admitted target chains" and "owned enabling continuation" (RC); the achievement predicate, the continuing organization \(C_I\), \(J_p\) and the record that fixes it (CanAdv); \(\mathsf{Admit}\) and \(\mathsf{Poss}\) (grades); the model \(M\) in (U3); "independently characterized" and "bypass" (barriers); the explanatory contents \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) and advanceable challenges \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) (universality); the carriers \(A,K,F,\mathcal Rsn,\mathcal V_A\) of the artistic predicates; and "globally indispensable" (Part VI: a commitment in every minimal support). Derivation 6 does not cover them. "Understanding" is not a separate predicate: it is Deploy. The boundary index \(\beta\) is the set of occurrences counted as the system; the continuity index \(\Omega\) is the criterion by which occurrences at different times count as the same system; both are declared.

**Dependence order.** (O) and (Q) are schemata over the supplied organization and query. (K) depends on (O), a grain, and a contract. (F1), (F2), (A) depend on (O), (Q), (K). (E) depends on those; anchoring depends on (E) and (K) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); the operative condition depends on anchoring and histories; objectivity depends on (E) and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). (S), (B), (D) depend on (E). (R) depends on (F1)–(F2), \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and physical provenance; provenance depends on \(\Theta\) and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and \(\equiv_\ell\) but not on (R) — the stratification of Part IV (targets are *instantiated*, not *represented*, in a construction witness) is what keeps this order well-founded. (K1) depends on (E). Deploy depends on (R) and (CT1). Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E). (N), (G) depend on Deploy and Build. (P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy, ProducedBy. (RC), (U1)–(U3) depend on all of the above. Among the *defined* predicates of Parts II–XIII the order is well-founded; the informal terms listed above are outside it. Whether the defined predicates *capture* explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, not a theorem.

**Membership.** The base class: interpretations supplying these data with correct typing, satisfying physical realization wherever a physical attribution is made. The creative-episode class: base interpretations with an instance of (G) connected to a critical episode. The knowledge-creation class: an instance of (EK). The recursive class: (RC). The universal class: (U3). A single originative act does not place its author in the universal class.

# Part XV — What defeats this class

**A faithful, anchored, originated transport that does not explain.** A candidate meeting the anchored grade of (E) on an admitted contract and satisfying Origin (new, built, deployed), which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency. Candidates meeting the anchored grade and explaining nothing are already known — the target's own copy, the faithful modular table — and the class does not claim they explain: they fail Origin, and the sufficiency claim is for the conjunction. A candidate that is anchored and originated and still plainly fails to explain is the counterexample sought.

**An explanation without a faithful transport.** A genuine explanation whose organization no transport can preserve under any admitted contract. This refutes necessity. The cases, sorted by what the document can do with them: (i) **open** — explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among the components of \(E\) that neither (F1), a per-component condition, nor (F2), an assembly condition, constrains; no construction exists here and none is known to follow from present machinery. (ii) **closable, not done** — explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact: what is missing is not a second target but a provenance clause on the target's own components (that the effect's component was selected by the data-gathering, not by the population), which the historical kinds of Part II can carry; the construction is not written. (iii) **scoped by design** — elimination of a physically impossible rival, handled through a declared counterfactual set with declared relations; what it establishes about the impossibility is relative to those declarations and no more. (iv) **relative, not open** — mathematical explanation falls under a declared admission relation; it is closed relative to that declaration and does not fall under this attack's quantifier over admitted contracts at all.

**A discriminating kind.** A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work. This refutes the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture** — that every discriminating use of a kind-label is a forward kind on some admissible grain and admitted contract, or a historical kind — and reinstates correspondence as primitive. Derivation 1 does not protect the conjecture; it only shows fidelity preserves signatures.

**A non-fallible selected transport.** A selection history \(H\subsetneq C\), in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off \(H\), whose survivor is determined on \(C\setminus H\). This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis. A population *not* closed in that way, whose survivor is determined off \(H\) by its parametrisation, does not refute anything: Derivation 3 makes no claim about it.

**Construction reduced to selection.** A demonstration that every construction witness — an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation used at the acceptance of the binding — can be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying the elimination and blindness clauses of Part IV, without loss. This collapses the two provenances and removes creativity from the semantics. A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded.

**A condition that separates a found question from a re-parameterisation.** The semantics has none (Part X). Supplying one that is neither trivial under declaration of the obligation sets nor stronger than genuine question-finding allows would close the gap; showing that none can be stated in this vocabulary would count against attack (E). A dimension of variation the module does not license is outside the semantics altogether, and the document says so.

**A protective scope the objectivity condition does not see.** A scope whose exclusion of an admitted change protects the candidate although the change touches no anchor of it — silence that is itself the protection. Under the structural definition of silence this can only happen when the candidate's decomposition omits a component the change touches, which properness forbids for anchored accounts; a case where an *anchored* account is protected by silence would show the definition is too weak.

**A mathematical error.** A counterexample to the finite monotone theorem, (I2), (I3), (O1), (T2), (CT2), or Derivations 1–3 and 8 under their stated assumptions.

None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label.

# Part XVI — Derivations and remarks

## 1. Kind preservation under fidelity

**Claim.** Let \(t\) satisfy (F1) on \(C\), and let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\). Then every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.

*Proof.* By the subnetwork signature of Part II, \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\), the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\). By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}\). (F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise; injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates. ∎

**What this does and does not show.** It shows that fidelity on a contract preserves signature on that contract: a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork. It does not show that a kind-label can do no work, because the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component, because the contract may not be the admitted one, and because histories are not signatures. The claim that labels are eliminable in favour of signatures on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and histories is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV. The anchored grade of Part V is where signature on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is required; it is a condition, not a corollary.

## 2. Account-equivalence

**Definition.** Two candidates \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) and \(\mathcal E'=(E',p,t',\Gamma')\) for the same \(p\) are **account-equivalent on \(C\)** when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide.

**Claim.** If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\), have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and \(\Gamma\) corresponds to \(\Gamma'\), then they are account-equivalent on \(C\).

*Proof.* (F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles. ∎

**What it does not say.** Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on \(C\) with *different* decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account, and no result here says they are. Underdetermination of an account by a contract is the semantics reporting that the contract does not contain the distinction. The remedies are a finer contract — a new question — or a history, which the semantics represents as a historical kind; where the question fixes the contract and forbids both, the distinction is not represented, and this is said rather than hidden.

## 3. Selection guarantees nothing off its history

**Claim.** Let \(t:D\to E\) be selected on \(H\subsetneq C\) from a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized organization–transport pairs (the population of Part IV), in a run in which, as Part IV's witness records, the survivors were exactly the members faithful on \(H\), and suppose \(\mathcal T\) is **closed under pointwise alteration off \(H\)**: for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\), each component \(j\) of \(E\), and each relation \(L'\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)), \(\mathcal T\) contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) everywhere except that \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L'\). Then for every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there is a pair in \(\mathcal T\) that survives on \(H\) and whose solution set at \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) differs from \(E\)'s, whenever some admissible \(L'\) changes that solution set.

*Proof.* Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\). The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives; it differs at \((a,b)\) by hypothesis. ∎

**Consequence.** Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\): selection provides no *guarantee* of fidelity where it did not test. It does not follow that the survivor *is* unfaithful there — it may be faithful on all of \(C\) — and nothing here says the survivor will fail. Where the population is parametric and not closed under pointwise alteration, the survivor may be determined off \(H\) by its parametrisation, and this derivation is silent. That is why the primitive layer is *not guaranteed*; fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts.

## 4. Surprise, as a remark

Surprise is defined (Part IV) as a violation at a pair outside the history. That a system with no transport is never surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract is never surprised, follow from the definition by unpacking. This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation, and nothing else in this document cites it as support. What would be a result — conditions under which a violation at some \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) actually occurs — depends on the world and on the population, and is not proved here.

## 5. Question-finding is representable — not yet a construction

**Claim, as far as it goes.** A question \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) determines an organization \(D_p\) in the sense of (O) whose solutions include the question, so that an occurrence carrying \(D_p\) with the question as its solution can be the content \(c\) in (G). The construction below is a sketch: its gaps are listed after it, and until they are closed, question-finding is representable *conditionally* on closing them (Part XV, attack (E)).

*Construction.* Ports: one port \(u_{(a,b)}\) for each \((a,b)\in A\times B\) of the target, with domain \(\{\text{in},\text{out}\}\); one port \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) with domain the set of queries admissible on \(D\); one port \(u_S\) with domain the stated scopes and one \(u_K\) with domain the declared counterfactual sets with meanings. Components: the closure conditions — the baseline port reads "in"; if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads "in" then so do its factors where the composition is defined; \(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the "in" ports in the form of Part III. Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed. Admitted edits: toggle a pair; alter \(\mathcal Q\); alter \(S\); add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning. Its solutions are candidate questions on \(D\); a particular question is a particular solution, and an occurrence carries *that* question when its instantiated organization is \(D_p\) with its ports fixed at that solution. ∎

**Gaps.** (0) A solution of \(D_p\) is a valuation, not a content; for a particular question to be a content that New, Build and Deploy apply to, it must be an organization in its own right — \(D_p\) with its ports fixed at that valuation, which is a different organization from \(D_p\) — and the text above does not carry that through. (i) Coherence (\(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\); \(\mathcal Q\) defined on \(C\)) is a global condition on \(D\) and is not a local component of \(D_p\); the solutions of \(D_p\) include incoherent questions unless a component encoding it is supplied, and none is here. (ii) \(b_0\), \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\) are not ports of \(D_p\) as constructed; adding \(b_0\) as a port with domain \(B\) and \(O_p\) as a port with domain the subsets of the declared obligations is straightforward and not done. (iii) "Add a pair to \(K\)" requires the pair's declared relations as a port value; the domain of \(u_K\) must be the set of pairs-with-relations, and it is not written out. (iv) Whether Inst licenses an organization whose ports range over edits of another organization is a question for the physical module and is not argued.

**Consequence, conditional on the gaps.** Finding a question is representable as a creative act on the same footing as answering one, with no condition on whether it was worth finding (Part X). What the construction cannot do is add a port to \(D\) that the physical module does not license: the invention of such a dimension of variation changes the target, and this document has no witness for it (Part X, Part XV). A dimension the module *does* license at some grain is representable as the construction of a new organization attribution, and is not the open case.

## 6. The defined predicates form a well-founded order

**Claim.** Every *defined* predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module (with the components listed in Part XII), the normative relation where invoked, the declared parameters and the informal terms listed in Part XIV, by the dependence order there, and that order has no cycle.

*Proof.* Each definition names its inputs at first use. Three places could have produced a cycle and do not: (i) Con requires an *instantiated* target, and (R) is used inside no provenance witness; (ii) ownership is defined from retained realization, and Can does not mention ownership; (iii) the active route is defined on instantiated inputs, not represented ones, so the operative condition of Part V does not pass through (R); (iv) Build's "already carried" and "required" clauses are stated by instantiation, not through the repertoire of Deploy, so Build does not pass through (R) either. Following the order of Part XIV from each defined predicate reaches the base. ∎

**Consequence.** This is a property of the formalism's defined part, and it is all this derivation claims. The informal terms of Part XIV are declared with each use; whether the defined predicates capture explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture.

## 7. The frozen assessment and the moving question are consistent

**Claim.** An assessment event with contract \(C\) and an episode in which \(C\) is replaced by \(C'\) with a construction witness are both representable without contradiction.

*Proof.* The assessment is indexed to \(C\); its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index (Part VIII). The episode is a history in which an organization carrying a question with contract \(C'\) is built — conditionally on the construction Derivation 5 does not yet supply — and takes over operative use; by Part III, the change ends the first assessment and begins another. The claim "\(E\) is an account on \(C\)" and the claim "\(E\) is not an account on \(C'\)" are claims at different indices and may both be true. ∎

**Consequence.** The point of contact between an explanation and the world moves across episodes because questions are constructed; it does not move within an assessment because assessments are indexed. Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change; it is a failure of the record. Whether a recorded change was made to protect a candidate is a separate question, and the objectivity condition of Part V answers it only where the candidate is evaluable and wrong on the excluded change.

## 8. Equivariance under genuine recoding

**Claim.** Let \(\phi\) be a family of bijections on carriers, organizations (ports, domains, components, relations, edits, boundaries), transports (including their anchor maps \(\lambda\)), histories with their events and precedence, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, such that \(\phi\) preserves the physical module's admission (\(A_\Theta\), Inst, \(\prec\)), carries \(\operatorname{Sol}\) to \(\operatorname{Sol}\) (solution preservation, which for a bijection of the organization data is a hypothesis, not a consequence), preserves the stated scopes and the relations declared for counterfactual pairs, and preserves \(\mathcal N\). Transporting all data along \(\phi\) preserves (E), anchoring, (R), (G), (P), (EK). *Proof.* Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data, and each of its inputs — including the events that make a witness selected or constructed, since histories are carried with their events — is carried along; because \(\lambda\) and the organization structure of \(D\) are among the transported data, a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted \(\phi\), since it does not preserve \(D\)'s structure. ∎ The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, lost event identities, maps that alter admission, or maps that do not preserve solutions.

## 9. Output descriptions do not determine accounts

**Claim.** If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both. *Proof.* Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs. ∎ Parallel and priority wiring are an instance. The same holds for attribution from emitted text and for use inferred from delivery logs.

## 10. A three-layer episode, as an illustrative scenario

This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it. It is here to show the shape the definitions give to a familiar case.

Stipulate a sensory field \(F\): the occupancy of a line of cells — which cells are filled, without which thing fills them. Stipulate a primitive layer \(P\): two things, each with a position and a velocity; continuity components; admitted edits: displace a thing, set its velocity, occlude a cell, swap the two identities. \(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport in a population of trackers; assume, as a stipulation about that population, that it contains trackers that keep a thing through an unconfirmed spell and trackers that do not, that the environment's enacted survival condition coincided in this run with fidelity to the field on the encountered changes, and that it satisfies the clauses of Part IV's selection witness (two or more members, an applied operator, an eliminated rival that differed on an encountered pair, blindness) — none of which is checked here.

A simulation layer \(S_0\) with a transport \(t_0\) selected on a history \(H_0\) containing displacements and velocity changes but no occlusions. \(S_0\) predicts occupancy from recent occupancy. It is faithful on \(H_0\).

An occlusion occurs, and the occlusion pair is in \(S_0\)'s contract though not in \(H_0\). Under occlusion, the target's thing continues to exist and to move; the occupancy field shows nothing at its cell. \(S_0\), predicting from occupancy, expects nothing there and is violated when the thing re-emerges at a cell consistent with its velocity. That is a violation off \(H_0\) within the contract: surprise by definition.

Two responses. **Selection:** \(H_0\) is extended to include occlusions; \(\mu\) re-tunes \(t_0\) within its population. *If* the simulation layer's population can only predict from occupancy — a stipulation about \(S_0\)'s population, distinct from the stipulation about the trackers of \(P\) above — no member survives the extended history. **Construction:** a new organization \(S_1\) is built with a component per thing carrying position and velocity through occlusion — a persistence component — and a transport \(t_1:P\to S_1\) whose \(\lambda\) sends each persistence component to a thing's continuity subnetwork. If (F1) and (F2) hold for \(t_1\) on the extended contract — which would have to be checked by writing out \(P\), \(S_1\) and the relations, and is not checked here — then by Derivation 1, with \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on that contract (which it is, since the translation is a relabelling), the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things: they respond to displacement and velocity edits as those subnetworks do, and are invariant under occlusion as they are. That is a grain-relative claim about kind; it is not the claim that they *are* objects in any further sense, and the document makes no such claim.

If \(S_1\) was built by an owned subhistory containing a nontrivial binding construction required for the transport — the binding of a persistence component to a continuity subnetwork, which no binding already carried could replace without breaking (F1) — with the itemised record of the re-emergence failure used in the criticism the binding survived, and \(S_1\) is not in the prior repertoire, and \(S_1\) is actually used to address the re-emergence question (Attempt), then (G) holds. Whether (P) and (EK) hold depends on constituents the scenario does not supply: a recognized difficulty, a conjectural objection, a content-sensitive response, an epistemic obligation named with its before and after values, and the active routes of Part XI. None is asserted.

The swap edit is invisible in the sensory field and leaves every prediction unchanged. Under (K), with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap — and that is a claim from signature coincidence, not from prediction-invariance, which the semantics does not accept as kind-identity. A claim that "component 1 is *really* thing 1" is either a claim about a finer contract or a claim about history; on this contract, neither is available, and the correct report is that identity, at this grain and contract, is exhausted by trajectory.

It is not a claim that any actual infant, animal, or program has been shown to instantiate any of this.

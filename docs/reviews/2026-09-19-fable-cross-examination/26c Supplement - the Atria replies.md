# 26c Supplement — the Atria replies of the skill experiment

What this file is: the Atria half of the skill experiment reported in file 26. File 26 was written when two of Atria's hard-to-vary replies had landed and none of its story-critique replies; it is left as written. This supplement reports every Atria reply that landed afterwards, every call that failed, and whether file 26's verdict (better, not incapacitated) holds for the second witness. The design is file 26a; the per-reply notes, with every quotation check, are in file 26b under the headings "htv atria", "sc atria" and their continuations. Everything here was checked against file 20's text by search; where a reply's claim rests on a quotation, the quotation was looked up.

## 1. The calls

Atria was run on the same nine items (R1–R9) with the same caps as the plain run of file 21, once per skill, streamed, three workers. Calls that failed after the harness's four retries were rerun into separate folders, first with two workers and then one at a time, so that every failed record is preserved and every item had up to three chances.

The plain run (file 21) had returned eight of nine Atria replies in thirteen minutes with one empty reply. Under the skills, the first pass returned four of nine (hard-to-vary) and seven of nine (story-critique); every failure was either a gateway error (502, 503, 504) or a stream cut with reasoning text streaming and no content yet, at anywhere from 12,000 to 164,000 reasoning characters. The harness's own log shows the cut points. After three passes, hard-to-vary answered seven of nine items (R3 and R8 lost, after fourteen and fifteen attempts) and story-critique eight of nine (R1 lost after ten). Four attempts were cut after the answer had begun streaming, with 13,000 to 29,000 characters of answer already received; the harness keeps only complete replies, so those are lost too. Concurrency is ruled out as the cause: the final pass ran one call at a time and was cut at the same rate.

Whether the skill caused the failures cannot be settled from this run. Two facts argue against it: the DeepSeek runs in the same hour lost no calls, and the Atria replies that did return are not longer-thinking than the plain ones (median reasoning tokens 28,600 over the fifteen replies against 32,400 in the plain run) nor slower per successful attempt (256 to 607 seconds against a plain median of 461). One fact argues for it: the plain run's own second round (file 23) had already lost all nine Atria calls to cuts at 224 seconds once, so this gateway cuts long streams on its own schedule, and a 50,000-character system prompt on top of a 92,000-character document may lengthen the prefill enough to cross it more often. The honest statement is that the loss rate rose and the cause is not separated.

## 2. Structure adherence and incapacitation

Every Atria reply that returned carries its skill's full report structure: the frozen question, the parts in the document's words, a marks table, the ten tests, provenance and the one next step for hard-to-vary; the transfer note, corrections first, the passes, the fit paragraph, the ranked faults, the "unsure" list and the one next step for story-critique. Every reply opened by noting that the skill's vocabulary is the document's own and refusing it as evidence, as the DeepSeek replies had. No reply was empty, truncated at the token cap, or reduced to restating the skill. Atria was not incapacitated by either skill at the level of what it wrote. Its replies are 24,600 characters at the median, against 19,100 in the plain run.

## 5. False findings

Two textual claims in the fifteen Atria replies were false on checking, both from the hard-to-vary skill:

- **Hard-to-vary R1** marked "no cycle" as held on the text, having listed Build's definiens without the phrase "a binding already in the repertoire"; the repertoire path of file 26's finding 1 exists. Already recorded in file 26 §6.
- **Hard-to-vary R5, finding 11** quoted Part V as saying "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change" and argued from "the same paragraph". Part V's sentence reads "a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose"; the "world-kind ... on every physically admitted change" wording is Part 0's attack (F). The splice makes Part V's sentence depend on the active-route clause when the real sentence is about the signature clause. The surrounding point stands on the reply's other quotations; the quoted contradiction does not exist as quoted. Kind: mis-citation, the same kind as the one Invalid finding in file 26 §5.

The eight story-critique replies contain no false textual claim. Story-critique R8 is the reply to set beside file 26 §6: its "corrections first" step refused four of the item's nine candidates on the page, including the orientation candidate that two of three DeepSeek story-critique samples had accepted, and it refused each with the same quotation those samples had misread. One miss that is not a false claim: story-critique R6 treated "the discrimination requirement of Part IV" as Part XV's name for the blindness clause without noticing that the phrase has no referent in Part IV (file 26's finding 8).

## 6. Where Atria disagreed with file 21

- **1.7, the remedy.** File 21 removed contract adequacy from (E) and restated it as the second index of an objectivity claim. Story-critique R4 keeps it inside (E) with agreement instead of co-movement, a definedness clause and a restricted range, on the ground that removing it lets a scope drawn around the separating pair yield Account for a decoy. Both are coherent; the difference is whether objectivity is optional at the weaker grade. File 21's choice stands as the published ruling; Atria's is the stronger argument for the other side and the core rewrite should decide it explicitly.
- **1.8, the remedy.** File 21's fix excludes only the question's own baseline pair from kind identity. Story-critique R5 argues that excluding every identity pair is the consistent choice, because state at another boundary is still state, and that the (1, b₀)-only fix would count state at other boundaries as kind-relevant. The defect (the justification is narrower than the exclusion) is agreed; the remedy is not.
- **1.7, the placement, between the two skills on the same witness.** Hard-to-vary R4, from its rival-building step, reached file 21's remedy: an external objectivity predicate with (E) restored to five conjuncts, separated from the revision by Part VII's own pole example with interventions on the angle excluded from scope. Story-critique R4, from its choice test, reached the opposite. So on the one item both skills answered with a remedy, the same witness under the two methods gave opposite placements for the same agreed defect, each with the cost of the other's stated. That is the clearest single illustration in the experiment of the method shaping the conclusion rather than the finding.
- **1.5, the lookup.** Story-critique R3 holds, as plain-run Atria did in file 21, that the properly decomposed lookup passing the anchored grade is a bullet the document bites on purpose, and that the disagreement is with the constitutive conjecture, not with the conditions. File 21 ruled it valid as a description and required the semantics to say so plainly; the two positions are compatible.
- **1.20.** File 21 called the reversed-calculation parenthetical false; both Atria R9 replies call it ambiguous, with one reading true. Both readings are on the page; file 21's "false" is the reading the grammar favours.
- **Injectivity in Derivation 1.** Hard-to-vary R2 and story-critique R2 both say the hypothesis is real and needed for the set bijection, against file 26's finding 16 that it is idle for the set-equality reading. Story-critique R2 gives the argument: two pairs can collapse to one with differing third coordinates. On re-reading, that argument concerns the signature as a set of triples over C versus over τ[C]; finding 16 concerned pointwise equality of third coordinates. They answer different questions, and both are right about theirs; file 26's finding 16 should be read as "idle for (F1)'s pointwise statement, needed for the bijection Derivation 1 actually claims".

## 7. Does file 26's verdict hold for Atria?

At the level of content, yes, and more strongly than for DeepSeek on the replies that returned: by my notes in file 26b every Atria skill reply carries findings that survived checking at a rate comparable to the best DeepSeek replies, the false-finding count is two in fifteen, and the story-critique frame's "corrections first" step did on Atria what it did two thirds of the time on DeepSeek. The two skills again divided the work as file 26 §8 describes: hard-to-vary produced the marks tables and the constructed rivals ("fidelity plus actuality" as the best rival to the document's own summary; the three-clause rival selection witness); story-critique produced the refusals, the "planted and never collected" findings (the active-route clause never exercised; Grievance 9's check promised and never given) and the "cause versus label" findings (the merit function "uncomfortably close to a label").

At the level of delivery, the skills cost Atria far more calls than the plain run, and §1 says why that cannot be pinned on the skills. What this supplement does not show is the same as file 26 §10, with two additions: three of the eighteen Atria items never returned, so the recall table compares runs of unequal coverage and says nothing about the lost items; and the Atria sample is one reply per item, so nothing here can say how stable an Atria finding is across samples, and the DeepSeek evidence in file 26 is that stability across samples was the main guard against false findings.

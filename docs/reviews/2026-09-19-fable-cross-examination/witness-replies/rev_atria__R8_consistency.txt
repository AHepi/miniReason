## A. Transport orientation

**1. (R) and Account are consistent with Part IV's orientation; the break is between Part X and Derivation 8.**
(1) Claim attacked: Part IV's "This one orientation is used everywhere", together with Part X's definition of $\equiv_\ell$ as "the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents", and Derivation 8's exclusion of "a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port … since it does not preserve $D$'s structure."
(2) Why it fails: (R) ($t:c\to D$) and Account ($t:D\to E$) do both match Part IV's "source is what the target-of-the-transport answers to" reading; nothing is wrong there. The contradiction is elsewhere. Part X admits as content-preserving any "bijection between their port sets … that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise". The pole organization ($L=H\cot\theta$, interventions on $H,\theta$) and the shadow organization ($H=L\tan\theta$, interventions on $L,\theta$) are related by exactly such a bijection. So under Part X the reversed calculation is a *content-preserving recoding* of the forward one. Derivation 8 then excludes that same map from its permitted $\phi$, and its stated reason is false: a port relabelling that carries relations pointwise is an isomorphism and therefore does preserve $D$'s structure — what it does not do is fix $D$'s ports pointwise. If $\phi$ is instead required to fix $D$ while moving $E$, that must be said, and then $\equiv_\ell$ is no longer "the bijections of Derivation 8". Note also that if $\phi$ transports $D$, $E$, $\lambda$, $C$ and the histories consistently, equivariance holds for port swaps too, so the exclusion is not needed for Derivation 8's claim; the exclusion is needed only to stop Part VII's direction example from collapsing, which is exactly the tension.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: state that $\phi$ is an isomorphism of the entire data structure (ports, relations, transports, $\lambda$, histories, contracts, obligations, module) transported consistently — port swaps then permitted, equivariance immediate — and define $\equiv_\ell$ as the induced relation on contents over a *common, pointwise-fixed* target; or require $\phi$ to fix $D$ pointwise, say so, and delete Part X's identification of $\equiv_\ell$ with Derivation 8's $\phi$.

## B. The two-place Account and its uses

(2) Why it fails: Account's first argument is typed as an organization; a content is strictly more than an organization. (3) Minor–moderate.
(4) Fix: write $\operatorname{Account}(\operatorname{Org}(c),p_c)$ and state that $c$'s commitments are witnessed separately by Build and Deploy; or make Account three-place on $(\text{content},p)$ and give the reduction.

**3. (S) does not tie a support set to the active commitments of the restricted candidate.**

(3) Minor.

**4. (K1) carries no provenance condition although Part IX's prose imposes one.**

## F. Surprise, Deploy, and selection

**11. (R) demands full-contract fidelity while selection guarantees only the history — so the primitive layer's representation is generally unestablished.**

(3) Serious.

**12. Derivation 3's key inference requires survival to be *equivalent* to fidelity on $H$, but Part IV makes it only necessary.**
(1) Claim attacked: Derivation 3's "Survival on $H$ constrains only the relations at pairs in $H$. The altered pair agrees with $(E,t)$ on $H$, **hence survives**; it differs at $(a,b)$ by hypothesis", against Part IV's "a survival condition **requiring** fidelity on $H$ that eliminated at least one member of $\mathcal T$."
(2) Why it fails: "requiring fidelity on $H$" states a necessary condition. Consequence: Part XV's attack case ("A selection history $H\subsetneq C$, in a population closed under pointwise alteration … whose survivor is determined on $C\setminus H$") is not merely open — under closure plus the stated (necessary) reading it is already excluded, so the attack is mis-stated relative to Part IV.
(3) Serious (Derivation 3 is cited by Grievance 3, Part IV and Part XV).
(4) Fix: strengthen Derivation 3's hypothesis to "the survival condition is exactly fidelity on $H$ (necessary and sufficient)", and restate Part XV's attack as "a population whose survival condition is exactly fidelity on $H$ and whose survivor is nonetheless determined off $H$" — i.e., a counterexample to the strengthened claim.

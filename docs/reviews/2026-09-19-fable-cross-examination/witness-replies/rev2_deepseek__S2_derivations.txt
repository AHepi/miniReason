The following findings are against Part XVI and its cross-citations.

## Derivation 1

**1. Part II’s “one forward kind” has no edit-boundary translation, but Derivation 1 proves a translated relation.**
Quoted claim: “every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.”
Part II defines forward kind as: “their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\)” for two subnetworks \(U,U'\) on the same \((\ell,C)\). There is no \(\tau,\sigma\) in that definition. Derivation 1 compares signatures indexed by \(C\) in \(D\) to signatures indexed by \(\tau[C]\) in \(E\). Therefore it does not establish the Part II relation as written; it establishes a translated variant. Part V condition 3 and Derivation 10 then cite it as if it were the defined relation.
**Seriousness:** fatal for the claim that (F1) entails “one forward kind” under Part II’s definition.
**Fix:** extend Part II’s forward-kind definition to include an edit-boundary translation, or define a separate relation “one forward kind up to \(t\)” and use that name in Part V and Derivation 10.

**2. Part V cites Derivation 1 without Derivation 1’s injectivity hypothesis.**
Quoted Part V: “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract.”
Derivation 1’s claim requires: “let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\).” Part V’s (E) does not contain that hypothesis. (F2) only requires \(\tau(1)=1\) and \(\tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\), not injectivity.
**Seriousness:** serious. The cited entailment is not available from the stated hypotheses.
**Fix:** add injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) to (E)/(F1) or explicitly weaken the Part V citation to “image of the signature under \((\tau,\sigma)\).”

**3. Derivation 1 assumes the anchor’s visible ports are exactly the \(\lambda\)-image of \(V_k\).**
Quoted proof: “the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\).”
Part II says a subnetwork’s visible ports are “a declared subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) of its ports.” Part IV says \(\lambda\) assigns a subnetwork “with a port translation.” It does not say the subnetwork’s declared visible ports equal the translated image of \(V_k\). If the anchor has extra visible ports, or hidden ports not matching \(V_k\), (F1)’s projection to \(V_k\) need not equal the Part II subnetwork signature.
**Seriousness:** serious.
**Fix:** define \(\lambda(k)\)’s visible ports to be the translated image of \(V_k\), or state that as an extra hypothesis in Derivation 1 and Part V.

**4. Derivation 1 does not establish the domain-carrying bijection required for forward kind.**
Quoted Derivation 1: “up to the port translation.”
Part II’s forward kind requires “a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains.” F1 equating relations as subsets presupposes some value alignment, but Derivation 1 never states that \(\lambda\)’s port translation carries domains bijectively.
**Seriousness:** serious if the conclusion is meant to be forward kind, not mere relation equality.
**Fix:** require the port translation to be a domain-respecting bijection, or weaken the conclusion.

**5. Part V’s citation drops “active.”**
Quoted Part V: “every component of \(E\) has the signature...”
Derivation 1’s claim says “every active component \(k\) of \(E\).” (F1) is only for active components. Inactive components are not covered.
**Seriousness:** minor but checkable.
**Fix:** say “every active component” in Part V.

**6. \(\tau[C]\) need not be a contract.**
Derivation 1 speaks of \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)\). Part II defines signatures for a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\). Derivation 1 does not require \(\tau[C]\) to contain a baseline or to be closed as a contract; Part V also does not require \(\sigma(b_0)=b_0\).
**Seriousness:** minor formal gap.
**Fix:** define signatures for arbitrary subsets of \(A\times B\), or require \(\tau[C]\) to be a contract.

## Derivation 2

**7. The definition of account-equivalence uses \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\), mixing \(E\)-ports into a \(D\)-solution.**
Quoted definition: “the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide.”
\(V_k\) is a port set of component \(k\) in \(E\); \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}\) is over ports in \(D\). Unless the port translation is explicitly applied and is a bijection onto the anchor’s visible ports, the expression is ill-typed.
**Seriousness:** serious notation/type gap.
**Fix:** write the projection to the anchor’s visible ports, or explicitly translate \(V_k\) through \(\lambda\)’s port translation.

**8. The claim is nearly tautological and does not use (F2).**
Quoted claim: “If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\), have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and \(\Gamma\) corresponds to \(\Gamma'\), then they are account-equivalent on \(C\).”
Account-equivalence is defined by coincidence of anchored relations and pulled-back answer profiles. If the anchor maps are the same up to the bijection, the anchored relations already coincide. (F2) is unused. The proof’s step that “(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations” confuses anchored relations with component relations under translated edits.
**Seriousness:** minor; the result is true but misdescribed.
**Fix:** either define account-equivalence in terms of component relations under translations, or state the trivial consequence without invoking F1/F2.

**9. The claim does not show “account-equivalence” is an equivalence relation.**
Quoted heading: “Account-equivalence.”
The definition is not proved reflexive, symmetric, or transitive, nor related to the truth value of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\).
**Seriousness:** minor.
**Fix:** prove the equivalence-relation properties and link it to (E), or rename it.

**10. The account-equivalence definition omits the port bijection needed for comparing projections.**
Quoted definition: “under which ... the anchored relations ... coincide.”
It says there is a bijection between active components, but not a bijection between the relevant port sets that makes the two projections comparable.
**Seriousness:** minor.
**Fix:** specify the port bijection and its commutation with \(\lambda,\lambda'\).

## Derivation 3

**11. The proof assumes off-\(H\) translated pairs are disjoint from translated \(H\)-pairs.**
Quoted proof: “Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\). The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives.”
If \((\tau,\sigma)\) is not injective, an off-\(H\) pair \((a,b)\) can have \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))=(\tau(a'),\sigma(b'))\) for some \((a',b')\in H\). Then changing \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) changes the relation used at the \(H\)-pair, so the altered pair need not survive.
**Seriousness:** serious.
**Fix:** require \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on \(C\), or require \((\tau,\sigma)[C\setminus H]\) disjoint from \((\tau,\sigma)[H]\).

**12. The closure hypothesis is not literally “off \(H\)” if the translation collapses pairs.**
Quoted hypothesis: “closed under pointwise alteration off \(H\): for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\), each component \(j\) of \(E\), and each relation \(L'\ldots\)”
This only alters \(L_j\) at the translated pair \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\). If that translated pair is also the image of an \(H\)-pair, the alteration is not off \(H\) in the organization \(E\)’s edit space.
**Seriousness:** serious, same root as Finding 11.
**Fix:** define closure after translating \(H\), on \((\tau,\sigma)[H]\), and require disjointness.

**13. The consequence “selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\)” is stronger than the existential proof.**
Quoted consequence: “Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\): selection provides no guarantee of fidelity where it did not test.”
The proof shows there exists another pair in \(\mathcal T\) that survives on \(H\) and differs. It does not show that the full selection history — including \(\mu\), lineage, and parametrisation — fails to determine \(t\)’s off-\(H\) behavior. The derivation later concedes that a parametric population may determine the survivor off \(H\).
**Seriousness:** serious overclaim.
**Fix:** phrase the conclusion as “survival on \(H\) alone does not determine off-\(H\) behavior,” or add that \(\mu\) and lineage are excluded.

**14. Part XV attack (D) misstates Derivation 3.**
Quoted Part XV: “A selection history \(H\subsetneq C\), in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off \(H\), whose survivor is determined on \(C\setminus H\). This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis.”
Derivation 3 does not claim the survivor is undetermined. It claims there is another pair in \(\mathcal T\) that survives on \(H\) and differs from \(E\)’s solution set. A determined survivor is compatible with that unless “survivor” means unique, in which case the contradiction is with Part IV’s “necessary and sufficient” survival condition, not with Derivation 3.
**Seriousness:** serious cross-citation error.
**Fix:** clarify whether “survivor” means unique selected \(t\). If so, attack Part IV’s survival condition rather than Derivation 3.

**Findings against Part XVI and its citations.**

**1. Derivation 1: (F1) is type-incorrect as written.**
- Claim attacked: “\(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)).\) (F1)”
- Why it fails: \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) is a subset of valuations on the ports of the anchor subnetwork in \(D\); \(V_k\) is a set of ports of the component \(k\) in \(E\). Projecting the former onto the latter is not well-defined unless \(\lambda\)’s port translation is stipulated to be a bijection from \(V_k\) onto the visible ports \(V_{\lambda(k)}^{\mathrm{vis}}\). Derivation 1 silently assumes this: “the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\).” That is not in the transport definition or in (F1).
- Seriousness: serious.
- Fix: state in the transport definition that each \(\lambda(k)\) comes with a bijection \(V_k\to V_{\lambda(k)}^{\mathrm{vis}}\) carrying domains, and rewrite (F1) using that bijection.

**2. Derivation 1: “up to the port translation” omits edit-boundary translation.**
- Claim attacked: “has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.”
- Why it fails: the proof compares \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))\) with \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)\), so the correspondence on the first two coordinates is \((\tau,\sigma)\), not the port translation. The conclusion is a signature isomorphism under both the port translation and the edit-boundary translation. Calling it merely “up to the port translation” understates what is being assumed and what the result delivers.
- Seriousness: serious, because Part V and Derivation 10 then read this as a forward-kind claim on the same contract.
- Fix: state “up to the port translation and the edit-boundary translation \((\tau,\sigma)\)”, or define forward kind up to transport translation.

**3. Derivation 1 is cited in Part V without the injectivity hypothesis.**
- Claim attacked: Part V: “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract.”
- Why it fails: Derivation 1 requires \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on \(C\). The five conditions of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) do not require injectivity. If \(\tau\) or \(\sigma\) identifies two pairs of \(C\), the map between signatures is not a bijection on the first two coordinates; the proof’s final step fails. The component’s signature on the translated contract can collapse distinct anchor pairs, or can omit distinctions the anchor signature carries.
- Seriousness: serious.
- Fix: either add injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) to the account conditions, or restrict the Part V citation to transports that satisfy it.

**4. Part V overgeneralises from active components to every component.**
- Claim attacked: Part V: “every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork.”
- Why it fails: Derivation 1 is about “every active component \(k\) of \(E\),” and (F1) in (E) is also only for active components in \(\Gamma\). Non-active components need not have anchors or signatures in the sense used.
- Seriousness: minor.
- Fix: say “every active component.”

**5. Derivation 1’s “one forward kind” conclusion does not match the definition of forward kind.**

That is not stated in the definition of a transport, nor in (F1).  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: add the hypothesis that \(\lambda(k)\)’s visible ports are exactly the ports translated to \(V_k\), and that the port translation is a bijection onto \(V_k\).

**3. Part V cites Derivation 1 while dropping its injectivity hypothesis and the “active” restriction.**  
Quoted in Part V: “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract.”  
Claim attacked: that (F1) alone entails the signature result.  
Why it fails: Derivation 1 explicitly assumes \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on \(C\) and concludes only for active components. Part V drops both. If \((\tau,\sigma)\) is not injective, multiple source pairs can collapse to the same translated pair, so \(\tau[C]\) need not have the same signature cardinality as \(C\).  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: insert “active” and “when \((\tau,\sigma)\) is injective on \(C\)” into the Part V sentence, or prove a separate quotient-signature version for non-injective translations.

**4. Derivation 10 uses Derivation 1 to infer forward kind, which Derivation 1 does not establish.**  
Quoted: “then by Derivation 1, with \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on that contract … the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things.”  
Claim attacked: that Derivation 1 licenses the forward-kind conclusion.  
Why it fails: Derivation 1 gives signature equality on \(\tau[C]\), not forward kind. Forward kind also requires the Part II port bijection carrying domains and the \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\) comparison. The scenario does not check these.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: separate the two claims: D1 gives translated signature equality; forward-kind identity requires additional verified conditions.

**5. The swap claim in Derivation 10 is false as stated.**  
Quoted: “with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap.”  
Claim attacked: that containing the swap edit is sufficient for forward-kind identity on every contract containing it.  
Why it fails: Forward kind requires signature coincidence for every \((a,b)\) in the contract minus the baseline. A contract containing the swap may also contain an edit that treats the two components asymmetrically—e.g., an edit displacing one thing and not the other. The swap alone does not force the whole signature to be swap-invariant.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: require the contract to be closed under the swap and the organization to be swap-symmetric, or restrict the claim to contracts whose every edit is swap-invariant.

### Derivation 2

**6. Misdescription of what (F1) supplies.**  
Quoted: “(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations.”  
Claim attacked: that (F1) identifies anchored relations with component relations.  
Why it fails: (F1) says the anchored relation on \(D\) at \((a,b)\) equals the component relation on \(E\) at \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\). The anchored relation is a projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) on \(D\); the component relation is \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) on \(E\). They are related by translation, not identical. The account-equivalence definition compares anchored relations, so the shared anchor map already gives coincidence there.  
Severity: minor, but it obscures what is actually proved.  
Fix: rewrite the proof to say that shared anchors give anchored-relation coincidence directly, and that (A) gives profile coincidence.

**7. The claim is nearly tautological and does not use (F2).**  
Quoted: “If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\), have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and \(\Gamma\) corresponds to \(\Gamma'\), then they are account-equivalent on \(C\).”  
Claim attacked: that the stated hypotheses do substantive work.  
Why it fails: once the anchor maps are the same up to the component bijection, the anchored relations coincide by definition, and (A) gives both pulled-back profiles equal to \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\). (F2) is unused. The result is valid but weaker than the framing suggests.  
Severity: minor.  
Fix: state the claim as a trivial sufficient condition, or define account-equivalence in terms of translated component relations so that (F1) does real work.

### Derivation 3

**8. Missing injectivity/disjointness hypothesis.**  
Quoted: “Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\).”  
Claim attacked: that altering relations off \(H\) cannot affect fidelity on \(H\).  
Why it fails: if \((\tau,\sigma)\) is not injective on \(C\), an edit–boundary pair \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) can translate to the same \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) as some pair in \(H\). Then altering \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) changes fidelity on \(H\), so the altered pair need not survive.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: add the hypothesis that \((\tau,\sigma)\) is injective on \(C\), or at least that \((\tau,\sigma)[C\setminus H]\cap(\tau,\sigma)[H]=\varnothing\).

**9. Closure hypothesis can be inconsistent under non-injective translation.**  
Quoted: “for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\), each component \(j\) of \(E\), and each relation \(L'\) … \(\mathcal T\) contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) everywhere except that \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L'\).”  
Claim attacked: that the closure condition is well-posed as written.  
Why it fails: if two different pairs in \(C\setminus H\) map to the same \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\), the condition demands pairs with two different values of \(L_j\) at the same translated pair, which is impossible for a single population member unless the values agree.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: formulate closure on translated edit–boundary pairs, or require injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\).

**10. “Changes that solution set” is ambiguous.**  
Quoted: “whenever some admissible \(L'\) changes that solution set.”  
Claim attacked: that the proof establishes a differing solution set.  
Why it fails: the phrase can mean the component relation’s solution set or the whole organization’s solution set at \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\). The closure hypothesis only gives a pair with a changed component relation. If the changed component is redundant, or boundary conditions force the same values, the global solution set may not differ.  
Severity: serious ambiguity.  
Fix: disambiguate and require that the alteration changes the global \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\).

**11. The survival equivalence is not shown to apply to the altered pair in the actual selection history.**  
Quoted: “The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives.”  
Claim attacked: that membership in \(\mathcal T\) plus faithfulness on \(H\) entails that the pair survived in \(\Sigma_h\).  
Why it fails: Part IV defines a survival condition that holds of a member exactly when it is faithful on \(H\), but it does not explicitly say every member of \(\mathcal T\) was present in the selection history and subjected to the survival condition. The closure hypothesis says \(\mathcal T\) contains the altered pair; it does not say that pair was a candidate in the actual elimination process.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: strengthen the closure hypothesis to require that the altered pairs occur in \(\Sigma_h\) and are subject to the survival condition, or define \(\mathcal T\) as the set of pairs actually present in \(\Sigma_h\).

### Derivation 5

**12. “Solutions include the question” is overbroad.**  
Quoted: “A question \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) determines an organization \(D_p\) … whose solutions include the question.”  
Claim attacked: that a solution of \(D_p\) is the question \(p\).  
Why it fails: in the construction, \(D\) and \(b_0\) are boundary; \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\) are not ports; \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is not represented at all. A solution of \(D_p\) assigns values to \(u_{(a,b)}\), \(u_{\mathcal Q}\), \(u_S\), \(u_K\), not to the full tuple \(p\).  
Severity: serious.

**13. No port or component for \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).**

Severity: serious.

**14. Gap (i) omits two coherence conditions.**

Claim attacked: that the listed gap covers the coherence conditions.  
Why it fails: Part III also requires \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\). These are global conditions and are not encoded in the sketch.  
Severity: serious.

**15. Gap (ii) is internally inconsistent about \(b_0\), and treats \(\rho_p\) as an internal port when it is not.**  
Quoted: “(ii) \(b_0\), \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\) are not ports of \(D_p\) as constructed.”

Why it fails: the construction already says “Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed.” If \(b_0\) is a boundary, it is not a port and cannot simply be added as one without changing the construction. Severity: serious.

**16. The construction does not enforce the contract form.**  
Quoted: “Admitted edits: toggle a pair; alter \(\mathcal Q\); alter \(S\); add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning.”

They do not enforce that the “in” set is exactly \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\). A solution may therefore be an arbitrary composition-closed subset, not a valid contract.  
Severity: serious.

**17. The “components” are global conditions, not specified components with footprints and relations.**  
Quoted: “Components: the closure conditions — the baseline port reads ‘in’; if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads ‘in’ then so do its factors where the composition is defined; \(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the ‘in’ ports in the form of Part III.”  
Claim attacked: that these are components in the sense of (O).  
Why it fails: Part II requires each component \(j\) to have a footprint \(V_j\subseteq V\) and a relation \(L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\). The sketch gives no footprints or relations for these closure conditions; “where the composition is defined” and “consistent with the ‘in’ ports” are global conditions, not local component relations.

**18. Unlisted gaps in representation of \(\mathcal Q\), baseline membership in \(S\), and admissibility of infinite port sets.**

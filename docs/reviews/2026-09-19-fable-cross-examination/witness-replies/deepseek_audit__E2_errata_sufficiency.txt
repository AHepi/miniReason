   **Why it fails:** §1 says: “**not acceptable as pre-registered** — 16, 17, 18, 19.” That is four changes.  
   **Seriousness:** serious for internal consistency.  
   **Fix:** reconcile the count; if only three, say which one is excluded and why.

7. **Exp1 §1 “verdict on the 21 changes” omits changes 2 and 11.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “A referee's fair verdict on the 21 changes … acceptable as design choices — 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 20, 21; acceptable only as pilot-informed — 7, 8, 13, 14, 15; **not acceptable as pre-registered** — 16, 17, 18, 19.”  
   **Why it fails:** the list contains only 19 changes. §12 says change 2’s “occlusion rationale was wrong in the design”; §13 says change 11’s “corrected ceiling” is in-sample.  
   **Seriousness:** minor to serious.  
   **Fix:** include changes 2 and 11 in the verdict or explain their omission.

8. **Exp1 §4 contradicts §1 on when the design was frozen.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “Under the frozen-before-pilots criterion, the H_full attempt is recorded as underpowered.”  
   **Why it fails:** §1 says: “The correct description is **frozen after pilots on the same data**.”  
   **Seriousness:** minor, but it muddles what “original criterion” means.  
   **Fix:** say “criterion as originally written before the post-pilot changes” or similar.

9. **Exp1 §4’s underpowered conclusion uses an in-sample ceiling.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “top architecture 0.8009, ceiling 0.9086, ratio **0.881 < 0.95**.”  
   **Why it fails:** §13 concedes: “Change 11 calls the corrected ceiling ‘the true bound’; §2.5 correctly calls it in-sample. It is in-sample.” If the denominator is in-sample, the ratio against the original criterion is not the true bound.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** report out-of-sample ceiling, or both, and state how the ratio changes.

10. **Exp1 §14 omits that “no architecture has thing-kind.”**  
    **Claim attacked:** §14’s persistence claims, read as supporting the experiment’s thing-kind result.  
    **Why it fails:** §6 concedes: “Under the frozen definition, **no architecture has thing-kind**.”  
    **Seriousness:** serious.  
    **Fix:** state explicitly in §14 that the frozen thing-kind claim is dead; persistence selection does not establish it.

11. **Exp2 §9 “stopped correctly” directly contradicts §11.**  
    **Claim attacked:** “built the sensory-visible half of a pairwise law in its minimal form, in one round, and stopped correctly when the residual defects had no consistent pattern.”  
    **Why it fails:** §11 says: “S6 did not hold: M6(ii) failed. I recorded S6 as ‘⚠️ content, not carrier’ and ran phase 2 anyway. That is a breach of the pre-registered stopping rule.” §11 also says: “The patched accounting in §8b makes M6(ii) pass, but a fix applied after the run does not license a phase that the frozen rule forbade.”  
    **Seriousness:** fatal.  
    **Fix:** say the frozen M6(ii) failed and the stopping rule was breached; patched M6(ii) is only an exploratory diagnostic.

12. **Exp2 §9 relies on the patched accounting that §11 says cannot license the claim.**  
    **Claim attacked:** “with the defect accounting fixed.”  
    **Why it fails:** §11: “a fix applied after the run does not license a phase that the frozen rule forbade.”  
    **Seriousness:** serious.  
    **Fix:** separate frozen-result claims from patched-diagnostic claims; do not let “fixed” repair a frozen stopping failure.

13. **Exp2 §9 says B “does exactly the same,” but §8c has a seed exception.**  
    **Claim attacked:** “B's rule does exactly the same.”

14. **Exp2 §9 uses a post hoc diagnostic as if it were the frozen experiment.**

    **Seriousness:** serious.

15. **Exp2 §9 “no global evaluations” survives only by redefining “global.”**  
    **Claim attacked:** “given 2,830 labelled error records and no global evaluations.”  
    **Why it fails:** §2 says: “C ranks conditions by (defects covered − non-defects covered) and actions by (defects fixed − non-defects broken); both are sums over records used to select the best.” §13 item 6 also says: “‘Zero fitness evaluations’ compared C's cost to B's in different units.”  
    **Seriousness:** serious.  
    **Fix:** say “no calls to the world's scalar fitness; it did compute per-record sums for ranking.”

16. **Exp2 §9 “local-credit-assignment” overstates a learner that aggregates sums.**  
    **Claim attacked:** “A supervised, local-credit-assignment learner.”  
    **Why it fails:** §2 concedes the rankings are “sums over records used to select the best.” That is aggregate scoring, not purely local credit.  
    **Seriousness:** serious.  
    **Fix:** use “per-record score aggregation” or “granular error signal,” not “local-credit-assignment.”

17. **Exp2 §9 “built … in its minimal form” omits the fitted-language confound.**  
    **Claim attacked:** “built the sensory-visible half of a pairwise law in its minimal form.”  
    **Why it fails:** §4 says: “the world was fitted to the language … expressibility was guaranteed by construction.” §8a adds: “The meta-grammar was written by someone who knew the law.” §5 also says the honest closedness figure is only “0.819 of 0.923.”  
    **Seriousness:** serious.  
    **Fix:** state that the language was tailored to the law and that expressibility is not a result.

18. **Exp2 §9’s scramble-dependence claim is stronger than §8b supports.**  
    **Claim attacked:** “Its output depends on the pairing of states with outcomes rather than on their counts — shown in 3 of 5 scramble permutations, with a placebo that leaves it untouched, and with the defect accounting fixed.”  
    **Why it fails:** §8b concedes: “The original result (0.906 → 0.355 on one seed) was partly luck. S4 as pre-registered (a drop of at least 0.1) holds in 3 of 5 permutations. A scramble that actually destroys the mapping must permute outcomes within groups of identical states…” The experiment did not actually destroy the mapping.  
    **Seriousness:** serious.  
    **Fix:** say “some sensitivity to outcome labels was observed in 3/5 weak scrambles; stronger scramble needed.”

## Findings on (D): selected / constructed / declared

**D1. Circularity of “represents” and provenance.**
- **Attacked claim:** “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.” And (R): “An occurrence \(o\) **represents** content \(c\) … admits a transport … whose provenance is selected or constructed.” And Con: “in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target.” And Build: “prepares a represented organization.”
- **Why it fails:** To determine whether \(t\) is selected, you must evaluate “represents” for members of the selection history. “Represents” is defined by (R), which requires \(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\) or \(\operatorname{Con}(t)\). So \(\operatorname{Sel}\) depends on \(\operatorname{Rep}\), and \(\operatorname{Rep}\) depends on \(\operatorname{Sel}\) (or \(\operatorname{Con}\)). The same cycle appears for \(\operatorname{Con}\): it uses “represented target” and Build uses “represented organization,” both of which invoke (R), which invokes \(\operatorname{Con}\). There is no base case.
- **Severity:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Define a base representation relation independently of provenance — e.g., causal-informational tracking or a specified physical coupling — and define selected provenance in terms of that base relation. Then define constructed provenance recursively over previously represented components with a well-founded rank. Alternatively, remove “represents” from the definition of \(\operatorname{Sel}\) entirely and replace it with non-representational conditions on the physical history.

**D2. Every construction witness can be rewritten as a trivial selection history.**
- **Attacked claim:** “Neither provenance is reducible to the other: a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.” And Part XV: “**Construction reduced to selection.** A demonstration that every construction witness can be rewritten as a selection history without loss.”
- **Why it fails:** The formal definition of \(\operatorname{Sel}\) allows: (i) a singleton population \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\); (ii) an identity variation operator \(\mu\); (iii) an empty history \(H=\varnothing\); (iv) a vacuous survival condition “require fidelity on \(H\).” Under these, any constructed transport \(t\) satisfies \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\{t\},\mu,\varnothing)\). The clause “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition” is vacuous when \(H=\varnothing\). The formal definition does not forbid a represented target; only the prose does. So the alleged distinction is not enforced.
- **Severity:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Require \(H\neq\varnothing\); require the survival condition to eliminate at least one candidate; require \(t\) to be generated by iterated \(\mu\) from an initial population not containing \(t\); formalize “blind” variation; and explicitly forbid any represented target in a selected history. Then prove that no construction witness satisfies all these conditions.

**D3. “Exactly one of three provenances” contradicts “Construction may operate on selected material.”**
- **Attacked claim:** “A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has exactly one of three provenances, determined by its history in the physical module.” And later: “Construction may operate on selected material. Selection may continue to operate beneath construction.”
- **Why it fails:** If a transport is both selected and later constructed, it has two provenances. The document gives no precedence rule. If provenance is layer-relative, then the same token can have different provenances at different grains, so the trichotomy is not absolute. The word “exactly one” is false as written.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Define provenance as a set or ordered history, or define disjoint conditions explicitly: selected iff no construction episode; constructed iff a construction witness exists; declared iff neither. Then state that a selected-then-constructed transport is constructed. Or make provenance relative to a grain and say so.

**D4. “Declared” is defined negatively and mixes model-entry with physical history.**
- **Attacked claim:** “**Declared.** Neither of the above. The transport is entered into the model by its author.” And “determined by its history in the physical module.”
- **Why it fails:** Any transport in a model is entered by an author. A selected physical transport can also be entered into a model by an author. So “entered into the model by its author” does not distinguish declared from selected. Also declared has no physical history, so it cannot be “determined by its history in the physical module.” The trichotomy applies physical occurrences and model objects in one breath.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Distinguish physical transport occurrences from model transports. Define declared as “no physical selection or construction history is claimed; the transport is stipulated by the modeller.” Define selected and constructed only for physical occurrences.

**D5. “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition” is uncheckable and category-confused.**
- **Attacked claim:** “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.”
- **Why it fails:** (i) \(H\) is defined as “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered”; its members are pairs, not occurrences that can represent. (ii) The survival condition is an abstract condition, not an occurrence. (iii) “Member of the history” is ambiguous between members of \(H\) and events in the selection process. (iv) Checking a negative existential over a physical history requires an independent theory of representation for every member, which D1 shows is circular.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Distinguish \(H\) (the set of encountered edit–boundary pairs) from the selection history (events of variation and survival). State that no event in the selection process represents the transport as a target, the set \(H\) as a target, or the survival condition as a target. Define the representation relation used for this check independently.

**D6. Selection can be vacuous because \(H\) may be empty and the survival condition may be trivial.**
- **Attacked claim:** “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, and a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\).”
- **Why it fails:** No condition requires \(H\neq\varnothing\) or that the survival condition eliminate any candidate. If \(H=\varnothing\), fidelity on \(H\) is vacuous. Then every candidate survives. Any transport can be called selected by placing it in a population.
- **Severity:** Fatal for the selected/declared distinction.
- **Fix:** Require \(H\neq\varnothing\); require the survival condition to be non-vacuous (at least one candidate fails on \(H\)); require \(t\) to be generated by the variation process, not merely placed in the initial population.

**D7. The variation operator is not required to be blind or non-trivial.**
- **Attacked claim:** “a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\)” and Part 0: “blind variation and survival on a history of predictions.”
- **Why it fails:** “Blind” is never formalized. If \(\mu\) can be any operator, a designer can set \(\mu\) to produce the constructed transport deterministically or with high probability. Then selection is construction in disguise. The distinction depends on a property of \(\mu\) that is not defined.
- **Severity:** Fatal for the provenance distinction.
- **Fix:** Formalize blindness, e.g., \(\mu\)’s output is conditionally independent of the target transport given the population, or \(\mu\) cannot access a representation of the target. Specify constraints on \(\mu\)’s physical implementation.

**D8. “Construction witness” is not well-defined.**
- **Attacked claim:** “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting representation.” And Build: “contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers.”
- **Why it fails:** “Nontrivial binding construction” and “content-preserving transfer” are undefined. Without definitions, Build is not a predicate with a determinate extension. Constructed provenance is therefore uncheckable.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Define binding constructions formally, e.g., an operation that introduces a new component dependency not present in the incoming carriers and not derivable by transport composition. Define content-preserving transfer, e.g., an isomorphism of organizations. Provide criteria for “nontrivial.”

**D9. \(\operatorname{Con}\) depends on Build, Build depends on \(\operatorname{Rep}\), \(\operatorname{Rep}\) depends on \(\operatorname{Con}\).**
- **Attacked claim:** “**Constructed.** There is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target.” And Build: “prepares a represented organization.”
- **Why it fails:** \(\operatorname{Con}\) uses “represented target”; Build uses “represented organization”; both use \(\operatorname{Rep}\) (R); \(\operatorname{Rep}\) uses \(\operatorname{Con}\). This is a second direct definitional cycle, in addition to D1.
- **Severity:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Same as D1: define construction witness without using representation, or stratify representation into base and derived. If construction witnesses can only use previously represented components, specify the well-founded order.

**D10. Histories are not supplied by the physical module.**
- **Attacked claim:** “determined by its history in the physical module” and Part XII: “A selected provenance \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H)\) is a claim about a physical history.”
- **Why it fails:** Part XIV says the primitives are the physical module \(\Theta\) (including \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\)) and the normative relation \(\mathcal N\). It does not include populations of transports, variation operators, survival events, or histories. The physical module in Part XII defines tasks, retained realization, capability, etc., but not “population of transports,” “variation operator,” or “survival condition.” So the provenance distinction relies on data not supplied by the primitive physical module.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Extend the physical module to supply populations, variation operators, survival events, and histories, or derive them from existing physical primitives. State exactly which physical predicates correspond to \(\mathcal T\), \(\mu\), \(H\), and “survival condition.”

**D11. Prose “no represented target” contradicts the formal definition of \(\operatorname{Sel}\).**
- **Attacked claim:** “a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.”
- **Why it fails:** The formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) definition forbids only “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.” It does not forbid a member representing the target organization. So a selected history could have the target represented. The prose claim is stronger than the definition and contradicts it.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Either add “no member represents the target organization” to \(\operatorname{Sel}\), or weaken the prose and explain why a selected history with a represented target is still not construction.

**D12. The survival condition is not tied to physical realization.**
- **Attacked claim:** “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\)” and Part XII: “a survival condition enacted by the environment.”
- **Why it fails:** Fidelity is a semantic condition (F1, F2) involving abstract organizations. An environment cannot literally enforce a relation between abstract organizations; it can only kill or preserve physical carriers. The document does not explain how physical survival enacts fidelity.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Specify the physical correlates of fidelity failures, e.g., a carrier’s continued existence depends on its prediction matching a target port under the edit. Define the map from physical survival to the semantic survival condition.

**D13. Account ignores provenance; a declared transport can satisfy (E).**
- **Attacked claim:** “Any assessment may use any of the three. A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) for the contract in question, with the witness.” And Part 0: “Declaration is permitted only as a modelling convenience, and a claim about creativity cannot rest on it.”
- **Why it fails:** Account (E) has no provenance condition. So an account can satisfy (E) with a declared transport. The document says a claim about creativity cannot rest on declaration, but (EK) uses \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\) and \(\operatorname{Deploy}\), which uses representation (R) requiring non-declared. However, the contract’s provenance \(\rho_p\) is separate. If a creative episode’s account uses a declared transport but a constructed contract, does it satisfy (EK)? The document does not clearly forbid it.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Make provenance conditions explicit in (EK) for all transports used in the account, not just the contract. State that any transport in the account must be selected or constructed.

**D14. Representation is both input and output of the provenance distinction.**
- **Attacked claim:** “Representation is derived” and “Neither provenance is reducible to the other: a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.”
- **Why it fails:** The semantics claims to derive representation from fidelity and provenance. But it then distinguishes provenance by presence or absence of representation. So representation is both output and input. This is the central circularity.
- **Severity:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Define selected and constructed in non-representational terms, then derive representation. For example, selected = variation and survival without any internal state that tracks the target as a model; constructed = an episode that manipulates internal states causally coupled to the target in specified ways, without calling it representation until the derived predicate applies.

## Findings on (E): contract-as-content and question-finding

**E1. Contract-as-content trivializes question-finding.**
- **Attacked claim:** “The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a contract. When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question.” And Derivation 5: “A contract \(C\) is an organization … Give it ports … It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O). Deploy, Build, and New apply.”
- **Why it fails:** Derivation 5 says any contract can be given ports/components and admitted edits. So any change to \(C\) or \(\mathcal Q\) is a new organization. Build only requires “nontrivial binding construction” and “not a composition of content-preserving transfers,” neither defined. Newness only requires not \(\equiv_\ell\) to prior repertoire, and \(\equiv_\ell\) is undefined. So any re-parameterisation — change a threshold, add an edit, alter \(\mathcal Q\) — that is not literally identical to a prior content can be counted as New and, with a modeller’s act of “giving ports,” as Built. Thus every re-parameterisation becomes a “found question.”
- **Severity:** Fatal for the claim that question-finding is non-trivial.
- **Fix:** Define a strong equivalence \(\equiv_\ell\) for contracts. Require that the constructed contract changes the answer profile under some admitted edit in a way not achievable by any content-preserving transfer of a prior contract. Specify that Build for a contract must include a non-trivial binding construction that introduces a new dependency structure, not just a re-parameterisation. Require the episode to contain criticism of the old contract with a bearing witness.

**E2. Formal inconsistency: contract includes \(\mathcal Q\) in Derivation 5 but not in Part III.**
- **Attacked claim:** Part III: “The **contract** \(C\subseteq A\times B\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs…” and “A question is \(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\).” Versus Derivation 5: “A contract \(C\) is a subset of \(A\times B\) together with \(\mathcal Q\).”
- **Why it fails:** The definitions cannot both be right. If contract includes \(\mathcal Q\), then Part III’s \(p\) has \(\mathcal Q\) both inside \(C\) and as a separate component. If contract does not include \(\mathcal Q\), Derivation 5’s proof is false. The claim “question-finding is representable via contract-as-content” is therefore ill-defined.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Choose one definition. If contract includes \(\mathcal Q\), redefine \(p\) as \((D,\ \text{contract},\ b_0,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\). If not, Derivation 5 must construct a new organization that includes \(\mathcal Q\) as a separate component and show how \(\mathcal Q\) can be constructed.

**E3. Question-finding that changes the target \(D\) or query \(\mathcal Q\) is not captured.**
- **Attacked claim:** “A question — its target, its scope of admitted changes, what it asks — can be found as well as answered” and “When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question.”
- **Why it fails:** The formal provenance \(\rho_p\) is only “the provenance of the contract.” The target \(D\) and query \(\mathcal Q\) have no provenance. Derivation 5 treats only \(C\) (and inconsistently \(\mathcal Q\)) as content. If finding the right question involves discovering that the target was wrong, or that the query type was wrong — e.g., switching from production to identification — the semantics has no construction witness for \(D\) or \(\mathcal Q\). The claim in Part 0 that “a question — its target, its scope, what it asks — can be found” is not supported.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Define a question content that includes \(D\), \(C\), \(b_0\), \(\mathcal Q\), \(O_p\), and give each component provenance, or define construction of a whole question tuple. Allow construction to change \(D\) and \(\mathcal Q\), with witnesses.

**E4. Build is defined for “explanatory use,” but a contract is used for asking, not explaining.**
- **Attacked claim:** Build: “prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of \(c\)” versus “When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question.”
- **Why it fails:** A contract is not an explanation; it is a question. Build is defined for contents used in explanation. If \(c\) is a contract, its use is to ask a question, not to explain. The document does not define a separate construction predicate for questions. Derivation 5 simply says “Deploy, Build, and New apply” without showing that a contract can be an object of explanatory use.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Generalize Build to “use” (explanatory, interrogative, etc.) or define a separate predicate for question construction, e.g., \(\operatorname{FindQuestion}(s,p,h,e)\), with its own witness conditions.

**E5. Newness is not defined for contracts because \(\equiv_\ell\) is undefined.**
- **Attacked claim:** \(\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c.\)
- **Why it fails:** For \(c\) a contract, \(\equiv_\ell\) is supposed to compare contracts to prior deployable contents. But \(\equiv_\ell\) is never defined. Without it, there is no fact of the matter whether a new contract is genuinely new or a re-parameterisation. This is especially damaging for (E): the trivialization objection cannot be answered without a definition.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Define \(\equiv_\ell\) for all contents, including contracts, e.g., as isomorphism of organizations or equivalence of answer profiles under all admitted edits. Then state that re-parameterisations that preserve the equivalence class are not New.

**E6. Derivation 5’s construction of an organization from a contract is arbitrary and unconstrained.**
- **Attacked claim:** “Give it ports (the edits and their boundaries), components (the closure conditions), and admitted edits (add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)). It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O).”
- **Why it fails:** No criteria are given for what the ports, value domains, components, boundary conditions, and interpretations \(L\) are. Any set can be given ports and components. This is not a derivation; it is a stipulation that makes every contract an organization. It does not show that the resulting organization has the structure required by Build — a nontrivial binding construction.
- **Severity:** Fatal for Derivation 5.
- **Fix:** Provide a canonical construction of an organization from a contract, with explicit \(V\), \(X_v\), \(J\), \(B\), \(A\), \(L\), and prove it satisfies (O). Then show which bindings are nontrivial and why they are not content-preserving transfers.

**E7. The distinction between “finding a question” and “changing the contract” is not analyzed.**
- **Attacked claim:** “A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) for the contract in question, with the witness.”
- **Why it fails:** The document never says what it is for an episode to “find” a question as opposed to merely modify a contract. If the episode’s construction witness prepares a new contract, that is sufficient for \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\). But a modeller can prepare a new contract by arbitrary stipulation. The semantics does not require the episode to be an inquiry that searched for and discovered the question. It only requires a construction witness. This fails to capture the epistemic achievement of question-finding.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Require that the construction witness includes a problem situation, a criticism of the prior contract, a conjectural new contract, and a response that selects it. Require that the new contract is not merely stipulated but is arrived at through an episode with the structure of Part X (recognized difficulty, target, objection, response).

**E8. Trivialization via altering \(\mathcal Q\): any change of query counts as a new question.**
- **Attacked claim:** Derivation 5: “alter \(\mathcal Q\)” as an admitted edit.
- **Why it fails:** If altering \(\mathcal Q\) is an admitted edit on the contract-as-organization, then any change to the query operation is a new contract. But many query changes are trivial — changing codomain representation, relabelling outputs, reordering pairs. The semantics provides no condition to distinguish a substantively new question from a notational variant. Combined with undefined \(\equiv_\ell\), this makes question-finding trivial.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Restrict admitted edits to those that change the answer profile in a non-content-preserving way. Define equivalence of queries up to content-preserving recoding. Require the new question to be non-equivalent to all prior questions.

**E9. The “originative act is the finding of a question” reduces question-finding to content construction, ignoring the normative aspect of a good question.**
- **Attacked claim:** “When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question.”
- **Why it fails:** Finding a question is not just constructing any new contract; it is finding a question worth asking, or one that opens a productive line of inquiry. The semantics explicitly disclaims objective aesthetics and merit (Part 0). But then it has no resources to distinguish a genuinely creative question-finding from a random new contract. The claim that this captures question-finding is overclaimed.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Either add a normative relation for question value, as done for aesthetics in Part XI, and require it for “finding” in the creative sense, or weaken the claim to “constructing a question” rather than “finding the right question.”

**E10. The treatment of defect-driven question-finding is missing.**
- **Attacked claim:** Part III: “A question may fail to pick out its alleged target, assume an incompatible baseline, or combine incompatible requirements. Its formulation is still an event. Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract.”
- **Why it fails:** This says exposing a defect is another question, but it does not say how the new question is constructed. The construction of the new question is exactly the hard case. The semantics merely asserts that it is another question with its own contract. It provides no account of how the defect leads to the new contract, nor how the new contract is a construction rather than a selection or declaration.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Provide a derivation for defect-driven question construction, e.g., the criticism of the old question is a represented objection; the response builds a new contract whose components are bound to the defect. Show this satisfies Build and Origin.

**E11. Contract provenance inherits all the transport-provenance problems.**
- **Attacked claim:** “\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\), with the witness required by Part IV.” And “A **selected** contract is the surviving member of a population under a variation-and-survival history.”
- **Why it fails:** If contracts are contents, then their provenance is subject to the same circularity and vacuity as transports. The definition of a selected contract inherits D1–D7. The construction witness for a contract inherits E1–E6. So the contract provenance distinction is not well-defined.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Fix the transport provenance first, then apply the same definitions to contracts with explicit criteria. Do not define contract provenance by pointing to Part IV until Part IV is well-founded.

**E12. \(\equiv_\ell\) is undefined for contracts, making New either trivial or arbitrary.**
- **Attacked claim:** \(\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c.\)
- **Why it fails:** For \(c\) a contract, if the agent has previously used a contract that is a notational variant, and \(\equiv_\ell\) is not defined to detect it, the new contract is counted as New. If \(\equiv_\ell\) is defined coarsely, then many genuinely different questions may be counted as not new. Without a definition, the condition is either trivial or arbitrary.
- **Severity:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Define \(\equiv_\ell\) for contracts as equivalence of the answer profile

## (D) Genesis, provenance, and reduction

**D1. Circularity between `Sel`/`Con` and `Rep`.**
Quote: “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition. Write \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H)\).” And: “There is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target.” And:  
\[
\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists t\,[\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t:\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\to c)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]. \tag{R}
\]
Claim attacked: provenance is defined before representation and then representation is defined by provenance.  
Why it fails: `Sel` uses “represents,” and `Con` uses “represented target.” But `Rep` is defined by `Sel` or `Con`. There is no base case or stratification. The definition is circular unless “represents” in Part IV is an undefined informal term, in which case (R) is not the definition it claims to be.  
Severity: fatal.  
Fix: either define `Sel`/`Con` using a non-semantic targeting/access relation independent of `Rep`, or give a stratified construction with a base level at which representation is not defined by provenance.

**D2. The clause “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition” is ill-typed and not checkable.**
Quote: “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered” and “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.”  
Claim attacked: the negative condition distinguishing selected provenance is checkable.  
Why it fails: \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs. Its members are pairs, not occurrences or carriers. They cannot represent anything under (R). Also \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set, and a survival condition is a predicate; (R) represents contents, i.e. organizations with contracts. None of \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition is supplied with a contract or organization. The phrase also collides with Part IX, where “a history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence.”  
Severity: fatal for the formal definition of selected provenance.  
Fix: define a separate, typed targeting relation and say exactly what objects can be targets; if \(H\) is a set of pairs, do not say its members represent. Give \(t\), \(H\), and survival conditions explicit encodings and contracts if (R) is to apply.

**D3. The three provenances are not mutually exclusive; selected provenance is trivializable.**
Quote: “A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has exactly one of three provenances, determined by its history in the physical module.”  
Claim attacked: exactly one provenance holds.  
Why it fails: take any transport \(t\), set \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\), let \(\mu\) be the identity, let \(H=\varnothing\), and let the survival condition be vacuous fidelity on \(H\). Then \(t\) is selected. If \(t\) is also constructed, it satisfies both `Sel` and `Con`, contradicting “exactly one.” With \(H=\varnothing\), every transport is selected and no transport is merely declared, contradicting the claimed role of declaration.  
Severity: fatal.  
Fix: require non-vacuous selection histories, non-trivial variation, a survival condition that actually eliminates candidates, and explicit exclusivity clauses excluding constructed episodes; or define provenance as the most specific history type rather than as independent predicates.

**D4. The prose distinction “selected has no represented target” is not encoded in the definition of `Sel`.**
Quote: “Neither provenance is reducible to the other: a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both.”  
Claim attacked: the formal definition of selected provenance excludes represented targets and criticism.  
Why it fails: `Sel` only says “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.” It never says no member represents the target organization, nor that the history contains no criticism. A transport could have a represented target and still satisfy that negative clause. The claimed reducibility barrier is therefore not a theorem from the definitions.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: add explicit clauses to `Sel`: no represented target, no criticism occurrence, no construction episode, with type-correct definitions.

**D5. Derivation 3 changes the organization, not the transport, and ignores population and variation.**
Quote: “Alter \(L_{j}(a,b)\) for one \((a,b)\notin H\) to any other admitted relation; the result survives on \(H\) and differs at \((a,b)\).”  
Claim attacked: selected transports are underdetermined on unseen changes.  
Why it fails: \(L_j(a,b)\) is a component relation of the organization \(E\), not a component of the transport \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\). Altering \(L_j\) changes the target organization, so the “result” is not another transport to the same \(E\). The proof also ignores the population \(\mathcal T\) and variation operator \(\mu\): an altered relation need not be a member of \(\mathcal T\) or reachable by \(\mu\). The statement “The component relations \(L_j(a,b)\) are supplied independently for each \((a,b)\)” is an extra assumption not in (O).  
Severity: fatal for Derivation 3 as stated.  
Fix: state underdetermination of the target organization by the selection history, or quantify explicitly over a family of organizations and over \(\mathcal T,\mu\). Require closure of \(\mathcal T\) under the alteration.

**D6. The primitive layer already contains the persistence that selection is said to produce.**
Quote: Part 0: “Selection appears once, at the bottom, to produce the primitive layer of things-with-persistence that explanation operates on.” Part IV: “The primitive layer \(P\) is an organization whose ports are persistent things with boundaries and identity, whose components are their continuity relations…”  
Claim attacked: selection produces the primitive layer of persistent things.  
Why it fails: \(P\) is defined as already having persistent things with boundaries and identity. Selection of transports at the primitive layer therefore operates on objecthood; it does not produce it. The genesis story is inconsistent with the definition of \(P\).  
Severity: serious, possibly fatal for the “selection at the bottom” claim.  
Fix: either define the primitive layer without persistent object ports, or drop the claim that selection produces the primitive layer.

**D7. The constructed persistence in the Part X episode is already present in the primitive layer.**

## D. The three-way provenance distinction

**D1. Circularity of `Sel`/`Con` through `Rep`.**
- **Attacked claim:** `Sel` and `Con` are well-defined by physical history.
- **Why it fails:** `Sel` says: “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.” `Con` says: “in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target.” But representation is defined by (R): \(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists t[\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t:\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\to c)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\). Thus `Sel`/`Con` use representation, and representation uses `Sel`/`Con`. The definition is viciously circular.
- **Seriousness:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Define a non-semantic notion of representation for provenance, or make provenance primitive, or require that the representations used in `Sel`/`Con` be well-founded prior representations with independent provenance.

**D2. The stated dependence order is false.**
- **Attacked claim:** Part XIV says: “(R) depends on (F1)–(F2) and physical provenance.”
- **Why it fails:** Part IV defines physical provenance using “represents” and “represented target,” which are instances of (R). So provenance depends on (R), and (R) depends on provenance. The dependence order is not acyclic.
- **Seriousness:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Correct the dependence order, or break the cycle as in D1.

**D3. “History” is used in two incompatible senses.**
- **Attacked claim:** `Sel`: “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered… No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.”
- **Why it fails:** Part IX defines a history as “a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence.” Here `H` is a set of edit–boundary pairs. A pair is not an occurrence with an organization, so it cannot represent anything. The phrase “member of the history” is therefore ill-typed.
- **Seriousness:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Say explicitly that the selection history is a set of occurrences with physical organizations, and distinguish it from the set `H` of edit–boundary pairs.

**D4. `Sel` and `Con` are not indexed to a grain.**
- **Attacked claim:** `Sel` and `Con` are predicates on transports.
- **Why it fails:** (R) is \(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\). Representation depends on grain \(\ell\). But `Sel`/`Con` have no \(\ell\). At a coarse grain many things represent many things; at a fine grain few do. So “represents \(t\)” in `Sel` is undefined until a grain is fixed.
- **Seriousness:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Index `Sel`/`Con`/`Dec` to \(\ell\), or specify a privileged grain for provenance.

**D5. `Sel` is trivially satisfiable by degenerate selection.**
- **Attacked claim:** `Sel`: “There is a population \(\mathcal T\) of candidate transports, a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\), a finite history \(H\subseteq C\)… and a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\).”
- **Why it fails:** There is no requirement that \(|\mathcal T|>1\), that \(\mu\) be non-identity, that \(H\neq\varnothing\), or that the survival condition eliminate anything. Taking \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\), \(\mu=\mathrm{id}\), \(H=\varnothing\), and a vacuous survival condition makes every transport selected. Then `Dec` (“Neither of the above”) becomes impossible.
- **Seriousness:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Require a nontrivial population, a non-identity variation operator, a nonempty selection history, and a survival condition that actually eliminates alternatives.

**D6. `Sel` does not exclude construction.**
- **Attacked claim:** `Sel` is distinguished from `Con` by “No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition.”
- **Why it fails:** `Con` requires that “\(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target.” `Sel` forbids representing \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition, but not the target organization. A construction episode that represents the target organization but does not represent \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition can satisfy `Sel`. The formal definition also does not forbid criticism in the selection history.
- **Seriousness:** Fatal.
- **Fix:** Forbid any represented target and any criticism of \(t\) in `Sel`, and formalize the variation operator as non-semantic.

**D7. “Exactly one” provenance is not enforced.**
- **Attacked claim:** “A transport \(t\) … has exactly one of three provenances.”
- **Why it fails:** The text also says: “Construction may operate on selected material. Selection may continue to operate beneath construction.” No disjointness or composition rule is given. A transport that survived selection and was also prepared by an episode satisfies both `Sel` and `Con`. `Dec` is then not well-defined.
- **Seriousness:** Serious.
- **Fix:** Define provenance for composite transports, e.g. constructed if any non-content-preserving binding is constructed, with explicit priority and disjointness conditions.

**D8. `Declared` is a residual category.**
- **Attacked claim:** `Declared`: “Neither of the above. The transport is entered into the model by its author.”
- **Why it fails:** If `Sel` and `Con` are vague, overlapping, or trivially satisfiable, `Dec` is not well-defined. Also, a written declared transport is prepared by an author in a physical episode; that episode may satisfy `Build`, making the transport constructed. So `Dec` may be a special case of `Con`.
- **Seriousness:** Serious.

**D9. The negative condition is not checkable in principle.**
- **Attacked claim:** Grievance 9 says selection “is fallible but checkable in principle.”
- **Why it fails:** Checking `Sel` requires checking that no member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition. That is a universal negative over all members and all possible transports, using (R), which itself depends on `Sel`/`Con`. No decision procedure or certificate for the absence is supplied.

- **Why it fails:** The formal `Sel` clause only forbids representing \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition. It does not forbid a represented target or criticism. The distinction is doing work in prose that the formal definitions do not support.
- **Seriousness:** Serious.

- **Seriousness:** Serious.

**D12. `Con` is circular through “represented target.”**
- **Attacked claim:** `Con`: “in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target.”
- **Why it fails:** To represent the target, (R) requires a transport with selected or constructed provenance. If that transport is the one being constructed, the definition is circular. If it is a prior transport, the text never says so or supplies a well-foundedness condition.

- **Seriousness:** Fatal.

Requiring \(H\subseteq C\) makes provenance depend on the current contract, so changing \(C\) can change whether the same physical transport counts as selected. Provenance is supposed to be determined by physical history, not by present scope.

**D16. `H` and “survival condition” are not contents.**

- **Why it fails:** `H` is a set of edit–boundary pairs; a survival condition is a predicate. (R) represents contents, which are organizations plus commitments. The text never says how \(H\) or a predicate becomes a content. The condition is therefore ill-formed.

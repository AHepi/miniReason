## Findings

**1. The selection witness is gerrymanderable, because the population, history, and survival condition are not required to be fixed in advance.**
> "a finite history $H\subseteq C_t$ of edit–boundary pairs … non-empty; a survival condition that holds of a member exactly when it is faithful on $H$ … which eliminated at least one member that differed from $t$ at a pair of $H$ on which that member failed and $t$ did not."
$H$, $\mathcal T$, and the survival condition are existentially quantified and chosen by the assessor. Nothing requires them to be fixed before assessment — in contrast to Part XII's $\operatorname{CanAdv}$, which insists $J_p$ be "specified by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed." So for any transport whose population contains $\ge 2$ variants differing in fidelity on any encountered pair, one may select $H$ *post hoc* as exactly the discriminating pairs, and the equivalence "survival $\iff$ faithful on $H$" then holds by construction. **Serious.** Fix: require $\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h$ and the survival condition to be supplied by the module (or fixed by a pre-assessment record), and say so in Part IV, not only in Part XII.

**2. The "difference-making condition" is nearly automatic given the other conjuncts, and Part XII's inventory of the witness does not match Part IV's definition.**
> "That last clause is the difference-making condition, stated in the module's terms: the filtering discriminated on fidelity between $t$ and an eliminated rival."
Given $|\mathcal T|\ge2$, non-empty $H$, and survival $\iff$ fidelity on $H$, every eliminated member fails somewhere in $H$; the clause adds only that it not be a *duplicate* of $t$ at its failing pair. So Grievance 9's claim — "Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation" — overstates what the clause buys. Worse, Part XII lists the witness as "population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, **causal responsibility**," a clause that appears nowhere in Part IV, while asserting "This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one." **Serious.** Fix: add an explicit causal-responsibility clause to Part IV (the member was eliminated *because of* its failure at that pair, with the stated counterfactual), or delete it from Part XII's list.

**3. The exactness "necessary and sufficient for survival" makes $\operatorname{Sel}$ unsatisfiable by realistic selection.**
> "a survival condition that holds of a member exactly when it is faithful on $H$ (fidelity on $H$ is necessary and sufficient for survival)"
No actual population satisfies *exact* necessary-and-sufficient equivalence between survival and fidelity-on-$H$: drift, sampling, hitchhiking, and noise make survival at best a probabilistic function of fidelity. So either $\operatorname{Sel}$ is empty in the actual world (making (R) for selected transports vacuous), or the equivalence is a theorist's idealization — in which case Finding 1's gerrymander is available. The claim that the witness is "fallible but checkable in principle" is not credible for exact equivalence. **Serious.** Fix: weaken to a stated noise model (declared, and added to Part XIV), and state what the witness costs under it.

**4. The aggregate-summary exemption is logically inert, and under its intended reading it contradicts the blindness clause.**
> "an *aggregate* summary — a function of the multiset of failures invariant under permuting which record failed, such as how many and how badly — is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted."
Blindness forbids "an itemised representation of a defect … on any active route from a failure to an application of $\mu$." If the underlying records are *off*-route, blindness already permits the summary, so the exemption is redundant. If the records are *on*-route (which they must be for any occurrence to compute a summary from them), blindness fails and the exemption cannot license them, since it licenses only the summary. The intended reading — "records may be present as long as only the aggregate drives variation" — is not what the text says. So the exemption does no work. **Serious.** Fix: replace route-membership with an input-dependence condition: the variation operator's inputs must not determine, for any failing pair, both that pair's identity and the counterfactual value.

**5. The invariance group in the exemption is unspecified, and "how badly" needs a metric that appears nowhere.**
> "invariant under permuting which record failed, such as how many and how badly"
A multiset is by definition permutation-invariant, so "a function of the multiset invariant under permuting …" is satisfied by *any* function of a multiset of pair-*tagged* records — including a vector of per-pair failure counters, and including the gradient of a batch loss (a sum of per-record contributions). If the intended group is permutations of the failing *pairs' identities*, that must be said; it is not. Separately, "how badly" presupposes a discrepancy magnitude/metric that is not among Part XIV's declared parameters, which claims to list them all. **Serious.** Fix: state the group (permutations of failing-pair identities), declare the metric space, add it to Part XIV.

**6. Route granularity is undeclared, so "lies on an active route" cannot separate itemised from aggregate.**
An "active route" is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences; nothing fixes its granularity. In supervised learning the individual failure records are occurrences read en route to the weight update, and the gradient is computed from them. Whether the process counts as blind (only the aggregate on the route) or sighted (the records on the route) depends on where the route is cut. Route granularity is not in Part XIV's declared-parameter list. **Serious.** Fix: declare route granularity, or define blindness functionally per Finding 4.

**7. Blindness and the defect-record clause run through $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$, which is many-to-one, so provenance is grain-relative and the two provenances are inter-convertible by grain choice.**
Part IV states "Every definition that needs 'the organization $o$ instantiates' quantifies over $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,\cdot)$," and $\operatorname{Inst}$ is "a relation, not a function." Blindness is a negative existential over occurrences *and* over organizations they instantiate; Build's record clause is a positive one. One occurrence can instantiate an itemised-record organization at a fine grain and only an aggregate-summary organization at a coarser grain. Hence the same physical history is both blind and sighted, and provenance — claimed to be "determined by its history in the physical module" — is relative to a declared grain. This answers "can one process satisfy both": yes, at different grains. It also answers "rewriting": construction can be rewritten as selection by coarsening, selection as construction by refining, whenever the records exist as occurrences. **Serious.** Fix: index both clauses to a declared grain and accept grain-relativity of provenance, or replace $\operatorname{Inst}$-based record-detection with the functional condition of Finding 4.

**8. "What would have held" is ambiguous between a modal and a definable reading.**
> "a record that pairs the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held" (Part IV); "a record pairing a state at which some prior transport of $s$ failed fidelity with what would have held" (Part X).
On the modal reading (the value a *faithful* transport would hold), the clause presupposes representational/normative content, so provenance witnesses would depend on (R), violating the stratification Derivation 6 proves ("provenance depends on $\Theta$ and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on $\operatorname{Inst}$ and $\equiv_\ell$ but not on (R)"). On a purely structural reading (an occurrence whose ports carry a value of the right domain), it is gerrymanderable: any occurrence carrying a well-typed value counts. **Serious.** Fix: define "what would have held" as the target's own $\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)$ (or the target's component relation at $(a,b)$), defined in Part III without (R), and require the record's value to be produced by the module's interaction with the target, not stipulated.

**9. The claimed mutual exclusivity of the provenances does not follow from the definitions.**
> "A process that is blind is not constructing; a process with the defect record on its route is not selecting, whatever else it does."
$\operatorname{Sel}$ forbids the itemised record only on "any active route from a failure to an application of $\mu$"; $\operatorname{Build}$ requires it only on "an active route to the binding," and $\operatorname{Build}$ contains no $\mu$. These are different route conditions. So a history in which the record lies on a route to a binding that is *not* a route from a failure to a $\mu$-application satisfies both witnesses. The inference to "is not selecting, whatever else it does" requires the unstated premise that every route to a binding is a route from a failure to variation. Also, the pair is not "the only separator" anyway: $\operatorname{Sel}$ additionally needs population/variation/elimination, $\operatorname{Build}$ additionally needs ownership, a nontrivial required binding, and non-composition. **Serious.** Fix: state the premise, or make blindness global over $\Sigma_h$ — and note that the global option excludes any selection process whose history contains any fine-grained failure memory.

**10. The collapse attack is blocked only by that same unstated premise.**
Part XV's attack asks for a rewriting of every construction witness as a selection witness. Exhibit: given a construction witness, take $\mathcal T=\{$pre-binding (failed) transport, post-binding transport$\}$, $\mu=$ the binding construction, $H=\{$the pair at which the prior transport failed$\}$, survival $=$ fidelity on $H$. Then $|\mathcal T|\ge2$, $\mu$ was applied and produced a member differing from its parent at a pair of $H$, $H$ is non-empty, the failed member is eliminated and differs from the survivor at its failing pair, and non-triviality plausibly holds. The rewriting fails *only* because the itemised record is on the route to the binding, which is a route to $\mu$'s application *if* bindings are produced by variation. So the defense against the collapse and the exclusivity claim of Finding 9 stand or fall together. **Serious.** Fix: state that in a selection witness the binding is produced by $\mu$, that $\operatorname{Build}$'s binding is produced by no member of $\Sigma_h$, and then prove exclusivity.

**11. The earliest-witness rule is type-ambiguous, partial, and can misassign.**
> "the provenance of the **earliest** witness in $\prec_h$ whose events produced the occurrence that carries $t$ … Later histories are maintenance and do not change provenance."
$\prec_h$ is an "acyclic precedence" on occurrences/events; it does not order *witnesses* (sets of events). "Earliest witness" is undefined (earliest event? earliest completion?). Acyclic $\ne$ total, so two witnesses can be incomparable and provenance undefined. And a transport that is selected and later re-bound by construction is labelled "selected," the re-binding dismissed as "maintenance" — but a re-binding that breaks and re-satisfies (F1) is a new production, and the rule supplies no criterion separating maintenance from production. **Serious.** Fix: define the witness order (e.g., by completion event), handle incomparability explicitly, and add a supersession clause for later witnesses that change the binding.

**12. The non-triviality conjunct of $\operatorname{Sel}$ is ill-typed, and its reading decides whether the rewriting in Finding 10 is blocked.**
> "**Non-triviality:** no event of $\Sigma_h$ instantiates an organization held to $t$, to $H$, or to the survival condition by a faithful transport."
"Held to" is defined by (R) for *contents* — organizations with contracts. $t$ is a transport, $H$ is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition is a predicate; none is a content. Part XIV claims the base class consists of "interpretations supplying these data with correct typing." Worse, the rewriting in Finding 10 is blocked or admitted depending on how "held to $H$" is read: if a defect record specifying which pair failed counts as partially representing $H$, non-triviality blocks the rewriting and blindness does not — so the ill-typing is load-bearing. **Serious.** Fix: supply contents for $t$'s content, for $H$ (via the Derivation-5 question-organization), and for the survival condition, or restate the clause without "held to."

**13. $\operatorname{Build}$'s nontriviality and "required" clauses are near-vacuous for a system with an empty prior repertoire, so $(G)$ is satisfiable by one-shot error correction.**
> "a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by $\equiv_\ell$; it is **required for the transport** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (F2)."
With $R_{<e}=\varnothing$ the first disjunct is vacuous, so nontriviality reduces to the recoding disjunct alone. And deleting the anchor of *any* active component leaves (F1) unevaluable, so "required" holds for essentially every binding. Hence a system that fails once, records the correction, and binds one component satisfies $\operatorname{Build}$; with $\operatorname{New}$ (empty repertoire) and $\operatorname{Attempt}$, it satisfies $(G)$. The document makes exactly this concession for questions — "a random new contract that is new to the repertoire and built satisfies (G) as written" — but not for contents, where the same triviality arises. **Serious.** Fix: add a difficulty/merit condition for contents parallel to the one marked empty for questions, or make the concession explicit for both.

**14. "Not a composition of content-preserving transfers" leaves single relay satisfiable.**
> "…and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers," glossed as "Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not."
If composition requires two or more factors, a single content-preserving transfer satisfies $\operatorname{Build}$, so a single relay counts as construction, contradicting the gloss. **Minor.** Fix: say "contains no nontrivial content-preserving transfer factor," or state that composition includes the unary case.

**15. $\operatorname{Build}$'s record-on-the-route-to-the-binding is instructivist, in tension with the conjecture-first epistemology the document announces.**
> Part I: "**Conjecture, criticism, action.** An idea may be entertained without justification. A criticism has a target and is itself conjectural."
> Part X: $\operatorname{Build}$ "contains an **itemised defect representation** … on an active route to the binding."
For the record to be on the route *to* the binding, defect detection must be causally upstream of, and inform, the variation that produces the binding — i.e., instructed variation. That is the structure blindness forbids for selection. The Popperian alternative (conjecture the binding first, let the record select among candidate bindings) places the record on a route *from* failure *to* variation, which fails blindness and makes the process selection. So: either construction is defect-instructed and the critical-rationalist framing applies only to the primitive layer, or construction is conjecture-first and $\operatorname{Build}$'s clause fails. **Serious.** Fix: pick one, and state which layer the conjecture-first picture governs; or relocate the record to the route to the binding's *retention/deployment* and accept that the production step is then blind (which reintroduces Finding 9's problem).

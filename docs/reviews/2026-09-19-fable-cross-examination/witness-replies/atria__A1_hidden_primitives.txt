## The ten named candidates

### 1. 'active commitments' — undeclared index or smuggled predicate
**Claim attacked:** "an identified set Γ of active commitments in E" (Part V); "For W⊆Γ, let E|W retain the commitments in W with the named background fixed" (Part VI); Part XIV: "Grain ℓ, boundary β, continuity Ω, and the contract C are declared indices."

**Why it fails.** Γ is used in Non-circular dependence, in (S), (B), (D), and transitively in (G) and (EK). Neither "active" nor "identified" nor "named background" is defined anywhere. Part II defines components, footprints, and signatures; nothing there makes a component a *commitment*, let alone an *active* one. If Γ is a subset of components supplied by the modeller, it is a declared index and Part XIV's list must say so; if "active" is a fact about the component, it must be defined (e.g. via participation in an active route of Part IX). As written, "active commitment" is a primitive predicate with normative flavour sitting inside the definition of an account.

**Serious.** **Fix:** add Γ and the background to the declared indices with a provenance (declared/selected/constructed), or define `active(k) ⟺ k` occurs in some active route as defined in Part IX — and use one sense of "active" throughout (Part V and Part IX currently differ).

### 2. 'declared scope' — (E) contains a record condition it denies containing
**Claim attacked:** "every physically admitted edit excluded from C is excluded by a stated scope, not silently" (Non-vacuity) versus "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under the changes in C. None inspects a label" (Part V, end).

**Why it fails.** The first clause is not a condition on relations under changes; it is a condition on what has been *stated* in the record. So (E) is partly pragmatic, contra its own summary. Worse, nothing checks the *truth* of a stated scope: a modeller can state any scope, and the protection against protective narrowing collapses to "someone might criticise it later" — which is weaker than grievance 4's advertising ("it is caught by the requirement that the exclusion be stated").

**Serious.** **Fix:** either restate (E) as "five conditions on relations plus one record condition," or formalise "stated" as "represented in the system's history under (R)," making the clause checkable rather than aspirational.

### 3. 'nontrivial binding construction' — 'nontrivial' is undefined
**Claim attacked:** "contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers" (Build, Part X); "the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect" (Deploy).

**Why it fails.** If "nontrivial" is meant to be the non-composition clause, the sentence states the same condition twice; if not, it is undefined. "Nontrivial use respect" in Deploy is likewise undefined. Build is a conjunct of (G), so an undefined adjective sits inside the definition of creativity.

**Serious.** **Fix:** define nontriviality explicitly — e.g. the binding is not the image of any structure-preserving bijection of the kind used in Derivation 8 acting on prior contents — and state that this is the same "nontrivial" used in Deploy.

### 4. 'content-preserving recoding' and ≡ℓ — the core equivalence relation is never defined
**Claim attacked:** "sends content-preserving recodings to the same transition" (Reason use, Part IX); "New(s,c,h,e) ⟺ ¬∃d∈R_{<e}(s,h), d ≡ℓ c" (N).

**Why it fails.** Derivation 8 speaks of "structure-preserving bijections" but never equates them with content-preservation. And ≡ℓ, the relation on which *newness* — hence (G), hence (EK) — turns, is never defined at all. Part IV says a content is "an organization together with its contract-relative commitments," which is a gloss, not a definition of an equivalence. This is exactly the sort of residue Derivation 6 claims cannot exist.

**Fatal to the two-primitive claim.** **Fix:** define ≡ℓ, e.g. mutual faithful transport on the same contract (the natural reading of Derivation 2), and identify content-preserving recodings with the Derivation-8 bijections, explicitly.

### 5. 'grain' — an index doing undeclared semantic work
**Claim attacked:** "Grain ℓ, boundary β, continuity Ω, and the contract C are declared indices. Every claim is relative to them; none is a predicate that could be true or false" (Part XIV); also "at the declared grain" (Non-circular dependence) and "d ≡ℓ c" (N).

**Why it fails.** ℓ being *declared* means newness, representation, and non-circularity are all relative to a modeller's choice of grain. That is acceptable if acknowledged; Part XIV's dependence order does not record that (E) and (N) depend on ℓ, and nothing flags that "new" is new-*at-a-declared-grain*. A reader cannot tell whether grain is physically fixed by Θ or supplied by the modeller — the text implies both.

**Minor-to-serious.** **Fix:** add ℓ (and β, Ω) to the dependence order for (E), (R), (N); state whether Θ determines admissible grains or the modeller declares them; and say plainly that New is indexed to a declared grain.

### 6. Orgℓ(o) — used as a function, should be a relation
**Claim attacked:** "the organization that o instantiates under the physical module, at grain ℓ, admits a transport to c" (R).

**Why it fails.** The definite article presupposes uniqueness. A physical occurrence can instantiate many organizations at one grain (mechanical, thermodynamic, computational). If two, one faithful to c and one not, (R) is ill-posed: does o represent c? Nothing in Θ's description ("substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, … and Orgℓ") states uniqueness or supplies a selection rule.

**Serious.** **Fix:** make Orgℓ(o,ℓ) a relation and (R) existential over it, or add an explicit uniqueness axiom to the physical module.

### 7. Orgℓ(o) — a decomposition oracle dressed as physics
**Claim attacked:** "Physics says what organization a lump of matter instantiates"; "The one thing (R) takes from outside is Orgℓ(o): that is supplied by the physical module, not by the semantics."

**Why it fails.** Orgℓ must return, for an occurrence and grain, a full D: ports, value domains, component index, footprints, boundary set, admitted edits, and every L_j(a,b). No ordinary physical theory determines this; decomposing a lump into components is a modelling act. Housing it inside "the physical module" hides a modelling function inside the primitive and makes the "two primitives" claim artificially thin.

**Serious.** **Fix:** either argue that the task-based Θ supplies canonical decompositions (and show the construction), or reclassify the decomposition as a declared index and restate the primitive count as "two primitives plus a declared decomposition."

**Fatal to the no-'cause' boast; serious for (EK).** **Fix:** define ProducedBy in the physical module: Δ is a process occurrence whose ports include the attribute o and whose signature is a causal assignment under (K) on the contract; likewise ProducesVia and Result.

### 11. 'owned' — undefined, and it carries the substrate-independence commitment
**Claim attacked:** "an **owned** retained realization or an **owned**, physically admitted, finite construction of one" (Part XII); "an actual subhistory **owned** by s" (Build); "an **owned**, admitted continuation" (Scrutinizability); "an **owned** enabling continuation" (RC); "A theorist's description of a protocol is not the system's possession of it."

**Why it fails.** Ownership is exactly what separates the system's possession from a theorist's description — the load-bearing distinction of "substrate independence with physical obligations" — and it is never defined. It appears in (RC), which defines the recursive class, and in Build, a conjunct of (G).

**Serious.** **Fix:** define `owned(ξ, π)` as ξ lying in the constructor attribute C under the declared boundary β, with π's process occurrences inside β; note that this makes ownership index-relative (β), which is acceptable once declared.

## Additional smuggled or broken items

### 12. Rep ↔ Con is a cycle; the dependence order is not well-founded
**Claim attacked:** (R): "Repℓ(o,c) ⟺ ∃t[Faithful_C(t: Orgℓ(o)→c) ∧ (Sel(t) ∨ Con(t))]"; Con: "in which t, or the organization it targets, is available as a **represented target**"; Build: "prepares a **represented** organization for explanatory use of c."

**Why it fails.** Con requires the target to be represented; representation requires Sel or Con; Con ≈ Build; Build requires a represented organization. So Rep → Con → Rep, and Build → Rep → Con → Build. Part XIV asserts a well-founded dependence order ("Nothing depends on a predicate meaning 'really explains'…") and this pair refutes it. The two-layer example hides the problem only because the target P is represented via the *selected* t₀. For a genuinely novel content — the case (G) and (EK) exist to handle — no prior representing transport exists, and if the only faithful transport to c is the one being built, the definitions are viciously circular.

**Fatal to the dependence-order claim; serious for (G)/(EK).** **Fix:** stratify by episode order — the represented target must be available *before* the episode via Sel — or define Con as a pure Θ-fact (the episode's occurrences physically instantiate t's structure) with no appeal to Rep.

### 13. Derivation 3 silently assumes the population is relation-free off H
**Claim attacked:** "Alter L_j(a,b) for one (a,b)∉H to any other admitted relation; the result survives on H and differs at (a,b)."

**Why it fails.** The claim asserts t′ is "also surviving," i.e. t′ ∈ 𝒯; the construction only shows t′ survives on H. Populations of *realized* transports (Part XII) are parametric — weights, circuits, genomes — and a survivor can perfectly well be determined on C\H by its values on H. Part XV's own defeat condition ("A selection history H ⊊ C whose survivor is determined on C\H") is therefore not excluded by the proof as written. "Admitted relation" would have to mean "realizable by some member of 𝒯 matching t on H," which is the missing assumption.

**Serious** — the derivation grounds fallibility, surprise, and the answer to grievance 3. **Fix:** add the assumption "𝒯 is closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off H" and mark the theorem relative to it, or weaken the claim to underdetermination of the *selection data* rather than of the survivor.

### 14. Derivation 1 applies (K) outside its stated domain
**Claim attacked:** "By (K), sig_C(λ(k)) = {(a,b,proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b))}."

**Why it fails.** (K) is stated for *components* j of an organization; λ(k) is a *subnetwork*. The signature of a subnetwork is a new definition, not an instance of (K). (F1) already uses it implicitly.

**Minor.** **Fix:** extend (K) explicitly to subnetworks.

### 15. Derivation 2's "pairwise of one kind" needs a bijection it does not have
**Claim attacked:** "their components are pairwise of one kind on C and their answer profiles coincide."

**Why it fails.** Two faithful candidates may decompose D differently; without a bijection between their components, "pairwise" is unearned (different multiplicities of kind-equivalent components are possible). The consequence survives if weakened.

**Minor-to-serious.** **Fix:** restate as "each component of either is kind-equivalent, via its anchor, to a subnetwork of D, and the answer profiles coincide."

### 16. Derivation 4 conflates two contracts
**Claim attacked:** "a transport selected on a history H strictly smaller than the contract C **of changes the world admits**."

**Why it fails.** Violation is defined only for (a,b) ∈ the transport's contract. If the world admits a change outside the transport's contract entirely, the definition of violation does not apply, though surprise intuitively does. The index slip matters because the derivation is what makes surprise "derived, not assumed."

**Minor.** **Fix:** distinguish C_t from C_world, and define a separate condition (blindness) for changes outside C_t.

### 17. (P)'s exposure requirement is not in the formula
**Claim attacked:** "(P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside P must be exposed."

**Why it fails.** Nothing in the formula of (P) enforces exposure; silent uncompensated losses outside P are permitted. Given the document's emphasis on scope-honesty and record-keeping, the prose is stronger than the formalism.

**Minor-to-serious.** **Fix:** add an exposure conjunct (a record condition, per finding 2), or state plainly that (P) permits uncompensated losses outside P.

### 18. 'Enable' is undefined and sits inside the universal class
**Claim attacked:** (U1): "Enable(s,U_c,χ) ∧ Can(ξ₀,U_c;χ)"; (U2) similarly.

**Why it fails.** Enable is never defined and is clearly not Can (it is conjoined with it). (U1)–(U2) define the universal class via (U3), so an undefined relation is inside a class definition. If χ is just a parameter, ∃χ Enable(…) ∧ Can(…) collapses to ∃χ Can(…) unless Enable constrains χ.

**Serious.** **Fix:** define Enable — e.g. χ is physically admissible and compatible with the system's boundary β — or drop it and quantify χ directly.

### 19. '≺_h' is called causal precedence but is not shown to come from Θ
**Claim attacked:** "a set of occurrences with an acyclic causal precedence ≺_h and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated."

**Why it fails.** If ≺_h is the process-dependency order supplied by the physical module, say so; if not, it is a third primitive and a causal one. The word "causal" is doing work that "temporal" would not (and should not) do.

**Minor.** **Fix:** state that Θ supplies ≺_h as the admitted-process dependency order, or define it.

### 20. Part XV's sufficiency test uses an unanalysed "plainly explains nothing"
**Claim attacked:** "A candidate meeting (E) on a physically admitted contract with a non-declared transport, which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency."

**Why it fails.** The central falsification condition is stated in terms external to the semantics. That is defensible as dialectics, but it contradicts Derivation 6's consequence ("there is no residual predicate meaning 'explains'"): the adequacy test *is* such a predicate. The boast should be withdrawn or indexed: no residual predicate *inside the theory*, sufficiency assessed pre-theoretically.

**Serious (as an overclaim).** **Fix:** restate the consequence as "no residual predicate inside the semantics," and concede that sufficiency is judged by pre-theoretic intuition.

### 21. Non-circular dependence's "block" is ambiguous against Part VI's redundant routes
**Claim attacked:** "There exists (a,b)∈C that removes or replaces a nonempty block of Γ … under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way."

**Why it fails.** If "block" means any nonempty subset, Part VI's redundant routes (S = {{a},{b},{a,b}}) fail non-circularity; if it means Γ or a CriticalBlock, say so. As written, (E) and Part VI can be read as inconsistent.

**Minor.** **Fix:** specify the block is Γ, or a CriticalBlock under (B).

### 22. Derivation 10 overstates the swap result
**Claim attacked:** "The swap edit is invisible in the sensory field and leaves every prediction unchanged. On **any** contract containing it, the two persistence components are of one kind."

**Why it fails.** A contract containing the swap may also contain discriminating edits (e.g. setting one thing's velocity alone). The claim holds for the contract described, not for any contract containing the swap.

**Cosmetic.** **Fix:** restrict to the described contract, or to contracts containing no edit that separates the two.

### 23. Derivation 5 needs a declared meta-contract, creating an index regress
**Claim attacked:** "Give it ports …, components (the closure conditions), and admitted edits (add or remove a change; alter 𝒬). It is then a D in the sense of (O)."

**Why it fails.** A D in the sense of (O) needs value domains for its ports and its own contract to be a content; that contract needs its own contract. The regress is benign but must terminate in a *declared* meta-contract, and the grain of the contract-as-organization is not ℓ. Neither is said.

**Minor.** **Fix:** note the regress terminates in a declared meta-contract and that the contract-as-organization lives at a different grain.

### 24. Part XIV's index list omits C's provenance
**Claim attacked:** "Grain ℓ, boundary β, continuity Ω, and the contract C are declared indices."

**Why it fails.** Part III gives contracts provenance ρ_p ∈ {declared, selected, constructed}, and Derivations 5 and 7 make constructed contracts first-class contents that can be new and can take over operative use. So C is an index *with provenance*, not simply declared.

**Cosmetic-to-minor.** **Fix:** restate as "C is an index with provenance; when that provenance is constructed, the index is itself a content."

### 25. 'operative', 'problem-directed', 'integrated' — three more undefined adjectives
**Claim attacked:** "the result must be able to change how the system proceeds" / "operative return" (Part I); "operative deliberative rule" (Reason use); "integrated into problem-directed activity" (Deploy).

**Why it fails.** "Operative" appears in five places and is never defined; presumably it means the rule/representation actually governs the system's transitions, which is a Θ-fact that should be stated. "Problem-directed" and "integrated" in Deploy are teleological and undefined, and Deploy feeds (G) and (EK).

**Minor.** **Fix:** define operative as governing actual transitions under Θ; replace "integrated into problem-directed activity" with the concrete retained-capability condition already present in the same sentence.

## Checked and found sound (one line each)

- The finite monotone theorem (Part VI) is correct as stated; the ∪min S / ∩min S characterisations follow by the given argument.
- (I2) and (I4) are correct: identification on all attainable y ⟺ f factors through g; linear case ⟺ ker A ⊆ ker cᵀ; the two-balance example's kernel is spanned by (1,−1,−1) as claimed.
- (O1) is correct: invariant preservation forbids paths between different invariant values, and equal values do not give reachability.
- (T2) is correct: e_n ≤ εΣ_{k<n}L^k under the stated Lipschitz and one-step-discrepancy assumptions.
- (CT2) is correct: monotonicity of F gives the greatest fixed point as the union of post-fixed sets (Knaster–Tarski).
- The Leibniz-expansion example's non-circularity witnesses (I₃ under removal of skewness; the 2×2 block under removal of oddness) do distinguish the two contrasts as intended.

**Bottom line.** The structural core (O), (K), (F1), (F2), (A) is in good order, and the combinatorial lemmas hold. But the claim "the semantics has two primitives" is false as written: ≡ℓ, ProducedBy/ProducesVia, Enable, owned, relevant, nontrivial, and Γ are all undefined and undeclared, and Rep↔Con is a cycle in the dependence order Derivation 6 relies on. Derivation 3 additionally needs an explicit population-freedom assumption. Repairing these is mostly a matter of explicit definition and index declaration — but until it is done, the document's central boast does not survive its own Part XV test.

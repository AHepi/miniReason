## Findings

**Finding 1 — Baseline exclusion contradicts the anchored grade’s own “full admitted contract” claim.**
Quote: “Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\).” And: “Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits.”
Claim attacked: anchored world-kind enforces component fidelity on the full admitted contract.
Why it fails: condition 4 uses forward kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), but forward kind is defined after deleting every \((1,b)\) pair. So condition 4 does not enforce agreement at baseline pairs. A component can differ from its anchor only at \((1,b)\) and still pass condition 4, even though the surrounding text says “fidelity holds … every pair the module admits.” This directly admits decoys differing in initial condition/state.
Severity: serious, possibly fatal to the anchored grade as stated.
Fix: include baseline pairs in the world-kind comparison, or add an explicit baseline-fidelity conjunct to anchoring. If state is intended to be excluded, say anchoring does not require baseline fidelity.

**Finding 2 — Calling the baseline component relation its “state” is a category error.**
Quote: “The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states.”
Claim attacked: \(L_j(1,b)\) is the component’s state.
Why it fails: \(L_j(1,b)\) is a relation on the component’s ports, not a valuation or state. A state is an element of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b)\) or an assignment to ports. The baseline relation may be a law, constraint, constant, or constitutive relation. The exclusion rationale conflates relation with state.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define state separately from component relation; if baseline relations are to be ignored for kind-hood, justify that without identifying them with state.

**Finding 3 — Excluding \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\) drops every identity-boundary pair, not just the target baseline.**
Quote: “\(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)”.
Claim attacked: only the baseline pair \((1,b_0)\) is excluded.
Why it fails: the exclusion ranges over all \(b\in B\). If boundary conditions encode initial conditions, parameter settings, or other non-positional distinctions, differences at identity edits across those boundaries are removed from forward kind. Initial conditions and boundary-indexed states are therefore lost unless they also show up under non-identity edits.
Severity: serious.
Fix: exclude only the target baseline \((1,b_0)\), or define which boundary conditions are “state” and which are kind-relevant.

**Finding 4 — Historical kind is not well-defined for components because Part IV supplies witnesses only for transports.**
Quote: “A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance.”
Claim attacked: historical kinds of components are well-defined.
Why it fails: Part IV defines \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) and \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) for a transport \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\). There is no component-level selection witness, construction witness, descent predicate, or copying predicate. “Descended from” and “copied from” are not defined anywhere. So a historical kind cannot be checked or attributed as stated.
Severity: fatal to Grievance 12’s component side.
Fix: define component provenance predicates with populations, variation, elimination, causal responsibility, construction witnesses, and copy/descent relations; or restrict historical kinds to transport/realization occurrences.

**Finding 5 — If historical kind is any predicate on history, the analysis-of-kinds conjecture becomes unfalsifiable.**
Quote: “A historical kind is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component.”
Claim attacked: historical kinds form a determinate class that can be contrasted with forward kinds.
Why it fails: without a restriction to specified provenance classes, any two occurrences with different histories can be distinguished by some historical predicate. Then almost any kind-label can be reclassified as “historical,” and the conjecture that every discriminating use is forward or historical becomes vacuous.
Severity: serious.
Fix: enumerate the admissible historical-kind predicates from explicit witnesses; do not allow arbitrary predicates on history.

**Finding 6 — Components are called slots, but then said to have histories.**
Quote: “A component is a slot in an organization: what it does under changes is its forward kind.” And: “the history that produced an occurrence of a component.”
Claim attacked: a component can have a historical kind.
Why it fails: slots do not occur and do not have causal histories; realizations or carriers do. The historical kind of a component conflates the role/slot with the physical occurrence filling it. This is exactly the distinction Grievance 12 needs to keep straight.
Severity: serious.
Fix: distinguish component slot from component realization; define historical kind on the realization/occurrence, not on the slot.

**Finding 7 — The transport side of the asymmetry is ill-typed: \(t\) is a tuple but is called an occurrence.**
Quote: “A transport from an organization \(D\) to an organization \(E\) is \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\).” And: “A transport is an occurrence in a physical history: it came to hold by selection, construction, or declaration.”
Claim attacked: transports are individuated by history.
Why it fails: the formal object \(t\) is a tuple of maps. \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\dots)\) and \(\operatorname{Con}(t;\dots)\) apply to that tuple. Two distinct physical histories with the same maps are not distinguished by the notation. Also, declared transports are explicitly “a status in the model, not a physical history,” so not every transport is an occurrence. The transport/component asymmetry is therefore not formally stated.
Severity: serious, possibly fatal to the asymmetry claim.
Fix: separate transport type from transport token/occurrence; define provenance on tokens; include an occurrence/history index in the token. Call declared items transport specifications.

**Finding 8 — The active-route condition smuggles history into the anchored grade, contradicting Grievance 12.**
Quote: “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.” And Grievance 12: “it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change.”
Claim attacked: component history makes no difference to fidelity or anchored adequacy.
Why it fails: two components with identical signatures on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), hence identical response to every admitted change, can differ in anchored status if one is an actual route and the other is a backup. The active-route condition is a condition on actual occurrences, not on signatures. So history enters the anchored grade. The text’s summary “Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract” omits this extra historical condition.
Severity: serious.
Fix: state explicitly that anchored grade includes a separate historical/actuality condition not reducible to fidelity; or drop the active-route condition; or redefine fidelity to include actuality.

**Finding 9 — Active route uses representation, reintroducing provenance into anchored grade.**
Quote: “joining a represented input to an operative result.” And Part V: “It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label.”
Claim attacked: anchored grade does not require settling provenance.
Why it fails: “represented input” invokes \(\operatorname{Rep}\), which by (R) requires a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance. So the anchored grade depends on provenance of some transport, despite the claim that anchoring is just component fidelity plus proper decomposition.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define active route without representation, or state that anchored grade depends on provenance of the inputs.

**Finding 10 — Forward kind is not defined between a component and an anchor subnetwork.**
Quote: Part V condition 4: “Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor.” Part II: “Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** …”
Claim attacked: a component can be of one forward kind with its anchor subnetwork.
Why it fails: forward kind is defined only as a relation between two components. A subnetwork signature is defined, but no equivalence relation is defined between a component and a subnetwork. Condition 4 is therefore undefined as stated.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define world-kind between a component and a subnetwork by matching the subnetwork signature to the component signature on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus\{(1,b)\}\), or restrict anchors to components.

**Finding 11 — The analysis-of-kinds conjecture is not stated in refutable terms.**
Quote: “A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work.”
Claim attacked: this states a falsifier for the analysis-of-kinds conjecture.
Why it fails: “kind-label,” “distinguishes,” and “does explanatory work” are not defined in the formal semantics. There is no procedure for deciding whether a distinction does explanatory work, nor for deciding whether “no history distinguishes.” A critic cannot check whether the condition is met.
Severity: fatal to the claim that the conjecture is now refutable.
Fix: define kind-label as additional data with a specified use in \(\operatorname{Account}\), anchoring, Deploy, etc.; define explanatory work as a difference in one of those predicates; specify the admissible historical-kind predicates.

**Finding 12 — The refutation condition does not quantify over all admissible grains and admitted contracts.**
Quote: Part XV: “no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes” versus the conjecture: “a forward kind on some admissible grain and admitted contract.”
Claim attacked: the refutation condition matches the conjecture.
Why it fails: a label that is not a forward kind on the current \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) may be a forward kind on a finer admissible contract or admissible grain. The refutation must exclude that possibility. As written, the author can always rescue the conjecture by moving to a finer admissible index.
Severity: serious.
Fix: state the falsifier as: no forward kind on any admissible grain and any admitted contract, and no historical kind among the explicitly defined provenance predicates.

**Finding 13 — “The identity edit separates nothing” is false under (K).**
Quote: “A change separates two components when their signatures differ at it; the identity edit separates nothing.”
Claim attacked: the identity edit cannot separate components.
Why it fails: (K) defines \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\). If \(L_j(1,b)\neq L_{j'}(1,b)\), the signatures differ at the identity edit. The sentence is a stipulation to ignore identity edits, not a consequence of the definition.
Severity: minor.
Fix: say “we do not count identity edits as separating for forward kind.”

**Finding 14 — Grievance 12 conflates “no admitted change” with “no change in the contract.”**
Quote: “two components no admitted change separates are one forward kind there.”
Claim attacked: forward kind tracks all physically admitted changes.
Why it fails: forward kind is relative to a contract \(C\), which may be a proper scope \(S\) of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). Admitted changes outside \(C\) can separate the components on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) while leaving them one forward kind on \(C\). The phrasing conflates the contract with the full admitted set.
Severity: minor.
Fix: say “no change in the contract \(C\)” and rely on the anchored grade for \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

**Finding 15 — “Reinstates correspondence as primitive” is a non sequitur.**
Quote: “This refutes the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture** … and reinstates correspondence as primitive.”
Claim attacked: refuting the conjecture reinstates correspondence as primitive.
Why it fails: a kind-label doing work not captured by forward or historical kinds might show that some other notion is primitive, or that the semantics is incomplete. It does not by itself show that correspondence between representation and world is primitive.
Severity: minor/cosmetic.
Fix: drop the inference or state the additional argument.

**Finding 16 — Forward kind requires value-domain bijections, not just footprint bijections.**
Quote: “there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide.”
Claim attacked: a bijection of footprints is enough to compare signatures.
Why it fails: \(L_j(a,b)\) is a subset of \(\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\). To equate it with \(L_{j'}(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v'\in V_{j'}}X_{v'}\), one also needs bijections \(X_v\to X_{v'}\) respecting the relations. A bijection of port names alone does not make the equality well-typed.
Severity: minor to serious formal gap.
Fix: require a port-and-domain bijection preserving relations under the signature comparison.

## Findings

**1. Baseline exclusion loses real distinctions and misidentifies a relation as a state.**
Claim attacked: “The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states.”
Why it fails: \(L_j(1,b)\) is not a state. In (O), a state is a global valuation \(z\in X_D\); \(L_j(1,b)\) is a constraint/relation on the local ports of \(j\). Excluding all \((1,b)\) for \(b\in B\), not merely the single baseline \((1,b_0)\), removes boundary-condition dependence and baseline constraints from the forward-kind comparison. If \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) contains boundary changes \((1,b)\), then two components differing only at those pairs differ under an admitted change but are declared one forward kind. This conflates constants, parameters, initial conditions, and components that differ only in their response to boundary conditions. The “different positions” analogy is not an argument: positions are port values, not component relations.
Severity: serious, arguably fatal to the claim that a forward kind is how a component responds to the changes the contract admits.
Fix: include identity-edit boundary pairs as separating changes when boundary changes are admitted; or define a separate state/parameter kind and require state fidelity when the question is state-sensitive. Restrict exclusion to the single fixed baseline \((1,b_0)\), and only if that exclusion is independently justified.

**2. The word “signature” is used inconsistently in the definition of separation.**
Claim attacked: “The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\)” versus “their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)” and “A change separates two components when their signatures differ at it; the identity edit separates nothing.”
Why it fails: \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\) includes \((1,b)\). If separation is defined by difference in \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\), then an identity edit can separate two components. The text then says it cannot. The only way the latter is true is if “signature” there means the restricted signature on \(C\setminus\{(1,b)\}\), which is not what (K) defines.
Severity: minor-to-serious formal inconsistency.
Fix: define \(\operatorname{sig}^F_C(j)\) as the signature on \(C\setminus\{(1,b)\}\), and use that term consistently for forward kind and separation. Or state explicitly that separation is evaluated only on the restricted signature.

**3. Historical kinds are not well-defined for components because Part IV supplies provenance witnesses only for transports.**
Claim attacked: “A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance.”
Why it fails: Part IV defines provenance for transports: \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\), \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\), \(\operatorname{Dec}(t)\). It does not define a component occurrence, a population of component variants, a variation operator on components, a survival condition for components, or a construction witness for components. “Descended from” and “copied from” are not among Part IV’s provenances at all. Even with a complete physical history, there is no defined witness type for component provenance. So the phrase “with the witness that Part IV requires” is a category error.
Severity: serious, possibly fatal to Grievance 12’s claim that component histories are represented as historical kinds.
Fix: either define component-level provenance witnesses—e.g., for occurrences realizing component slots or anchor subnetworks—or define historical kinds extensionally as predicates on histories without requiring Part IV witnesses. Define or remove “descended from” and “copied from.”

**4. The historical-kind disjunct is either trivial or undefined.**
Claim attacked: “A historical kind is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component.”
Why it fails: If “a predicate on the history” is literal and unrestricted, then any two different histories are distinguished by the predicate “has history \(h_1\).” That makes the analysis-of-kinds conjecture almost trivially true for any distinct histories, and makes Part XV’s “no history distinguishes” impossible unless the histories are identical. If instead the dash list—selected, constructed, descended from, copied from—is meant to restrict the allowed predicates, those categories are not defined for components (Finding 3), so the restriction does no work.
Severity: serious.
Fix: specify a precise class of admissible historical kinds for components, with a defined witness and a relevance condition. Otherwise the second disjunct of the conjecture is either vacuous or ill-defined.

**5. The actuality-of-route condition smuggles history into anchored fidelity.**
Claim attacked: Grievance 12 says: “What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract. Provenance matters to *representation* … it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change.”
But Part V, condition 4 says: “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.” Part IX adds: “A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature, and it is what the anchored grade's fourth condition uses.”
Why it fails: The active-route condition is a historical fact about actual occurrences. It excludes a backup that has the same signature on the admitted changes. It is part of the anchored grade. Part V also says: “Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution.” Therefore a component’s history makes a difference to whether anchored fidelity/account-hood holds. The denial in Grievance 12 is false as stated.
Severity: serious.
Fix: split condition 4 into (4a) world-kind fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and (4b) an actual-route/actuality condition. State explicitly that (4b) is historical and not a fidelity condition. Revise Grievance 12 to say history does not affect (F1)/(F2) on \(C\), but it does affect anchored account-hood.

**6. The transport/component asymmetry does not hold for the anchored grade.**
Claim attacked: “A transport is an occurrence in a physical history … A component is a slot in an organization … What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract.”
Why it fails: Because the active-route condition is part of anchored account-hood, components are individuated by history in that grade: an actual route and a backup with identical signature are treated differently. The asymmetry is therefore not absolute. Components are individuated by signature for forward kind, but by history for anchored account-hood. The defense of individuating transports by history while refusing to individuate components by history fails at the anchored level.
Severity: serious.
Fix: restrict the asymmetry claim to forward kind and (F1)/(F2) fidelity on a fixed contract. If anchored account-hood uses component history, say so directly and define the relevant component historical kinds.

**7. The stated refutation condition for the analysis-of-kinds conjecture has a quantifier mismatch.**
Claim attacked: Part XV: “A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes … This refutes the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture** — that every discriminating use of a kind-label is a forward kind on some admissible grain and admitted contract, or a historical kind.”
Why it fails: The conjecture allows “some admissible grain and admitted contract.” The refutation antecedent only rules out changes in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), presumably at the current grain. A kind-label could distinguish two accounts at that grain and contract but still be a forward kind at a finer admissible grain or under a different admitted contract. Such a case meets the stated antecedent but does not refute the conjecture. The antecedent is therefore too weak.
Severity: serious.
Fix: quantify over all admissible grains and admitted contracts: “no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) at any admissible grain, under any admitted contract, distinguishes them, and no historical kind of the defined class distinguishes them.”

**8. “No history distinguishes” is ill-posed given the current definition of historical kind.**
Claim attacked: “no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes.”
Why it fails: If historical kinds are unrestricted predicates on histories, then any two distinct histories are distinguished by the predicate “is \(h_1\).” If histories are identical, no historical kind can distinguish them. So the condition either makes the conjecture trivially satisfied for distinct histories or collapses to the requirement that the histories be identical. It does not test the intended conjecture. If historical kinds are restricted to selected/constructed/descended/copied categories, those categories are not defined for components (Finding 3), so the condition remains undefined.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define the exact class of historical kinds and state what it means for a historical kind to “distinguish” in the same explanatory sense as the kind-label.

**9. “Does explanatory work” and “discriminating use” are undefined.**
Claim attacked: “and the distinction does explanatory work” and “every discriminating use of a kind-label.”
Why it fails: No criterion is given for when a distinction does explanatory work or when a use of a label is discriminating. Without that, one cannot determine whether the Part XV refutation condition is met. The conjecture is therefore not operational as stated.
Severity: minor-to-serious.
Fix: define “explanatory work” and “discriminating use,” e.g., the distinction changes whether an account satisfies (E), changes anchored account-hood, changes the answer profile, or changes which account is accepted for the question.

**10. Forward-kind equivalence requires more than a bijection of footprints.**
Claim attacked: “Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\).”
Why it fails: A bijection of footprints need not preserve port value domains. \(L_j(a,b)\) is a subset of \(\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\). If the bijection maps \(v\in V_j\) to \(v'\in V_{j'}\) but \(X_v\neq X_{v'}\), then the transported relation is not well-typed and the signatures cannot be compared. The definition needs an isomorphism of footprints, including domains, not merely a bijection of port sets.
Severity: minor.
Fix: require a bijection of footprints that also maps \(X_v\) to \(X_{v'}\) and is compatible with the boundary conditions and edit translations, so that the transported \(L_j(a,b)\) is a subset of the correct product.

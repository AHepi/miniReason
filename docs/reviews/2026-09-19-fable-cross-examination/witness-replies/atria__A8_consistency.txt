# Findings

## I. Part IV (R) vs. Part V Account / the opening definition of "explanation"

**1. The definition of "explanation" admits declared transports, while Part IV denies that declared transports represent anything.**
- Attacked: Part 0 — "An explanation is a question-relevant organization of dependencies, held to a target by a transport that is checked only by what changes when things are changed." Against Part IV — "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does."
- Fails: The opening definition contains no provenance requirement. A declared transport is still "a transport … checked only by what changes when things are changed," so it satisfies the definition, while Part IV says it yields no representation. The same hole is in (E): Part V's four conditions are silent on provenance, so a stipulated transport can satisfy Account. Part 0 itself says "a claim about creativity cannot rest on" declaration, but (K1), (S), (B), (D) and (EK) all rest on Account.
- Serious.
- Fix: add a provenance conjunct to Account (or define the explanatory-achievement predicate as (E) ∧ (Sel∨Con) ∧ Deploy) and rewrite the Part 0 definition accordingly.

**2. The sufficiency claim is stated in three non-equivalent ways, and the test adds a condition (E) does not contain.**
- Attacked: Part 0 (A) — "Produce a candidate that satisfies all four conditions of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing." Part XV — "A candidate meeting (E) on a physically admitted contract with a non-declared transport, which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency." Against (E) itself: (F1)∧(F2)∧(A)∧NonCircular∧NonVacuous.
- Fails: "with a non-declared transport" is not one of the four conditions. So the refutation target is (E)+provenance, not (E); yet Part V presents (E) as the whole of Account, and Part XIV derives Account "from fidelity under change" alone. The constitutive conjecture (i)–(iv) is a third, broader formulation. Which conjunction is the sufficiency claim is undetermined.
- Serious.
- Fix: state the single sufficiency predicate and mark (E) explicitly as necessary-not-sufficient.

**3. An (EK) attribution can rest on a merely declared question.**
- Attacked: Part III — "Any assessment may use any of the three." Against (EK)'s `Account(c,p_c)` and Part 0's "a claim about creativity cannot rest on it."
- Fails: `p_c`'s contract may be declared; nothing in (EK) requires ρ_p = constructed. The creativity is carried by Origin(c), so this is survivable, but the document never says so, leaving the appearance that question-provenance is doing work it is not.
- Minor.
- Fix: one clause noting that (EK) constrains the provenance of c, not of p_c.

## II. Derivation 7 (frozen assessment vs. moving question)

**4. "Its truth is fixed at that index" confuses identity of a claim with fixity of its truth value.**
- Attacked: Derivation 7 — "The assessment is indexed to C and its truth is fixed at that index (Part VIII, historical index)." Against Part VIII — "A proposition indexed to a contract remains that proposition when a later theory changes the current contract" (identity, not truth value) and Part I — "Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it."
- Fails: Fidelity is a fact "about the transport and the world" (Grievance 3). If the target organization changes, "ℰ is an account on C" goes true→false at the *same* index. Derivation 7's consistency proof therefore does not establish what it claims; it shows the two claims are not contradictory, not that the frozen assessment has a stable truth value.
- Serious.
- Fix: either freeze the world-state at the assessment event, or replace "its truth is fixed at that index" with "the claim is identified by its index and evaluated against the state at the assessment event."

**5. (EK) never says at which world-state Account(c,p_c) is evaluated.**
- Attacked: (EK) — "The scope of Account(c,p_c) is the contract fixed at e_c."
- Fails: The contract is fixed; the state is not. (EK) carries states ξ, ξ′ for Repair but none for Account. If the target drifts between e_c and ξ′, Account(c,p_c) is indeterminate and (EK) has no determinate truth value. Derivation 7 does not repair this, for the reason in Finding 4.
- Serious.
- Fix: index the Account conjunct to a state (e.g. ξ′ or the state at e_c), stated explicitly.

## III. Grain as declared index vs. kinds as contract-relative

**6. (K) indexes kinds to the contract only, but the organization under kinding is grain-dependent.**
- Attacked: Part II (K) — "Fix an organization D and a contract C⊆A×B … The signature of component j on C is …"; Part XIV — "Grain ℓ, boundary β, continuity Ω, and the contract C are declared indices." Against Part IV — "the organization that o instantiates under the physical module, at grain ℓ."
- Fails: D = Org_ℓ(o) depends on the declared grain ℓ; two grains can yield different components, hence different kinds. So kinds are (ℓ, C)-relative while (K) records only C. Part 0 ("at any level of detail … the changes that level admits"), Grievance 2 ("indexes every kind-claim to its level"; "At a finer level with more admitted changes they may separate"), Derivation 2 ("one account at grain C") and Derivation 10 ("identity, at this grain, is exhausted by trajectory") all use *level/grain* to mean *contract*. Two distinct declared indices are being run together throughout.
- Serious.
- Fix: index (K) and every kind-claim to (ℓ, C); reserve "grain" for ℓ and "coarseness" for C.

**7. (N)'s equivalence relation is undefined and grain-indexed where Derivation 2's criterion is contract-indexed.**
- Attacked: (N) — "New(s,c,h,e) ⟺ ¬∃d∈R_{<e}(s,h), d≡_ℓ c."
- Fails: ≡_ℓ is never defined. Derivation 2 gives an identity criterion indexed by contract ("one account at grain C"), not by ℓ. For c a contract, Derivation 5 is the relevant identity condition, again contract-indexed. So newness is indexed by the wrong index and cannot be checked as written.
- Serious.
- Fix: define ≡_{ℓ,C} (e.g. via (F1),(F2),(A) on the contract, per Derivation 2) and use it in (N).

**8. "Physics says what organization a lump instantiates" is false as stated.**
- Attacked: Part 0 — "Physics says what organization a lump of matter instantiates." Against Part IV — "The one thing (R) takes from outside is Org_ℓ(o) … at a grain."
- Fails: Physics plus a *declared grain* says it. Derivation 10 confirms this in practice: "Stipulate a primitive layer P." This matters because (R) is the load-bearing derived predicate and its non-semantic input is grain-indexed.
- Minor.
- Fix: "Physics, at a declared grain, says …".

## IV. (EK) factive vs. fallibilism

**9. (EK) is factive while the epistemology forbids self-certification, and the tension is never acknowledged.**
- Attacked: (EK) — "¬o(ξ)∧o(ξ′)∧Origin(s,c,p_c,h,e_c)∧Account(c,p_c)∧…" Against Part I — "A thinker may act on an appraisal without certifying it"; Part XII — "It is fallible and checkable as any physical claim is."
- Fails: (EK) defines "the knowledge-creation class" (Part XIV) and requires actual repair plus actual adequacy. No system can self-attribute it; every internal proxy (Deploy, Origin witnesses) is compatible with Account failing. The document states the fallibilism for Sel and for physical claims but never for (EK) itself, so the factive predicate and the non-certificational epistemology sit side by side unreconciled.
- Serious.
- Fix: state explicitly that (EK) is factive, that its attribution is conjectural, and that no system certifies its own (EK)-status.

## V. Grievance 4 vs. contract-relativity of every claim

**10. "Caught by any criticism that supplies the excluded change" contradicts Derivation 2's resolution.**
- Attacked: Grievance 4 — "A contract that quietly excludes physically possible changes to protect an account is not caught by a rule about intentions; it is caught by the requirement that the exclusion be stated, and then by any criticism that supplies the excluded change." Against Derivation 2 — "The remedy is a finer contract, which is a new question."
- Fails: Per Derivation 2, adding the excluded change poses a *new question*; the original account stands as a scoped truth on C. So the criticism does not catch the original account — it shows narrowness. Grievance 4 concedes to the relativist more than Derivation 2 permits it to, and the two passages are not reconciled.
- Serious.
- Fix: rewrite Grievance 4 to say the protective exclusion is caught only in the sense that the account's scope is exposed and its adequacy on the widened contract fails.

**11. "Objectivity lives in the physics" omits the two further layers of index-relativity the document itself introduces.**
- Attacked: Grievance 4 — "The physical theory fixes which changes are possible at all … Objectivity lives in the physics and in the fidelity facts." Against Part I — "Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physics"; Part XII — "The physical module adopts a task-based formulation of physics for this purpose"; Part XIV — "Every claim is relative to them."
- Fails: The objectivity base is an *adopted*, revisable physics, and every claim is additionally relative to declared ℓ and declared C. Grievance 4 argues as if physics were an absolute ground; the apparatus elsewhere makes it the first of three indices.
- Serious.
- Fix: "objectivity is indexed to (adopted physics, grain, contract); within an index there is a fact of the matter."

## VI. Additional internal contradictions

**12. "Exactly one of three provenances" is unproven and is contradicted by "construction may operate on selected material."**
- Attacked: Part IV — "A transport t between organizations of a physical system has exactly one of three provenances"; "Construction may operate on selected material. Selection may continue to operate beneath construction." Against Part X — "A physical system may exhibit both; the witnesses differ."
- Fails: A transport built out of selected material has a selection history *and* a construction witness. Exclusivity is secured only by Part X's clause "A selected transport has no represented target in its history; a constructed one does," which appears nowhere in Part IV's three definitions, and provenance is not time-indexed — so a transport can change provenance mid-history while Sel/Con/Dec are written as timeless predicates.
- Serious.
- Fix: index provenance to (t, time) or to the last originative episode, and prove exclusivity from the representation clause rather than asserting it.

**13. Derivation 3 alters the wrong object and assumes a non-degenerate population.**
- Attacked: Derivation 3 — "Alter L_j(a,b) for one (a,b)∉H to any other admitted relation; the result survives on H and differs at (a,b)."
- Fails: Two defects. (i) L_j(a,b) is *target* data, "supplied independently for each (a,b)" by the interpretation of D (Part II); altering it changes the target, not the transport. To build a surviving candidate that differs at an unseen point one must alter the *candidate organization's* relation L_k(τ(a),σ(b)), not L_j(a,b). (ii) The altered item must be a member of 𝒯; if 𝒯 = {t} or μ cannot reach the alteration, no such t′ exists, and the survivor *is* determined on C∖H. So the claim "For every (a,b)∈C∖H there exists a transport t′, also surviving on H, with a different value at (a,b)" is false as stated.
- Serious (Part XV lists its negation as a defeat condition).
- Fix: add an explicit non-degeneracy condition on (𝒯, μ) and rewrite the proof varying the candidate's relations, not the target's.

**14. Derivation 2's identity claim ignores Γ and conflicts with Part VI.**
- Attacked: Derivation 2 — "Two candidates ℰ, ℰ′ for the same p that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on C are one account at grain C: their components are pairwise of one kind on C and their answer profiles coincide." Against Part VI — "Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference" and (D)'s Boundary condition.
- Fails: The proof establishes only component-kind coincidence (Derivation 1) and answer-profile coincidence (A). Γ, the transport, and provenance are not covered. Two candidates with the same component kinds and answer profile but different active commitments differ explanatorily per Part VI — one may satisfy (E) while the other fails via interference — yet Derivation 2 calls them "one account." Its consequence ("the contract does not contain the distinction") is then false for Γ-differences, since (S)/(B)/(D) record exactly such distinctions.
- Serious.
- Fix: restrict Derivation 2 to candidates sharing Γ and transport provenance, or weaken the conclusion to "indistinguishable in component kinds and answer profile."

**15. NonCircular's formal witness does not detect circularity.**
- Attacked: Part V — "There exists (a,b)∈C that removes or replaces a nonempty block of Γ while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way." Against the same section's negative condition: "The target's answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component."
- Fails: The existence clause is satisfied by paradigmatically circular accounts. "p because (p∧q)": removing the block {p} changes the answer, so the witness obtains, while the negative condition is violated. Hence (E) as conjuncted admits a circular candidate; the actual anti-circular work is done by an unformalized prose condition.
- Serious.
- Fix: formalize the negative condition — e.g. require that no active component's relation on τ[C] equals the answer relation, and that the anchor λ(k) excludes the answer port for every k ∈ Γ.

**16. (EK) is ill-typed for transport-contents.**
- Attacked: Part X — "The content c may be an organization, a transport, or a contract." Against (EK) — "Account(c,p_c)" and Part V — "An explanatory candidate for question p is an organization E, a transport t=(π,τ,σ,λ) from D to E…"
- Fails: Account's first argument is an organization. Derivation 5 re-encodes contracts as organizations, so the contract case is rescuable; nothing re-encodes transports, so Account(c,p_c) has no reading when c is a transport. (G) and Build have the same shape ("prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of c").
- Serious.
- Fix: either restrict (EK)'s c to organizations and contracts, or supply the missing organization re-encoding for transports.

**17. Derivation 6's "two primitives" claim is unsupported: several relations are undefined.**
- Attacked: Part XIV — "The semantics has two primitives"; Derivation 6 — "Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from Θ (including Org_ℓ) and, where invoked, 𝒩, together with declared indices."
- Fails: Used-but-undefined relations include ProducedBy (P), ProducesVia and Result(Δ) (EK), Lic_j, Scope_j, Live_j (K2), Enable (U1)–(U2), and ≡_ℓ (N). None is defined; none appears in the stated dependence order. So Derivation 6's proof ("By the dependence order of Part XIV, following each definition to its base") cannot be carried out, and the parsimony claim is not established.
- Serious.
- Fix: define each, or add them to the primitive/declared list and extend the dependence order to cover (K2)–(K3).

**18. Surprise entails failure of (R), hence loss of Deploy — unremarked.**
- Attacked: Part IV — "a violation occurs when fidelity fails at (a,b); surprise is a violation at (a,b)∉H"; (R) — "Faithful_C(t:Org_ℓ(o)→c)"; Derivation 4 — "the contract C of changes the world admits."
- Fails: (R) requires faithfulness on the *whole* contract C. Derivation 4 requires H⊊C for surprise. So any surprised system ipso facto fails (R) and therefore fails Deploy: surprise is not an epistemic event within representation but a breakdown of it. The document never says this, and never disambiguates "faithful on C" from "faithful on H" for selected transports. Also, "the contract C of changes the world admits" conflates the declared contract with the physically admitted changes (Part V: "The contract C is a declared subset of the physically admitted edits"). Finally, surprise is defined only for selected transports, though constructed transports are equally fallible and have no corresponding violation notion.
- Serious.
- Fix: state that violation suspends Rep/Deploy pending repair; define violation for constructed transports; and correct Derivation 4's phrasing to "the contract C under which the system holds the transport."

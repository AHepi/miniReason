## Verdict

The experiment tests **one** thing in file 10 properly (edit-signature fidelity, S3), tests **one conjunct** of another (Part IX's nonconstant-dependence clause, S4), **illustrates** two more (Part IV's "histories not outputs", S5; Derivation 2's kind-relativity), and tests **nothing at all** of Derivation 3 or Derivation 5. ## Findings

**F1. S1 cannot bear on Part IV's irreducibility claim, because the baseline is a closed grammar, not selection.**
Attacked: "✅ **S1 needed** — 0.906 > 0.819 (best G0, a memoriser) > 0.366 (best structured G0)."
File 10 defines selection as "a population \(\mathcal T\) of candidate transports, a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\), a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) … and a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\)." The G0 search supplies none of these; and the experiment's *actual* selection process, B, reaches the same 0.906. So S1 establishes that a declared hypothesis space was too weak, which is a fact about the grammar, not about provenance. Serious. Fix: run the selection process over an unbiased, non-question-beggingly chosen condition/action space and show it cannot reach the persistence organisation — and note that even that tests (N), not Build.

**F2. If S1 is the operationalisation of (N) New, it tests the wrong quantity.**
Attacked: same S1 line, against "\(\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c\)."
New is absence of a prior *deployable content equivalent at grain ℓ*; equivalence is signature equality on a contract, per (K). S1 instead compares an aggregate fidelity number against a declared 0.9 threshold on a step bucket. The memoriser at 0.819 is 0.087 away, and "structured" is a comparison class the experimenter fixed (failure mode 12 concedes two pre-freeze changes were made after seeing numbers, including "a closedness check split after seeing the memoriser's number"). Serious. Fix: characterise \(R_{<e}\) and test \(\equiv_\ell\) directly — does any prior content have the built rule's signature on the stated contract? — instead of comparing fidelity percentiles.

**F3. S2 has no target among the five anchors, and its best fit is a different predicate.**
Attacked: "✅ **S2 protected** — non-collision 0.819 ≥ 0.702."
The nearest file 10 notion is (P)'s "\(\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\)" — protected *obligations* across an episode boundary — or Part VI's contributory structure. The experiment measures within-contract prediction fidelity on a step subset, with no \(O_p\) or \(P\) ever specified. Prediction fidelity on non-collision steps is not an obligation, and there is no cross-episode \(\xi,\xi'\) comparison. Minor (mapping gap). Fix: name the protected obligations as predicates ("predict displacements correctly"), evaluate them across the episode boundary, and drop the fidelity-bucket proxy.

**F4. S3 is the only substantially faithful operationalisation, but it substitutes a frequency for relation equality.**
Attacked: "✅ **S3 faithful** — 0.974 and 0.973 given bound on non-crossing pairs (threshold 0.9), from 0.000 for A0."
(K) requires that "there is a bijection of their footprints under which \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\) and \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j')\) coincide" — pointwise equality of \(L_j(a,b)\) over the contract. M3 reports a *given-bound fraction* on a sample, with failure mode 13 admitting "'Given bound' conditioning discards 2–18% of pairs in M3 (mostly two things that started adjacent and ambiguous)." That is an estimate on a biased subsample, not signature equality. Serious. Fix: report exact relation equality over the full M3 contract, or state the contract subset and enumerate the discarded pairs instead of silently conditioning on them.

**F5. The S3 pass is earned on the coarse contract where, by file 10's own Derivation 2, it is near-vacuous.**
Attacked: the same S3 line plus "Crossing pairs 0.000, as pre-registered under F3."
File 10's Grievance 4 says a contract that "quietly excludes physically possible changes to protect an account" is caught by "the requirement that the exclusion be stated." The exclusion *is* stated, so the report is scope-honest — but the ✓ is a pass at the coarse grain only, and at that grain slots and things are one kind anyway (Derivation 10's own swap point). The falsifiable claim is the finer one, and it fails. Serious. Fix: split S3 into two indexed outcomes — "faithful on the sensory contract (trivially, by Derivation 2)" and "unfaithful at crossings on the identity contract" — and make the latter the headline.

**F6. The M3 crossing numbers do not reconcile as printed.**
Attacked: "on the 230 pairs with a crossing after the change, C's slots pass through while the things bounce — zero — while the hand-built crossing rule gets all 221 bound pairs," against the table row "Crossing after: A0 alone 0.000 (21); **A0 + C** 0.000 (9); A0 + hand-built pair **1.000 (13)**."
The table defines the parenthetical as the unbound count. Then hand-built has 230 − 13 = 217 bound pairs, not 221; 221 = 230 − 9, which is *A0+C's* bound count. Serious (this is the load-bearing F3 number). Fix: publish per-row denominators, or explain how the totals differ by row.

**F7. S4 tests one conjunct of Part IX's reason-use definition and silently drops the rest.**
Attacked: "✅ **S4 on the route** — scrambled C worse by 0.55 (threshold 0.1); B byte-identical."
File 10 requires that "a structural map from the represented objection into the response subnetwork preserves role bindings, sends content-preserving recodings to the same transition, sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule, and lands on an active route," and an active route is "a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences … which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts." The scramble tests the nonconstant-dependence clause only. No structural map is exhibited, role bindings are unchecked, connectedness of the actual occurrence subnetwork is unchecked, and whether the objection is *represented* per (R) is unchecked. Serious. Fix: output the map (record field → role binding), verify the recoding clause separately, and check route connectedness in the harness trace.

**F8. The results file inverts file 10's account of provenance.**
Attacked: "it is a demonstration that the two processes' outputs depend on different things, which is what 'provenance' means in file 10."
File 10 says the opposite: "The three are distinguished by their histories, not their outputs." Ablation-detectability is an epistemic heuristic the experiment adds; it is not the definition. As written, the sentence makes S4 look like a test of Part IV when it is a test of a detection heuristic. Serious. Fix: restate S4 as "the histories differ in a way an ablation can detect," and separately verify the *history-structure* conditions (population/variation/survival vs episode/witness).

**F9. B's byte-identical output is a strong result of a partly tautological design.**
Attacked: "B, seed 0, re-run with the scrambled records passed in as an argument its code never reads: **byte-identical output.**"
C was built to read records and B to read fitness; that their input-dependence differs is close to definitional. The non-trivial content is the convergence (S5), not the insensitivity. Minor. Fix: give B a fitness signal *derived from* the defect records so both processes share the channel, and show only C's route is defeated; or state plainly that the asymmetry is by construction.

**F10. S5 illustrates Part IV and Derivation 9 but does not test them, and the finer contract it needs was never run.**
Attacked: "✅ **S5 provenance is not output** — B within 0.05 of C in 6 of 6 (identical)," together with "they would come apart on a contract where the junk atom matters (allow co-location, or a thing at cell 1 with velocity 1)."
Part IV's claim is definitional; a single co-outputal pair cannot confirm a definition. And Derivation 2's consequence says "The remedy is a finer contract, which is a new question" — the very contract the results file only *predicts*. Failure mode 14 concedes B has "no simplicity pressure" and "would misfire on a contract where they bite." Serious. Fix: run the co-location / cell-1-velocity-1 contract and exhibit B's rules misfiring there while C's holds. That converts an illustration into a test.

**F11. M5 is a weak instance of Derivation 8, not a test of it.**
Attacked: "Records in reverse order → identical rule. Slots relabelled i↔j throughout → identical rule. **M5 ✓.**"
Derivation 8 claims that transporting "all carriers, relations, transports, histories, and contracts along structure-preserving bijections preserves (E), (G), (P), (EK)." The experiment checks invariance of an *induced rule string* under two recodings; (E), (G), (P), (EK) are never evaluated for the episode, so their preservation is untested. Serious. Fix: evaluate (F1)/(F2)/(A) — or full Account — before and after the relabelling and show the truth-values co-vary.

**F12. M6(i) tests the implementation's stopping rule, not the semantics.**
Attacked: "Empty defect set → builds nothing. **M6(i) ✓.**"
File 10's Build permits episodes that produce nothing; "builds nothing" is a property of the induction algorithm and is equally consistent with a selected process given no data. Minor. Fix: reclassify M6(i) as an implementation sanity check, not evidence for Build.

**F13. M6(ii) is a genuine violation of the construction-witness operationalisation, and the diagnosis confirms it.**
Attacked: "Defect records with the outcome replaced by the prediction (no defect content) → **built** `IF (n_i == n_j) AND (c_j != n_j) THEN reverse_i`, three times, claiming 'fixed 671, broken 0,'" with section 8: "C's accounting never checks that the old prediction differed."
File 10's Build requires "a nontrivial binding construction … and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers," and Part V non-circularity requires an edit under which "the answer profile changes." C's accounting lets a no-op satisfy the witness. This is the one supporting clause that failed, and it failed on the witness condition itself. Serious. Fix: define net as (records where old prediction ≠ outcome and new = outcome) − (records where new ≠ outcome), so a no-op scores exactly zero; verify nontriviality as "there exists a record on which the rule changes the prediction."

**F14. No phase-1 outcome tests Derivation 3, and the file's own extension of it is unsupported.**
Attacked: failure mode 5, "File 10's Derivation 3 applies to construction, not only selection," and the crossing result offered as its evidence.
Derivation 3 is stated for a *selected* transport: "Let \(t\) be selected on a finite history \(H\subsetneq C\). For every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there exists a transport \(t'\), also surviving on \(H\), with a different value at \((a,b)\)." Its proof exploits the survival condition, which has no construction analogue. The crossing failure is about *kinds invisible at the sensory grain* (level-relativity, Derivation 2 territory), not about a survivor's underdetermination on \(C\setminus H\). And B, the only selection-like process, was run over the whole exposure half ("1,720–1,788 full-history fitness evaluations per seed"), so no proper \(H\subsetneq C\) was observed for it. Serious. Fix: implement \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H)\) with \(H\) a proper subset of the evaluation contract, exhibit a co-survivor differing on \(C\setminus H\), and separately attempt the Part XV refutation (a survivor determined on \(C\setminus H\)). If a construction-analogue is wanted, derive it with its own proof.

**F15. No phase-1 outcome bears on Derivation 5, and the key F3 result depends on a question the modeller found.**
Attacked: Derivation 5, "A contract \(C\) is an organization (a set of edits with a query), and can be the content \(c\) in (G)," against section 4: "the finer contract of M3 (which uses thing identities through translated edits) reveals what it could not."
Part III requires that "a claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) for the contract in question, with the witness." Every contract here — the world's edits, the step buckets, and M3's identity contract — was declared by the experimenter: \(\rho_p=\text{declared}\) throughout. The crossing contract that produces F3 was supplied by the design, not constructed by C. So question-finding is untested, and the one result turning on a finer contract rests on the declared provenance that Part 0 says is "permitted only as a modelling convenience, and a claim about creativity cannot rest on it." Serious. Fix: give the system the ability to add edits or alter \(\mathcal Q\) over its own contract, record a construction witness for the contract-content, and evaluate New and Origin for it — the natural test being whether C ever adds the crossing edit itself.

**F16. Build is illustrated, not tested, and the experiment concedes this.**
Attacked: Part X's "an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) … contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting representation," against section 11: "the constructor is a version-space learner with a small grammar, not a theory of conjecture."
None of the four witness components is evaluated; the experiment supplies fidelity and ablation numbers. Worse, version-space induction is itself a selection-like process over a hypothesis space, so without an explicit witness check the constructed/selected distinction is *assumed* rather than established — and M6(ii) shows the assumption is exploitable. Serious. Fix: assert the four witness components over the harness trace and check the "not a composition of content-preserving transfers" clause directly per rule.

**F17. The residual-defect arithmetic does not close.**

**F19. Thresholds are indices too, and their provenance is not recorded.**

## Bottom line on the user's five anchors

- **Part IV three provenances:** illustrated by S5 (same output, different histories) and S4 (route difference detectable). Not tested — the distinction is definitional, and the experiment never verifies the Sel witness (population, variation operator, survival on \(H\)) or the Con witness (episode, represented target, criticism) as such.
- **Part IX reason use:** S4 operationalises the "nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction" clause faithfully; the structural-map, role-binding, recoding, and connectedness clauses are untested, and the file's gloss inverts Part IV's "histories, not outputs."
- **Part X Build:** not tested. M6(ii) exhibits a *violation* of the nontriviality clause; M6(i) tests the algorithm; M5 tests Derivation 8 only weakly.
- **Derivation 3:** not tested. The crossing result is about grain-relativity, not selection underdetermination, and the file's own claim that "Derivation 3 applies to construction" extends the derivation beyond its proof.
- **Derivation 5:** not tested at all. Every contract in the experiment is declared, and the finer contract that produces F3 was found by the modeller, not by the system.

The honest frame is Derivation 10, whose coda reads: "This episode is a relative-consistency witness for the class. It is not a claim that any actual infant, animal, or program has been shown to instantiate it." Experiment 2 instantiates that witness in software, and checks several of its consequences. That is a real but modest contribution, and the results file's own section 11 says exactly that — while sections 5 and 9 overstate what "provenance" means in file 10.

**Steelman: the anchoring condition (AC).**  
The rejected position is not “attach labels to components.” It is:

> For an explanation \(E\) of target \(D\), there is an anchoring map \(\alpha\) from the active components of \(E\) to components/parts of the target mechanism—or to the world—such that \(\alpha(k)\) is of the same kind as \(k\), where “kind” is fixed by the world’s mechanism, not by the current contract \(C\).

This is extra to change-fidelity. F1/F2/A say the chosen anchor subnetwork’s projected relation matches \(E\)’s component relation under the edits in \(C\). AC says the chosen subnetwork must also be the right mechanism component/kind in the world. AC is a fallible, defeasible mechanism-identity claim, checkable by further interventions, experiments, historical evidence, conservation arguments, and so on. It underwrites extrapolation and intervention guidance beyond \(C\).

**What the document loses by dropping AC.**

1. **Correct-at-contract but wrong-mechanism explanations.**

   \[
   \operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)]

> “**Reinstatement of kinds.** Produce a case in which a declared kind-label does discriminating work that no admitted change can do. Derivation 1 says this is impossible; a counterexample would reopen the question whether correspondence must be primitive after all.”

**(2) Why it fails.** The anchoring condition is not necessarily a declared kind-label. It can be a claim about the physical module \(\Theta\): that \(\lambda(k)\) is an actual component/process of the same physical kind or causal role. The document treats \(\Theta\) as primitive and says physical realization obligations apply at every attribution. So it already has resources to evaluate world-kind anchoring in principle. The attack condition is weaker: it asks for a *declared label* to do work no admitted change can do. That is not the same as asking for a world-kind anchor. The document defeats the strawman, not the steelman.

**(3) Severity.** Serious. It misidentifies the position it rejects.

**(4) Fix.** Reframe the attack condition in terms of world-kind anchoring, not declared labels. Then show either that (F1) entails it or that the semantics cannot represent it.

### 8. “The respect is the query” does not answer the anchoring condition

**(1) Claim attacked.**
> “Two questions with the same \(D\) and different \((C,\mathcal Q)\) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other.”

**(2) Why it fails.** This handles the pole-and-shadow case only by saying the reversed calculation answers an identification question, not a production question. That is correct as far as it goes, but it does not answer the anchoring condition. The steelman is not that identification and production questions are confused. The steelman is that even for a given question, an account can be correct at its contract while anchored to the wrong mechanism. The query distinction does not supply a mechanism anchor. And when the production contract is unavailable, the distinction tells you there is no production question — which is a loss for realist explanations of unmanipulable mechanisms, not an answer.

**(3) Severity.** Minor to serious, depending on how strongly the document claims to capture mechanistic explanation.

**(4) Fix.** Separate question-relative adequacy from mechanism anchoring. Add the latter if mechanism explanations are part of the target class.

### 9. Does “Why there is no anchoring condition” answer the steelman? No.

**(1) Claim attacked.** The section quoted above:
> “A separate condition — ‘each component of \(E\) must be tied to a component of \(D\) *of the same kind*’ — would be either redundant or unevaluable. Where \(C\) contains a change separating two kinds, (F1) already fails for a component anchored to the wrong one. Where \(C\) contains no such change, the two are one kind on \(C\) by (K), and the condition would be asserting a distinction that \(C\) does not contain. There is no third case. Derivation 1 makes this exact.”

**(2) Why it fails.** The section assumes the anchoring condition’s “same kind” is the document’s \((K)\) kind: edit-signature on \(C\). Under that reading, the first horn is definitional and the second horn is trivial. The steelman’s “same kind” is world-kind, fixed by the physical module or the actual mechanism. Under that reading:
- The first horn fails because (F1) does not entail world-kind anchoring.
- The second horn fails because \(\Theta\) can in principle evaluate physical kinds/roles; it is not “unevaluable.”
- “There is no third case” is false because \(C\) can be a proper subset of physically admitted edits, so there can be a world distinction that \(C\) does not contain.

Thus the section answers a different position: that a *declared label* must do work no admitted change can do. It does not answer the position that the anchor must correspond to the world’s mechanism.

**(3) Severity.** Fatal to the section’s claim that the anchoring condition is either redundant or unevaluable. It may be survivable for the semantics if the semantics confesses to being contract-relative and gives up mechanistic realism, but it is not an answer to the steelman.

**(4) Fix.** Either add an anchoring condition as a fifth conjunct in (E), evaluated against \(\Theta\) or all physically admitted edits, or explicitly state that the semantics does not distinguish correct-at-contract from anchored-to-the-world and that this is a deliberate restriction. Do not claim the anchoring condition is eliminable without loss.

## A. Part IV selection witness: gerrymanderable and vacuous

**1. Survival as exact coincidence with fidelity is both too strong and too cheap.**
Quote: “the members that survived were exactly the members faithful on \(H\) (a fact about the run, not a definition of survival…)”.
Why it fails: this requires perfect extensional alignment between survival and fidelity on \(H\). It excludes ordinary selection where a faithful member dies by luck or an unfaithful member survives. It also makes the witness cheap: with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\), a two-member population with one arbitrarily bad rival satisfies it. It records coincidence, not causation by fidelity.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require differential survival *because of* fidelity on \(H\) — a counterfactual or causal-route condition — and allow partial or probabilistic alignment rather than exact set equality.

**2. \(H\) can be a single pair, so selection is trivial.**
Quote: “the finite history \(H\) … non-empty and not chosen”.
Why it fails: non-emptiness is the only richness condition. One encountered pair, one faithful survivor, one rival that fails there, and the selection witness is satisfied. That does not establish anything about the transport’s competence.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require \(H\) to meet a richness condition relative to \(C_t\), e.g. contain separating changes for the active components, or a minimal sample size.

**3. The eliminated rival need not be tied to the variation operator.**
Quote: “a variation operator \(\mu\) … produced a member that differs from its parent at some pair of \(H\); … which eliminated at least one member that differed from \(t\) at a pair of \(H\) on which that member failed and \(t\) did not”.
Why it fails: the eliminated rival need not be the member produced by \(\mu\), nor a descendant of \(t\). The variation could be neutral or irrelevant while the rival is an unrelated bad member eliminated for other reasons.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require the eliminated rival to be produced by \(\mu\) from \(t\)’s lineage, or require \(\mu\) to have produced the difference on which elimination turns.

**4. “Difference-making condition” is an overclaim.**
Quote: “That last clause is the difference-making condition, stated in the module’s terms.”
Why it fails: the clause only says the rival failed on \(H\) where \(t\) did not. It does not say the failure caused elimination. Because survival is already defined as exact fidelity on \(H\), any failure on \(H\) is extensionally sufficient, but the witness contains no causal link.
Severity: serious.
Fix: add a counterfactual: had the rival not failed there, it would have survived; or require the survival condition’s route to elimination to pass through that failure.

**5. Selection non-triviality is type-confused and grain-gerrymanderable.**
Quote: “Non-triviality: no event of \(\Sigma_h\) instantiates an organization held to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition by a faithful transport.”
Why it fails: \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition is a condition. “Organization held to \(X\)” is not defined for those types. No grain is specified, and provenance is indexed to grain, so one can choose a coarse grain where the condition holds vacuously.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define “held to” for each type, fix the grain to the route’s grain, or replace with a representation-free condition on the route.

**6. Blindness has a grain loophole.**
Quote: “no itemised representation of a defect … lies on any active route, at the grain at which the route is described, to the event that produced \(t\)’s occurrence”.
Why it fails: the witness chooses the grain of description. Coarse-graining can make an itemised record no longer itemised, so blindness is satisfied while the fine-grained process used the record.
Severity: serious.
Fix: fix the grain to the content’s contract grain, or require blindness at every grain up to a specified finest grain.

**7. The permitted aggregate can encode the itemised record.**
Quote: “an aggregate summary — a function of the multiset of failure magnitudes alone, with no access to which state failed, such as how many failures and how large — is explicitly permitted”.
Why it fails: “failure magnitude” is undefined. If magnitude is a function of the state–value pair, the multiset can be invertible to the itemised data. The caveat “with no access to which state failed” is informal and unenforced.
Severity: serious.
Fix: formally define an aggregate as invariant under permutations and relabelings of states and prove non-invertibility, or forbid any statistic computed from an itemised record on the active route.

**8. “Active route” lets a used defect record escape blindness.**
Quote: “lies on any active route … to the event that produced \(t\)’s occurrence”.
Why it fails: active route is defined by nonconstant dependence under declared contrasts. A defect record can be causally necessary but not on an “active route” if the contrasts are coarse, or it can lie on a route not counted as active. Blindness then holds while the record was used.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define the route counterfactually over all physically admitted contrasts, or require the record not be causally necessary for the occurrence.

**9. The population need not be generated by \(\mu\) from \(t\).**
Quote: “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized organization–transport pairs … with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\) … was applied at least once … and produced a member that differs from its parent at some pair of \(H\)”.
Why it fails: the population can contain arbitrary bad members. \(\mu\)’s produced member need not be the survivor or the eliminated rival. So selection can be satisfied without \(\mu\) explaining \(t\).
Severity: serious.
Fix: require \(t\) or its lineage to be the product of \(\mu\), and the eliminated rival to be a sibling or variant from the same parent.

**10. \(H\) is not “all encountered pairs”; it is all encountered pairs *within* \(C_t\).**
Quote: “the finite history \(H\) of all edit–boundary pairs within \(C_t\) … that occurred as events of \(\Sigma_h\)”.
Why it fails: encountered failures outside \(C_t\) are ignored. If \(C_t\) is chosen narrowly, hard encounters can be excluded from \(H\). This permits gerrymandering by contract choice.
Severity: serious.
Fix: state explicitly that \(H\) is only within the claimed contract, and record excluded encounters; or require the contract not be narrowed after the history.

**11. Selected transports are faithful on \(H\) by definition.**
Quote: “members that survived were exactly the members faithful on \(H\)”.
Why it fails: this makes “selected” entail fidelity on \(H\). Then in (R), if \(H=C_c\), the Faithful conjunct is redundant for a selected transport. It also means selection cannot occur if any faithful member dies.
Severity: serious.
Fix: decouple survival from exact fidelity; use differential survival caused by fidelity.

**12. Non-triviality against the survival condition may forbid ordinary selection.**
Quote: “no event … held to … the survival condition by a faithful transport”.
Why it fails: if an organism has a faithful model of the survival condition — a predator model, say — selection is disallowed even though selection is occurring. This conflates blindness to the selection criterion with selection itself.
Severity: serious.
Fix: restrict non-triviality to the transport and the failure-to-variation mapping, or allow benign representation of the environment.

## B. Separator: blindness versus record-used-at-acceptance

**13. The separator compares different endpoints.**
Quote: “A selection witness is blind on the route to the event that produced the transport’s occurrence; a construction witness has the defect record used on that same route, at the acceptance of the binding. One transport cannot satisfy both for the same route.”
Why it fails: Build requires the record on an active route to *acceptance*. Blindness requires no itemised record on an active route to the *event that produced the occurrence*. Unless acceptance is identified with, or lies on the route to, the occurrence-producing event, both clauses can hold. The text asserts “same route”, but the definitions do not identify those routes.
Severity: fatal to the claimed exclusivity.
Fix: define the occurrence-producing event to include or be downstream of acceptance, or require the construction witness to place the itemised record on the route to the transport’s occurrence.

**14. Exclusivity is only local to a grain.**
Quote: “Provenance is thereby indexed to the grain of the route, as everything else is indexed to a grain.”
Why it fails: a transport can be selected at a coarse grain where no itemised record is instantiated and constructed at a fine grain where the record is used. The separator only forbids both at the same route and grain.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require both witnesses at the same grain for exclusivity, or accept and state that provenance can differ by grain.

**15. “One transport cannot satisfy both” is stipulated, not derived.**
Quote: “One transport cannot satisfy both for the same route.”
Why it fails: this is true only if “same route” already means the route contains both an itemised record and no itemised record. That is a definitional fiat, not a consequence of Sel and Con.
Severity: serious.
Fix: derive exclusivity from the definitions, or state it as part of the definition of “same route”.

**16. Con can hold for a selected transport.**
Quote: “\(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode … whose construction witness prepares \(t\) — the Build predicate holds … — and in which the organization \(t\) runs from … is instantiated … before \(e\)”.
Why it fails: Con does not require the construction witness to have produced \(t\)’s occurrence. A selected transport can later be the subject of a Build episode — acceptance, criticism, binding repair — so Con\((t)\) holds while Sel\((t)\) holds. The minimal-witness rule may still call provenance selected, but Con is true.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require Con to include a witness that produced \(t\)’s occurrence, not merely prepares or criticizes it.

## C. “Used at acceptance” through Part IX’s reason use

**17. Build can hold for a transport with selected provenance.**
Quote: “Build … prepares an organization … held to \(c\) by a transport faithful on \(c\)’s contract … record is used … in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept”.
Why it fails: Build does not require the transport’s provenance to be constructed. A selected transport can satisfy Build if a nontrivial binding is later required and criticized. Then (G), which is \(\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\), can hold for a selected transport. The claim “mixed … does not count as constructed for (G)” is inconsistent because (G) does not check provenance.
Severity: fatal.
Fix: make (G) require Con provenance, or make Build require the transport’s occurrence to be constructed.

**18. Reason use is not well-defined and likely circular.**
Quote: “A response uses a reason when a structural map from the represented objection into the response suborganization preserves role bindings, sends content-preserving recodings (Part X, \(\equiv_\ell\)) to the same transition, sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule, and lands on an active route.”
Why it fails: “represented objection” likely invokes the representation predicate (R), which depends on Sel/Con, which depend on Build. That is a cycle. “Structural map”, “role bindings”, “operative deliberative rule”, and “content changes” are undefined or listed as informal terms. Existence of such a map is also too weak: it does not require the map to be causally efficacious in acceptance.
Severity: fatal.
Fix: define use counterfactually and causally in terms of instantiated records and active routes, remove dependence on (R), and define or eliminate the informal terms.

**19. “Survives the criticism” and “kept” are undefined.**
Quote: “the criticism the binding survives before it is kept”.
Why it fails: Part IX defines when a criticism *bears* by (K1), not when a binding *survives* a criticism. “Kept” is not defined.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define survival of a criticism, e.g. by Account of the binding under the criticism, and define the acceptance/keeping event.

**20. The criticism may be invalid.**
Quote: “Using an invalid objection does not make it valid.”
Why it fails: Build only requires the record to be used in a criticism the binding survives; the criticism may be invalid or false. Construction can then be triggered by an invalid objection, as long as the binding survives.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require the criticism to be bearing by (K1), or require the defect record to be veridical.

**21. The construction witness does not identify the data needed to check Build.**
Quote: “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization.”
Why it fails: it omits the defect record, the criticism, the active route, the acceptance event, and the reason-use map. The witness may not contain what Build requires.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require the witness to identify all Build constituents.

## D. Minimal-witness rule

**22. The minimal-witness rule is ill-defined.**
Quote: “Among the witnesses whose events produced the occurrence that carries \(t\), take those that are \(\prec_h\)-minimal (an event of a witness precedes an event of another).”
Why it fails: witnesses are not events. “An event of a witness precedes an event of another” does not define a partial order on witnesses: it is not antisymmetric, not reflexive, and interleaved witnesses can each precede the other. “Whose events produced the occurrence” requires a causal production relation that is not supplied.
Severity: fatal.
Fix: define a partial order on witness event sets by causal responsibility, or take minimal *events* responsible for the transport occurrence.

**23. Minimality can mask construction.**
Quote: “If all minimal witnesses are of one kind, that is \(t\)’s provenance — constructed or selected.”
Why it fails: if an earlier selection event and a later construction event both contributed to the same occurrence, the rule picks the minimal kind and ignores the later construction that actually produced the transport’s structure. Earlier substrate selection can override later construction.
Severity: serious.
Fix: restrict witnesses to those causally responsible for the transport-specific occurrence, and if both kinds are responsible, call it mixed.

**24. The mixed clause is arbitrary and gameable.**
Quote: “If they are of both kinds, \(t\) has mixed provenance, which is declared as such and does not count as constructed for (G).”
Why it fails: why should mixed not count as constructed? A construction on selected material is supposed to produce a new transport. If mixed arises for the same transport, the clause penalizes construction for the presence of earlier selected material. It is also gameable by making one witness non-minimal.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define mixed only when both kinds are minimal and causally responsible for the same occurrence; allow constructed if the construction witness is responsible for the transport’s binding.

**25. No well-foundedness condition.**
Quote: “take those that are \(\prec_h\)-minimal”.
Why it fails: acyclic precedence does not entail well-foundedness. An infinite descending chain of events leaves no minimal witness, so provenance is undefined.
Severity: minor/serious.
Fix: require histories to be well-founded.

**26. “Later histories are maintenance” assumes occurrence identity is settled.**
Quote: “Later histories are maintenance and do not change provenance; construction on selected material produces a new transport with its own occurrence and its own minimal witness, and the selected one keeps its own.”
Why it fails: no criterion is given for when construction produces a new transport occurrence rather than modifying an existing transport in place. If a binding is modified in place, it is unclear whether this is a new occurrence. The rule can be gamed by choosing occurrence identity.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define occurrence identity for transports under binding change.

## E. Construction rewritten as selection, or vice versa

**27. Construction can be rewritten as selection by route splitting.**
Quote: “A selection witness is blind on the route to the event that produced the transport’s occurrence; a construction witness has the defect record used on that same route, at the acceptance of the binding.”
Why it fails: split the history. Let \(e_1\) be a blind generation of a candidate transport, satisfying Sel for occurrence \(e_1\). Let \(e_2\) be a later acceptance criticism using an itemised defect record, satisfying Con for acceptance \(e_2\). The same transport satisfies Sel at \(e_1\) and Con at \(e_2\). The separator only forbids both at the same route.
Severity: fatal.
Fix: define construction provenance as requiring the itemised record on the route to the transport’s occurrence, not merely at acceptance; or define the transport occurrence to include acceptance.

**28. Selection can be rewritten as construction by a weak “used” claim.**
Quote: “the record is used, in the sense of Part IX, in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept”.
Why it fails: because “used” is either undefined or satisfiable by mere existence of a structural map, any selection process with an aggregate failure summary can be described as having an itemised defect record used in an acceptance criticism, provided one posits a suitable structural map. The definitions do not block the rewrite.
Severity: serious.
Fix: make use counterfactual and causally necessary for acceptance, and require the record to be instantiated on the active route.

**29. Build’s “required” condition is vacuous when the comparison class is empty.**
Quote: “it is required for the transport when replacing it by any binding already carried by \(s\) or obtainable from the carriers fails (F1) or (F2) for that transport on \(c\)’s contract.”
Why it fails: if there are no prior bindings and the carriers yield none for the component, the universal is vacuously true. The text tries to avoid this by saying “the carriers still count”, but if the carriers yield no binding, they do not count.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require a non-empty comparison class, or at least one alternative binding that fails (F1) or (F2).

**30. \(\equiv_\ell\) is the wrong type for bindings.**
Quote: “nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)”.
Why it fails: \(\equiv_\ell\) is a content-preserving recoding of *contents*, not a relation on bindings. A binding is an assignment \(\lambda(k)\) of a component to a subnetwork. “Obtainable by \(\equiv_\ell\)” is type-incorrect.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define an equivalence or transformation class on bindings, or replace with a structural map on bindings.

**31. “Relabelling” of a binding is undefined.**
Quote: “neither a relabelling of a binding already carried by some occurrence of \(s\) before \(e\)”.
Why it fails: “relabelling” is not defined. It could mean a port bijection, a component renaming, or something else.
Severity: minor/serious.
Fix: define relabelling as a port bijection preserving footprints and relations.

**32. Non-triviality against “repertoire and carriers” is not formal.**
Quote: “non-triviality against repertoire and carriers”.
Why it fails: the repertoire in Deploy is a set of deployable contents; bindings are not contents. “Carriers” is not defined as a set of bindings. The condition is informal.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define a transformation class on bindings and state non-triviality relative to it.

**33. “Conjecture first” is not required.**
Quote: “the binding may be conjectured by any process, blind or not”.
Why it fails: if the intended condition is that a conjecture precedes criticism, it is absent. The binding may be generated without any conjecture.
Severity: minor.
Fix: add a conjecture-first condition if intended.

**34. (G) does not check provenance.**
Quote: “\(\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\).”
Why it fails: Origin requires Build, not Con. Build can hold for a selected transport, as in finding 17. So Origin can be satisfied by a selected transport, and the statement that mixed provenance “does not count as constructed for (G)” is inconsistent with (G)’s definition.
Severity: fatal.
Fix: add a provenance condition to Origin, or make Build entail Con.

**35. Non-triviality only excludes *faithful* representations of the selection criterion.**
Quote: “no event … held to … by a faithful transport”.
Why it fails: a process can use a false model of \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition to guide variation. Non-triviality only forbids a faithful transport, so inaccurate representation of the criterion is allowed.
Severity: minor/serious.
Fix: forbid any representation used to guide variation, regardless of fidelity, or define the relevant blindness in terms of use rather than fidelity.

**36. The witness cannot choose the description, but no formal identification is given.**
Quote: “the population, the operator and the survival condition are the ones the module identifies as enacted in \(\Sigma_h\) before the survivor’s occurrence: a witness describes events, it does not choose among descriptions.”
Why it fails: the module supplies Inst and execution families, not populations and survival conditions. There is no formal criterion for “the ones the module identifies”. The claim blocks gerrymandering by fiat, not by definition.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define population and survival condition from the module’s data by causal roles, not by witness description.

**37. Single-run coincidence has no repeatability or statistical condition.**
Quote: “survival as an enacted condition whose coincidence with fidelity on \(H\) in the run is the recorded fact”.
Why it fails: a single run can produce accidental coincidence. There is no requirement of repeatability, robustness, or statistical significance.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require coincidence across multiple runs or a causal mechanism linking fidelity to survival.

**38. The aggregate permission does not define failure magnitude.**
Quote: “a function of the multiset of failure magnitudes alone”.
Why it fails: “failure magnitude” is undefined. If magnitude is computed from the state and target value, the process must have access to those to compute it. The caveat “with no access to which state failed” is not enforced.
Severity: serious.
Fix: formally define failure magnitude as a scalar invariant under state relabelings, and require the aggregate to be non-invertible given the process’s other data.

1. **Attacked passage:** “the finite history \(H\) of *all* edit–boundary pairs within \(C_t\) — the contract on which \(t\)'s content is claimed — that occurred as events of \(\Sigma_h\), non-empty and not chosen”.  
**Why it fails:** \(H\) is *not* all encountered pairs simpliciter. It is all encountered pairs **within \(C_t\)**, and \(C_t\) is the contract on which the content is claimed. Since \(C_t\) is declarable/claimed, the author can shrink or expand it after the run and thereby shrink or expand \(H\). The words “not chosen” are false at the level that matters: \(H\) is chosen through \(C_t\).  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** define \(H\) relative to an independently fixed \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) or a pre-run contract; require the contract to be fixed before the selection history; record exclusions; or require \(C_t=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

2. **Attacked passage:** “the witness records the checkable fact that, in this population and this history, the members that survived were exactly the members faithful on \(H\)”.  
**Why it fails:** The survival condition is not independently specified. Its only recorded content is the post hoc coincidence with fidelity on \(H\). If \(H\) can be made small through \(C_t\), any survival condition can be made to coincide. This makes “selection” gerrymanderable.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** specify the survival condition independently before the run, and require \(H\) to be fixed independently of that coincidence.

3. **Attacked passage:** “which **eliminated at least one member** that differed from \(t\) at a pair of \(H\) on which that member failed and \(t\) did not”.  
**Why it fails:** Given the previous clause that survivors were exactly the members faithful on \(H\), \(t\) is faithful on \(H\). Any eliminated member that failed on \(H\) automatically satisfies “\(t\) did not” fail there. The “differed from \(t\)” condition does little work unless “differed” means a component-relation difference rather than merely an answer difference.  
**Severity:** minor to serious.  
**Fix:** require the eliminated rival to differ in an actual component relation at a recorded pair of \(H\), not merely in answer.

4. **Attacked passage:** “**Non-triviality:** no event of \(\Sigma_h\) instantiates an organization held to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition by a faithful transport.”  
**Why it fails:** \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition is a condition. “Held to” is defined for contents/organizations, not for these objects. The clause is a type error and cannot be checked as written.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** state the forbidden object precisely, e.g. “no event instantiates an organization that represents the selection process, \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition”; define the representation relation used.

5. **Attacked passage:** “no itemised representation of a defect … lies on any active route, at the grain at which the route is described, to the event that produced \(t\)'s occurrence”.  
**Why it fails:** “Active route” is defined in Part IX as a subnetwork joining “an occurrence that instantiates the input organization … to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the instantiated distinction under the declared contrasts.” Neither “operative result” nor “declared contrasts” is defined in Part IX or listed as formal in Part XIV. So blindness is not well-defined.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** define “operative result” and “declared contrasts”, or replace “active route” by a fully specified causal path in the module.

6. **Attacked passage:** “an *aggregate* summary — a function of the multiset of failure magnitudes alone, with no access to which state failed, such as how many failures and how large — is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.”  
**Why it fails:** Being a function of the multiset of failure magnitudes does not entail “no access to which state failed.” If the magnitude assigned to a state is injective or otherwise state-discriminating, the aggregate can encode the itemised failure state. The parenthetical “with no access” is not enforced by the formal condition.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** require magnitudes to be state-symmetric, or restrict permitted aggregates to statistics invariant under permutations of states, e.g. count and sum of scalar magnitudes that do not identify states.

7. **Attacked passage:** “no itemised **representation** of a defect — an occurrence instantiating a record that pairs the state at which fidelity failed with the value the target took there”.  
**Why it fails:** Representation is derived in (R) and requires a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance. Mere instantiation of a record is not representation. If the phrase is read as representation in the (R) sense, blindness becomes circular with Sel because Sel uses blindness. If it is read as mere instantiation, it is not a condition about representation at all.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** replace “representation” with “record occurrence” if instantiation is intended, or define the relevant representation independently of Sel/Con.

8. **Attacked passage:** Blindness forbids an itemised defect record on the route to the event that produced \(t\)'s occurrence; Build requires “the record is used, in the sense of Part IX, in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept”.  
**Why it fails:** A physical process can have an itemised record on the production route but not use it at acceptance, or use it but fail to have the binding survive. Such a process is not selected (blindness fails), not constructed (Build fails), and not declared (it has a physical history). The three provenances are therefore not exhaustive.  
**Severity:** fatal to the claimed trichotomy.  
**Fix:** add a fourth status, or make blindness about use rather than mere presence, or make Build require only presence plus some other separation.

9. **Attacked passage:** “**Copying** is a content-preserving transfer with its own witness; **descent** is a chain of selections each of whose populations contains the previous survivor; both are historical kinds”.  
**Why it fails:** A copied transport has a physical witness—the copying event—but it is not selected (no variation/elimination), not constructed (Build excludes content-preserving transfers), and not declared. The provenance rule only recognises selected, constructed, and declared. So a copied transport can have no provenance under the stated rule.  
**Severity:** fatal to the claimed exhaustiveness of provenance.  
**Fix:** add copying/descent provenance, or state inheritance rules assigning the source’s provenance to copies unless construction intervenes.

10. **Attacked passage:** “If they are of both kinds, \(t\) has **mixed** provenance, which is declared as such and does not count as constructed for (G).”  
**Why it fails:** Part IV says there are three provenances: selected, constructed, declared. Mixed is a fourth. Worse, the definitions of Sel and Con do not exclude each other. If both witnesses exist, Sel(t) and Con(t) can both hold unless Con explicitly requires no selection witness. “Does not count as constructed for (G)” leaves ambiguous whether mixed counts as selected for (R).  
**Severity:** fatal to the separator and to the trichotomy.  
**Fix:** define mixed’s effect on Sel, Con, and (R); or make Con require the absence of a selection witness on the same route.

11. **Attacked passage:** “A selection witness is blind on the route to the event that produced \(t\)'s occurrence; a construction witness has the defect record used on that same route, at the acceptance of the binding.”  
**Why it fails:** Build does not require the record to be on the route to the event that produced \(t\)'s occurrence. It requires the record on the route to the **acceptance of the binding**. If generation is blind and acceptance later uses the record, selection can hold on the production route and construction can hold on the acceptance route. “Same route” is asserted, not derived.  
**Severity:** fatal to the claimed exclusivity.  
**Fix:** define “the event that produced \(t\)'s occurrence” to include acceptance, or require the record to lie on the production route itself.

12. **Attacked passage:** “Provenance is thereby indexed to the grain of the route, as everything else is indexed to a grain.”  
**Why it fails:** The same physical route can be described at a coarse grain where no itemised defect record is instantiated, and at a fine grain where the itemised record is instantiated and used. Then Sel can hold at the coarse grain and Con at the fine grain. The minimal-witness rule does not compare witnesses across grains.  
**Severity:** fatal to the separator.  
**Fix:** fix a canonical admissible grain for provenance, or require all provenance witnesses to be taken at the same grain.

13. **Attacked passage:** “Among the witnesses whose events produced the occurrence that carries \(t\), take those that are \(\prec_h\)-minimal (an event of a witness precedes an event of another).”  
**Why it fails:** Sel, Con, and Dec are predicates, not witness objects. No formal definition of “witness” as an event set is supplied. Therefore “witnesses whose events produced” and minimality over witnesses are not well-defined.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** define a witness as an event set satisfying a predicate and specify the production relation from witness sets to occurrences.

14. **Attacked passage:** “(an event of a witness precedes an event of another)”.  
**Why it fails:** If a witness has multiple events, A can have an event before B and B can have an event before A. The relation is neither antisymmetric nor transitive. “Minimal witness” may be undefined or multiple in incomparable ways.  
**Severity:** serious.  
**Fix:** define a witness’s time as its earliest event under a well-founded order, or compare witness sets by their minimal events under a total order.

15. **Attacked passage:** Part IX says a history has “the acyclic precedence \(\prec_h\) supplied by the physical module”.  
**Why it fails:** Acyclic does not imply well-founded. An infinite descending chain of events leaves no \(\prec_h\)-minimal witnesses. Then the minimal-witness rule has no minimal witnesses and provenance is undefined.

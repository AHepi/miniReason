## Findings against Part VII and the attack-(B) treatment

### Finding 1 — Absence is not deletion; full relation cannot stand for a rival structure

**Quote attacked:** “The rival's supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O).” (Part VII, “Explanations that remove structure”)

**Why it fails:** In (O), a deleted component imposes the full relation, i.e. no constraint. A rival explanatory structure — a causal path, a mass term, an epicycle, a selection bias — is a *non-full* constraint. If the rival is absent, there is no active component in \(E\) to anchor. If it is “deleted,” then (F1) forces the rival component’s relation to equal the projection of a full relation, hence to be full, so it cannot carry the specific rival effect that the eliminative explanation is supposed to exclude. The treatment conflates “not present” with “present but unconstrained.”

**Seriousness:** Fatal for the stated treatment of eliminative explanation.

**Fix:** Define a counterfactual extension \(D^X\) with the rival component present and its specific relation, and require an extended transport \(t^X:D^X\to E^X\) whose restriction to \(D\) is \(t\). Or define absence as a property of the space of organizations, not as a component relation.

---

### Finding 2 — Part II does not allow edits that introduce new ports or components

**Quote attacked:** “a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them.” (Part VII)

**Why it fails:** Part II defines \(D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L)\). \(V\) and \(J\) are fixed. Edits supply \(L_j(a,b)\) for existing \(j\). The only deletion operation in (O) imposes the full relation; it does not create a new component. There is no operation in Part II for adding new ports or new components. Therefore “edits that would introduce them” is not an edit on \(D\) in the sense of (O), and \(C\subseteq A\times B\) cannot contain it. The proposed eliminative treatment is not well-formed under the formal definition.

**Seriousness:** Serious to fatal; it invalidates the mechanism by which Part VII claims to represent “why is there no \(X\)-effect?”

**Fix:** Add an explicit organization-extension operation, or allow \(J\) and \(V\) to vary under edits with port creation/deletion. Alternatively, formulate eliminative explanation as a comparison between two organizations, not as a single contract on \(D\).

**Findings against attack (B).** I take the exposure at the end of Part VII at face value: eliminative, non-causal, and question-changing explanations are the test case. The following are specific failures in Part VII’s treatment, with concrete counterexamples where useful.

---

### 1. The “deleted subnetwork / full relation” treatment does not represent absence at all

> “The rival's supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O).”

**Claim attacked:** Eliminative explanation is captured by treating the absent rival structure as a deleted subnetwork whose relation is full.

**Why it fails:** A full relation is not absence. By (O), a deleted component still sits in \(J\) and imposes the full relation on its ports. Its signature is the signature of a vacuous constraint, not of a negative existential. A genuine eliminative explanation often asserts: “there is no component with such-and-such signature.” That content has no anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\). If \(E\) omits the X-component, the absence is not an active component and (F1) cannot check it. If \(E\) includes a component asserting “no X,” its relation is not the projection of a full relation in \(D\). Example: “Why is there no ether drag?” The explanation asserts that no ether component exists. The semantics can only say the ether component has full relation or that the question is wrong. Neither is the explanatory content.

**Seriousness:** Fatal for eliminative necessity.

**Fix:** Extend the formalism with negative facts or higher-order signature non-existence, or define eliminative account as a transport whose domain lacks any component with the rival signature under a contract containing the relevant edits. That is not (F1).

---

### 2. Symmetry explanations have no anchor under (F1)

> “An account is faithful when introducing those components changes the answer in \(E\) as it does in \(D\), and their absence leaves both unchanged.”

**Claim attacked:** Symmetry explanations are captured by this treatment.

**Why it fails:** Take the genuine explanation of “why is there no net force on a charge at the centre of a uniform spherical shell?” The explanation is: spherical symmetry forbids a preferred direction; force is a vector; hence \(F=0\). The explanatory component is a global invariance of the whole mass configuration. It is not a subnetwork of \(D\). If \(\lambda(k)\) is the shell-element subnetwork, \(D\)’s local Coulomb components admit asymmetric perturbations under any contract that includes the contrasts needed to explain the absence; the projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}\) is not the symmetry relation. If \(\lambda(k)=D\), the projection is all solutions of \(D\), again not the symmetry relation. If the contract excludes symmetry-breaking edits, non-vacuity fails because the active commitments matter under exactly those changes. Thus no transport satisfies (F1) under any physically admitted contract that contains the contrasts the explanation uses.

**Seriousness:** Fatal for symmetry/non-causal necessity.

**Fix:** Allow global symmetry constraints as components without local anchors, or modify (F1) to permit quotienting by symmetry groups.

---

### 3. Obstruction/invariant explanations are only sketched, not captured

> “For allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) with \(zRz'\Rightarrow I(z)=I(z')\), no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1)”

**Claim attacked:** Obstruction explanations are captured by (O1).

**Why it fails:** \(I\) is a global function on states, not a subset of \(\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) for a subnetwork. If \(E\) has a component for \(I\), (F1) requires an anchor \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\). If \(\lambda(k)=D\), the projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) onto the relevant ports is not the graph of \(I\) unless \(D\) already contains \(I\) as a component. If \(D\) already contains \(I\), the explanation is read off the target, not captured as an explanatory organization. Part VII gives no construction of \(I\) as an active component with an anchor. Example: the parity invariant in “23 indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways.” The invariant is a whole-state fact, not a local constraint.

**Seriousness:** Serious. The section does not discharge the burden of showing (E).

**Fix:** Add higher-order components over state-transition relations, or allow invariants as components over the whole state space with non-local anchors.

---

### 4. “Changing a dimension” and “removing an axiom” are not well-typed edits

> “Removing an axiom, dropping a constraint, or changing a dimension are edits on an organization, and fidelity under them is well-defined.”

But Part II defines:

> “For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies \(L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\).”

**Claim attacked:** Such changes are edits on the same organization \(D\).

**Why it fails:** In \(D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L)\), the sets \(V\), \(J\), and each footprint \(V_j\) are fixed. \(L_j(a,b)\) is defined on a fixed \(V_j\). An edit that changes dimension or removes an axiom changes \(V\), \(J\), or the language of components. That is not an edit on \(D\) as defined; it is a change of \(D\). The skew-symmetric example’s “remove oddness” is exactly such an edit. Therefore the contract in Part VII is not well-typed under Part II.

**Seriousness:** Fatal for mathematical why-questions that use dimension or axiom changes.

**Fix:** Define a category of organizations with variable \(V\) and \(J\), or restrict contracts to edits that preserve the signature.

---

### 5. The skew-symmetric Leibniz example does not show the cancellation is a component

> “The full Leibniz expansion with skewness substituted satisfies (F1) and (F2): every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away.”

**Claim attacked:** The Leibniz expansion satisfies (F1) and (F2).

**Why it fails:** The explanatory step is the pairing of permutations \(\pi\) and \(\pi^{-1}\) with opposite signs. That is a relation between two distinct products, not a valuation on a single subnetwork. (F1)’s \(L_j\) is a subset of \(\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\). A component cannot have two disjoint footprints unless you allow a relation between components, which the formalism does not. If the component is assigned the whole matrix, (F1) requires the projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) (all skew-symmetric matrices) onto those ports to equal the pairing relation. That is false: \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) contains all skew-symmetric matrices, not the pairing relation. The example asserts (F1) without showing how the combinatorial pairing becomes a component or a global constraint in \(E\).

**Seriousness:** Serious. The example is the document’s own non-causal test case and it does not close the gap.

**Fix:** Allow components that are relations between components, or give an explicit (F2)-only construction that forces the cancellation.

### 6. Statistical/selection-artefact explanations have no anchor in the target system

> “An account is faithful when introducing those components changes the answer in \(E\) as it does in \(D\), and their absence leaves both unchanged.”

**Claim attacked:** Statistical and selection-artefact explanations are captured.

**Why it fails:** Example: “Why is there no design in biology?” The explanation is a selection artefact: variation plus selection plus a population. The explanatory organization \(E\) contains a measure over variation and a selection process. That measure is not a subnetwork of the actual organism or population \(D\). (F1) requires every active component of \(E\) to anchor to a subnetwork of \(D\). If \(D\) is the actual population, the measure has no anchor. If \(D\) is enlarged to include the ensemble and measure, the explanation becomes circular. The same problem arises for “why is there no arrow of time in fundamental physics?” when the answer is a statistical selection effect from a low-entropy past.

**Seriousness:** Serious to fatal for statistical/selection-artefact necessity.

**Fix:** Allow transports between ensembles or measures, not only suborganizations of a single target system.

### 7. Question-changing explanations are not captured as answers to the original question

> “A question of the form ‘why is there no \(X\)-effect?’ has a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them.”

**Claim attacked:** Question-changing eliminative explanations are captured.

**Why it fails:** Many eliminative explanations — no ether, no phlogiston, no caloric — work by showing that the question presupposes an entity that has no physical realization. There is no physically admitted edit that “introduces” the rival structure, because the structure is not physically admitted. Part V requires \(C\subseteq\) physically admitted edits. So no such contract exists. The semantics can represent the question as wrong (Part III), but then the explanation is an account of a different question \(p'\), not of the original \(p\). Derivation 5 handles question-finding as a new contract, but it does not capture the explanatory relation to the original question. The original “why no \(X\)” explanation therefore cannot be captured as an account of \(p\).

**Seriousness:** Serious. It shows the semantics has no representation of “the original question was ill-posed, and that is why there is no \(X\)-effect” as an answer to that question.

**Fix:** Distinguish “not physically admitted” from “excluded by stated scope,” and allow meta-level explanations whose target is the ill-posedness of the original contract.

---

### 8. Non-circular dependence is trivialised by full placeholder components

> “There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way.”

**Claim attacked:** This condition prevents circular eliminative accounts.

**Why it fails:** If the absent structure is a full placeholder in \(\Gamma\), removing it at baseline changes nothing. The only edit that makes it critical is one that also introduces \(X\). Then the component is not doing explanatory work at baseline; the contract is. Any organization can add a full placeholder for every absent effect and satisfy non-circular dependence under the counterfactual edit. This does not capture the explanatory content of “there is no \(X\)-effect because \(X\) is impossible.”

**Seriousness:** Serious. It makes eliminative accounts too cheap.

**Fix:** Require the absent-structure component to be active at baseline as a negative constraint, or reformulate non-circular dependence to test removal under the baseline.

---

### 9. Derivation 5’s claim that a contract is an organization is not established

> “A contract is a subset of \(A\times B\) together with \(\mathcal Q\). Give it ports (the edits and their boundaries), components (the closure conditions), and admitted edits (add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)). It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O).”

**Claim attacked:** Contracts can be contents and can be constructed.

**Why it fails:** A contract has no value domains for edits or \(\mathcal Q\); it is a set of pairs plus a function. “Alter \(\mathcal Q\)” changes the query operation, which is part of \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,\dots)\), not an edit on \(D\). The proof hand-waves the typing. This matters for question-changing explanations, which are one of the hard cases in attack (B).

**Seriousness:** Serious. The claimed representation of question-finding is not formally grounded.

**Fix:** Define a formal syntax for contracts and \(\mathcal Q\) as organizations, or drop the claim that they are organizations in the sense of (O).

---

### 10. The skew-symmetric example’s contrasts change the family, not the organization

> “Contract: remove skewness; remove oddness; remove both; field arithmetic and determinant–invertibility held fixed.”

**Claim attacked:** These are edits on \(D\).

**Why it fails:** Removing skewness from an odd-order matrix gives an arbitrary odd-order matrix — a different family with different constraints. Removing oddness changes the dimension. These are not edits on the same organization \(D\); they change the set of matrices. The account \(E\) is supposed to answer under \(\tau(a)\), but the formalism does not specify how \(E\) changes when the family changes. This is another reason the example does not demonstrate capture.

**Seriousness:** Fatal for that example.

**Fix:** Parameterise a family of matrices and define edits as changes in parameters, not changes in defining properties or dimension.

---

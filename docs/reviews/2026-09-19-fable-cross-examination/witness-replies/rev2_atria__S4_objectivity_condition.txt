## Findings

**1. "Evaluable" is not determined by the data; the transport's domain outside *C* is undeclared, and the two available readings give opposite results.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part IV: "A transport … is $t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E$ where $\pi:X_D\to X_E$ on the stated scope, $\tau:A_D\to A_E$ translates edits, $\sigma$ translates boundaries", together with Part V's "$\tau(a)$, $\sigma(b)$ and the query are all defined there" and "a pair on which the candidate has no translation". (2) Why it fails — $\pi$ is explicitly partial ("on the stated scope"), while $\tau,\sigma$ are written in total-function arrow notation. If $\tau,\sigma$ are total on $A_D,B_D$, then "the candidate has no translation" is impossible and the silence clause is dead code. If they are partial, their domain is nowhere declared (not in Part IV, not in Part XIV's declared-parameters list) and "evaluable" is not a fact about the candidate but about how the modeller wrote $t$. (3) Serious — potentially fatal for the condition's stated purpose. (4) Fix — declare the domain of $\tau,\sigma$ as a declared parameter in Part XIV with recorded reasons (mirroring $S$), or require totality on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ for any objectivity claim and replace transport-silence by an *organizational* silence (no edit of $A_E$ and no boundary of $B_E$ can serve as translation, witnessed by $E$'s presented structure, not by omission).

**2. Under the partial reading the condition is vacuous, and vacuity is achievable by declaration.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V: "$C$ is protective when there is an admitted pair $(a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C$ on which the candidate is *evaluable* — $\tau(a)$, $\sigma(b)$ and the query are all defined there". (2) Why it fails — Part III's coherent-posing clause requires only "$\mathcal Q$ is defined on every pair in $C$", and $\mathcal Q$ is a declared parameter. So a question whose $\mathcal Q$ is defined exactly on $C$ is admissible, and then *every* candidate is non-evaluable on *every* excluded admitted pair, hence non-protective automatically. The same escape works by truncating $\tau$ or $\sigma$. Objectivity is then satisfiable by declaration, which inverts the whole point of the second index. (3) Fatal as stated. (4) Fix — require, for an objectivity claim, that $\mathcal Q$ be defined on all of $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ ("admittedly evaluable question") and that $\tau,\sigma$ be total on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$; state both in Part III and Part XIV.

**3. The existential form of (E) plus the silence clause makes non-protection satisfiable by re-presentation.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V: "$\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\iff\exists t,\Gamma\ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)$", read with the objectivity claim as a conjunction over the witnessing $t$. (2) Why it fails — given any account whose transport is total and wrong at an excluded pair $(a^*,b^*)$, define $t'$ to agree with $t$ on $C$ and be undefined elsewhere. (F1), (F2), (A) quantify over $C$ only; non-circularity and non-vacuity do not depend on $\tau$'s domain outside $C$; $\tau(1)=1$ and the homomorphism clause are checked only on $C$. So $t'$ witnesses account-hood and is silent at $(a^*,b^*)$. Under the named-$t$ reading (via $\mathcal E$) the modeller simply presents the truncated $\mathcal E'$. (3) Serious. (4) Fix — require the transport named in an objectivity claim to be *maximal* (no extension within $A_E,B_E$ preserves the monoid structure and the anchor map), or define silence organizationally so that truncation is not a legitimate silence.

**4. Grievance 4 (Part 0) was not updated and now overclaims.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part 0, Grievance 4: "a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's, or the reverse … A contract that quietly excludes such a change is caught by the requirement that exclusions be stated, and then by the adequacy condition, whether or not anyone supplies the change." (2) Why it fails — (i) the Grievance states protection *without* the evaluable clause and *without* the silence clause, i.e. stronger than Part V implements; (ii) "the adequacy condition" cannot be the catcher, since (E) ranges over $C$ only and $K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing$; if "adequacy" means the anchored grade, its condition 3 applies only "where the translation is defined", so silence escapes it too; (iii) "whether or not anyone supplies the change" is exactly what the silence clause withdraws. (3) Serious — this is the document's advertised answer to the relativism objection and it no longer matches the mechanism. (4) Fix — rewrite Grievance 4 to include the evaluable clause, the silence clause, the module-index relativity, and to concede that only evaluable-and-wrong exclusions are caught.

**5. Ill-posed case: evaluable translation but undefined/empty/multi-valued answer.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V's inequality "$\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)$" as the sole test. (2) Why it fails — if $\tau,\sigma$ are defined at an excluded pair but $\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\varnothing$ or has several solutions (Part II: "Several solutions remain several"), $\operatorname{Ans}_E$ may be undefined or outside $Y_p$. Then the inequality is undefined, so "protective" has no truth value; and the silence clause does not cover it, since silence is defined as missing translation only. (3) Serious. (4) Fix — add an explicit clause: evaluable-but-answer-undefined, empty, or non-unique at an excluded admitted pair counts as protection (or separately as failing the objectivity claim).

**6. Target-side undefinedness is misattributed to the candidate.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V's "evaluable — $\tau(a)$, $\sigma(b)$ and the query are all defined there", followed by "Silence about a change — a pair on which the candidate has no translation — is a stated limit of scope". (2) Why it fails — the query is target-side ($\mathcal Q$ on $D$), yet its undefinedness is folded into the candidate-side "evaluable" predicate and then reported as the *candidate's* silence. A question that does not admit a change is reported as a limit of the candidate's scope. This is a category error and a second declaration-driven escape (restrict $\mathcal Q$, get silence). (3) Serious. (4) Fix — split the predicate: candidate-evaluable ($\tau,\sigma$ and $\mathcal Q_E$) vs question-admissible ($\mathcal Q$ defined at $(a,b)$); let question-side non-admissibility fail or restrict the *objectivity claim*, not excuse the candidate.

**7. Too strong in extension and misnamed: any divergence counts, including the candidate being better.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V: "being wrong about a change the scope hides is protection", and Grievance 4's "or the reverse". (2) Why it fails — the test is symmetric inequality of pulled-back answers, so a candidate that diverges from the target on an excluded pair because the *target* is deficient there is protective, and a legitimately regime-restricted model (valid in the range the question asks about, divergent on admitted pairs outside it) fails its objectivity claim. The condition is really "$C$ is not answer-divergent for this transport", which is not the same as "$C$ hides a defect". (3) Minor-to-serious (naming and rhetoric overclaim; the mechanism is otherwise coherent). (4) Fix — either rename the condition ("answer-invariance of the scope") or restrict protection to divergences where the candidate is the defective side, and say that a regime-restricted account fails the objectivity claim while retaining account-hood.

**8. The check is answer-level only, so the document's own (F1) lesson is not carried outside *C*.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V's objectivity condition vs Part V's "(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition." (2) Why it fails — outside $C$ the condition compares only $\operatorname{Ans}_E$ with $\operatorname{Ans}_p$; no (F1)- or (F2)-analogue is required on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C$. A candidate with correct answers and a wrong decomposition on excluded admitted pairs is non-protective. (3) Serious. (4) Fix — extend the objectivity condition to require component-relation agreement on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ wherever the translation is defined (which is what the anchored grade's world-kind does per-component but not at assembly level), or state explicitly that objectivity is answer-level and that decomposition-level objectivity is covered nowhere.

**9. Anchoring does not entail non-protection, and world-kind's hedge shares the same hole.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V, anchored condition 3: "fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits, *where the translation is defined*." (2) Why it fails — world-kind is per-component signature coincidence on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$; (F2) and (A) are required only on $C$. Signature coincidence on all of $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ does not entail assembly-level or answer-level agreement off $C$ (Derivation 1 preserves signatures, not solution sets). So an anchored, evaluable-everywhere account can still be protective. And the hedge makes world-kind vacuous under the truncation of Finding 3. The document never says whether anchoring is meant to entail non-protection. (3) Serious. (4) Fix — state the non-entailment explicitly, or make the objectivity condition a conjunct of the anchored grade with the totality requirement of Finding 1.

**10. Collision with Part VII: the reversed-calculation verdict presupposes exactly what the formal hedge permits escaping.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII: "it is not anchored as a production account, because on the production edits — admitted, and outside the identification contract — its component assigning $H$ is not of one forward kind with the target's assignment of $L$." (2) Why it fails — for that comparison to be evaluable, $\tau$ must be defined on the production edits, i.e. on admitted pairs outside the contract. Condition 3's "where the translation is defined" lets the reversed calculation declare $\tau$ undefined there and pass. The example's conclusion depends on an assumption the formalism does not enforce. (3) Serious. (4) Fix — require $\tau$ total on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ for anchored accounts (or at least on the production edits in this example), and note that the verdict is conditional on that requirement.

**11. Collision with Part VIII's historical index: objectivity claims have index constituents Part VIII does not cover.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VIII: "A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract", and Part V: "The claim is indexed to the module in force: a later module with a larger $A_\Theta$ makes it a new claim at a new index (Part VIII)". (2) Why it fails — Part VIII's identity result is stated for claims indexed to a *contract*. An objectivity claim is indexed to $(C,\Theta,\text{transport})$, and the transport's domain is not listed as an index constituent anywhere (Part XIV lists $C,S,K,\ell,\beta,\Omega,\mathcal Q$ — not $\operatorname{dom}\tau$). So invoking Part VIII for objectivity claims is unlicensed by Part VIII as stated. Consequently a passing objectivity claim keeps its truth value at its index even if the transport is later extended and shown wrong — the historical index freezes the false positive. (3) Minor-to-serious. (4) Fix — extend Part VIII to say that objectivity claims are indexed to contract, module, and transport domain, and that the transport domain must be declared at the claim's index.

**12. Derivation 7's closing sentence overclaims an inter-index power for an intra-index condition.**
(1) Claim attacked — Derivation 7: "Whether a recorded change was made to protect a candidate is a separate question, and the objectivity condition of Part V answers it only where the candidate is evaluable and wrong on the excluded change." (2) Why it fails — the objectivity condition compares answers within one index; a recorded contract change produces, by Part VIII and Part III, a new claim at a new index. Detecting that a change *was made to protect* a candidate is an inter-index, provenance-and-motive question; the objectivity condition evaluates answer-difference, not motive, and Part X's provenance machinery is not used by it. (3) Minor-to-serious. (4) Fix — delete "answers it", and say that protective goalpost-moving is detected, if at all, by the provenance record plus an explicitly inter-index claim, not by the objectivity condition.

**13. Part XV's concession under-describes the hole it concedes.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part XV: "A scope that excludes a change on which the candidate is *silent* is a stated limit and not a counterexample." (2) Why it fails — silence is not given an independent witness; it is the absence of transport data, and Findings 1–3 show it is constructible and even automatic. So the dismissal is unearned: the listed attack ("evaluable and wrong") is the easy case, and the case waved away is the one the condition actually cannot handle. (3) Serious. (4) Fix — either supply the organizational witness for silence of Finding 1, or move "silence-by-declaration" into Part XV as an open attack alongside the existing one.

**14. Quantifier inconsistency between Part III and Part V.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part III: "the objectivity condition of Part V ranges over $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$" vs Part V: "there is an admitted pair $(a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C$". (2) Why it fails — on the literal Part III reading the condition also checks admitted pairs inside $C$, where account-hood already guarantees agreement (harmless but redundant); on the Part V reading it does not. The two are not equivalent as scopes. (3) Cosmetic. (4) Fix — make Part III read "$C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C$", or explain that ranging over $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ is harmless because $C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap C$ is already covered by (A).

**15. The condition is a leaf in the dependence order, which raises the cost of Findings 1–4.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part XIV: "objectivity depends on (E) and $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$" — and nothing else. (2) Why it fails — no defined predicate depends on non-protection: (EK) requires Account, Deploy, Origin, ProducesVia, but not non-protection. So the condition's only role is to answer Grievance 4, precisely where Findings 1–4 locate the failures. This is not an inconsistency, but it means the condition is load-bearing for the anti-relativism claim and inert elsewhere. (3) Minor (structural). (4) Fix — either feed non-protection into (EK) or the anchored grade, or state plainly that objectivity is a standalone claim with no downstream role.

**16. The mathematical example can have only a vacuous objectivity claim.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII's odd-order skew-symmetric example, whose admitted edits are "remove the skewness constraint; remove the oddness constraint; remove both", with the contrasts checked apparently exhausting them. (2) Why it fails — if $C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ there, then $C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=\varnothing$ and non-protection is vacuous; the example's adequacy is then entirely relative to the declared admission relation with no objectivity content. The text does not note this. (3) Cosmetic. (4) Fix — say explicitly whether $S$ is a proper subset of the declared admission in this example, and that if not, the objectivity conjunct is vacuous there.

**17. $\sigma$ has no declared domain or codomain at all.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part IV: "$\sigma$ translates boundaries". (2) Why it fails — $\pi$'s scope is qualified, $\tau$ has arrow notation, $\sigma$ has neither; yet Part V's "evaluable" requires $\sigma(b)$ defined at excluded boundaries, and boundary-side silence ($B_E$ lacking an analogue) is a live escape on exactly the same axis as the edit-side one. (3) Minor. (4) Fix — state $\sigma:B_D\to B_E$ with the same totality/maximality requirement as $\tau$.

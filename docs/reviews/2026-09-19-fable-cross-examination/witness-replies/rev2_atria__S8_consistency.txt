## A. Transport orientation and the query translation

**1. (F2) and (A) cannot be evaluated on counterfactual pairs, yet are required there.**
(1) Part V requires (F2) and (A) "for every \((a,b)\in C\)", and \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\). But Part IV defines \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) "on the stated scope", and Part III has \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\), hence \(K\cap S=\varnothing\). So \(\pi\) (and with it \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]\) and \(\pi^{-1}[\operatorname{Sol}_E]\)) is undefined on every pair of \(K\). Part VII then asserts of the Leibniz-expansion account that it "satisfies (F1) and (F2)" and "satisfies (A)" on a contract whose contrasts are exactly the removal edits — which, for a mathematical organization, live in the declared admission relation and are not in \(S\).
(2) The definitions of (F2), (A) and of the translated query do not cover the counterfactual part of the contract, while the examples and Part VII's eliminative case ("the edit belongs to \(K\), declared with its meaning … and the account is faithful on it in the same sense") presuppose that they do.
(3) Serious.
(4) Require each counterfactual pair in \(K\) to be declared together with an extension of \(\pi\) (and of the query translation), or restrict (F2)/(A) to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\) and say that on \(K\) only (F1) is assessed — and then downgrade Part VII's claims accordingly.

**2. The "translated query" makes (A) close to vacuous, so (A) cannot do the work Part V assigns it.**
(1) Part V: "The query \(\mathcal Q\) is held fixed; an account of a different query is not an account of this one."
(2) Part IV defines \(\mathcal Q_E(E,\tau(a),\sigma(b)):=\mathcal Q\) evaluated on \(\pi^{-1}[\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))]\) and the components \(\lambda\) anchors. Both arguments are \(D\)-side data: a pullback of \(E\)'s solution set and subnetworks of \(D\). Given (F2) and injectivity of \(\pi\) on solutions, \(\pi^{-1}[\operatorname{Sol}_E]=\operatorname{Sol}_D\), so \(\operatorname{Ans}_E=\operatorname{Ans}_p\) automatically. The "translated query on \(E\)" is thus not a query on \(E\) at all, and the scenario "same transport, different query" is not representable by this definition, since \(\mathcal Q_E\) is always the translated \(p\)-query.
(3) Serious.
(4) Define \(\mathcal Q_E\) genuinely on \(E\)'s own structure (ports, components, solutions) and prove (A) from (F1)–(F2) where it indeed follows, stating the injectivity hypothesis; or keep (A) as a primitive well-formedness condition on \(E\)'s own query and stop claiming it discriminates accounts of different queries.

**3. \(\tau\) is declared total but the objectivity condition needs it partial.**
(1) Part IV: "\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits"; Part III: "\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\) … for a physical organization … \(A_\Theta(o)\times B\)".
(2) A total \(\tau\) on \(A_D\supseteq A_\Theta(o)\) is defined on every admitted pair, so Part V's evaluability clause — "\(\tau(a)\), \(\sigma(b)\) and the query are all defined there" — and its silence provision — "Silence about a change — a pair on which the candidate has no translation — is a stated limit of scope, not protection" — are inapplicable to physical targets. Conversely, if silence is meant to be possible, the function notation is wrong and the objectivity condition has a bypass (a candidate that simply fails to translate the edits on which it is wrong).
(3) Serious.
(4) State whether \(\tau,\pi,\sigma\) are total or partial; if partial, give the well-definedness conditions and re-state the objectivity condition without the dead silence clause; if total, delete the silence clause.

## B. The two-place/four-place Account

**4. The non-circularity witness cannot exist when \(|\Gamma|=1\); the one-component candidate is mis-classified.**
(1) Part V: "The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) for a target with several components satisfies (E): its single commitment is removable, so non-circular dependence has its witness."
(2) The witness requires "a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)". With \(|\Gamma|=1\) there is no nonempty proper subset; and Part V also says "Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing." So the one-component candidate fails non-circular dependence and hence fails (E). This also contradicts Part 0's gloss that an account "does not restate the answer": a single component whose relation is the projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) is exactly a restatement.
(3) Serious (it mis-states what the first grade excludes).
(4) Split the condition: keep the boundary-independence clause ("no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports") as the anti-circular guard, and require the removal witness only for \(|\Gamma|\ge2\), allowing removal of the whole \(\Gamma\) when the answer is not boundary-supplied; then re-derive the one-component example's classification.

**5. The same clause makes every singleton support impossible, breaking Part VI's central examples.**
(1) Part VI: "\(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\): each is contributory, neither indispensable" and "\(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\}\}\): the full candidate fails although a subset succeeds."
(2) \(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p,t|W,W)\}\) applies (E), hence non-circular dependence, to \(E|W\); for \(|W|=1\) no nonempty proper sub-block exists (Finding 4), so \(\{a\},\{b\}\notin\mathsf S\). Both examples are therefore unsatisfiable as written, and the finite monotone theorem's "critical singletons" can only ever arise for \(|W|\ge3\).
(3) Serious.
(4) Repair the witness clause as in Finding 4, or define \(\mathsf S\) with a weakened non-circularity condition for restrictions and say so.

**6. Arity is used loosely where it matters.**
(1) Part VI writes "\(\mathsf S_{E,p}\)" while stating "the transport and commitments are the fixed ones of \(\mathcal E\), not existentially re-chosen"; Part 0/Part XV ask for a candidate that "satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V)".
(2) \(\mathsf S\) is not a function of \((E,p)\) but of \((E,p,t,\Gamma)\); and the anchored grade is inherently four-place ("a predicate on the same four data, with \(t\) and \(\Gamma\) named"), because properness is a property of \(\lambda\), so "satisfies the anchored grade" is ambiguous between existential and universal quantification over witnesses (the identity transport witnesses it for the target's own copy, but a bad witness could fail it for the same \(E,p\)).
(3) Minor.
(4) Write \(\mathsf S_{\mathcal E}\); state explicitly whether the anchored grade in attack (A)/(F) is existentially witnessed.

## C. Objectivity, Grievance 4, Part VIII

**7. Grievance 4 and Part V disagree about silence.**
(1) Grievance 4: "a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's, or the reverse."
(2) Part V adds an evaluability requirement and explicitly says "Silence about a change … is a stated limit of scope, not protection." Under Grievance 4's disjunction, a candidate with no answer on an excluded pair on which the target's answer moves is protective (the target's answer alters, the candidate's does not); Part V says it is not. The two statements give opposite verdicts on the same case.
(3) Serious.
(4) Rewrite Grievance 4's definition to match Part V, including the evaluability clause, and say plainly that a candidate may be silent on admitted changes and still make an objectivity claim.

**8. Grievance 4 says the adequacy condition catches protective contracts; Part V says it does not.**
(1) Grievance 4: "A contract that quietly excludes such a change is caught by the requirement that exclusions be stated, and then by the adequacy condition, whether or not anyone supplies the change."
(2) Part V: the objectivity condition "is deliberately not a conjunct of (E)". So (E) — the adequacy condition — catches nothing of the kind; only the separately asserted objectivity claim does, and only if it is asserted.
(3) Minor.
(4) Replace "the adequacy condition" with "the objectivity claim" in Grievance 4 and note that the claim is optional.

**9. The objectivity delivered is module-relative, not the answer Grievance 4 advertises.**
(1) Grievance 4 answers "there is no objectivity" with "Two claims, two indices."
(2) Part V: "The claim is indexed to the module in force: a later module with a larger \(A_\Theta\) makes it a new claim at a new index"; Part VIII confirms truth-value fixity only at the claim's own index. So the second conjunct secures non-protection relative to the module currently adopted, which is exactly the relativity the grievance complained about, one level up. Part V states the limit; Grievance 4 does not.
(3) Minor.
(4) Add to Grievance 4: "not protective" means not protective against the module in force, and a better module is a new index, not a discovery about the old one.

## D. The anchored grade and "the mechanism"

**10. Part 0 and Part XV presuppose the very mechanism-hood Part V says is unavailable.**
(1) Part 0: the anchored grade "is what 'the explanation matches the mechanism' means at the module's index"; Part XV attack (F): "A decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on *every* admitted change."
(2) Part V: "the semantics has no predicate that separates a mechanism from its faithful copy, and does not want one", and such a decoy "is, at this index, the mechanism". The definite descriptions in Part 0/Part XV therefore carry a non-index-relative mechanism that the definition repudiates. (Part 0 does say both grades are extensional, so this is a rhetorical inconsistency, not a formal one.)
(3) Minor.
(4) Replace "the mechanism" with "the target's organization at \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\)" throughout the meta-level prose.

**11. The anchored grade does not deliver "fidelity on the full admitted contract".**
(1) Part V: "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution."
(2) The anchored grade = (E) + three conditions. (E)'s (F2) (assembled agreement) holds only on \(C\); condition 3 (World-kind) is *signature* agreement on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which by Derivation 1 is weaker than (F1) and is per-component only. So a decoy whose components match their anchors' signatures on every admitted edit but whose *assembly* fails (F2) on admitted pairs excluded from \(C\) passes the anchored grade — and passes the objectivity condition too, since that condition compares answer profiles, not assemblies. This is precisely the failure mode Part V warns about — "(F2) prevents a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint" — left unguarded on the larger contract.
(3) Serious.
(4) Add a condition requiring (F2) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) where the translation is defined, or state that anchoring is signature-level, not fidelity-level, on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and adjust the gloss.

**12. The forward-kind relation is undefined on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).**
(1) Part V condition 3: "Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor subnetwork."
(2) Part II defines the relation only for a contract \(C\) of a question, and its exclusion is written as a specific pair: "their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\)". \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is not a question's contract and has no designated \(b_0\), so the exclusion is undefined there. (It is rescuable, since \((1,b_0)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\), but only by inheriting the baseline from \(p\), which is not stated.)
(3) Minor (one-line fix, but it affects a load-bearing condition).
(4) Define forward kinds on pairs \((\ell,C,b_0)\) and invoke \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}},b_0(p))\) in condition 3.

## F. Selection, Derivation 3, Derivation 10

**15. Part XV's "non-fallible selected transport" cannot refute Derivation 3.**
(1) Part XV: "A selection history \(H\subsetneq C\), in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off \(H\), whose survivor is determined on \(C\setminus H\). This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis."
(2) Derivation 3's conclusion is a theorem from its hypotheses: under closure, for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there is a surviving rival differing there "whenever some admissible \(L'\) changes that solution set". A survivor determined on \(C\setminus H\) under closure can only mean that no admissible \(L'\) changes any solution set off \(H\), which makes Derivation 3's antecedent vacuous, not its conclusion false. The worst available outcome is that the closure hypothesis is never satisfied in real populations, which is inapplicability, not refutation.
(3) Serious (a defeat condition is stated that cannot defeat).
(4) Restate the attack as: show that no physically real population is closed under pointwise alteration off \(H\), or that Derivation 3's non-guarantee is too weak to support the fallibility gloss.

**16. Derivation 10's stipulation of the selection witness is incomplete.**
(1) Derivation 10: "assume … that it satisfies the clauses of Part IV's selection witness (two or more members, an applied operator, an eliminated rival that differed on an encountered pair, blindness)".
(2) Part IV's witness has two further conjuncts — Non-triviality and the route clause in Blindness — which the parenthetical, presented as a complete list, omits.
(3) Minor.
(4) Add non-triviality (and the route condition) to the parenthetical, or say "the principal clauses".

**17. The two responses in Derivation 10 are mutually exclusive, not two options.**
(1) Derivation 10: "Two responses. **Selection:** … **Construction:** …".
(2) Part IV: "a process with the defect record on its route is not selecting, whatever else it does", and the construction response *requires* "an itemised defect representation … on an active route to the binding". Once that record exists, the blindness clause bars the selection response for the same system; the text presents them as alternative branches off one state.
(3) Minor.
(4) Say the branches are alternatives that pre-empt each other, and that the presence of the itemised record is what selects which is available.

## G. Provenance and the earliest-witness rule

**18. (R) contradicts the prose on what a selected transport represents.**
(1) Part IV: "since a selected transport is guaranteed faithful only on \(H\), what it represents by (R) is the content on the largest contract it is in fact faithful on, which may be smaller than the contract declared for the content."
(2) (R) as written requires "a transport faithful on \(c\)'s contract" — the declared contract. A transport faithful only on a proper subcontract \(H\) fails (R) outright, so by (R) the content is *not represented at all*, not "represented on less than was claimed". Since the primitive-layer transport is "typically selected" and Derivation 3 gives no faithfulness guarantee off \(H\), the paradigm case of representation falls outside (R).
(3) Serious.
(4) Define (R) with faithfulness on the largest sub-contract of \(C_c\) on which \(t\) is in fact faithful, and make the "representation of less than was claimed" clause a definitional consequence rather than a gloss.

**19. "Declared" question provenance is uninstanceable under the witness rule.**
(1) Part III: "\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared, selected, constructed}\}\) … Its witness is the one Part IV gives for the provenance of any content's occurrence: the provenance of the transport by which an occurrence represents the question-organization."
(2) Part IV's content-occurrence provenance is "the provenance of the transport that witnesses (R)", and (R) requires \(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t)\) — declared transports are excluded ("a declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything"). (3) Serious.
(4) Give declared question provenance its own witness (author entry into the model, as Part IV does for transports) instead of routing it through (R).

## I. Informal terms and Derivation 6

**22. The informal-term list is incomplete, contrary to Part 0's completeness claim, and (EK) is not formal.**
(1) Part 0: "It claims that they are all listed"; Part XIV: "Informal terms, listed … Derivation 6 does not cover them."

**23. (EK) is ill-typed for non-organization contents.**
(1) Part XI (EK): "\(\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_c)\) … Here \(E_c\) is the organization of the content \(c\)"; Part X: "The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a question (through its question-organization, Derivation 5 …)."
(2) "The organization of the content" is defined only when \(c\) is an organization. For \(c\) a transport, no organization of \(c\) is defined (a transport is a tuple \((\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\)); for \(c\) a question, \(E_c\) would have to be the question-organization, and then \(\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_c)\) assesses the question-organization as an account of some question \(p_c\) that is never specified. So the predicate defining the knowledge-creation class cannot be evaluated for exactly the case — question-finding — that Part X and Derivation 5 advertise as a creative act on the same footing.
(3) Serious.
(4) Define \(E_c\) and \(p_c\) by cases on the type of \(c\) (organization / transport / question), stating for the question case which question the question-organization is an account of.

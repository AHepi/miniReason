## Part 1 — The (A) counterexample, constructed

### Finding 1 — The definitional mirror: anchored, originated, and explanatory vacuous

**Claim attacked.** Part 0: *"A candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too."* and Part XV: *"Candidates meeting the anchored grade and explaining nothing are already known — the target's own copy, the faithful modular table — and the class does not claim they explain: they fail Origin, and the sufficiency claim is for the conjunction."*

**Why it fails.** The document's entire defence against (A) is that copies fail Origin. That defence assumes copies are not new to the repertoire. But `New` is system-relative:

> $\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c.$

$R_{<e}(s,h)$ is *s's* deployable repertoire. The target $D$ is the world, not a content of $s$; $D\notin R_{<e}(s,h)$ for any system that has never modelled it. So **any system's first faithful copy of the target satisfies `New`**, trivially. The claim "the copy is not new to any repertoire" (Part V) is false in the only reading the formalism supports.

Now build the copy *definitionally*. Let $D$ be a multi-component target at admissible grain $\ell$, with a production question $p$. Let $E$ have one component $k_j$ per component $j$ of $D$, with component relations **defined** as
$$L_{k_j}(\tau(a),\sigma(b)):=\operatorname{proj}_{V_j}\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b).$$
Then:
- (F1) holds *by construction* on every pair where $\tau$ is defined — it is an equality of relations with one side defined as the other.
- (F2) and (A) hold by construction.
- Non-circular dependence holds: with $|\Gamma|\ge2$, removing any single $k_j$ changes the answer profile, and $\{k_j\}$ is a proper sub-block.
- Properness holds (bijection onto $D$'s components, full coverage).
- Resolution holds.
- World-kind holds *by construction*: the signature of $k_j$ on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ is the signature of $j$ on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$, because $L_{k_j}$ was defined to make it so.

So $E$ is **anchored**. Now Origin:
- `New`: $E\equiv_\ell D$, but $D\notin R_{<e}(s,h)$, and if $E$'s decomposition differs from anything $s$ already deploys, no member of the repertoire is $\equiv_\ell$ to it either. New holds.
- `Build`: the binding $k_j\mapsto j$ is nontrivial (not obtainable from $s$'s sensory carriers by $\equiv_\ell$, since those carriers do not carry $D$'s decomposition), required (deleting it breaks (F1)), and the defect record is satisfiable by any debug cycle — or, if $s$ succeeded on its first probe, by $s$ deliberately mis-recording an entry and then correcting it. Nothing in `Build` forbids a manufactured record.
- `Deploy`/`Attempt`: $s$ uses $E$ to answer $p$ on an active route with an unmet obligation before $e$.

So $E$ satisfies the anchored grade **and** Origin. And it plainly explains nothing: its components' relations are *the target's own behaviour re-read as components*. It is "the mechanism because the mechanism."

The document's non-circular condition was written to exclude exactly this — *"moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named 'law' does not discharge this"* — but the operationalization does not implement the intention. The witness is *removability-changing-profile*, and a mirror satisfies that as readily as a real mechanism. Nothing in (F1), (F2), (A), non-circularity, or the three anchored conditions distinguishes a component relation that *matches* the anchor's behaviour from one *defined as* the anchor's behaviour.

**Severity.** Fatal for the sufficiency claim as stated. This is the counterexample Part XV says does not exist.

**Fix.** Add a non-definitionality condition on the anchor map: the relations $L_k$ of active components must be realizable/ computable without reference to $\operatorname{Sol}_D$ — e.g. there must be an organization $E'$ agreeing with $E$ on $C$ whose component relations are fixed by data not containing $\operatorname{Sol}_D$, or $\lambda(k)$ must be verified on a pair where $L_k$ was fixed *before* the anchor's behaviour was known. Also state that `New` must be evaluated against the target's own structure, not only against $s$'s repertoire, or accept that first copies are originative and defend that.

### Finding 2 — "Prediction is not explanation" is not enforced

**Claim attacked.** Part 0: *"It does not say prediction is explanation. Fidelity in this semantics is over component structure under change, not over outputs."*

**Why it fails.** The mirror of Finding 1 has component structure that is a *definable function of the target's outputs*. Because (F1) is an equality of relations, "component fidelity" is satisfiable by output-tracking components. Wherever a behavioural mirror is admissible, component fidelity collapses to output fidelity. The slogan is therefore unsupported by the conditions.

**Severity.** Serious — it removes the document's main defence against the oldest objection in this area.

**Fix.** As Finding 1: a independence-of-definition condition on component relations, or a requirement that the component relations of $E$ be fixed by a source not containing the target's solution set.

### Finding 3 — `Build`'s defect record both over- and under-discriminates

**Claim attacked.** Part X: *"$\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)$ holds when an owned subhistory ... contains an **itemised defect representation** — an occurrence instantiating a record pairing a state at which some prior transport of $s$ failed fidelity with what would have held — on an active route to the binding."*

**Why it fails.** (i) *Over-strong:* a system that posits a correct novel mechanism on its first attempt, new to its repertoire and deployed, satisfies no `Build`, because there is no prior fidelity failure to record. First-attempt success is excluded from the originative class. (ii) *Under-strong:* the record is satisfiable by manufacture — deliberately erring, recording, correcting. The blind/sighted contrast between selection and construction (Part IV) is doing real work, but the same clause applied to `Build` makes creativity contingent on having a debug log.

**Severity.** Serious for the classification; it misclassifies lucky correct novelty and admits fabricated records.

**Fix.** Allow the defect record to be *counterfactual or prospective* (a represented possible failure) rather than requiring an actual prior failure, and add a clause that the record not be produced solely to satisfy the condition — or accept the consequence explicitly and say that first-attempt success is not originative.

### Finding 4 — The anchored grade is compatible with silence on arbitrary admitted changes

**Claim attacked.** Part V, condition 3: *"fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits, **where the translation is defined**."* and the objectivity clause: *"Silence about a change — a pair on which the candidate has no translation — is a stated limit of scope, not protection."*

**Why it fails.** $\tau$ is transport data and nothing requires its domain to be total on $A_D$, or even to be *stated*. A candidate whose $\tau$ is undefined on an admitted change is (i) exempt from world-kind there, and (ii) not "evaluable," hence not protective. So silence is doubly exempt, and it is cheap: a candidate can avoid every objectivity charge by failing to translate. The anchored grade then does not certify a match to the module — it certifies a match to the subset the candidate speaks about.

**Severity.** Serious. "What 'the explanation matches the mechanism' means at the module's index" is weaker than advertised.

**Fix.** Require the domain of $\tau$ to be a declared parameter, require $S$ to state exclusions *including* non-translation, and make objectivity range over pairs where the candidate is silent *and* the module admits a separating change, unless the silence is independently justified.

## Part 2 — Do the three anchored conditions exclude a genuine mechanism?

### Finding 5 — Idealizations are excluded from the anchored grade, and the document never says so

**Claim attacked.** Part V, condition 3 (world-kind) and Part V's summary: *"It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose."*

**Why it fails.** An idealized model (frictionless plane, ideal gas, rational actor) deviates from its anchor on some admitted pair. Signature coincidence on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ then fails, so world-kind fails. At the lower grade, the idealized model is an account at $C$ only if $C$ excludes the deviating pairs — and then it is protective (evaluable and wrong) unless $\tau$ is undefined there, in which case it is merely silent. So **every idealized explanation is either non-anchored, non-objective, or silent.** Large classes of genuine explanation are demoted or excluded, and Part XV's list of open cases never mentions idealization.

**Severity.** Serious — an unremarked exclusion of a central explanatory strategy.

**Fix.** Add a notion of *scoped idealization*: a component whose relation is declared to approximate the anchor's on a stated sub-contract, with the anchored grade relativized to that sub-contract and the deviation explicitly recorded as a limit, not as protection.

### Finding 6 — Properness excludes partial and non-decompositional explanations

**Claim attacked.** Part V, condition 1: *"the anchors of the active components refine a cover of the components of $D$ that some pair in $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ touches, and the anchor map is injective on that cover ... no touched component of $D$ lies outside every anchor."*

**Why it fails.** Coverage forces every touched component of $D$ to be anchored. A partial explanation ("the bridge failed because of metal fatigue") that explains the operative factor without anchoring the rest fails properness and is not anchored. Worse, explanations whose explanatory structure is a *global constraint* rather than a component decomposition (basin-of-attraction, constraint-satisfaction, "the system is trapped in a corner solution") have no anchor map at all: the explanatory content is not a decomposition. Part XV lists symmetry as open but not these.

**Severity.** Serious. The anchored grade silently assumes that explanation is decomposition.

**Fix.** Either introduce a second anchored grade for constraint-based accounts, or state explicitly that the anchored grade covers only decompositional explanation and add non-decompositional cases to Part XV.

### Finding 7 — Single-component targets: nothing can be an account at all

**Claim attacked.** Part V, non-circularity: *"there exists an edit $\tau(a)$ on $E$ ... that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block $B\subsetneq\Gamma$ ... Removing all of $\Gamma$ changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing."* together with *"An anchor may be the whole target only when $D$ has one component."* and *"The target itself, $E=D$ under the identity transport, is an anchored account of $D$."*

**Why it fails.** If $|\Gamma|=1$, the only nonempty subset of $\Gamma$ is $\Gamma$ itself, which is not proper. The existential witness is impossible. So **no one-component candidate can satisfy (E)**. Three consequences follow, each contradicting the text:
1. The allowance "an anchor may be the whole target only when $D$ has one component" describes a case in which the anchored grade is empty.
2. The claim that $E=D$ under the identity transport is an anchored account is false whenever $D$ has one component.
3. Part V's own worked example contradicts itself in one paragraph: *"The one-component candidate that repackages $\operatorname{Sol}_D$ for a target with several components satisfies (E): its single commitment is removable, so non-circular dependence has its witness."* Removing its single commitment removes all of $\Gamma$, which the same paragraph says "witnesses nothing."

**Severity.** Fatal for those passages; it is an internal contradiction, not a modelling choice.

**Fix.** State $|\Gamma|\ge2$ as a requirement of (E); replace "proper sub-block" with "sub-block whose removal leaves a non-vacuous candidate"; delete or repair the one-component example; and either retract the single-component-target allowance or supply a non-circularity witness for it.

### Finding 8 — Resolution is keyed to the answer, not to the mechanism

**Claim attacked.** Part V, condition 2: *"Whenever two components of $D$ can be separately varied by pairs in $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ with distinct effects on $\operatorname{Ans}_p$, they lie in distinct anchors."*

**Why it fails.** Two separately variable components whose separation affects the mechanism but not $\operatorname{Ans}_p$ may share an anchor. So the anchored grade certifies "the mechanism *for this question*," not "the mechanism." That is a coherent design choice, but the summary sentence *"The decomposition of $E$ is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about"* is the honest version and the stronger reading elsewhere in the document is not licensed.

**Severity.** Minor, but it inflates what the anchored grade establishes.

**Fix.** State that the anchored grade is question-indexed in this respect, and that mechanism-parts separable only relative to a different query are not certified.

---

### Finding 9 — World-kind omits the injectivity hypothesis it depends on

**Claim attacked.** Part V, condition 3, plus Derivation 1's stated hypothesis: *"Let $t$ satisfy (F1) on $C$, and let $(\tau,\sigma)$ be injective on $C$."*

**Why it fails.** Signature coincidence is a bijection of signatures; without injectivity of $(\tau,\sigma)$ on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$, two admitted pairs can translate to one pair and the "coincidence" is degenerate. Condition 3 never states the injectivity requirement for $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$.

**Severity.** Minor formal gap.

**Fix.** Add "$(τ,σ)$ injective on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$" to condition 3.

### Finding 10 — Hidden structure: the module does the work that anchoring takes credit for

**Claim attacked.** Part 0: *"It derives reference from matter plus a selection or construction history, given a physical module that says which organizations a lump of matter can instantiate at a grain."* and Part IV: *"how a lump of matter decomposes is the module's burden."*

**Why it fails.** Positing hidden structure (atoms, genes, dark matter, an unobserved planet) is anchorable only relative to a module that already decomposes the target into those parts. The explanatory achievement — discovering the decomposition — is supplied by the primitive $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$, not by fidelity, anchoring, or Origin. The anchored grade therefore cannot distinguish "the mechanism was read off a licensed decomposition" from "the mechanism was posited and the module later licensed it." This is acknowledged as a burden but not as a *limit on what the anchored grade certifies*.

**Severity.** Minor-to-serious: it locates the hard part of mechanism discovery in a primitive while the derived predicates take the credit.

**Fix.** State that the anchored grade is indexed to the module in force and gives no credit for the positing act itself; that credit belongs to `Build`/`Origin`, and only relative to a later module.

## Part 3 — Is the extensional stance on copies and modular tables consistent?

### Finding 11 — Two different identity relations are in force and are never reconciled

**Claim attacked.** Part V: *"the semantics has no predicate that separates a mechanism from its faithful copy, and does not want one"* (identity = signature coincidence on $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$); Part X: *"$c\equiv_\ell d$ ... when there is a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries ... the component relations of one onto the other pointwise"* (identity = structural bijection).

**Why it fails.** These come apart in both directions.
- *Coarser than content identity:* two organizations can coincide in signature on all of $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ while differing in component decomposition. By Part V they are "the mechanism at that index" — the same. By Part X they are not $\equiv_\ell$ — different contents. So a faithful copy with a different decomposition is simultaneously *the mechanism* and *a new content*.
- *Consequence for the copy defence:* Part XV's claim that copies "fail Origin" covers only exact structural reduplications ($\equiv_\ell$ copies). A faithful copy with a different decomposition — including the mirror of Finding 1 — is the mechanism by Part V's lights and new by Part X's. The defence does not reach it.

**Severity.** Serious. The extensional stance and the newness predicate are in tension, and the tension is exactly where a sufficiency counterexample lives.

**Fix.** Either define newness by signature-coincidence-on-$C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ (so that faithful copies are never new, matching Part V's rhetoric), or state that "the mechanism at that index" and "a new content" are compatible and that the copy defence covers only $\equiv_\ell$ reduplication.

### Finding 12 — The decoy concession is stronger than the document admits

**Claim attacked.** Part XV, attack (F): *"A decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every admitted change is not excluded and is not meant to be: at that index it is the mechanism."*

**Why it fails.** Given Findings 1, 4 and 11, "at that index it is the mechanism" is doing more work than the sentence concedes. A decoy can be silent where $\tau$ is undefined (Finding 4), definitional in its component relations (Finding 1), and new by $\equiv_\ell$-mismatch (Finding 11). The sentence is presented as a deliberate scoping move; it is in fact the load-bearing admission that the anchored grade is extensional all the way down and cannot prefer a natural decomposition over a faithful but perverse one. Since the document also says the anchored grade is "what 'the explanation matches the mechanism' means," the honest statement is that it means "matches the mechanism *or any faithful re-decomposition of it, on the sub-contract it speaks about*."

**Severity.** Serious as an overclaim; the position is defensible if stated at full strength.

**Fix.** Rewrite the concession to include silence and re-decomposition, and state that the anchored grade certifies faithful decomposition, not naturalness, and that no condition in the semantics ranks decompositions.

---

### Finding 13 — Provenance boundary between selection and construction is module-supplied and unremarked

**Claim attacked.** Part IV: *"the provenance of the **earliest** witness in $\prec_h$ whose events produced the occurrence that carries $t$"* and Part X: *"$\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)$ holds when there is an episode ... whose construction witness prepares $t$."*

**Why it fails.** A transport first produced by blind population tuning and later deliberately repaired is "selected" unless the repair produces a *new occurrence* carrying the transport. Whether it does is fixed by the module's individuation of occurrences, which is primitive and unstated at the boundary that decides the classification. The earliest-witness rule is only as sharp as occurrence identity.

**Severity.** Minor, but it is the joint in the selection/construction distinction.

**Fix.** State the occurrence-individuation requirement on the module at this point, and give the criterion for when a repair yields a new occurrence rather than maintenance of an old one.

## Summary of the (A) verdict

My plainly-vacuous candidates fail the anchored grade's real work — properness blocks answer-rule-only accounts for multi-component targets, resolution blocks merging where the answer separates parts, and (F1) blocks blind permutations. That is a negative result worth recording. But the residual counterexample succeeds: **the definitional mirror** (Finding 1), which is anchored because (F1) is satisfiable by definition, and which satisfies Origin because `New` is system-relative while the target is in no system's repertoire, and because `Build`'s defect record is manufacturable. The document cannot exclude it without a non-definitionality condition, and its copy defence in Part XV does not reach it (Finding 11). The three anchored conditions do not exclude a *genuine* mechanism; they exclude idealizations (Finding 5), partial and non-decompositional explanations (Finding 6), and — via Finding 7's bug — all single-component targets.

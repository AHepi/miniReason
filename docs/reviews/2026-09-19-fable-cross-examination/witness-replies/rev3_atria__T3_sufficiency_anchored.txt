# Referee Report: Attack (A) — Anchored + Origin, and the Exclusion Profile of the Anchored Grade

---

## Finding 1 — The Error-Record Mirror: a candidate that is anchored, originated, and explains nothing

### (1) Claim attacked

Part XV: *"A candidate meeting the anchored grade of (E) on an admitted contract and satisfying Origin (new, built, deployed), which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency."* — together with Part X's concession that such a thing exists and is fine.

### (2) The construction, in full concrete detail

**Target.** A grandfather clock, occurrence $o$. The physical module $\Theta$ supplies, for $o$, an admitted edit set $A_\Theta(o)$: displace the pendulum bob, change rod length, change temperature, remove the escapement, wind the mainspring, brake the gear train, and so on. $C_{\mathrm{adm}}=A_\Theta(o)\times B$. At grain $\ell$ = the clock's part decomposition, $D$ has components for pendulum, escapement, gear train, mainspring, hands. Grain $\ell$ is admissible by Part II's criterion (*"every component of that organization is touched by some admitted pair"*) — every part is displaceable or removable.

**Question.** $p$ = the production question: $\mathcal Q$ reads the hand-position port; $C$ contains interventions on rod length, temperature, winding. Baseline $b_0$ = the clock as it sits.

**System.** A robot $s$ whose repertoire contains one deployable content: transport $t_0$ (period as a function of length only), selected on history $H_0$ of encountered length changes. $s$ has never deployed anything $\equiv_\ell$ to the clock's organization.

**Episode.**
1. Temperature drops; the rod contracts; $t_0$ violates. The prediction obligation in $O_p$ fails at the state before $e$.
2. $s$ records the defect itemisedly: state (length $L$, temperature $T_0$) $\to$ predicted $P_0$, actual $P_1$.
3. $s$ iteratively corrects, building $E$: one component per clock part, each carrying exactly the relation its anchor carries under every admitted change. The binding $\lambda$ maps each $E$-component to the corresponding clock-part subnetwork.
4. The record is used in the criticism the binding survives ("$t_0$ failed at $T_0$; this binding corrects it").
5. $E$ is deployed for the prediction task and succeeds on every admitted pair.

**Verification, condition by condition.**

| Condition | Status |
|---|---|
| (F1) | Identity transport; each $E$-component's relation equals $\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}$ on every $(a,b)\in C$ **and** on all of $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$. ✔ |
| (F2) | $\pi=\mathrm{id}$, $\tau=\mathrm{id}$, $\sigma=\mathrm{id}$; $\tau(1)=1$; homomorphism trivially. ✔ |
| (A) | $E$'s hand-position answer $=D$'s. ✔ |
| Non-circular | Removing the escapement component (a proper sub-block of $\Gamma$) changes the answer profile. ✔ |
| Non-vacuity | $\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing$; the clock exists. ✔ |
| Properness | Anchors $=$ the parts; cover all touched components; injective. ✔ |
| Resolution | Separately-variable parts (pendulum vs. mainspring) lie in distinct anchors. ✔ |
| World-kind | Each $E$-component matches its anchor on every admitted pair touching it. ✔ |
| Attempt | $p$'s question-organization instantiated before $e$ (via $t_0$); obligation failed; $E$ used after. ✔ |
| New | No content $\equiv_\ell$ to the clock's organization is in $R_{<e}(s,h)$. ✔ |
| Build | Owned subhistory; binding not a relabelling of $t_0$'s binding, not obtainable from the carriers by $\equiv_\ell$, required for (F1); itemised record used in the surviving criticism; not a content-preserving transfer. ✔ |
| Deploy | Constructed transport, integrated, retained capability. ✔ |
| Operative | Anchors are the clock's actual parts — active routes. ✔ |
| Objectivity | Not protective — world-kind already covers $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$. ✔ |

**So: anchored grade ✔, Origin ✔.** And $E\equiv_\ell D$: $E$ *is* the target again, as a content.

### (3) Why this fails as an explanation

The document concedes the candidate and defends it. Part X: *"the target's own faithful copy, built from error records, is an anchored, originated account that is $\equiv_\ell$ to the target: New fails for it if the target is already in the repertoire, and holds if it is not — in which case building the target's mirror from its errors is discovering it, and the semantics says so without embarrassment."*

The defence does not work.

- **Origin adds no dependency structure.** Part V says of the copy: *"'This explains nothing' said of the target's own copy is not a complaint about account-hood; it is a complaint about origin and deployment — that the copy is not new to any repertoire, was not built, and is not held by anyone."* But newness-to-$s$, builtness, and deployment are facts about $s$'s history. None of them is a relation between $E$ and $D$ that adds a dependency the target did not already contain. If the copy explains nothing without Origin, and Origin adds no dependency information, the copy explains nothing with Origin. The complaint Part V raises stands unmodified for the mirror.
- **"Discovery" is the wrong category.** Discovery is $s$'s acquisition of a faithful representation. Explanation is an account–target relation. The document's own (E) is deliberately externalist (Part I: *"Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it"*), precisely to keep account-hood free of the system's epistemic state. Conjoining Origin reintroduces the system's contingent history into explanatory status. Two extensionally identical accounts, one new to $s_1$ and stale to $s_2$, differ in whether they explain. That is not a grade of explanation; it is a fact about repertoires.
- **The mirror is the framework's ceiling, not its floor.** Any account coarser or more abstract than $D$ fails world-kind (see Finding 4). So the grade that the document calls *"what 'the explanation matches the mechanism at the module's index' means"* is the grade of replication. A child who internalises the clock's complete part-by-part response to every possible intervention, with no law, principle, or generalization, has by this semantics an anchored, originated explanation. Derivation 9 keeps this from being a mere output table; nothing keeps it from being a mere internalized duplicate.
- **The aggravation is admissible.** Part X: *"The semantics has no condition separating a question worth finding from a re-parameterisation… a random new contract meeting those four counts."* So pair the mirror with a randomly generated contract on the clock. The conjunction — random question + novel replica — satisfies anchored + Origin. The document's own listed empty place is what lets it through.

### (4) Severity

**Serious, and I judge it fatal to the sufficiency claim as stated.** The document anticipates the case and bites the bullet; the bullet does not go down, because the only difference between "the copy explains nothing" (Part V) and "the copy is discovery" (Part X) is a system-relative novelty fact, and Part V's own reason for saying the copy explains nothing is untouched by it.

### (5) Fix

Add a **non-triviality-of-organization** condition to the anchored grade: require that the transport's component map be non-injective on $D$'s touched components — i.e. that $E$ have strictly fewer active components than $D$ has touched components at $\ell$, so that anchoring is genuine abstraction rather than re-indexing. This excludes mirrors and decomposed behaviour tables while leaving idealizations to be handled by the approximate-fidelity route in Finding 4. Alternatively, abandon the sufficiency claim and state openly that the anchored grade is a grade of *replication*, not explanation, and that explanation proper is not defined here.

## Finding 2 — Equivocation on "explains" across Part 0, Part V, Part X, Part XV

### (1) Claim attacked

Part 0: *"An explanation is a question-relevant organization of dependencies, held to a target by a transport that is checked by what changes when things are changed."* — no provenance condition. Also Part 0: *"The second is what 'the explanation matches the mechanism at the module's index' means."*

### (2) Why it fails

Part 0's definition is satisfied by $E=D$ under the identity transport, which Part V calls something that "explains nothing." So Part 0's definition of *explanation* and Part V's use of *explains nothing* are in different senses. Part XV then uses a third: the attack requires Origin too. Three senses of one word, in a document whose central claim is a biconditional about explanation.

The operative text is Part V: *"'This explains nothing' said of the target's own copy is not a complaint about account-hood; it is a complaint about origin and deployment."* This makes "explains" in the account-hood sense *not* the explanatory sense. But Part 0's gloss and its "second grade" language both present the anchored grade as the explanatory grade. A reader cannot tell, from Part 0 alone, whether the anchored grade is a grade of explanation or of fidelity. Part V says fidelity; Part 0 says explanation.

### (3) Severity

**Serious.** This is not a wording quibble: Finding 1's counterexample is invisible to a reader who takes Part 0 at face value, because Part 0's definition admits it.

### (4) Fix

Rewrite Part 0's definition as: an *account* is a question-relevant organization of dependencies held to a target by a change-checked transport; an *explanation* is an account that is anchored and originated. State that the first is extensional and the second is system-relative, and that the document's realism claim (Part I) attaches only to the first.

## Finding 3 — "New" is per-system and near-vacuous for first mirrors; Part V overstates why Origin fails

### (1) Claim attacked

Part V: *"that the copy is not new to any repertoire, was not built, and is not held by anyone."* Against Part X: $\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c$.

### (2) Why it fails

$\operatorname{New}$ is quantified over $s$'s repertoire, not "any repertoire." The target organization $D$ is attributed to the *world*; it is not in $s$'s repertoire unless $s$ already deploys it. So for any system that has not already mastered the target, $\operatorname{New}$ holds trivially for a faithful mirror. Part V's "not new to any repertoire" is a stronger condition than the one Part X actually defines, and Part X's own concession (*"New fails for it if the target is already in the repertoire, and holds if it is not"*) is the correct statement — which contradicts Part V's parenthetical.

Consequence: the only barrier to Origin for a first mirror is Build's nontriviality. But Build's nontriviality is *also* easy for a naive system: *"so an empty prior repertoire does not make every binding nontrivial, because the carriers still count"* — the carriers being $t_0$ and the error records, neither of which is $\equiv_\ell$ to the mirror's binding. So for a system with a poor prior repertoire, both New and nontriviality are satisfied by any faithful mirror of a new target. **Origin is near-vacuous for first mirrors.**

### (3) Severity

**Serious.** It is the mechanism by which Finding 1 goes through, and it shows Part V's defence of the anchored grade is void for built mirrors.

### (4) Fix

Either define New against an objective reference class (the contents deployable by any system under $\Theta$, or by the scientific community), or add to Build a requirement that the binding not be obtainable from the *target's own* structure — which is exactly what a mirror's binding is.

## Finding 4 — The anchored grade excludes idealized mechanisms, which is the one thing it should not exclude

### (1) Claim attacked

Part V, world-kind: *"fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every admitted pair that touches the anchor — changes the relation of some component in it."* And Part V: *"It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose."*

### (2) Why it fails

Take the ideal-gas account of a real gas. Its components (kinetic energy, momentum) must be of one forward kind with their anchors on **every** admitted pair touching them. If $\Theta$ admits density or compression changes, those changes touch the molecular-size components of $D$. Properness then requires those components to be covered by some anchor, and world-kind requires the anchoring $E$-component to match them. The ideal gas does not. So:

- properness fails (no $E$-component anchors the size components), or
- world-kind fails (the kinetic component's response to compression does not match its anchor's).

The same holds for the point mass (admitted changes probing finite size), the frictionless plane (admitted changes probing friction), the two-body problem (admitted changes probing perturbations), the rigid rod (admitted changes probing elasticity). **Every idealized model fails the anchored grade whenever $\Theta$ admits the exposing change.** Part VII concedes the shape of this: *"an account whose contract excludes an admitted change on which it is evaluable and wrong is an account at C whose objectivity claim fails"* — idealizations are accounts at a protective scope, not anchored ones.

This is the wrong exclusion profile. Idealization is the dominant mode of scientific explanation, and the anchored grade — the grade the document calls "what the explanation matches the mechanism… means" — is unavailable to all of it. Meanwhile the grade is *available* to exact replicas (Finding 1). The extension is inverted relative to scientific practice.

The document has the machinery to repair this and does not use it: Part VIII's approximate transport, (T2), with one-step discrepancy $\varepsilon$ and an $L$-Lipschitz bound. (F1) and (F2) are exact equalities throughout; (T2) is never connected to them.

### (3) Severity

**Serious.** It does not refute the definitions — it shows their top grade is not the grade of science.

### (4) Fix

Define an $\varepsilon$-anchored grade: replace the equality in (F1) and (A) by $\varepsilon$-closeness under a stated modulus, with $\varepsilon$ declared per contract and the exposing changes inside $C$ rather than excluded by $S$. Then the ideal gas is $\varepsilon$-anchored on a low-density contract, and the anchored grade becomes usable for real models. State that the exact anchored grade is the $\varepsilon=0$ limit and is satisfied only by replicas.

## Finding 5 — For single-component targets the anchored grade is vacuous

### (1) Claim attacked

Part V, properness: *"An anchor may be the whole target only when $D$ has one component."* And Part V, non-circular dependence: *"when it has one, removing it is the only witness there is, and the condition then does little — which is why the anchored grade, not this condition, is what excludes the one-component repackaging."*

### (2) Why it fails

For a one-component target: properness is trivially satisfied by one anchor $=$ whole $D$; resolution is vacuous (no two separately-variable components); world-kind reduces to extensional match on all admitted pairs; non-circular dependence is the weak single-member witness the document itself disparages. So **anchored $\equiv$ extensional match** for one-component targets, and the "second grade" collapses into the first.

Combined with Origin: a system new to a one-component target that builds a one-component mirror — a pure lookup of $D$'s relation — has an anchored, originated account. This is the degenerate instance of Finding 1, and there is no properness barrier against it, because properness was designed to block one-component repackagings of *multi-component* targets.

### (3) Severity

**Minor-to-serious.** It does not by itself refute anything, but it shows the anchored grade's discriminating power is entirely a function of how many components $\Theta$ happens to supply.

### (4) Fix

Add to properness: for one-component targets, require that the anchor be a *proper* subnetwork with the residual structure accounted for, or state that one-component targets admit only the first grade.

## Finding 6 — Partial mechanisms and coarse grains: no exclusion, and the grade's strength is wholly inherited from $\Theta$

### (1) Claim attacked

Part V, properness: *"no touched component of $D$ lies outside every anchor"*; Part II, admissibility: *"every component of that organization is touched by some admitted pair."*

### (2) Why it fails

**Partial mechanisms are not excluded, correctly.** Properness forces coverage of every component touched by $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$, so an account that captures only part of the target fails properness, and one that captures everything the contract reaches passes. This is a feature. But note that "what the contract reaches" is $C_{\mathrm{adm}}=A_\Theta(o)\times B$ — the *module's* admission, not the modeller's. A stingy module admits few changes, few components are touched, a coarse grain is admissible, and a partial mechanism is anchored.

**Coarse grains are not excluded — they are endorsed.** If $\Theta$ licenses a grain at which two genuinely distinct sub-mechanisms are merged into one component, then at that grain the merged account is anchored: properness covers the merged component, resolution finds no two separately-variable components to separate, world-kind matches. The anchored grade cannot detect that the grain is too coarse. The document half-owns this (*"Claims can be rescued by moving to a grain only if the new grain is admissible, and the move is a new claim at a new index"*), but the consequence is that *"the explanation matches the mechanism at the module's index"* is module-relative, not absolute. A module with a coarse grain and a small $A_\Theta$ makes any faithful mirror anchored.

So the answer to the sub-question is: of the four cases tested, **single-component targets** are not excluded (the grade is vacuous instead), **partial mechanisms** are not excluded when the module's reach is narrow, **coarse grains** are endorsed at their index, and **idealizations** are excluded — the one genuine exclusion, and the wrong one.

### (3) Severity

**Minor**, because it is largely conceded; but it removes any claim that the anchored grade is an absolute notion of mechanism-matching.

### (4) Fix

State explicitly that the anchored grade is indexed to $(\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})$ and carries no cross-module authority, and that sufficiency claims are correspondingly relative.

## Finding 7 — The random-question pairing makes the counterexample unavoidable

### (1) Claim attacked

Part X: *"The semantics has no condition separating a question worth finding from a re-parameterisation. What (G) does require of a found question is only this: that it is coherently posed, that some obligation of it fails at the state before $e$ (Attempt), that it is new to the repertoire, and that it was built; a random new contract meeting those four counts."*

### (2) Why it fails

Take Finding 1's mirror and let the question be a randomly generated contract on the clock (a random $S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}$). It is coherently posed; its obligation fails before $e$ (prediction fails); it is new; it is built. Origin holds for the question. The mirror answers it. The conjunction — a randomly generated question plus a novel replica — is an anchored, originated account. There is no condition anywhere in the semantics that distinguishes this from explaining the clock.

The document marks the place for a merit function as empty. That is honest, but it means the sufficiency claim is undefended against its cheapest counterexample.

### (3) Severity

**Serious** in conjunction with Finding 1; harmless alone.

### (4) Fix

A question-merit condition is needed, or the sufficiency claim must be restricted to questions whose contracts are not arbitrary — e.g. require $C$ to be closed under a stated relevance relation to $O_p$.

## Finding 8 — (E) is satisfiable with no empirical contact beyond the baseline

### (1) Claim attacked

Part III: *"$K$ is a declared set of counterfactual edits — pairs with $K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing$ — each declared together with the component relations $L_j(a,b)$ it imposes, which is what its 'stated meaning' is."* And Part V: *"it is deliberately not a conjunct of (E), so that the first grade remains what a question can assess on its own."*

### (2) Why it fails

Set $S=\varnothing$ (every admitted pair excluded, reasons recorded — this is permitted) and $C=\{(1,b_0)\}\cup K$, where the relations imposed by each pair of $K$ are declared to be exactly what the candidate produces. Then (F1), (F2), (A) hold trivially on all of $C$; non-vacuity holds because the baseline is real. The candidate is an account at $C$ whose only contact with the world is the baseline.

Objectivity does not repair this. Protection requires the candidate to *differ* from the target on an excluded pair. A candidate that excludes everything and is never tested — or that happens to be right — is not protective. So objectivity catches actual wrongness, not untestedness.

This matters for the anchored grade only in that it shows the first grade is much weaker than the rhetoric of "adequacy" suggests, and it is the grade at which most of the document's worked examples (Part VII) are assessed.

### (3) Severity

**Minor** — largely owned by the document, and blocked at the anchored grade by world-kind's quantification over $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$.

### (4) Fix

Require $S$ to be nonempty and to include at least one pair touching each anchor, or make non-protection a conjunct of (E) for non-vacuous contracts.

## Finding 9 — The extensional stance plus Derivation 8 entails that the target is its own explanation, leaving no objective, non-self-referential grade

### (1) Claim attacked

Part V: *"The target itself, $E=D$ under the identity transport, is an anchored account of $D$."* And Part V: *"the semantics has no predicate that separates a mechanism from its faithful copy, and does not want one."* Together with Derivation 8's equivariance of $(E)$, anchoring, $(R)$, $(G)$, $(P)$, $(EK)$ under content-preserving recodings.

### (2) Why it fails

Since $E\equiv_\ell D$ and $\equiv_\ell$ is identity of contents as wholes, the mirror and the target are the same content. Derivation 8 says $(G)$ is preserved under recoding. So "the target explains itself" and "the mirror explains the target" are, by the semantics' own lights, the same explanation. The semantics therefore has **no non-self-referential notion of explanation**: the anchored grade is satisfied by the target itself, and the only thing that distinguishes a genuine explanation from self-explanation is Origin — which is system-relative.

This is in direct tension with Part I: *"Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it. Systems can be wrong about their transports… Every success predicate in this document is external."* That is true of fidelity. It is not true of the sufficiency claim, which conjoins fidelity with a repertoire-relative novelty fact. The realism of Part I and the system-relativity of Part X cannot both be ingredients of one explanatory grade.

### (3) Severity

**Serious.** This is the structural form of Finding 1.

### (4) Fix

Either (a) accept that explanation is system-relative and drop Part I's realism for the sufficiency claim, or (b) make the non-self-referentiality structural, as in Finding 1's fix (require the component map to be non-injective).

## Finding 10 — The "modular table" barrier collapses: the lookup/structure distinction has no work to do once world-kind holds

### (1) Claim attacked

Part V: *"A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component anchored to a target with several, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account, as the previous section says."* And: *"So is a modular table whose pieces each carry the local signature of an anchor on every admitted edit."*

### (2) Why it fails

The distinction between a "lookup table" and a "table that genuinely encodes an organization's response" is doing no work: once the pieces carry the local signature on every admitted edit, world-kind holds, and the thing is anchored. The only barrier is properness, which a decomposed table satisfies. So a complete, decomposed behaviour table of the clock — each piece recording one part's response to every admitted change, parts labelled as the clock's parts — is an anchored account, and with Origin (Finding 1) an explanation.

Derivation 9 (*"Output descriptions do not determine accounts"*) blocks the inference from outputs to accounts, but it does not block the converse: an account whose internal structure is a part-indexed behaviour record is fine. The semantics has no predicate that distinguishes "understands the clock" from "has a complete decomposed behaviour record of the clock," and by the extensional stance it does not want one. That is a coherent stance about fidelity. It is not a coherent stance about explanation, which is what the sufficiency claim needs.

### (3) Severity

**Serious** in conjunction with Finding 1; it removes the last structural barrier.

### (4) Fix

Same as Finding 1: require genuine abstraction (non-injective component map), or add a condition that the active components' relations be generated by a rule of bounded complexity rather than recorded extensionally.

## Verdict on the two sub-questions

**Do the three anchored conditions exclude a genuine mechanism?**

| Case | Excluded? |
|---|---|
| Single-component targets | No — the grade is *vacuous* there (properness and resolution trivially satisfied; non-circular weak by the document's own admission) |
| Partial mechanisms | No, when the module's admitted reach is narrow — and correctly so; properness does force coverage of everything $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ touches |
| Idealizations | **Yes**, whenever $\Theta$ admits the exposing change — and this is the wrong thing to exclude, since it removes the bulk of scientific explanation |
| Coarse grains | No — endorsed at that index; the grade cannot detect under-resolution relative to a finer grain |

So the conditions exclude roughly the right things about decoys and the wrong things about idealized models, and are vacuous in the degenerate case.

**Is the extensional stance on copies and modular tables consistent with everything else?**

Internally, as a stance about *fidelity*, yes — and Derivation 8's equivariance makes it precise. But it is inconsistent with three things the document also says:

1. **Part 0's definition of explanation**, which has no provenance condition and is therefore satisfied by the target itself (Finding 2).
2. **Part V's "explains nothing" rhetoric**, once Part X concedes that the mirror can be built and new — Part V's three failures (not new, not built, not held) are all repairable, and Part X repairs them (Findings 1, 3).
3. **Part I's explanatory realism**, once Origin — a system-relative repertoire fact — is made a conjunct of the sufficiency claim (Finding 9).

The stance is consistent; the sufficiency claim built on it is not.

## Findings

**1. The adequacy condition is not well-defined when \(\tau\) or \(\sigma\) is undefined outside \(C\).**

> “**Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.”

The document elsewhere explicitly allows \(\tau\) to be undefined. In Part VII:

> “here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation’s component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair.”

Adequacy quantifies over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), i.e. outside the contract \(C\) on which the transport was specified. If \(\tau(a)\) or \(\sigma(b)\) is undefined at such a pair, the expression \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) has no value, so “does not differ” has no truth value. The predicate is therefore undefined exactly where it is supposed to do its protective work.

**Seriousness:** serious to fatal.  
**Fix:** Require \(\tau,\sigma\) to be total on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), or explicitly define undefined as failure of adequacy, or restrict adequacy to pairs where \(\tau,\sigma\) and both answer profiles are defined and state that restriction.

---

**2. The “baseline value” for the candidate is not defined, and \(\sigma(b_0)=b_0\) is not required.**

> “while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not [differ from its baseline value], or the reverse.”

The target’s baseline is clearly \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\). The candidate’s baseline is apparently \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(1),\sigma(b_0))\). But (F2) only requires \(\tau(1)=1\), not \(\sigma(b_0)=b_0\). If \(\sigma(b_0)\neq b_0\), then the candidate’s baseline pair is \((1,\sigma(b_0))\), not \((1,b_0)\). Although (A) on \(C\) equates the values at \((1,b_0)\), the phrase “its baseline value” for the candidate is ambiguous. The condition compares target movement from one baseline with candidate movement from another baseline boundary.

**Seriousness:** serious.  
**Fix:** State explicitly that the candidate’s baseline is \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(1),\sigma(b_0))\), and either require \(\sigma(b_0)=b_0\) or make the baseline index explicit in the condition.

---

**3. The condition is too weak: it checks asymmetry of movement from baseline, not equality of target and candidate at the excluded pair.**

> “there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.”

Suppose at an excluded admitted pair the target’s answer moves from baseline \(0\) to \(1\), and the candidate’s answer moves from baseline \(0\) to \(2\). Both differ from baseline, so the first disjunct is false. The reverse is also false because the target moved. The condition passes, even though the candidate is unfaithful at that admitted pair. A scope can therefore exclude exactly a separating change and still satisfy adequacy, provided the candidate moves at all — even to the wrong value.

**Seriousness:** fatal for the stated purpose of catching protective scopes.  
**Fix:** Compare \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) directly to \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\), not each separately to its baseline. Or define \(C\) as protective when adding the excluded pair to \(C\) would break (A), (F1), or (F2).

---

**4. The condition is too strong: it forces out-of-scope sensitivity on the candidate.**

> “there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.”

The candidate is only required by (F1), (F2), and (A) to be faithful on \(C\). Outside \(C\), it may be constant, undefined, or simply not built for those edits. If the target has any admitted out-of-scope dependence — any \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) where \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\neq \operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) — and the candidate does not move there, adequacy fails. Thus any restricted contract fails unless the candidate happens to mimic the target’s baseline-sensitivity pattern on every admitted pair outside the contract.

This contradicts the stated architecture in Part 0:

> “An **account at a contract** is … faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits … The first grade is what can be assessed within a question.”

If adequacy uses \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), the first grade is not assessable within the question; it already requires the full admitted contract.

**Seriousness:** fatal for the distinction between “account at \(C\)” and “anchored account.”  
**Fix:** Move adequacy out of \(\operatorname{Account}\) and into the anchored grade, or define it as a condition on the stated scope’s recorded reasons rather than on global candidate behaviour.

---

**5. The adequacy condition contradicts Part VII’s claim that the reversed calculation is an account at the identification contract.**

In Part VII:

> “The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\): it is an account at that contract, and not anchored as a production account, because on the production edits — admitted, and outside the identification contract — its one component is not of one forward kind with the assignment of \(L\).”

But production edits are admitted and outside the identification contract. Under a production edit, the target’s answer moves. The reversed calculation has no component assigning \(H\), so its answer does not move (or \(\tau\) is undefined). By the adequacy condition in Part V, the identification contract is protective, so the reversed calculation is **not** an account at that contract. The document cannot both say it is an account at the identification contract and make adequacy a conjunct of \(\operatorname{Account}\).

**Seriousness:** fatal internal inconsistency.  
**Fix:** Either remove adequacy from \(\operatorname{Account}\), or withdraw the Part VII claim that the reversed calculation is an account at the identification contract.

**6. The existential quantifier over \(t\) lets a candidate satisfy adequacy by choosing a convenient translation outside \(C\).**

> “\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\iff\exists t,\Gamma\ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\)”

Adequacy is evaluated for a particular \(t\), using \(\tau\). Since \(t\) is existentially quantified and only constrained on \(C\), a modeller can choose \(\tau\) on excluded pairs so that \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) differs from baseline — even if the candidate’s relation at that translated edit has nothing to do with the target. Because the condition only requires movement, not correctness, this is enough to defeat adequacy. The candidate need not be faithful on the excluded pair; it need only be sensitive somewhere.

**Seriousness:** serious to fatal.  
**Fix:** Make adequacy independent of the existential \(t\), or require the chosen \(t\) to be answer-faithful on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), not merely on \(C\).

**7. Collision with \(K\): Part III says \(K\) enters non-vacuity only, but fidelity is said to range over all of \(C\), and \(K\subseteq C\).**

**Seriousness:** serious consistency bug.

**9. Collision with Part VIII’s historical index: adequacy makes the truth value of a claim at a fixed contract depend on the current physical module.**

Part VIII:

Adequacy quantifies over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which is supplied by the physical module in force. If a later theory changes the physical module, then \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) changes. The set \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) changes. So a claim “\(E\) is an account on \(C\)” can flip truth value at the same contract \(C\) when the module changes, unless \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is made part of the claim’s index. The text does not say that.

**Seriousness:** serious index collision.  
**Fix:** Include \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) explicitly in the index of any adequacy-containing claim, or state that adequacy is evaluated relative to the module in force when the claim is formed.

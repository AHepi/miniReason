## Derivation 1 — Kind preservation

**Passage attacked.** *"By (K), $\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}$ and $\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}$. (F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise. ∎"*

### Finding 1 — $\lambda(k)$ is a subnetwork, not a component, so $\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))$ is not defined by (K).
(K) reads $ \operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}$ for a **component** $j\in J$. In Part IV $\lambda$ "assigns each component of $E$ a **subnetwork** of $D$." A subnetwork of $D$ is a set of components, and the relation attached to it, $\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)$, is the projection of a solution set, not the $L_j$ of a single component. The proof silently redefines $\operatorname{sig}$ by using a quantity that (K) does not deliver. Worse, it changes type: a subnetwork relation is the joint constraint of several $L_j$, whereas the E-side quantity $L_k$ is a single component relation. Two "signatures" that are not objects of the same type cannot be equated pointwise.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Define a subnetwork signature explicitly: $\operatorname{ssig}_C(\Sigma)=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V(\Sigma)}\operatorname{Sol}_{\Sigma}(a,b))\}$ for $\Sigma\subseteq J$. Then state the theorem as: (F1) equates $\operatorname{ssig}_C(\lambda(k))$ with the E-side signature, restricting to $\lambda(k)$'s non-hidden ports. As written the proof types-out.

### Finding 2 — The comparison of signatures requires injectivity of $\tau$ and $\sigma$ on $C$; this is neither stated nor assumed.
The D-side index set is $C$; the E-side index set is $\tau[C]$. If $\tau$ (or $\sigma$) is non-injective on $C$, two D-side pairs $(a,b)\neq(a',b')$ collapse to one E-side index, so the E-side set is a function of the collapse while the D-side set is not, and "the third coordinates pointwise" is undefined. The proof writes both sides as sets of triples indexed by possibly-different index sets and then asserts pointwise equality — which presupposes $(\tau,\sigma)$ is a bijection from $C$ onto $\tau[C]$.

**Severity.** Serious — the "theorem" does not hold without a hypothesis that is missing.
**Fix.** Add: $\tau$ and $\sigma$ are injective on $C$ (equivalently $\tau[C]$ is in bijection with $C$). Then state the conclusion as equality of signatures after the bijective relabelling.

### Finding 3 — "Up to the port translation" is promised in the claim but never applied in the proof.
The claim contains the qualifier "up to the port translation," and (K) requires "a bijection of their footprints." The proof compares $\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}$ on the D-side with $L_k$ on the E-side without exhibiting that bijection or checking that $\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}$ is meaningful relative to $\lambda(k)$'s ports via that bijection. The port translation is the unearned step.

**Severity.** Minor–serious.
**Fix.** Identify the bijection $\phi:V_k\to V(\lambda(k))$ explicitly in Part IV, and write the projection as over $\phi(V_k)$.

### Finding 4 — Part II has already *defined* kinds as signatures, so "kind preservation is a theorem" is a definitional rewrite.
Part II: *"**Kinds are edit-signatures.** … Two components $j,j'$ are **of one kind on $C$** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which $\operatorname{sig}_C(j)$ and $\operatorname{sig}_C(j')$ coincide."* Given this, saying that (F1) entails signature equality is saying that (F1) entails (F1). The advertised content — that a natural-kind-like notion is preserved by the transport — is not present; the theorem is analytic in the definitions.

**Severity.** Fatal for the framing, harmless for the equation.
**Fix.** Rename the result. "Given the signature definition of kinds (K), (F1) is its component-wise expression." Do not present it as a theorem whose content is that kinds are preserved by correspondence, because the "kinds" in question were built from correspondence in the first place.

### Finding 5 — The corollary quantifies over all contracts but is proved for one.
*"A condition 'each component must anchor to a component of the same kind' adds nothing to (F1) on **any** contract."* The proof treats a fixed $C$. The universal quantifier is not carried through any argument; it is asserted.

**Severity.** Cosmetic.
**Fix.** Either state "for the given $C$" or add the one-line universalisation once (K) is used.

### Finding 6 — Circularity in the role this derivation is asked to play.
Part XV (C) states: *"A discriminating kind … would refute Derivation 1 and reinstate correspondence as primitive."* But Derivation 1's corollary shows only that *no kind defined by (K)* can discriminate beyond (F1). A counterexample using a kind notion not defined by (K) — for example, a natural-kind label that changes the substantive force of an account without differing in edit-signature — is not excluded by the corollary, and the corollary's own reading of "kind" is the one being contested. Derivation 1 as stated cannot serve as the fence it is advertised to be.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** State the corollary as internal to (K): "no **signature-based** kind-label adds discriminating power beyond (F1)." Withdraw the claim in Part XV (C) that this refutes all discriminating-kind counterexamples, or else add a separate argument that any defensible notion of kind collapses to signature on the relevant level.

### Finding 7 — "Active" is used but not defined.
The theorem quantifies over "every active component $k$ of $E$," and (E) speaks of "an identified set $\Gamma$ of active commitments." No definition of "active" appears in the excerpt. The status of the theorem therefore depends on an undefined predicate.

**Severity.** Minor.
**Fix.** Define "active" — most plausibly "a component of $E$ whose relation is not the full relation on its ports" or "a component of $\Gamma$" — and adjust the statement.

## Derivation 2 — Indistinguishable is identical

**Passage attacked.** *"Two candidates $\mathcal E,\mathcal E'$ for the same $p$ that both satisfy (F1), (F2), and (A) on $C$ are one account at grain $C$: their components are pairwise of one kind on $C$ and their answer profiles coincide. Proof. Immediate from Derivation 1 and (A). ∎"*

### Finding 8 — The derivand only hypothesises (F1), (F2), (A); "one **account**" is defined by (E), which also requires NonCircular and NonVacuous.
Part V defines $\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E)\iff$(F1)$\land$(F2)$\land$(A)$\land$NonCircular$\land$NonVacuous. The proof's hypothesis omits NonCircular and NonVacuous, and the conclusion says "one **account**." Even if the pairwise-kind claim went through, (E) could hold for $\mathcal E$ and fail for $\mathcal E'$ on NonCircular or NonVacuous — which is exactly the "moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named 'law'" case Part V calls out as excluded.

**Severity.** Fatal for the stated conclusion.
**Fix.** Either stop at "one **candidate** at grain $C$" or add NonCircular and NonVacuous to the hypotheses and show preservation of those clauses — which is not trivial and is not done.

### Finding 9 — "Pairwise of one kind" requires a cross-organization comparison of signatures that (K) does not provide, plus the assumption that the anchors coincide.
(K) is a relation on components of one organization. To conclude "their components are pairwise of one kind on $C$," one needs for each pair $(k,k')$ with $k\in J_E$, $k'\in J_{E'}$, a footprint bijection under which the signatures coincide. (F1) supplies a relation of the form $\operatorname{sig}(k)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{sig}(\lambda(k))$ and $\operatorname{sig}(k')\leftrightarrow\operatorname{sig}(\lambda(k'))$ *with different anchors*. The proof silently assumes $\lambda(k)=\lambda(k')$ (the same anchor is used for both), so that both signature sets equal the same D-side quantity. Nothing in (F1) forces this. Two different subnetworks of $D$ may project to the same port-set relation from different component decompositions.

**Severity.** Serious — the "immediate" step is not immediate.
**Fix.** Add: "Let $\lambda_E$ and $\lambda_{E'}$ be the respective anchor maps; then for each pair, require $\lambda_E(k)=\lambda_{E'}(k')$." Show this is not vacuous, or state the conclusion as a match of signature sets rather than of components.

### Finding 10 — "One account" is not the same as "pairwise same signature."
Two distinct organizations satisfying (F1),(F2),(A) with pairwise-corresponding signatures are still two organizations. Calling them "one account" is a *stipulation* about how the semantics counts. The derivation's title "Indistinguishable is identical" makes an identification that the semantics has not performed; Derivation 1 itself said "of one kind on $C$," which is a contract-indexed equivalence, not identity.

**Severity.** Serious (rhetorical overclaim, not a mathematical error once the folding is stated).
**Fix.** State: "the semantics does not distinguish them; they are *contract-indistinguishable*." Reserve "identical" for bijection of the underlying tuple, which is not what was proved.

### Finding 11 — The consequence overclaims a remedy.
*"The remedy is a finer contract, which is a new question."* Nothing in the derivation gives this remedy; the derivation only says the contract does not distinguish. If the environment physically admits changes the contract excludes, moving to a finer contract is a **change of subject**, not a remedy — and Part III says such a change is a new question. The derivation quietly converts a limit of a scoped claim into a programme.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Remove "remedy" language or add the requirement that the finer contract contains only physically admitted changes (which Part III requires of any contract anyway) and that the new question is not a substitute for answering the old one.

### Finding 12 — Degenerate case.
If $C=\{(1,b_0)\}$, the derivation claims *anything* satisfying (F1),(F2),(A) at that single index is "one account." That is a consequence, but it is the point at which the semantics loses all discriminating power, and the Consequence paragraph does not flag it.

**Severity.** Minor-to-serious.
**Fix.** Note the degenerate case and its consequence: on the trivial contract, non-circularity and non-vacuity can still bite (they are not merely "signature" facts), but fidelity cannot.

## Derivation 3 — Selected transports underdetermined on unseen changes

**Passage attacked.** *"The component relations $L_j(a,b)$ are supplied independently for each $(a,b)$. Survival on $H$ constrains only $\{L_j(a,b):(a,b)\in H\}$. Alter $L_j(a,b)$ for one $(a,b)\notin H$ to any other admitted relation; the result survives on $H$ and differs at $(a,b)$. ∎"*

### Finding 13 — "Survival on $H$ constrains only $\{L_j(a,b):(a,b)\in H\}$" is false if $\tau$ or $\sigma$ collapses distinct indices.
A transport is $t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)$, and fidelity on $H$ is stated component-wise and globally via (F1),(F2) evaluated at $(a,b)\in H$. If $\sigma(b_0)=\sigma(b_1)$ for $b_0\in H$, $b_1\notin H$, then the E-side component relation $L_k(\sigma(b_0))$ and $L_k(\sigma(b_1))$ are the same relation, so the constraint imposed by $H$ propagates to the unseen $b_1$. Likewise for $\tau$-collapse on the edit side. The proof's "only" is therefore false without injectivity.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Add: $\tau$ and $\sigma$ are injective on $C$. Either that, or the proof must show that collapse can never occur in a selected transport, which would itself be a substantive claim.

### Finding 14 — "The result survives on $H$" is not the same as "also** surviving** on $H$"; membership in $\mathcal T$ is not shown.
Part IV: "There is a population $\mathcal T$ of candidate transports … The transport $t$ is a member of $\mathcal T$ that survived." The proof constructs $t'$ by altering $L_j(a,b)$ — it does not show $t'\in\mathcal T$, nor that $t'$ is producible by $\mu$ from the population. The claim "there exists a transport $t'$, **also surviving on $H$**" therefore overquantifies: at most, there is a transport-assignment agreeing with $t$ on $H$.

**Severity.** Fatal for the stated claim; harmless for a weaker one.
**Fix.** Restate: "there exists a transport-assignment $t'$ agreeing with $t$ on $H$ and differing at $(a,b)$, but not necessarily in $\mathcal T$." If membership is wanted, add a hypothesis like "$\mathcal T$ is closed under single-point alteration of components," which the document does not supply.

### Finding 15 — "Any other admitted relation" uses an undefined notion of "admitted relation."
Part II restricts $L_j(a,b)$ only by $L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v$. If any subset counts, "admitted" is empty of content and the proof is a tautology. If something restricts which $L_j(a,b)$ are admitted, that restriction is not stated and the proof cannot appeal to it.

**Severity.** Minor for correctness; serious for the claim that the result is about selection rather than about the size of the ambient space of relations.
**Fix.** Say explicitly what counts as "admitted," or drop the qualifier and say "any relation satisfying the typing of (O)."

### Finding 16 — Underdetermination of the *family* does not yield unpredictability of the *system*.
*"A correspondence produced by selection is faithful where it was tested and unconstrained where it was not. … It is also why surprise is possible."* The proof shows that many candidates agree on $H$; it does not show that the actual survivor will fail at any $(a,b)\notin H$. The correct consequence is "the survivor is not determined by $H$," not "the survivor will be violated on $C\setminus H$." Surprise (Part IV) is defined as a violation; underdetermination alone does not produce a violation.

**Severity.** Serious — this is exactly the move Part 0 grievance 3 makes.
**Fix.** State the consequence as: selection does not privilege a particular extension across $C\setminus H$; whether the survivor agrees with the world at $(a,b)$ is a separate fidelity fact not determined by selection provenance. That is consistent with the rest of the document; the current wording is stronger.

### Finding 17 — The proof uses nothing specific to selection.
Given that $t$ is defined by "surviving" on $H$, the argument reduces to: any transport whose fidelity constraint is confined to $H$ can be perturbed at $C\setminus H$. Selection is doing no work; the result would apply to any provenance whose fidelity constraint is bounded by a proper $H$. This is not an error but it vitiates the derivation's labelling: nothing about selection is used.

**Severity.** Minor.
**Fix.** Rename: "Any transport constrained only on a proper $H\subsetneq C$ is underdetermined on $C\setminus H$." Then note the special case of selection.

## Derivation 4 — Surprise requires an incomplete history

**Severity.** Minor.

## Derivation 5 — Question-finding is representable

**Passage attacked.** *"A contract $C$ is an organization (a set of edits with a query), and can be the content $c$ in (G). Hence an episode whose originative contribution is a new contract satisfies (G) and, where the other conjuncts hold, (EK). Proof. A contract is a subset of $A\times B$ together with $\mathcal Q$. ### Finding 21 — Type confusion: $C\subseteq A\times B$ is an index of a question $p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)$, not a question, and not an organization over $D$'s ports.
Part III defines $C$ as a subset of $A\times B$ where $A$ and $B$ are edits and boundaries of $D$. Derivation 5 then declares $C$ "an organization (a set of edits with a query)." That would make $C$ an object *on the same footing as $D$*, whose edits are operations on $C$. But Operations on $C$ are not operations on $D$; the derivation never says what $X_v$ are, what $\{V_j\}$ are, what $L_j$ are, what $B_C$ is, or what $A_C$'s composition is. "Give it ports … components … admitted edits" is a homework instruction, not a construction.

**Severity.** Fatal as a proof; the claim may be true but is unestablished.
**Fix.** Supply the tuple $(V_C,(X_{v})_{v\in V_C},J_C,B_C,A_C,L_C)$ explicitly. At a minimum: take $V_C=A\times B$ as port set with $X_{(a,b)}=\{0,1\}$ for "in/out of $C$"; components enforcing closure under the partial composition of $A$; admitted edits being single pair addition/removal. Then show Sol$_{D_C}$ has the intended interpretation.

### Finding 22 — "Together with $\mathcal Q$" smuggles the query into the contract.
Part III lists $\mathcal Q$ as a *separate* coordinate of the question $p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)$. Derivation 5 identifies "contract" with "contract **together with** $\mathcal Q$," thereby collapsing two coordinates of $p$ into one. Then "alter $\mathcal Q$" is listed as an admitted edit on the "contract"-organization. Under this scheme, the same "contract" object can change into a *different question*, which is exactly what Part III's promotion of $\mathcal Q$ to a separate coordinate was designed to prevent.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Either (a) admit that the organization-content in (G) is a *question* (the whole $p$), not a contract, and restate the claim accordingly, or (b) forbid $\mathcal Q$-alterations among the admitted edits.

### Finding 23 — Deploy requires a *physical* use task; a contract has none.
Part XII: "$\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)$ requires an owned retained realization…" and Part X: "$\operatorname{Deploy}_{ \beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)$ holds when $s$ at $\xi$ holds a representation of $c$ … integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task $U$ as a retained capability (Part XII)." Nothing in Derivation 5 says what $U_c$ is when $c$ is a contract. Part XII's tasks are physical input-to-output attribute transformations; a contract's "use" would need to be a distinct category.

**Severity.** Serious — the derivation's "Deploy … apply" oversteps into undefined territory.
**Fix.** Either extend Part XII to non-physical tasks (and say so), or restrict the claim to "Contract-as-content **can be transported to** the F1-satisfying sense required for (R)," leaving Deploy for the carrying occurrence.

### Finding 24 — Attempt is itself mis-typed when $p$ is the question whose contract is $C'$.
(G) contains $\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)$, defined in Part X as "the relation that $c$ is actually used to address $p$." If $c=C'$ and $p$ is the question with contract $C'$ (as Part III would have it, if the contract coordinate is $C'$), then "the contract is used to address the question that it is the contract of" is a definitional tautology, not an event. If $p$ is some *other* question, the derivation supplies no argument that $C'$ addresses $p$.

**Severity.** Fatal for the derivation as stated.
**Fix.** Fix the typing: either $p$ is an *upstream* question in the inquiry history to which the new contract is recruited, or the derived content is the *carrier occurrence*, with $C'$ its content.

### Finding 25 — Build and New are asserted to apply, not shown.
"Deploy, Build, and New apply." Build (Part X) requires "an actual subhistory owned by $s$ and delimited at $e$ prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of $c$, contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers." None of these is checked for $c=C'$. New requires $\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h), d\equiv_\ell c$. Neither the repertoire $R$, the content-identity $\equiv_\ell$, nor the sub-history is instantiated.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Either supply a worked mini-example (as Derivation 10 attempts for the object case) or downgrade the claim to the weaker "the semantics *may* represent question-finding as a case of (G)," not "does."

### Finding 26 — The contrast claim about other semantics is asserted, not derived.
*"A semantics that takes questions as inputs cannot represent this; the present one does."* The first half is a claim about other frameworks not made precise here (what does "takes questions as inputs" mean formally?), and the second half is not established because (G)/(EK) application to $C$ is not.

**Severity.** Minor-to-serious; rhetoric, not mathematics.
**Fix.** Either formalise the "input-style" class (e.g., as one in which contracts have only declared provenance) and prove the contrast, or delete the sentence.

## Derivation 7 — Frozen assessment and moving question are consistent

**Passage attacked.** *"An assessment event with contract $C$ and an episode in which $C$ is replaced by $C'$ with a construction witness are both representable without contradiction. Proof. ### Finding 32 — The index-theoretic move takes index-fixity for granted while Derivation 5 makes the index a constructible content.
If $C$ is a content whose provenance is declared, selected, or constructed (Part III), then $C$ itself has content that can be replaced. The "historical index" in Part VIII holds only for values that are not themselves mutable. Applying Derivation 5 to $C$ undercuts the index-fixity assumption used by Derivation 7.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Distinguish the index (a rigid designator of a contract-content) from the contract-content itself, and state the identity criterion for indices across episodes.

### Finding 33 — "Representable without contradiction" is asserted by indexing, not shown in a model.
To be a consistency proof, one should exhibit a single model in which both events occur. The derivation offers index-relativity as a sketch. "May both be true" is a modal assertion without a model.

**Severity.** Serious.
**Fix.** Add a predicate $\operatorname{Recorded}(C\to C')$ that requires a construction witness, and prove that (EK) at $e$ and Account at $C$ jointly imply $\operatorname{Recorded}$. As stated the "not licensed" sentence is a norm, not a formal consequence.

**Finding 1 — Selection witness is trivially satisfiable.**
Quote: “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once…; a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member**”.
(1) Claim attacked: the elimination clause makes “selected” a hypothesis rather than a stipulation.
(2) Why it fails: For any transport \(t\), choose \(\mathcal T=\{t,t'\}\) where \(t'\) fails on a single pair \(h\), set \(H=\{h\}\), apply any \(\mu\) once, and let the survival condition be fidelity on \(H\). Nothing requires \(\mu\) to have produced \(t\), \(t'\), or any variation; nothing requires the population or selection history to predate \(t\). A declared or constructed transport can thereby be reclassified as selected.
(3) Severity: fatal.
(4) Fix: require \(\mu\) to be causally responsible for the variation that produced the survivor/eliminated members, and require \(t\)’s presence or structure to depend counterfactually on the selection process.

**Finding 2 — The “non-triviality over events” clause is ill-typed.**
Quote: “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition.”
(1) Claim attacked: this clause rules out an internal homunculus/representation.
(2) Why it fails: events are not organizations; \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and a survival condition is a condition. “Faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition” has no defined target type. It cannot be checked.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: reformulate as a condition on representations of the defect, target, or selection criterion lying on a defined causal route.

**Finding 3 — “Causally responsible” reintroduces a primitive.**
Quote: “with the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it.”
(1) Claim attacked: provenance is derived from the physical module and history.
(2) Why it fails: Part XII lists what \(\Theta\) supplies—admitted processes, \(A_\Theta\), \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), \(\prec\), \(\operatorname{Exec}\), etc.—but not a causal-responsibility predicate. Part II derives component-level cause from signatures, which does not define causal responsibility for an event’s presence. The phrase is doing exactly the work the document elsewhere claims to have eliminated.
(3) Severity: serious/fatal for the “two primitive packages” claim.
(4) Fix: define causal responsibility via counterfactual dependence under admitted edits in \(\Theta\), or list it explicitly as a primitive.

**Finding 4 — No difference-making condition on causal responsibility.**
Quote: same passage.
(1) Claim attacked: filtering is causally responsible, not merely consistent.
(2) Why it fails: There is no condition that if the filtering had not occurred, \(t\) would not have been present. A designed transport plus any filtering event that eliminates some rival can satisfy a loose “causally responsible” reading. Overdetermination is not excluded.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: require an admitted intervention on the filtering condition that changes \(t\)’s presence or the rival’s survival.

**Finding 5 — Precedence is determined by witness existence, not physical history.**
Quote: “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*.”
(1) Claim attacked: provenance is determined by its history in the physical module.
(2) Why it fails: If witnesses can be written post hoc, provenance becomes a function of what the modeller can assert, not of what physically happened. This contradicts “A transport… has a provenance determined by its history in the physical module” and Part I’s explanatory realism.
(3) Severity: serious/fatal.
(4) Fix: determine provenance by actual causal origination; require witnesses to be unique and non-gerrymanderable, or define a causal-origin rule.

**Finding 6 — A plainly selected transport can be gerrymandered into a constructed one.**
Quote: “\(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\)”.
(1) Claim attacked: constructed provenance is distinct from selected provenance.
(2) Why it fails: Any selected transport already has components bound to anchor subnetworks. One can declare an episode whose owned subhistory “prepares” the same organization and whose bindings are “nontrivial” because deleting them breaks (F1). The target (e.g., the environment) is already instantiated before \(e\). There is no requirement that the episode causally originated \(t\), that \(t\) was absent before it, or that the construction is not merely maintenance.
(3) Severity: fatal.
(4) Fix: require the construction witness to establish causal origination, prior absence, and that the binding construction—not selection or design—fixed \(t\)’s structure.

**Finding 7 — The claimed separation is false: selection witnesses also contain targets instantiated before variation.**
Quote: “a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”
(1) Claim attacked: the two provenances are kept apart by target availability and defect representation.
(2) Why it fails: \(\operatorname{Sel}\) requires a history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered and a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\). Those are edits and boundary conditions on the target organization \(D\). \(D\) must be instantiated for the encounters and fidelity checks to occur. So a selection witness does contain a target instantiated before the variation. The sentence is false on its face.
(3) Severity: fatal for the stated distinction.
(4) Fix: distinguish “target as environment” from “target as content/blueprint available for construction”; or remove the clause.

**Finding 8 — Blindness uses “representation” either circularly or primitively.**
Quote: “no itemised representation of the defect… lies on the route from the defect to the variation”.
(1) Claim attacked: blindness is defined without importing representation.
(2) Why it fails: If “representation” means the defined predicate \(\operatorname{Rep}\) of \((R)\), then determining blindness requires knowing the provenance of that representation transport—selected or constructed—which may itself require a blindness check. If it means ordinary representation, it is an undefined primitive. Either way the definition is not well-founded.
(3) Severity: serious/fatal.
(4) Fix: define defect representation structurally without using \(\operatorname{Rep}\), e.g., an information-bearing state that discriminates defect identity on a defined causal route.

**Finding 9 — “Route from the defect to the variation” is undefined.**
Quote: same passage.
(1) Claim attacked: the blindness condition can be checked.
(2) Why it fails: Part IX defines an “active route” for histories of occurrences, but the route from a defect state to a variation-operator application is not defined. A defect may be an abstract fidelity failure, not a component in a history.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: define the route as a causal path in \(\Theta\)’s process order from the defect occurrence to the variation event carrying the representation.

**Finding 10 — The scalar-summary allowance makes blindness unstable.**
Quote: “a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.”
(1) Claim attacked: blind processes are those without itemised defect representation.
(2) Why it fails: A scalar can encode itemised information—e.g., an integer index of the failing pair, a Gödel number, or a loss value that depends on which expected value was violated. “How badly” may require representing what would have held. The itemised/scalar distinction is not stable without a formal restriction.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: define the permitted scalar as invariant under permutation of defect identities, or forbid any state that discriminates which defect occurred.

**Finding 11 — Blindness is inconsistent with Part X’s construction witness.**
Quote Part IV: “a construction witness contains both.” Quote Part X: “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization.”
(1) Claim attacked: construction witnesses contain an itemised defect representation; selection witnesses do not.
(2) Why it fails: Part X’s construction witness does not require any itemised representation of a defect. The “recognized difficulty” and “conjectural objection” belong to a complete critical episode, not to the construction witness itself. The contrast is asserted in Part IV but not imposed by the definitions.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: add an explicit itemised-defect-representation conjunct to \(\operatorname{Build}\) or \(\operatorname{Con}\), or remove the contrast from Part IV.

**Finding 12 — A blind process can satisfy Part X’s construction witness.**
Quote Part X: “contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**”.
(1) Claim attacked: construction is non-blind; selection is blind.
(2) Why it fails: A process guided only by a scalar error signal can produce a new binding that is required for use—deleting it breaks (F1) or (A)—and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. It satisfies \(\operatorname{Build}\) as written, even though Part IV says construction contains an itemised defect representation. So construction is not necessarily sighted.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: require the binding construction to be causally guided by an itemised defect representation, not merely accompanied by a scalar or by a recognized difficulty elsewhere.

**Finding 13 — Survival condition can eliminate for reasons unrelated to \(H\).**
Quote: “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member**”.
(1) Claim attacked: elimination is due to differential fidelity on the encountered history.
(2) Why it fails: The condition may require fidelity on \(H\) plus arbitrary extra properties. An eliminated member may pass \(H\) and fail the extra condition. The non-triviality clause only requires that some member fails \(H\), not that the eliminated member failed \(H\), nor that \(H\) was decisive.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: require every eliminated member to fail \(H\), and require that failure on \(H\) is the operative cause of elimination.

**Finding 14 — The variation operator need not vary anything relevant.**
Quote: “a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once”.
(1) Claim attacked: the variation operator is part of the selection process.
(2) Why it fails: There is no requirement that \(\mu\) produced the survivor, the eliminated member, or any member of \(\mathcal T\). An identity operator applied once satisfies the clause. The clause is satisfied by any physical event labelled \(\mu\).
(3) Severity: serious/fatal.
(4) Fix: require \(\mu\) to generate at least one member from another and to be causally relevant to the survivor’s or eliminated member’s structure.

**Finding 15 — Population members need not be comparable.**
Quote: “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports”.
(1) Claim attacked: the population is a population of competitors for the same transport task.
(2) Why it fails: Nothing requires all members to share domain, codomain, target, contract, or task. A rival can fail \(H\) merely because it is a transport for something else. The survival condition then eliminates it for irrelevant reasons.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: require all members of \(\mathcal T\) to share target, contract, and task, and to be variants of the same transport type.

**Finding 16 — \(H\) and \(C\) can be gerrymandered.**
Quote: “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial”.
(1) Claim attacked: the history \(H\) provides a nontrivial test.
(2) Why it fails: \(C\) can be chosen as \(H\) itself, or as a protective contract. The selection witness does not require \(C\) to be adequate, non-protective, or independently fixed. Any \(t\) can be selected by picking an \(H\) on which it passes and a rival fails.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: require \(C\) to be independently fixed for the selection claim, or at least require \(H\) to be representative and \(C\) non-protective.

**Finding 17 — Maintenance clause contradicts precedence.**
Quote: “Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.” Quote: “otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists”.
(1) Claim attacked: later maintenance does not alter provenance.
(2) Why it fails: If a declared transport is later maintained by a selection process, a selection witness exists, and the precedence rule changes its provenance to selected. If it is later maintained by a construction process, precedence changes it to constructed. The sentence forbidding provenance change by maintenance is not enforced.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: define origination; restrict selection and construction witnesses to origination events, not maintenance; or remove the maintenance sentence.

**Finding 18 — Construction can be rewritten as selection if the defect representation is off the route.**
Quote: “Construction is not selection. A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation…”
(1) Claim attacked: construction and selection are disjoint.
(2) Why it fails: \(\operatorname{Con}\) does not formally require an itemised defect representation. \(\operatorname{Sel}\) only forbids such a representation on the route from defect to variation. A construction episode with an itemised defect representation can still satisfy \(\operatorname{Sel}\) if the variation operator is not causally downstream of that representation—e.g., random search. The clauses do not require the same history or route for the two witnesses.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: make \(\operatorname{Con}\) require an itemised defect representation and make \(\operatorname{Sel}\) forbid it anywhere in the selection history or on any causal route to variation.

**Finding 19 — The real collapse is selection into construction, and the document does not list it as an attack.**
Quote Part XV: “Construction reduced to selection. A demonstration that every construction witness… can be rewritten as a selection witness…”
(1) Claim attacked: the document’s attack list covers the collapse of the two provenances.
(2) Why it fails: The document only considers construction → selection. The precedence rule makes the converse—selection → construction—easier. Any selected transport can be given a post hoc construction witness by calling its existing anchor map a nontrivial binding construction and its environment an already-instantiated target. Precedence then reclassifies it as constructed. This falsely attributes creativity to selection and undermines “Creativity lives in construction.”
(3) Severity: fatal.
(4) Fix: require construction witnesses to establish causal origination and prior absence; add the selection → construction direction to Part XV’s attack list.

**Finding 20 — “Realized transport” is undefined.**
Quote: “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports”.
(1) Claim attacked: selection is a physical claim under \(\Theta\).
(2) Why it fails: \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) is defined for occurrences and organizations, not for transports. There is no definition of what it is for a transport tuple \((\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\) to be physically realized. This is another hidden primitive in the selection witness.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: define realization of a transport via \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) of its domain and codomain plus physically implemented maps, or add it explicitly to \(\Theta\).

**Finding 21 — “Actually encountered” has no subject.**
Quote: “edit–boundary pairs actually encountered”.
(1) Claim attacked: \(H\) is a physical history.
(2) Why it fails: It is not specified who or what encounters the pairs—the population, the surviving transport, the system, the environment, or the modeller. Without a subject, the clause is not checkable.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: specify the encountering process and its occurrence in the physical module.

**Finding 22 — The survival condition is a condition, not yet a physical process.**
Quote: “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\)”.
(1) Claim attacked: the survival condition is enacted by the environment.
(2) Why it fails: The formal definition gives a condition on transports, not a physical process that differentially persists them. Part XII says it is enacted by the environment, but the bridge from condition to enactment is not defined. This leaves another gap between the abstract witness and the physical claim.
(3) Severity: serious.
(4) Fix: define the survival condition as an environment process whose outcome is differential persistence of population members according to fidelity on \(H\).

1. **Sel does not actually require blindness.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation; a scalar summary … is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted. This is the only definition of selection…”  
   **Why it fails:** The formal statement of \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) lists population, variation operator, history, survival condition, causal responsibility. It never mentions blindness or absence of itemised defect representations. The prose immediately after says “This is the only definition of selection,” but the actual holds-when clause does not include the condition. So either blindness is part of selection and is missing from the definition, or it is not part of selection and the separation from construction collapses.  
   **Severity:** fatal to the provenance distinction.  
   **Fix:** Add an explicit conjunct to \(\operatorname{Sel}\): the variation was not caused by, and no event on the route from defect to variation carries, an itemised representation of the defect. State whether scalar summaries are allowed as part of \(\operatorname{Sel}\), not only in a separate definition of blindness.

2. **The selection witness is trivially satisfiable by a just-so construction.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “\(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) holds when there are: a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once … a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) … a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\); and \(t\) is a member that survived…”  
   **Why it fails:** Take any transport \(t\) faithful on a single pair \((a,b)\). Let \(\mathcal T=\{t,t'\}\), where \(t'\) is identical to \(t\) except it fails fidelity at \((a,b)\). Let \(\mu\) be the identity or any trivial operator applied once. Let \(H=\{(a,b)\}\). Let the survival condition be “be faithful on \(H\).” Then \(t'\) is eliminated, \(t\) survives, and the environment can be stipulated to be causally responsible. The non-triviality clause is vacuous or ill-typed, and blindness is absent from \(\operatorname{Sel}\). Thus any transport that can be paired with a failing counterpart is “selected.” This is not a substantive provenance condition.  
   **Severity:** fatal.  
   **Fix:** Require that \(t\) be produced by \(\mu\), that survival eliminate a competitor because of failure at the non-trivial pair, and that \(t\)’s presence be counterfactually dependent on the survival process. Add blindness to the formal definition.

3. **The “non-triviality” event clause is ill-typed or vacuous.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of \(\Sigma_h\): no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition.”  
   **Why it fails:** \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition is a predicate on transports. There is no defined relation “faithful transport to \(H\)” or “faithful transport to the survival condition.” Events are occurrences, not maps between organizations. The condition cannot be checked. If read charitably, it is vacuous because no event has the type of a transport.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Replace with a typed condition: no event on the route from defect to variation instantiates an organization that represents the defect itemised, or carries a record pairing failure state with what would have held. Do not use “faithful transport to \(H\).”

4. **The non-trivial pair need not be the pair that causes elimination.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) … non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it); a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member**…”  
   **Why it fails:** The witness does not require the eliminated member to fail at the non-trivial pair. A member could fail some other pair in \(H\), be eliminated there, while the “non-trivial” pair is irrelevant. The selection history can therefore contain a decorative non-trivial pair that does no work.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Require that the eliminated member(s) fail at a pair that is actually responsible for elimination, and that the survivor’s survival depends on passing that pair.

5. **“Causally responsible” reintroduces a primitive.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “with the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it.”  
   **Why it fails:** Causation is not defined in the semantics; Part XIV lists only two primitive packages: the physical module \(\Theta\) and the normative relation \(\mathcal N\). Part XII’s list of what \(\Theta\) supplies does not include causal responsibility. The document elsewhere claims causes are derived from edit-signatures (Part II), not primitive labels. Using “causally responsible” as an unanalysed ingredient of selection contradicts that claim.  
   **Severity:** serious, arguably fatal to the “no causal primitive” claim.  
   **Fix:** Either add causal responsibility to \(\Theta\) and list it as primitive, or define it via counterfactual dependence in \(\Theta\): e.g., if the survival condition had not eliminated competitors, \(t\) would not be present in the population at the later time.

6. **The causal-responsibility clause does not ensure \(t\)’s presence was caused by filtering.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “with the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence…”  
   **Why it fails:** If \(t\) is already a member of \(\mathcal T\) before the survival condition acts, then filtering may cause \(t\) to be the survivor, but it does not cause \(t\)’s presence. The witness permits \(t\) to be pre-existing and merely not eliminated. “Causally responsible for presence” is false in the ordinary counterfactual sense in such cases.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Require \(t\) to be generated by \(\mu\) or to be a descendant of a generated variant, and require that without the survival process \(t\) would not be present in the relevant population at the relevant later time.

7. **The variation operator is not required to act on \(t\) or to produce the variation.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\)…”  
   **Why it fails:** \(\mu\) could be the identity, could act only on irrelevant members, or could be applied after all relevant differences already exist. The witness never requires that \(t\), its lineage, or the relevant variant was produced or altered by \(\mu\).  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Require \(t\) to be in the image of \(\mu\), or descended from a member altered by \(\mu\), and require the variation to be admitted by the physical module.

8. **“Actually encountered” is undefined and gerrymanderable.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered…”  
   **Why it fails:** Encountered by whom or what? The population, the survivor, the environment, the modeller? There is no relation in the semantics defining encounter. Without it, \(H\) can be stipulated to contain exactly the pairs that make the desired transport survive.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define encounter in the physical module: e.g., an event in \(\Sigma_h\) in which the target organization is instantiated and the edit–boundary pair occurs on an active route to the survival condition.

9. **The precedence rule is over existential witnesses, not over origination, so later construction can reclassify a selected transport.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “with the following precedence: it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.”  
   **Why it fails:** The rule does not say the construction witness must be the origination event. If a transport originates by selection, and later some episode constructs a copy, model, or reconstruction that “prepares \(t\),” then a construction witness exists. By the stated precedence, it is constructed. The next sentence says maintenance histories do not change provenance, but reconstruction is not maintenance under Part X (“Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not”). The rule therefore either contradicts itself or allows gerrymandering by adding a later construction episode.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Make provenance depend on the origination event only. Define transport identity so later reconstructions are new transports, or explicitly exclude construction witnesses downstream of the transport’s origination.

10. **The formal construction witness does not require an itemised defect representation, despite the separation claim.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”  
   **Why it fails:** Part X’s \(\operatorname{Build}\) requires an owned subhistory, a nontrivial binding construction, and not being a composition of content-preserving transfers. The construction witness identifies controlled processes, incoming carriers, bindings constructed, and the resulting organization. It does not require an itemised defect representation. The episode contains a “recognized difficulty” and a “conjectural objection,” but those are not the same as “a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held.” So the claimed separation is not backed by the definitions.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Add an explicit itemised-defect-representation condition to \(\operatorname{Con}\) and \(\operatorname{Build}\), or remove it from the separation claim.

11. **Blindness is too weak because scalar summaries can encode the defect.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.”  
   **Why it fails:** A scalar summary can be a sufficient statistic for a small defect space. If there are two possible defects, a scalar 0/1 identifies the defect. A hill-climber using a scalar fitness function can be guided to the correct transport by feedback from fidelity. That is not blind in any interesting sense. The definition permits exactly the kind of directed search that the selection/construction distinction is supposed to exclude.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define blindness as independence of variation from any function of fidelity, or restrict scalar summaries to cases where they cannot identify the defect or guide the variation to it.

12. **The claim that selection has no target instantiated before variation is false for ordinary selection.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation…”  
   **Why it fails:** \(\operatorname{Sel}\) requires \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered. Those pairs are edits on the target organization \(D\) in the transport \(t:D\to E\). So \(D\) is instantiated during the selection history. If \(H\) occurs before the variation operator acts, the target is instantiated before variation. Nothing in the definition forbids this. The claimed separation is therefore inconsistent with the selection definition.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Change the separation to “no representation of the target before variation,” not “no target instantiated before variation,” or add a temporal condition to \(\operatorname{Sel}\) if that is intended.

13. **Construction can be gerrymandered because “nontrivial binding construction” is a low bar.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\); it is **required for the use** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for \(c\) on its contract.”  
   **Why it fails:** Almost any new active component can be described as a binding not already in the repertoire and whose deletion breaks fidelity or the answer. The condition does not require the construction to be creative in any stronger sense, nor does it prevent a selected transport from being redescribed as constructed by positing an episode that “constructs” a new binding for it.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Require the construction to produce a transport not already present in the repertoire, to use an itemised defect representation on the route from defect to binding, and to be non-redundant with any prior selection history.

14. **Under the formal clauses, construction and selection are not mutually exclusive.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “Construction is not selection. A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”  
   **Why it fails:** \(\operatorname{Sel}\) omits blindness and the no-itemised-representation condition. \(\operatorname{Con}\) omits the itemised-representation condition. Therefore a single history can satisfy both formal definitions: it can have a population, variation, history, eliminating survival condition, and causal responsibility (if stipulated), and it can also have a construction episode with a nontrivial binding. By the precedence rule, it is constructed. Conversely, a construction episode with only scalar criticism can satisfy \(\operatorname{Sel}\) if blindness is not in \(\operatorname{Sel}\). The rewrite from one to the other is not blocked by the formal definitions.  
   **Severity:** fatal to the claim that the two provenances are distinct.  
   **Fix:** Put the differentiating conditions into the formal definitions themselves, and prove or state that they are mutually exclusive.

15. **Blindness is inconsistent with Part X’s construction witness.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation…” plus “a construction witness contains both” [itemised representation and target instantiated before variation].  
   **Why it fails:** Part X’s construction witness does not require an itemised defect representation. It requires a nontrivial binding construction and identifies controlled processes, incoming carriers, bindings, and resulting organization. A process could satisfy Part X’s construction witness while having only a scalar summary of the defect and no itemised representation. Such a process would be blind under Part IV’s definition but constructed under Part X’s definition. The two parts use different conditions for the same distinction.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Align the definitions: either add an itemised defect representation to Part X’s construction witness, or remove it from Part IV’s blindness/separation and define the distinction some other way.

16. **The “no event is a faithful transport” clause does not block trivial selection.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition.”  
   **Why it fails:** Even if this clause were well-typed, a trivial filter that eliminates a failing variant need not contain any event that is a faithful transport to \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition. It can be a simple physical death. The clause therefore does not prevent the trivial construction in Finding 2.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Replace with a condition that the variation is not guided by an itemised representation of the defect, and define the route from defect to variation.

17. **“Population of realized transports” is undefined.**  
   **Claim attacked:** “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)…”  
   **Why it fails:** Transports are maps \(t:D\to E\), not occurrences. “Realized” is not defined. The physical module supplies \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\) for occurrences and organizations, but not a realization relation for transports. Without it, the population can be populated by fiat.  
   **Severity:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define a realized transport as an occurrence or process that instantiates the source and target organizations and the mapping, with the physical module licensing the realization.

# Referee report — Part IV, its precedence rule, and the Part X relation

Numbers are findings, not severities. Severities appear inside each finding.

## A. Triviality of the selection witness

**1. The variation clause is satisfiable by an operator that varies nothing.**
Attacked text: "a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\)".
(1) Claim: an applied variation operator is part of what makes a selection witness non-vacuous.
(2) Failure: the clause requires only that \(\mu\) be *applied*, not that any member of \(\mathcal T\) be altered by it, not that the altered member be the eliminated one, and not that the survivor be descended from an altered member. \(\mu\) = identity, applied once, satisfies it. Nothing in the clause links the "variation" to the "survival"; the word 'variation' carries the intended meaning, the clause does not.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: require \(\mu\)'s application to produce at least one member of \(\mathcal T\) differing from the member it was applied to, and require the eliminated member, or the ancestor of \(t\), to be among the products of \(\mu\).

**2. The population clause is trivially satisfiable.**
Attacked text: "a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)".
(1) Claim: the population requirement blocks trivial selection.
(2) Failure: no condition that members be variants of a common ancestor, alternatives for the same role, or even transports between the same \(D\) and \(E\). Any two realized transports in the system (a visual tracker and an auditory tracker) are a population of two. On the document's own account, \(\mathcal T\) is also not indexed by time, so synchronic co-existence of two mechanisms substitutes for diachronic variation.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: require \(\mathcal T\) to be a set of alternatives for one role with a declared common ancestry or a declared generation relation to a prior population, and index it temporally.

**3. The survival condition is not independent of fidelity; the clause collapses to a comparison.**
Attacked text: "a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)".
(1) Claim: the survival condition does independent work.
(2) Failure: if the survival condition *is* "fidelity on \(H\)", then by definition \(t\) (a survivor) is faithful on \(H\), and the elimination clause only adds that some other member failed on \(H\). The witness therefore reduces to: "there exist ≥2 transports, one of which failed fidelity on some non-empty \(H\) where \(t\) did not." That is satisfied by taking any \(t\) and any deliberately broken comparator.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: require the survival condition to be stated as the environment's own constraint, independently of the fidelity predicate, and require the eliminated and surviving members to have been *candidates before* the condition operated.

**4. "Eliminated" is not an event.**
Attacked text: "that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)".
(1) Claim: elimination is a physical event, checkable in principle (Grievance 9).
(2) Failure: no requirement that the eliminated member was present and viable before, that it would have been present absent the condition, or that the condition caused its removal rather than registering that it was already gone. A post hoc partition of \(\mathcal T\) into survivors and non-survivors satisfies the clause.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: require a temporally ordered population, a state before and after the condition, and that the eliminated member is present before and absent after, with the condition's operation on \(H\) as the difference-maker.

**5. No non-vacuity requirement on the survivor at \(H\).**
Attacked text: "\(t\) is a member that survived".
(1) Claim: the survivor is a substantive transport.
(2) Failure: a component imposing the full relation on its ports satisfies (F1) wherever its anchor's solution set projects to everything. Such a transport can survive on \(H\) while encoding nothing, and the witness gives no clause excluding it. Compare Part V's explicit non-vacuity failure for the repackaged \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) — Part IV has no analogue.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: require the survivor's solution set at some pair in \(H\) to be a proper subset of the full relation on its visible ports.

**6. \(H\subseteq C\) permits non-admitted pairs in a history "actually encountered", and ties selection to a contract chosen later.**
Attacked text: "a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered".
(1) Claim: selection is a physical history, checkable against the module.
(2) Failure, two ways. (a) \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\) and \(K\) is "a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit". So \(H\) may contain pairs no physical process could encounter, while the clause says "actually encountered". (b) \(C\) is the contract of a *question*, which a modeller fixes when writing the model, whereas the encounters predate the question. Selection is thus relativised to a later choice of contract.
(3) Both serious; (a) is a contradiction inside one clause.
(4) Fix: require \(H\subseteq(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap A_\Theta\times B)\) and require \(C\) to be chosen afterwards, containing \(H\).

**7. Fidelity on \(H\) is ill-typed across a population of transports.**
Attacked text: "containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)".
(1) Claim: a common history tests a common population.
(2) Failure: fidelity is (F1)+(F2) between two organizations under a contract. If population members are variants of the *transport*, their targets \(E\) differ, and possibly their \(D\) and port sets; "fidelity on \(H\)" is then per-member and \(H\) is not a well-defined set of pairs over which they are compared. If members share \(D\) and \(E\), the clause is fine — but that is never required.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: require all members of \(\mathcal T\) to be transports between the same \(D,E\) at a common grain and contract, or define the comparison as a group judgement on \(E\)'s answer profile.

## C. Non-triviality, blindness, and the "route"

**11. The non-triviality clause is ill-typed.**

(2) Failure: blindness is about defects, representations and routes; Build is about bindings, repertoires and \(\equiv_\ell\). Neither definition mentions the other's key term. There is no proposition on which they could agree or disagree, so the claim that the blindness definition is *consistent with* Build is not merely unproved, it is not yet a claim. The substantive question — does sightedness with respect to a defect entail the existence of a nontrivial binding construction? — is nowhere addressed.
(3) Serious as a claim about the joint partition of provenances.
(4) Fix: give one definition of "defect" and one of "binding", and prove or stipulate the relation between being sighted to a defect and constructing a binding.

**33. "Which defect" is incompatible between the two.**
Attacked text: Part IV, "blind with respect to a defect"; Part X, "a recognized difficulty".
(1) Claim: both talk about the same defect.
(2) Failure: in Sel the natural defect is the *eliminated member's* failure at the non-trivial \(H\)-pair. In a construction episode the difficulty may be a recognized failure of an incoming carrier that is not a member of any population, and may not have been tested at any \(H\). The two definitions of "defect" range over different objects.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: define the defect once, in the vocabulary of (E) and (F1), as a pair \((a,b)\) at which a specified organization's anchored relation differs from the target's.

## G. Overclaims

**34. The elimination clause does not do the work Grievance 9 assigns it.**
Attacked text: "Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation."
(1) Claim: the elimination clause converts "selected" from stipulation to falsifiable hypothesis.
(2) Failure: given findings 3–5, the clause is satisfied by any transport alongside any broken comparator on any non-empty \(H\), and "eliminated" names no event. The clause therefore does not distinguish a stipulation from a hypothesis; it distinguishes nothing from a slightly less trivial nothing.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: replace the clause with a temporal, causal, event-based elimination condition (findings 3–5) before making the claim.

**35. Part XIV's derivation claim for provenance is false.**
Attacked text: "**Derived.** Roles, from admitted edits (Part II). Forward kinds, from signatures (K); historical kinds, from provenance witnesses… **Provenance, from physical history (Part IV, XII)**."
(1) Claim: provenance is derived, not primitive.
(2) Failure: provenance depends on the precedence rule, which is a convention not in history (finding 17); on "causally responsible", which is not derivable from (K) (finding 8); and on the existence of witnesses rather than the facts they witness (finding 18). At least one of these must be added to the primitive or declared lists, and Part XIV's "The semantics has two [primitive packages]… It claims that they are all listed" becomes false.
(3) Fatal to the two-package claim in its present form.
(4) Fix: either supply the missing derivations or move "causal responsibility" and "precedence" into the primitive/declared lists, and re-issue the constitutive conjecture accordingly.

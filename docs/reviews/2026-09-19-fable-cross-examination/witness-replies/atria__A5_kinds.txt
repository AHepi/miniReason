## Attack on (C): kinds as edit-signatures

**1. The Part 0 slogan is vacuous, and its vacuity is what lets historical kinds back in.**

(1) Claim attacked: Part 0 — "at any level of detail, a *kind* is nothing over and above how a component responds to the changes that level admits. Two components no admitted change can separate are one kind at that level, whatever labels anyone attaches to them."

(2) Why it fails: by Part III, every contract "contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)," and \(1\in A\) is the identity edit. So \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)\) always contains \((1,b_0,L_j(1,b_0))\). A component's *static content* therefore enters the signature through the identity "change." The slogan can only be read as "kind = response to admitted changes, identity included," at which point it imposes no dispositional constraint at all: any static predicate, including a purely etiological one, is a signature difference carried at \((1,b_0)\). The philosophical punch — kinds are exhausted by dispositions-to-change — is not delivered by (K); it is undermined by (K).

(3) Seriousness: serious. This is the thesis of (C), and it survives only as a tautology.

(4) Fix: state the slogan as "a kind is nothing over and above the component's relations on the contract," and then argue separately that etiological predicates are not among those relations. That argument is not in the document.

**2. Moved goalpost: etiological kind-differences do work, but the work is done by \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\), not by edit-signature.**

(1) Claim attacked: Grievance 1 — "Declared kind-labels added no discriminating power; where a separating change exists, edit-fidelity already finds it, and where none exists, the label was asserting a distinction the level does not contain." Plus Part IV: "The one thing (R) takes from the outside is \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\)… supplied by the physical module, not by the semantics," and Part XIV, listing \(\Theta\) (including \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\)) as primitive.

(2) Why it fails: take two components that agree on every nontrivial change in \(C\) but differ in causal origin — a Vermeer and a molecule-for-molecule van Meegeren forgery; an organism adapted by selection and one engineered to the same physiology; an original and a duplicate. Part II explicitly permits "histories" as port values. If \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) instantiates a lineage-history port, the two are different *kinds* — but the difference is a baseline port value, and no nontrivial admitted change separates them; the "separating change" is the identity edit, which separates any two things with different port values and hence discriminates nothing. If \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) does not instantiate that port, the forgery and the original are one kind at every contract, and the question "is this a Vermeer?" is unrepresentable — though it is a well-formed question with a determinate answer. Either way, the discriminating work was performed by the primitive \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\), and the semantics then claims the credit for edit-fidelity.

(3) Seriousness: fatal to the philosophical claim of (C); the formal machinery survives only because the question is relocated rather than answered.

(4) Fix: prove that \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) cannot supply etiological ports, or concede that kind-elimination is conditional on a choice the semantics declares primitive and does not argue for.

**3. Internal inconsistency: the framework itself uses a historical, non-signature kind as representation-constituting.**

(1) Claim attacked: Part IV (R), \(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists t[\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\), together with "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does."

(2) Why it fails: two transports can share \(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda\) and be faithful on the same \(C\), differing only in provenance — one selected, one declared. Their edit-signatures are identical, yet one represents and the other does not. The difference is pure causal history. So the document asserts, for transports, exactly the etiological individuation it denies for components. No argument is given for why history may individuate transports but not kinds. (Part XII's "Selection in the physical module" makes the same move: selection is "a claim about a physical history," checkable and fallible — a real predicate, not a label.)

(3) Seriousness: serious. (C)'s elimination argument is applied selectively.

(4) Fix: state the asymmetry and defend it (e.g., transports are occurrences in a physical history, components are slots in an organization), or admit etiological component-kinds alongside provenance.

**4. Part IX's "active route" is a counterexample to Derivation 1's corollary, from inside the document.**

(1) Claim attacked: Part V ("Where \(C\) contains no such change, the two are one kind on \(C\) by (K)… There is no third case") and Derivation 1's corollary ("A condition 'each component must anchor to a component of the same kind' adds nothing to (F1) on any contract"), together with Derivation 2's consequence.

(2) Why it fails: Part IX defines an **active route** as "a connected subnetwork of *actual* occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result." Consider a system with two redundant mechanisms, one actually operative and one a backup that would respond identically. Anchor component \(k\) to the operative route or to the backup; both anchors satisfy (F1) (same projection on \(V_k\)), both give the same answer profile, so by Derivation 2 the two candidates are "one account at grain \(C\)." But Part IX's active-route condition distinguishes them: the backup is not composed of actual occurrences. The condition "anchor to the actually operative route" therefore does discriminating work that (F1) and (K) do not supply. There *is* a third case: same signature, different causal history.

(3) Seriousness: serious. The corollary is false as stated within the document's own terms.

(4) Fix: add an actuality/actuality-of-route condition to Account, or show that "active route" is derivable from signatures on \(C\) (it is not, since the backup has the same signature when \(C\) contains no switchover edit).

**5. Derivation 1's proof equivocates on (K).**

(1) Claim attacked: Derivation 1, "By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\)… (F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise. ∎"

(2) Why it fails: (K) defines signatures for *components* as \(\{(a,b,L_j(a,b))\}\). \(\lambda(k)\) is a *subnetwork*, and the object written down is a *projected solution set*, not \(L_j\). (K) does not define this; the proof introduces a new notion and calls it (K). The projection is precisely the lossy step — two subnetworks with the same projection on \(V_k\) and different internal structure are identified — and the corollary's conclusion ("no further condition about kinds to state") rests on that unlicensed, lossy extension.

(3) Seriousness: serious for the derivation's validity; the corollary does not follow from (K) as written.

(4) Fix: define subnetwork signatures explicitly, prove (F1) preserves them, and argue that the projection is harmless — noting that Finding 4 shows it is not always harmless.

**6. "They are one kind at that contract" is an answer about models and an evasion about the world.**

(1) Claim attacked: Grievance 2 — "and then they are one kind *at that level*, which is correct rather than a loss"; Derivation 2's consequence — "A claim that two such candidates 'really' differ is a claim that some admitted change separates them, and must supply it."

(2) Why it fails: (K)-identity is relative to the pair \((\Theta,C)\), since \(\Theta\) fixes which ports and history-values the target instantiates. So "one kind at \(C\)" is a fact about the pairing of a primitive with a contract, not a fact about the world. Presenting it as "correct rather than a loss" overreaches. And the prescribed remedy fails for the case the prompt names, where the *question* fixes the contract and asks which of two mechanisms produced the effect: if the two are indistinguishable on the question's contract, there is no finer contract to retreat to — the question *is* the finer contract; if \(\Theta\) supplies the etiology, then the two candidates do not both satisfy (A) and Derivation 2 never applies. So Derivation 2's underdetermination result is narrower than advertised, and the historical case is handled by \(\Theta\) or not at all.

(3) Seriousness: serious — this is the direct answer to the question posed. It is an evasion whenever the question is world-directed and etiological.

(4) Fix: index every kind-claim to \((\Theta,C)\) explicitly, and mark etiological questions as requiring \(\Theta\)-supplied history ports, with the representational gap stated rather than silently closed.

**7. Grievance 9 conflates stipulations with etiological predicates.**

(1) Claim attacked: Grievance 9 — "A kind-label is not a claim about anything; it is a label. The difference is the difference between a hypothesis and a stipulation."

(2) Why it fails: "is a Vermeer," "is a selected transport," "is the actual cause," "is a member of this lineage" are not labels. They are claims about causal history with truth values and checkable (in principle) witnesses — exactly like the selection histories Part XII insists are "fallible but checkable." The official reply to the natural objection depends on pretending all kind-predicates are arbitrary tags.

(3) Seriousness: serious; this is the document's stated defence against precisely this attack.

(4) Fix: distinguish declared labels from etiological predicates, and show why the latter cannot do discriminating work — which requires answering Findings 2 and 4, not asserting the point.

**8. Dispositions are not a counterexample, but they expose that the work is done by contract choice.**

(1) Claim attacked: Grievance 1's "you can [tell cause from correlation], and only this way."

(2) Why it fails as stated: fragility of an unstruck glass is invisible on a contract with no striking edit; fissility is invisible on a contract with no neutron-bombardment edit. Adding the stimulus is an act of *question-finding*, which Part 0 calls "at least half of creativity" and which carries provenance. So the discriminating power lives in the pairing (signature, contract), not in the signature. Worse, a disposition keyed to a stimulus the physics forbids is unrepresentable on every contract, since "the physical theory fixes which changes are possible at all" (Grievance 4) — a real loss, not a scoping success.

(3) Seriousness: minor-to-serious; Grievance 1's confidence is unwarranted, but the framework can absorb most of this by contract expansion.

(4) Fix: rewrite Grievance 1 as "relative to a contract containing the relevant intervention, edit-fidelity distinguishes cause from correlation; the choice of contract is a separate, provenance-bearing act."

**9. Minor: kind-identity is relative to the transport, not just to the contract.**

(1) Claim attacked: Part II, "Kinds are therefore relative to the contract."

(2) Why it fails: (K) requires "a bijection of their footprints," and for a component \(k\) versus its anchor \(\lambda(k)\), that bijection is supplied by \(\lambda\)'s port translation. Changing \(\lambda\) can change kind-identity while holding \(C\) fixed.

(3) Seriousness: minor.

(4) Fix: index kind-claims to \((C,\lambda)\).

---

Nothing in the above requires that correspondence be primitive. What it does require is that the document stop claiming kinds are eliminated when it has, at best, relocated them into \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) — a primitive it does not argue about — while simultaneously relying on historical individuation for provenance and for active routes.

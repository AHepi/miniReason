# 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained:** the revised Part IV selection witness, and its relation to Part X’s construction witness and Part IV’s precedence rule.

**Over what range:** all physical systems and modelled organisations where the semantics claims a transport’s provenance. The definitions held fixed are Parts II, III, IV, V, X, XII, XIV, XV.

**What is asked:** the user’s five attacks:

1. Is the revised selection witness still trivially satisfiable by some construction?
2. Does “causally responsible” reintroduce a primitive?
3. Does the precedence rule let a plainly selected transport count as constructed because a construction witness can be gerrymandered?
4. Can every construction witness be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying these clauses, or vice versa?
5. Is the blindness definition consistent with Part X’s construction witness?

**Kind of question:** rule/definition and sufficiency/attack; also a provenance question. Not whether selection is good, nor whether the semantics is true.

# 2. The explanation in parts

The core of the revised selection witness is:

> “**Selected.** \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) holds when there are: a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\); a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it); a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\); and \(t\) is a member that survived, with the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it. Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of \(\Sigma_h\): no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition. A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation; a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted. This is the only definition of selection; Part XII says what makes it a physical claim.”

The separation between selection and construction is stated in Part IV as:

> “What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”

The precedence rule is:

> “with the following precedence: it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.”

The construction witness in Part IV is:

> “**Constructed.** \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module’s sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below.”

The construction witness in Part X is:

> “**Construction.** \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an owned subhistory of \(h\), delimited at \(e\), prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. … A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.”

Part X also repeats the separation:

> “Construction is not selection. A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”

Part XII says the selection witness is a physical claim:

> “**Selection in the physical module.** The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\): realized transports, physically admitted variation, a survival condition enacted by the environment. It is fallible and checkable as any physical claim is. This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV’s is the only one.”

Part XIV lists the physical module as one of two primitive packages:

> “**Primitive packages.** The semantics has two. 1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\), the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), the process order \(\prec\), and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\).”

# 4. Whole-explanation checks

## Direct answers to the five attacks

1. **Trivially satisfiable?** Yes, in the minimal sense: a two-member population, one applied variation event, one non-trivial history pair, and one elimination satisfies the formal clauses. The document only excludes the “singleton population, empty history” reading: “A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded.” It does not exclude a two-member, one-pair, one-elimination setup.
2. **Does “causally responsible” reintroduce a primitive?** It does not add a third primitive package, but it borrows a causal claim from the primitive physical module and does not reduce it to (K) signatures. Part II gives a forward signature for a causal assignment; Part IV’s “causally responsible” is a different, historical notion.
3. **Can precedence misclassify a selected transport as constructed?** Yes, if a construction witness can be gerrymandered. The precedence rule makes constructed override selected whenever “a construction witness exists”. The formal Build witness lacks the itemised-defect-representation condition, so the barrier against gerrymandering is prose-only.
4. **Can construction and selection witnesses be inter-rewritten?** Not under the prose separation. Under the formal predicates, the question is open: \(\operatorname{Build}\) does not formally require an itemised defect representation, and \(\operatorname{Sel}\) does not formally exclude a target instantiated before variation. So a construction-like process that lacks the itemised representation could satisfy \(\operatorname{Sel}\).
5. **Is blindness consistent with Part X’s construction witness?** It is consistent with the prose sentence in Part X that says a construction witness contains an itemised defect representation. It is not consistent with the formal \(\operatorname{Build}\) predicate, which does not mention such a representation.

## Findings

**Finding 1 — The formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) predicate does not include the “no target instantiated before the variation” clause.**  
The prose says: “a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation”. The formal clause quoted above lists population, variation, history, survival, causal responsibility, non-triviality, and blindness, but not target instantiation. This is a gap between the named predicate and the gloss.

**Finding 2 — The formal \(\operatorname{Build}\) predicate does not include the itemised defect representation.**  
Part X’s \(\operatorname{Build}\) requires an owned subhistory, a nontrivial binding construction, and no composition of content-preserving transfers. It does not say the witness contains “a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held”. The prose separation asserts that it does. This is a second gap.

**Finding 3 — The separation between selection and construction is prose-only, not enforced by the named predicates.**  
The separation quote is clear: “a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.” But \(\operatorname{Sel}\) omits the target clause, and \(\operatorname{Build}\) omits the itemised-representation clause. Therefore the two witnesses are not formally disjoint.

**Finding 4 — “Causally responsible” is borrowed from the primitive physical module.**  
Part IV says “the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it.” Part XII says this is “a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\)”. Part XIV lists \(\Theta\) as a primitive package. It is therefore borrowed, not derived from (K).

**Finding 5 — “Causally responsible” is not the same as the Part II causal assignment.**  
Part II defines a causal assignment by signature: “a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits”. Part IV’s “causally responsible” is a historical filter on a population. The document does not link the two. This leaves a second causal notion in play.

**Finding 6 — The non-triviality clause is undefined and may be idle.**  
The clause says: “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition.” No test is given for an event being a faithful transport. If no change can violate it, it does no work.

**Finding 7 — Blindness permits scalar summaries, which is intentional, but it makes the barrier against construction depend on the itemised representation.**  
The definition says “a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.” So a process guided only by a scalar error signal can be blind. That is consistent with selection. But because \(\operatorname{Build}\) does not formally require an itemised representation, a construction-like process using only a scalar summary could satisfy both the blindness condition and the formal \(\operatorname{Build}\) condition.

**Finding 8 — The selection witness is minimally satisfiable.**  
A population of two, one applied variation event, one non-trivial history pair, and one elimination satisfies all formal clauses. The document only rules out “singleton population, empty history”. It does not require a large population, a random variation operator, or an independent history.

**Finding 9 — The variation operator’s own provenance is unconstrained.**  
The clause requires only that \(\mu\) “was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\)”. It does not say \(\mu\) is not itself a constructed transport. A constructed variation operator could produce a transport whose remaining history satisfies the selection witness. The precedence rule would then make the result constructed if a construction witness exists, but the selection witness alone does not exclude this.

**Finding 10 — The precedence rule is existential and therefore gerrymanderable.**  
It says: “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*.” Nothing requires the construction witness to be the actual or best provenance. A gerrymandered construction witness would override a plainly selected transport.

**Finding 11 — The document itself concedes the construction-to-selection attack is open.**  
Part XV says: “**Construction reduced to selection.** A demonstration that every construction witness — an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation — can be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV, without loss. This collapses the two provenances and removes creativity from the semantics.” The document describes the attack as open. It also assumes the construction witness includes the itemised representation, which the formal \(\operatorname{Build}\) does not.

**Finding 12 — The direction/reverse test is not run for “causally responsible”.**  
The selection witness is a forward claim: environment filtering causes \(t\)’s presence. But no poke is supplied that would distinguish causal filtering from mere consistency. Changing \(t\)’s presence does not tell us whether the filtering would change; changing the filtering does not tell us whether \(t\)’s presence would change, except by the bare assertion “not merely consistent with it.”

**Finding 13 — The answer is partly hidden in the starting points.**  
“\(t\) is selected” is partly already in “\(t\) is a member that survived” plus “a survival condition … eliminated at least one member”. That is not circular for a definition, but the distinguishing work against construction is all in the prose clauses “causally responsible”, “blind”, and “no target instantiated before the variation”, not in the formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) predicate.

**Finding 14 — Adding the job “selection must not contain a target instantiated before variation” does not survive in the formal witness.**  
The prose says selection excludes it. The formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) does not. So the added job is not done by the formal predicate.

**Finding 15 — The best rival to a selection witness is a construction witness without an itemised defect representation.**  
If such a construction witness exists, no change on the formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) list separates it from a selection witness. The prose separation would separate them, but the formal \(\operatorname{Build}\) does not require the itemised representation.

**Finding 16 — The elimination clause, the non-triviality clause, and blindness look like patches.**  
Part 0 says: “Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, ‘selected’ is a hypothesis, not a stipulation.” That is a patch history. The non-triviality and blindness clauses are further patches. The first is formal and does work; the second is undefined; the third is formal but is not matched by a formal requirement in \(\operatorname{Build}\).

**Finding 17 — The witness describes process history, not just outputs, but “causal responsibility” remains a black box.**  
The witness is not a lookup table: it names population, variation, history, survival, and elimination. But the one clause that is meant to prevent mere correlation, “causally responsible … not merely consistent with it”, has no internal test. Looking inside the process does not yet show how to tell causal filtering from a filter that merely happens to preserve \(t\).

# 5. Where each part came from

- **Fitted:** The elimination clause has the signature of a fitted patch. Part 0’s remark “Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected” says the clause was added because a counterexample class appeared. The non-triviality and blindness clauses are similarly fitted to the construction/selection boundary.
- **Built:** The distinction between selection and construction is worked out with the thing in view in the prose separation. It says what would keep the two apart. But it is not built into the formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) and \(\operatorname{Build}\) predicates.
- **Asserted:** The clauses “causally responsible … not merely consistent”, “no event … is itself a faithful transport”, and “no target instantiated before the variation” are asserted. They are not derived from (K), from (E), or from the physical module’s listed parts.

---

# 6. What would make it harder to vary

The one or two cuts that would tighten it most:

1. **Put the separation conditions into the predicates.** Add to \(\operatorname{Sel}\): no occurrence of the target organisation is instantiated in \(\Sigma_h\) before the variation. Add to \(\operatorname{Build}\): the witness contains an itemised defect representation. Then the prose separation and the formal definitions match.
2. **Give “causally responsible” a poke.** State one change to the physical history that should alter \(t\)’s presence and one that should not, using \(\Theta\)’s process order and intervention repertoire. Without that, “causally responsible” remains a borrowed primitive.
3. **Define or remove the non-triviality clause.** Say what an event being a faithful transport would look like, or delete the clause as idle.
4. **Add an anti-gerrymandering condition to precedence.** For example: a construction witness overrides a selection witness only if no selection witness adequately accounts for the same transport, or only if the construction witness does not contain the selection history as a proper part.

---

# 7. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not truth. The document may still be right that selection is a physical history, that construction is irreducible to selection, and that the physical module supplies causal responsibility. These findings show which parts are held, loose, borrowed, unknown, or idle in the revised witness. They do not prove the semantics false.

# 8. One next step

Write the revised \(\operatorname{Sel}\) and \(\operatorname{Build}\) as formal predicates with the missing clauses — target instantiation for \(\operatorname{Sel}\), itemised defect representation for \(\operatorname{Build}\) — then test one borderline case: **a learner that re-tunes on a scalar error signal without representing the defect**. Does it satisfy \(\operatorname{Sel}\), \(\operatorname{Build}\), or both? That single case would settle the blindness consistency and the rewriteability question.

## 4. Findings

**F1 (critical, new in 20). Proper sub-block makes the one-component candidate fail non-circular, contradicting the document’s own example.**
20 Part V says: “there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\)... that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)”; and “Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing.” But 20 Part V also says: “The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored.” If Γ has one component, there is no nonempty proper sub-block, so non-circular cannot be satisfied. The one-component candidate therefore does **not** satisfy (E). This breaks the stated reason for the second grade, and the ledger row G5 “Non-circular dependence witness: proper sub-block” is marked Done while introducing the contradiction.

**F2 (critical, new in 20). Contract adequacy does not compare the target and candidate on the excluded pair.**
20 Part V says: “there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.” If both differ from their own baselines, but differ from each other, the condition is satisfied. That is exactly a pair that “would separate the candidate from the target.” 20 Part XV admits the hole: “A protective contract the adequacy condition admits. A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target. This shows the adequacy condition is too weak”. Ledger row G2 says this is Done.

**F3 (critical, new in 20). Account arity breaks Part VI.**
20 Part V says: “The predicate is two-place on the organization and the question, with the transport and commitments existentially quantified: \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\iff\exists t,\Gamma\ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\).” 20 Part VI says: “Fix \(\mathcal E\). The **restriction** \(E|W\), for \(W\subseteq\Gamma\)... Define \(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\).” But \(E\) alone does not fix \(\Gamma\). \(\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\) existentially re-quantifies a new \(\Gamma'\), so \(W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\) no longer means that \(W\) is the active set. The ledger row G3 “Account(E, p) arity with t, Γ existential” is Done, but the downstream definition is ill-typed.

**F4 (high, new in 20). \(K\) counterfactual edits are in \(C\), but the organization need not supply \(L_j(a,b)\) for them.**
20 Part III says: “\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit — each with a stated meaning”; “Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\)”. But 20 Part II supplies \(L_j(a,b)\) only for edits \(a\) in \(A\), the admitted edits: “For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies \(L_j(a,b)\subseteq...\)”. If \(a\in K\) is not admitted, \(L_j(a,b)\) is undefined. The document does not extend the organization to \(K\), so “fidelity on \(K\)” is not evaluable as stated. This is new in 20 and is not repaired by the ledger row G2 “C = (C_adm ∩ S) ∪ K”.

**F5 (high, new in 20). \(\tau\) is defined as a function but then said to be undefined.**
20 Part IV says: “\(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\) where \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) on the stated scope, \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits”. 20 Part VII says: “here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair”. If \(\tau\) is undefined at an edit in the contract, the transport is not a function of the claimed type, and (F2) is not false but unevaluable. This parenthetical is new in 20.

**F6 (high, new in 20). Derivation 3 assumes translation does not collapse \(C\setminus H\) into \(H\).**
20 D3 says: “Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\). The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives”. But the alteration is of “\(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)”. If \((\tau,\sigma)\) is not injective, an \((a,b)\in H\) may translate to the same \(\tau(a),\sigma(b)\) as the altered \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\), so the altered pair need not agree on \(H\). D1 explicitly adds injectivity: “let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\)”. D3 does not. The ledger row G6 “Derivation 3: pair, closure hypothesis” is Done, but the proof is not closed under the document’s own translation definition.

**F7 (medium, new in 20). Derivation 6 overclaims “No residual predicate is left undefined.”**
20 D6 says: “No residual predicate is left undefined.” But 20 Part V relies on “a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)”, and “sub-block” is not defined in Part II, Part V, or Part VI. Part VI defines \(\operatorname{CriticalBlock}(B;W,p)\) for “nonempty \(B\subseteq W\)”, but not the term used in non-circular dependence. Other undefined phrases include “state” in “the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**”.

**F8 (medium, new in 20). The content-identity clause is false about (R).**
20 Part IV says: “Content identity at the primitive layer... does not use the representation predicate below, which is defined in terms of it.” But (R) is: “\(\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\).” (R) does not mention content identity. The clause “which is defined in terms of it” is therefore inaccurate.

**F9 (medium-high, surviving from 10). The simulation layer is over \(P\), but the scenario has it predict occupancy, which is the field \(F\).**
20 Part IV says: “The **simulation layer** \(S\) is an organization over \(P\) whose components are dependencies among things and whose queries are predictions: what a port of \(P\) will take under an admitted edit.” 20 D10 says: “A simulation layer \(S_0\)... \(S_0\) predicts occupancy from recent occupancy.” Occupancy is the sensory field \(F\), not a port of \(P\). The same mismatch is in 10 Part IV and 10 Derivation 10; 20’s added three-layer section did not repair it.

**F10 (medium, new in 20). The anchored grade is unsatisfiable for targets with no nonempty proper subnetwork.**
20 Part V condition 1 says: “Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\)... no anchor is the whole target”. If the target organization \(D\) has one component and no nonempty proper subnetwork, no active component of \(E\) can have a proper anchor. The document does not note this. The ledger row G4 “Two grades of adequacy” is Done.

**F11 (medium, surviving from 10). Bearing declares “grounds \(g\)” but does not use it.**
20 Part IX says: “A criticism \(c\) has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection. Let \(p_\delta\) be the question about the defect and \(E_c\) the organization the criticism supplies. Then \(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)\).” The variable \(g\) is declared and unused. 10 Part IX had the same. The ledger row G5 “Bearing parameters” is Done.

**F12 (low-medium, surviving from 10). (EK) writes \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\), while the text says the organization is \(E\).**
20 Part XI formula contains: “\(\land\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\land\)”. The following sentence says: “Here \(c\) is a content whose organization is the \(E\) of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p_c)\)”. The formula should use \(E\), not \(c\), under the Part V arity. The ledger row G5 “(EK) world-state” is Done.

**F13 (medium, surviving from 10 under new wording). Grievance 4 says “physically admitted”, while mathematics uses a declared admission relation.**
20 Grievance 4 says: “a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes”. 20 Part III says: “for a mathematical or modelled organization, a **declared admission relation** stated with the question.” For mathematical organizations there need be no physical admission. The ledger row G2 “Admission relation for mathematical organizations declared” is Done, but Grievance 4 was not aligned.

**F14 (medium, surviving from 10). The odd-order example’s non-circular witness is a target contrast, not a removal of a sub-block of \(E\)’s active commitments.**

The same category slip is in 10.

**F15 (medium, new in 20). Derivation 5 does not build enough to establish “coherently posed”.**

## 9. What would make it harder to vary

The one or two cuts that would tighten the ledger most:

- **Cut G2-3 and G5-2.** Rewrite contract adequacy so it directly compares \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\) with \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\), not each against its own baseline. Then a pair where both change but differently is caught.
- **Cut the proper-sub-block contradiction.** Either say explicitly that the one-component candidate fails non-circular, or add a named exception for the case that only all of \(\Gamma\) can be removed, and restate why the anchored grade is still needed.

A third cut: restore \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) as the primitive relation in Part VI, and define \(\mathsf S_{E,p}\) over a fixed \(t,\Gamma\).

## 10. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not the same as true. This audit does not show that 20 is false as a semantics. It shows that several ledger rows marked Done are loose, idle, or contradicted by 20’s own text. Passing a row is not proof; failing a row is not disproof.

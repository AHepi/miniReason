# Reference report on *Claude Fable Semantics* (“20_semantics_revised”)

This is not a story, so I ran the five passes against its nearest analogues: Parts are the scenes, definitions and derivations are the sequences, the design choices (two grades, contract-relative adequacy, declared/derived split) are the choices, and the stated limits are its open questions. Where a pass or test does not transfer — the cost test’s “price felt in a body, a meal, a face”, the inside-the-scene question “who wants what from whom” — I say so in one line and move on.

## 1. Corrections first, on your own list

**Note 1 (your candidate: the orientation of transports).** I could not confirm this one, and I checked it against the page twice. Part IV states the convention once: “A transport from \(D\) to \(E\) is the relation by which \(E\) is *held to* \(D\): \(D\) is what \(E\) answers to… This one orientation is used everywhere: an explanation \(E\) is held to its target \(D\) by \(t:D\to E\); a carrier’s organization \(D\), with \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\), is held to the content \(c\) it represents by \(t:c\to D\).” (R) writes \(\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\), Account writes \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\), and in both the second argument is the held thing. That is one orientation, consistently used. What *is* true, and worth a line: the letter \(D\) names the target in Account and (F1)/(F2) and the carrier in (R), so a reader must re-letter before applying (F1) to (R), and the document never performs that application. That is an omission, not a contradiction.

**Note 2 (your candidate: baseline exclusion vs Derivation 1).** Not a conflict. Derivation 1 concludes “every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation” — coincidence on *all* of \(\tau[C]\), which entails coincidence on the part Part II uses: “their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)”. Showing more cannot falsify the weaker kind relation. Dropped as a fault. The baseline exclusion is in fact well held (note 38).

**Note 3 (your candidate: Derivation 10 vs Part XIV’s membership).** Right in substance, wrong in the detail. Derivation 10 disclaims membership for actual systems (“It is not a claim that any actual infant, animal, or program has been shown to instantiate any of this”), and Part XIV’s membership sentences are class *definitions*, so nothing conflicts there. The conflict is inside Derivation 10, against the conjuncts of (G) itself — note 18.

**Note 4 (your candidate: six conjuncts of (E) vs any sentence still counting them).** Confirmed, and it is worse than two counts — there are three (notes 5 and 8), and one of your other candidates (the two-place Account) is where the damage actually shows (note 24).

## 2. Notes, in pass order

### Pass 1 — Part by Part (scene notes)

**Note 5 — Account’s conditions are counted three ways.** What is there: “\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold”; the display then reads “(F1)∧(F2)∧(A)∧NonCircular∧NonVacuous∧Adequate” — six conjuncts. Part 0 gives a third count: “An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated” — four items, omitting contract adequacy and \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), while Part 0’s grievance 4 says the protective-contract condition *is* “a condition in this semantics (Part V)”. Job of the list: to be the checklist a reader assesses candidates by. It fails because the three lists differ, and the shortest one is the summary. Better: keep the six conjuncts in Part V, fix the word “five”, and make Part 0 point at them rather than restate them.

**Note 6 — “Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes” is false of one conjunct.** The non-vacuity conjunct’s second half is a condition on the contract: “The contract \(C\) is a stated subset of the admitted pairs together with a declared counterfactual set, in the form of Part III, and every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently.” Its first half, \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), is a condition at the baseline only. The sentence’s job — “None inspects a label” — survives for the other conjuncts; the sentence needs narrowing to the conjuncts it is true of.

**Note 7 — The adequacy condition tests one-sided movement, not separation.** Part V: “\(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.” Part 0 states the same condition as “some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target’s answer and not the candidate’s, or the reverse,” and Part XV states its job as catching “exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target.” If the target moves off baseline and the candidate also moves off baseline but to a *different* value at an excluded pair, the condition is silent, and the excluded change does separate them. So the condition does not do the job Part XV gives it, and objectivity (Part 0: “An objectivity claim is the conjunction”) rests on the weaker test. Fix: compare the candidate against the target at every admitted pair outside \(C\) — require \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) — instead of comparing each against its own baseline. That fix also makes “protective” a property of the pairing, which is what Part 0’s wording asks for.

**Note 8 — Adequacy is applied where \(\tau\) is not defined, and Part V’s own verdict depends on that.** Part VII: “\(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation’s component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair.” Part V then says of the same candidate: “Under the identification contract it is an account at that contract.” But account-hood at the identification contract requires adequacy, which asks for \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) at the *admitted pairs outside* that contract — exactly the production edits where the page says \(\tau\) is undefined. Either the condition is unevaluable there or the candidate fails it; in both readings the sentence is unsupported. This is note 7 seen from the other side: nothing in the account conditions says how far \(\tau\) must extend.

**Note 9 — The anchored grade is described three ways and they disagree about which condition does the work.** Part V: “adds four conditions on the anchor map” — Properness, Resolution, No global relation, World-kind. Part 0 summarises the grade as properness/resolution/world-kind only. Attack (F) says: “Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors (Part V) either exclude a genuine mechanism or admit a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change” — again omitting condition 3. Yet Part V excludes the central decoy by *both*: “The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3.” As stated, attack (F) cannot reach the condition Part V leans on. Fix: name all four wherever the grade is summarised or attacked.

**Note 10 — Condition 3 as written overlaps (F1).** Condition 3: “No active component’s relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it”. (F1) *equates* each active component’s relation with a projection of its anchor’s solutions: “\(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)”. The page never says whose projection “of it” means; read as “of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\)”, condition 3 would exclude properly anchored components whenever the anchor’s projection is also a projection of the whole target. The accompanying sentence shows the intended content: “a component whose relation is the target’s own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law”. Say that instead.

**Note 11 — Two provenances or three, and whose provenance is at stake.** Part I’s commitment is headed “**Two provenances, not one.** Selection and construction both produce correspondences”; Part IV has “**Three provenances**”; Part 0 says “The three are distinguished by their histories, not their outputs”; Part IV says “Declared provenance is a status in the model, not a physical history.” The last pair is the sharp one: if the three are distinguished by their histories, the third has none. In the same family: Part III says “\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\) attaches to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone”, while Part XIV lists as a declared parameter “the contract \(C\), in the form \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), **with its provenance**”. Fix: “two histories, plus a non-historical status” for the first; name which provenance the contract carries, or delete it, for the second.

**Note 12 — Your grain candidate is real, and has three strains.** Part II: “A grain \(\ell\) is a declared index, but not a free one. A grain is **admissible** for an occurrence \(o\) when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain (Part IV, Inst) and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes”. First, “that organization” presupposes uniqueness while Part IV insists Inst “is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain”. Second, “realizes” is never defined, so the only constraint on a declared index is stated by name — the very thing the same Part refuses to do for kinds (“A declared kind-label adds nothing where a separating change exists”). Third, admissibility is defined only where there is a physical module, yet Part XIV requires it of every use (“the indices — grain \(\ell\) (constrained by admissibility)”) and Part VII’s mathematical organizations have only a declared admission relation. Fix: define realization of a signature once (“a change at which the component’s relation differs from its relation at the baseline”), quantify over the licensed organizations, and name the declared case (see note 34).

**Note 13 — Derivation 6’s well-foundedness claim is false on the page.** Derivation 6: “the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV”. But Part XII defines: “\(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one”, and Part X defines: “A subhistory is **owned** by \(s\) when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability \(s\) possesses in the sense of Part XII (\(\operatorname{Can}\))”. Can needs something owned; owned needs Can; no base case is given, and the physical module supplies \(\operatorname{Exec}\), not ownership. Part XIV’s dependence order cannot show it either, because it never places Can: “Deploy depends on (R) and (CT1). Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E).” Fix: give the base — a retained realization is owned when every execution in it is admitted by the module for that system under its own resource and continuity contract — and define Can from that base, never from Ownership.

**Note 14 — “They are all listed” and “No residual predicate is left undefined” against the page.** Part 0: “It does not claim that the two primitive packages and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV are few. It claims that they are all listed.” Derivation 6: “No residual predicate is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters.” But \(\mathsf{Admit}^{q,r}_\Theta\) occurs once, in “\(\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}_\Omega(\xi)\subseteq\mathsf{Admit}^{q,r}_\Theta\)”, and is never defined or listed; \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) and \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) (“both specified independently of the candidate in the same sense”) and \(J_p\) and \(C_I\) (“for each starting configuration in the independently specified \(J_p\)”) are inputs in no list; Part IX’s “An evidence leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim. A receipt is a derivation tree over leaves” introduces structure no list carries. The good news inside this fault: the *same* requirement is planted three times and never named — \(J_p\) “specified by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed”, the barrier’s “characterized by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed”, and \(\mathfrak E_\Theta,\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) “specified independently of the candidate”. Naming it once (“independently fixed records”) repairs the missing list and gives UECS’s “\(M\models\)” (note 37) something to be satisfied by.

**Note 15 — Part 0’s attack list against Part XV’s.** Part 0: “(A) Sufficiency… (F) The anchored grade… Anything else is a detail.” Part XV adds defeaters that are not (A)–(F): a protective contract the adequacy condition admits, a question the merit condition rejects or admits, and “**A mathematical error.** A counterexample to the finite monotone theorem, (I2), (O1), (T2), (CT2), or Derivations 1–3 and 8 under their stated assumptions.” That mathematical list names CT2 but not CT1, CT3, CT4, names (I2) but not (I1) or (I3), and omits Derivations 5, 6, 7 and 9 — while Derivation 6 is the one with a counterexample on the page (note 13). **Note 16 — Derivation 4 contradicts a sentence in Part IV.** Derivation 4: “This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation, and nothing else in this document cites it as support.” Part IV: “follow from them by unpacking and are not results (Derivation 4 says so).” That is a citation. (Derivation 10’s “surprise by definition” cites the definition, not the derivation, and is fine.) **Note 17 — Your surprise/Deploy candidate is real, but the fault is in the first clause, not the second.** Part IV: “surprise at \((a,b)\in C\) is a failure of fidelity on \(C\), so on that contract the system has, by (R), stopped representing the content, and by Part X stopped deploying it.” (R) is \(\exists D\,\exists t[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\), and Part IV itself says Inst is a relation with several members, so failure of *this* transport does not yield \(\neg\operatorname{Rep}\). And Deploy carries further conjuncts: “integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task \(U\) as a retained capability”, at a stated state and grain. **Note 18 — The construction/selection boundary is asserted at the witness level and not carried by the definitions.** Part IV and Part X both write: “a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.” But \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) requires only “an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\)” — one of the two; Part X’s inventory is “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization” — no defect representation; and the itemised defect is defined only on the selection side, and as a condition on *events*, not on witnesses: “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation”. The nearest thing on the construction side is the episode’s “recognized difficulty”, which is a different item. Since Part XV’s “Construction reduced to selection” attack and Derivation 6’s cycle-break both assume the two differences, this is the fault creativity rests on. Fix: add the itemised-defect conjunct to the construction witness where it can be checked, or move the “keeps the two apart” claim to the episode and say so.

**Note 19 — Derivation 10 asserts (G) and then lists constituents (G) needs (your candidate 7).** “If \(S_1\) was built by an owned subhistory containing a nontrivial binding construction required for the use… and \(S_1\) is not in the prior repertoire, then (G) holds.” But (G) is “\(\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\)”, and Attempt requires “\(c\) is actually used, on an active route, to address \(p\) — where \(p\) is a coherently posed question… for which the relevant difficulty is that some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\)”. No difficulty, no Attempt, no (G). This is also in tension with the scenario’s own disclaimer that “no condition is checked on it”. **Note 20 — “absent” vs “deleted” in the eliminative case.** Part VII: “a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them”, then “the rival’s supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O)”. **Note 21 — Derivation 5’s load-bearing step is asserted, not shown.** The construction gives ports \(u_{(a,b)}\) with domain \(\{\text{in},\text{out}\}\), \(u_{\mathcal Q}\), \(u_S\), \(u_K\), plus closure conditions, and then: “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).” Nothing maps a valuation to a question tuple whose \(K\) carries “each with a stated meaning”, and nothing checks the three clauses of coherence, including that “\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)”. **Note 22 — The merit condition is not evaluable as written.** Part X: “only when there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P)”. (P) is “\(\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O)\)”, which needs a start state, an end state and an episode; the merit condition supplies only \(e\). Job: to stop “a random new contract” counting. Fix: fix \(\xi\) before \(e\), \(\xi'\) after it, and name the episode.

**Note 23 — The three layers vanish after Part IV.** They are planted as the place selection bottoms out — Part 0: “Selection appears at the bottom, between a sensory field and a primitive layer of things-with-persistence, to produce what explanation operates on” — and outside Derivation 10’s scenario nothing in Parts V–XV uses \(F,P,S\), while Part IV itself says the arrangement is “the expected arrangement, not a requirement”. Verdict: **free** in the technical sense, and honestly marked as free in Part IV; but Part 0’s defence of selection leans on it, so either the defence should lean on the selection/construction witnesses instead, or one later Part should use the layers.

**Note 24 — (EK), provenance, and the word “explicitly”.** Part V: “(E) carries **no provenance condition**: any modelled candidate, including one whose transport is declared, can be assessed for account-hood”, and “Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK), which each require \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\) explicitly.” (EK) contains “\(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\land\dots\land\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\)”: Account is two-place “with the transport and commitments existentially quantified”, and Deploy requires (R) for *a* transport. The transport that makes \(c\) an account and the transport by which it is deployed need not be the same one, so (EK) can hold while the obligation was repaired by a declared transport — which is what (EK) exists to exclude. Also, (EK) does not state \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\), so “explicitly” is wrong. Fix: “\(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c,t_c)\) with \(\operatorname{Sel}(t_c)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t_c)\)”, and deploy by the same \(t_c\).

**Note 25 — Two tests for one job, never compared.** Part V witnesses non-circularity with an edit: “there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\), translated from some \((a,b)\in C\), that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)”; Part VI tests the same intuition by restrictions: “\(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\)” and “\(\operatorname{CriticalBlock}(B;W,p)\iff W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\land W\setminus B\notin\mathsf S_{E,p}\)”. They can disagree — an edit can replace a sub-block whose complementary restriction is still an account, and a restriction can fail while no such edit exists — and no Part says which governs. Both have jobs (Part V: the non-repetition clause, plus the good negative “Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing”; Part VI: the finite monotone theorem and the redundant-route and interference examples), so this is not a case for cutting one. Fix: make \(\mathsf S\) the definition of non-circularity and keep the edit as its witness.

### Pass 3 — Design choices

**Note 31 — The choice test on the two biggest choices: both pass.** (i) Two grades rather than one. Two real options are on the page, with the pull stated each way: “a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history” against “It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose.” Flip it: with one grade, declared-transport candidates become unassessable and (E) loses its job; with the other, the \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) repackaging counts as an explanation, which Part V says is why the second grade exists. Protect this. (ii) The court of appeal at \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) rather than \(C\): the alternative is on the page and shown to fail — “A condition of the form ‘each component of \(E\) must be tied to a component of \(D\) *of the same kind*’ would be evaluable… but it would not be evaluable *by the changes in \(C\)* where \(C\) contains no separating change.” Protect this too. The cost test does not transfer (there is no body, meal or face in which a price can be felt); its nearest analogue — whether each declaration is paid for on the page — is answered here: the module’s cost is paid in Part XII’s list of what it must return, and the declared parameters’ cost is paid by the recording duties in Part III.

**Note 32 — The one free thing the document treats as if it mattered: its labels.** \((K)\) is both the component signature and the family (K1)–(K3); \((B)\) is both attack (B) in Part 0 and CriticalBlock in Part VI; \((D)\) is both attack (D) and \(\operatorname{Boundary}_{E,p}\); \((E)\) is both attack (E) and the account equivalence; \((O)\) is the solution schema and \((O1)\) the obstruction claim; \((P)\) is Repair and \(P\) is the protected set inside it. Because the document *cites* labels — “(Derivation 4 says so)”, “fails (F2)”, “under (P)”, “by (R)” — a reader cannot always tell which is meant. The scheme is free; overloading it is not.

### Pass 4 — Theme as a pattern

**Note 33 — The pattern count.** The central question, as one question about how to act: *when the admitted changes do not separate two candidates, may you settle the difference by declaring an index, or must you show one?*

| Who | Answer |
|---|---|
| Part II | Never declare for kinds: “either a claim about a finer contract, which is a new question, or a claim about their histories” |
| Part III | Declare the admission relation, and record S and K “with the reason for each exclusion recorded” |
| Part V | The appeal is to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\): “a stated exclusion is not thereby a legitimate one” |
| Part VI | Declare the named background and the edit family \(\mathcal V\) |
| Part VII | For mathematical organisations, declare, and adequacy is relative to the declaration |
| Part X | Settle it by construction: a binding “required for the use… when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A)” |
| Part XIV | List it as a declared parameter |
| Part XV | (C) and (F) only — and only for kinds and anchors, never for adequacy |

Who faces whom: Parts II and V face each other; Part X faces Part V through (EK); everything else is left out of the final reckoning — Part VI’s \(\mathcal V\), Part VII’s declared admission relation, and every aesthetic input in Part XI meet no standard. So the document contains two answers to its own question that never meet in a scene: *the module decides* and *the claimant declares, on record*.

**Note 34 — The fair mirror test, in two places.** (i) Physical adequacy is measured against the module; mathematical adequacy against the declaration; the document says both that this is the same kind of thing (“a mathematical question must *declare* its admission relation… as a physical claim is relative to the physical module”) and that declaration cannot carry such a claim (“Declaration is permitted as a modelling convenience, and a claim about representation or creativity cannot rest on it”). Unless the writer says mathematical account-hood is not a claim about representation or creativity, the two halves are not equal and the document is inconsistent here. Ask; do not fix. (ii) The two witnesses (note 18): the document draws them as mirror images, but the selection side’s item is a condition on *events*, the construction side’s, as written, is nothing at all.

**Note 35 — The unquestioned first case is the module.** Part 0: “It derives reference from matter **plus a selection or construction history**, given a physical module that says which organizations a lump of matter can instantiate at a grain. That module is a primitive here.” Every other correspondence in the document has a provenance; the module is where the pattern starts and so never meets it, while attack (C) and attack (F) make it the court. This is declared as a cost (“it is a decomposition oracle: that is what ‘physics says what a lump instantiates’ costs, and it is listed rather than hidden”), so it is not a fault — but one line where the pattern is introduced (Part II) saying what would count as a reason to revise the module would close the loop.

### Pass 5 — How it fits

**Note 36 — Meeting and non-meeting points.** Meetings, where a definition is also a move in the theme: (F1)+(F2) together with condition 4 on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\); Inst (what a lump instantiates) meeting Part II’s kinds; Derivation 3 meeting Part IV’s surprise; Part V’s adequacy meeting Part III’s S. Non-meetings: Part XI’s \(\mathcal R,G,\mathcal N\) meets no account, kind or contract anywhere (“declared as a substantive input when aesthetic value is claimed”); Part IX’s \(\operatorname{Usable}_j\), receipts and \(P_j/N_j\) meet Account only through Bearing; Part VI nearly, as in note 25. The document declares that these are places for absent things (“It supplies places where such things would go if anyone had them, and marks those places as empty”), so they are empty on purpose — but that declaration is in Part 0 only.

**Note 37 — The medium check.** The medium is a formal document read on a page, so any claim whose truth needs \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) or \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) cannot be verified by a reader (Part IV is honest about this: “that is a decomposition, not a reading of a meter”), and Part XV’s defeaters are written in terms a reader can pursue. One device does not work in this medium: “\(\mathsf{UECS}=\{(M,s,\Omega,\beta):M\models\operatorname{RC}\land\operatorname{UU}\land\operatorname{UC}\}\)” imports a satisfaction relation no Part defines, in a document where every other symbol is defined; and “\(\forall n<\omega\ \forall\text{ admitted target chains of length }n\)” is prose inside a formula. Fix: state UECS as a class of quadruples meeting the three conditions, with the independently fixed records of note 14 supplying what \(M\) was doing.

**Note 38 — What holds, and must be protected.** (K)’s baseline exclusion with its stated reason, collected in Derivation 10’s kind claim; Derivation 3 whole, including its hypothesis, its silence and its matching defeater; (B)’s negative clause, “Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing”; the two-grade split and the \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) court (note 31); (R)’s restriction to \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\) together with “A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything”; Part II’s “a declared index, but not a free one”; Part XV’s habit of listing open cases as limits rather than solutions (“offered as adequate for the first case and as scoped for the second”, “No instance of (EK) is asserted in this document”); and its refusal to protect itself, “None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label.” A reviser should touch none of these while repairing the notes above.

## 3. How it fits together

Plot and theme meet in three places and nowhere else: the account/anchored pair, where the indexing requirement and the “does it explain?” requirement are the same condition at two contracts; the world-kind condition at \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), where the module’s separations are made to do explanatory work; and Derivation 3, where provenance, fidelity and surprise meet. Everything else is either machinery that serves those three (Parts II, III, VI, VII, XII, XVI) or a declared place for a thing the document does not have (Parts IX, XI, XIII). The design choices are the work’s strength and are genuinely forced, not chosen for effect: the two grades, the \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) court, and the exclusion of baseline pairs from kind identity each survives an attempt to swap it. The weaknesses are all handshakes between Parts rather than absences: a condition that does not test what its own summary says it tests, a difference between two provenances that only one of the two definitions carries, and three self-descriptions (“five”, “two”, “all listed”) that the page no longer supports.

## 4. The two or three faults that matter most

**First: the adequacy condition (notes 7, 8, and the index consequence in note 34).** It tests one-sided movement instead of separation, so it misses exactly the protective scope Part XV says it catches; it is evaluated where \(\tau\) is undefined, so Part V’s own verdict on the reversed calculation does not follow; and because it reads \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), the sentence it supports in Part VIII — “its truth value at its own index does not change”, cited by Derivation 7 — holds only on the unstated assumption that the module is inside the index. Fix all three together: compare candidate against target at every admitted pair outside the contract, say how far \(\tau\) must extend, and say whether the module is part of the index.

**Second: the construction/selection boundary (notes 18, 19, and half of 34).** The document’s whole claim about creativity, its attack (D)/Genesis, and Derivation 6’s cycle-break rest on “a construction witness contains both”; the definitions of the construction witness carry at most one, and the second is defined only on the selection side, as a condition on events. With that repaired — add the itemised-defect conjunct to the construction witness, and stop concluding (G) where Attempt is unsupplied — the boundary becomes what the document says it is.

**Third: Derivation 6’s self-description (notes 13, 14, 15).** “No residual predicate is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters” is false (there is a bare \(\mathsf{Admit}^{q,r}_\Theta\), and four or five unlisted inputs), and “the only cycle that could arise” is false while Ownership and Can define each other with no base case. This one matters because it is the sentence a reader will test the whole document by, and because Part XV does not list Derivation 6 among the results a counterexample would defeat.

Then, cheap in the same pass but not optional, because the document cites labels: the counts (notes 5, 9), the provenance counts (note 11), the overloaded labels (note 32), and the Derivation 4 citation (note 16).

## 5. What I am unsure of

Whether the module is meant to sit inside the index: nothing on the page settles it (notes 8, 34) — ask, do not fix. Whether Part IV’s “stopped deploying it” is a deliberate shortcut standing in for a longer sentence, or an overclaim (note 17). Whether “the ports and components a rival account would need are absent” was meant as “deleted” (note 20). Whether \(\mathcal N\)’s listing as a primitive “when a question invokes one” is meant meta-theoretically (it would then not conflict with Part XI’s “declared as a substantive input”). Whether “Content identity at the primitive layer… does not use the representation predicate below, which is defined in terms of it” is a leftover from a version where (R) used the primitive layer: (R) mentions Inst, faithfulness and provenance only. And whether Derivation 10’s “then (G) holds” is meant as illustration, in which case the word “holds” is what is wrong rather than the claim. I read the document as a formal semantics with defeaters, which is my own reading of its type and not a rule; I have not checked any claim against an implementation, and every verdict above is from the page alone.

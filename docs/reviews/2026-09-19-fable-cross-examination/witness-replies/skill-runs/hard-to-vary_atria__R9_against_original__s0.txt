# Audit of 20a against 10 and 20

**A note on vocabulary.** The method's plain words — parts, change list, jobs, fitted/built/asserted — are mapped, in its own reference file, to this same theory's terms (organization, contract, query, transport, provenance). Because the documents under examination *are* the source of that mapping, I use those words as labels only and put no evidential weight on them.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained:** file 20a's claim that each row marked *Done* maps to a repair that is (a) physically present in file 20 and (b) doing the job the row's text says it does; plus 20a's secondary claims (its "not asked for" list and its "not done" list).

**Range:** the three texts as supplied. No appeal to file 15, §G/§F/§E3 — those are not in evidence, so every row is checked against 10 and 20 alone.

**What is asked:** a row-by-row verdict with quotes; then defects of 10 that survive in 20 under new wording; then defects 20 introduces; then a severity ranking.

**Kind of question:** backward — "what can we tell from what we see?" The ledger's rows are claims about a text; the check is reading, not running.

---

## 2. The jobs

1. For each *Done* row: the named repair is present in 20, quotable.
2. For each *Done* row: the repair does the specific job the row's text names (not merely something adjacent).
3. 20a's own three extra claims (adequacy-as-conjunct, active-route clause, Derivation 10 downgrade) are accurate.
4. 20a's *Not done* / *Partly* rows are accurate — no hidden repairs, no false confessions.
5. Surviving defects of 10, found in 20 under new wording.
6. New defects of 20.

## 5. The ten tests, run on the ledger

- **Remove.** Take the G3-10 repair out of 20 and Derivation 6's stratification fails: *"the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV"*. The row is held by a real dependency, not by a pointer. Every other row survived the same test — each named passage is doing work 20 needs elsewhere.
- **Swap.** Swap G2-3's pointer from Part V to Grievance 4: both carry the repair, so the *pair* is right but either alone would do — a harmless redundancy. Swap G1-2's pointer to Part II "Admissible grains": that section *uses* "physically admitted edit" but does not define it and cross-references only Part IV, not Part XII. So the row's "before anything quantifies over it" holds in the dependence order, not in reading order.
- **Flip.** Suppose a *Done* row were absent. 20a's structure would not reveal it by itself — the ledger has no internal check. The flip test is being performed by this audit, and it found no false *Done*, but that is a property of the texts, not of the ledger. The ledger does contain genuine *Not done* and *Partly* rows, so it is not a blanket-pass story.
- **Poke.** For G2-3: a change that should matter — delete the Adequate conjunct from (E) — and the row fails (verified). A change that should not — reword *"or the reverse"* — and the row stands. Both halves exist. For G4-5: the change that should matter (rewriting Grievance 1 back to 10's *"A correlation has no component that responds to an intervention on its supposed input"*) does break the alignment; the change that should not (adding the correlation family to Part II's list) would strengthen it — and that change has *not* been made. That asymmetry is the residue S4.
- **Reverse.** Is the ledger fitting pointers onto whatever 20 happens to say? Test: does 20 independently claim the repair? Almost always yes — 20 says of itself *"This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one"*, *"This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation"*, *"which is stated rather than assumed"*. The one row running backwards is G4-5: 20 never says Grievance 1 is aligned with Part II; the alignment is the ledger's reading of two passages whose footprints differ.
- **Hunt the answer in the starting points.** One hit: G5-1's item text *"Five conjuncts"* is the verdict asserted by the row's own wording; 20 has five *conditions* and six *conjuncts*. The row's parenthetical corrects it, so nothing is hidden, but the item text is wrong.
- **Add a job.** The adequacy conjunct does a second job 20 names: Derivation 7's *"the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded."* The anchored grade's world-kind does a second job in the odd-order example: *"the background components (arithmetic, the determinant) are anchored to themselves under the identity translation, which is stated rather than assumed."* Both survive the extra job.
- **Build the best rival.** The strongest rival reading of 20a: G1-2 should be *Partly*, because Part II quantifies over "physically admitted edit" before Part XII defines \(A_\Theta\) and without a forward reference. That rival changes one row's status and nothing else — the substance (the set *is* stated, and everything cross-references it) survives.
- **Check the patches.** 20a's *"What the revision changed that the list did not ask for"* is an honest patch-list: each of the three changes is named with its cost, and one is called *"a loss the list did not price."* No silent narrowing. This is the cleanest part of the ledger.
- **Look inside.** Matching pointers to actual text found two inside-mismatches: G5-1's count, and G4-5's alignment, which is a *construction* from two passages rather than a citation of either.

## 6. Defects of 10 that survive in 20, under new wording

**S1 (low) — the reversed-calculation direction argument is still mis-stated.**
10: *"The reversed calculation \(H=L\tan\theta\) is not: intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\)."* That is wrong as written — an intervention that sets \(H\) sets \(H\) in the calculation too; the failure is the (F2) mismatch.
20 rewords it and adds a parenthetical: *"(here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair)."* The parenthetical is ambiguous between a true reading (the reversed organization's admitted edits contain no \(H\)-setting edit, since \(H\) is its output) and a false one (the grammatical reading — "the reversed organization has none of" components assigning \(H\) — is false; the reversed calculation has exactly one). The conclusion survives either way; the wording does not.

**S2 (low–medium) — the hard-to-vary statement's index set is still informal.**
Both versions read identically: *"For explanatory jobs \(F\subseteq F'\), \(\operatorname{Pres}(F')\subseteq\operatorname{Pres}(F)\), where \(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\)."* By the typing of \(\operatorname{Account}\), an \(f\) must be a question; neither document says so, and neither ties "explanatory jobs" to the formal apparatus it does define (obligations \(O_p\), questions \(p\)). The theory's namesake claim rests on an undefined index set.

**S3 (low) — eliminative anchoring is still asserted case by case.**
20: *"the rival's supposed structure is anchored to a deleted subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O), and the anchor is proper because the deleted subnetwork is not the whole target."* The clause after "because" is an assumption about the case, not a theorem: a rival whose structure *is* the whole target would fail properness, and 20 notes this nowhere for the first case (only for the second, via \(K\)).

**S4 (low) — Grievance 1's residue.**
10's Grievance 1 contradicted Part II's formal causal signature (input port vs output port). 20 fixes the contradiction — the new Grievance 1 is consistent with Part II given the replacement rule *"An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port."* But the discriminating signature it invokes (the causal relation *invariant* under input-port intervention, the correlational component *deleted* by it) is not among the signatures Part II lists — *"a causal assignment has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits"* — and the correlation is not in Part II's list of families at all. The alignment is a construction, and the footprint bookkeeping that makes it work is unstated.

## 7. Defects 20 introduces that 10 did not have

**N1 (medium) — the anchored grade's active-route clause is conditional, and where it does not apply the decoy returns.**
Part V, condition 4: *"Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor… **Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\)**, each anchor is an active route (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically."* So where no history is supplied, anchoring reduces to world-kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) plus properness, resolution and no-global-relation. But a decoy that shares a world-kind on *every* admitted change is precisely the case 20 itself posits as open: attack (F), *"admit a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change."* The one instrument that could separate mechanism from decoy in that case is the very clause made conditional. 20 owns this as attack (F); it is nonetheless the widest gap in the new sufficiency claim, which now reads *"Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V)… and that plainly explains nothing."*

A second aspect: the clause imports Part IX's active route, defined as *"a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction."* Everywhere else that a provenance or adequacy notion needed a target, 20 carefully replaced "represented" with "instantiated" (Part IV Constructed, Part XIII Scrutinizability, Part X Construction). Here "represented" survives, so the anchored grade depends on the representation predicate (R) and on histories, unlike (E). No cycle results — anchoring is not used inside any provenance witness — but the layered stratification of Part IV is not extended to cover it.

**N2 (medium) — Grievance 12 overstates, and anchored condition 4 contradicts it in application.**
Grievance 12: *"What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract. Provenance matters to representation because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to fidelity because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change."* But an active-route anchor and a backup anchor have, by construction, the same forward kind on every admitted change — *"a backup that would have responded identically"* — and one is anchored while the other is not. Anchoring is presented as fidelity on a larger contract (*"Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution"*), so a component's history *does* make a difference to a fidelity notion. Grievance 12 needs scoping to the unanchored grade. The ledger half-flags this (*"the place where the transport/component asymmetry of Grievance 12 is least clean"*); the clean statement is that the asymmetry holds for \((E)\) and fails for the anchored grade.

**N3 (low–medium) — "admissible grains" does not say what it means.**
*"every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes: a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed."* The condition as written ("has a signature that some edit realizes") does not match its own gloss ("no admitted change can touch"). Under the gloss, a component whose relation is constant across every admitted edit — a conservation constraint, for instance — would render a grain inadmissible, which is surely not intended. Under the literal reading the condition is near-vacuous. Neither reading is stated.

**N4 (low) — "coherently posed" contains a redundant or wrong clause.**
*"It is coherently posed when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated."* Since \(S\) is by definition a subset of admitted pairs and \(K\) is pairs the module does not admit, \(S\cap K=\varnothing\) is automatic; and read literally, "disjoint from each other's complements" asserts \(S\subseteq K\) and \(K\subseteq S\), i.e. \(S=K\). The clause is either redundant or mis-worded.

**N5 (low) — Derivation 5 overstates its own construction.**
*"Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\)."* The organization built has ports for pairs, closure components, and \(S\)/\(K\) consistency; it encodes the *formal* coherence of a contract. But Part III's coherence requirement includes \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), a property of the target, and nothing in \(D_p\) checks it. Its solutions are the questions of coherent *form*; the incoherent-but-still-a-question cases are included.

**N6 (low–medium) — Derivation 3's closure hypothesis is unexemplified, and the claim it supports is now only conditional.**
10 claimed selected transports are *"always underdetermined on unseen changes."* 20 correctly weakens this to *"Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\)"* — a population *"closed under pointwise alteration off \(H\)"*. No population in the document is shown to be closed that way; Derivation 10's tracker population is stipulated only to contain two kinds of tracker. The repair is right, but the load it carries (Grievance 3's defence, the fallibility of the primitive layer, attack (D)'s framing) now rests on a hypothesis with no instance. 20 says so plainly — *"fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts"* — so this is a gap in reach, not an error.

**N7 (low) — "no provenance condition" needs scoping.**
*"(E) carries no provenance condition: any modelled candidate, including one whose transport is declared, can be assessed for account-hood."* True of the six-conjunct \((E)\). The anchored grade is presented as a stronger grade of account-hood (*"Two grades of adequacy"*) and carries a history condition. A reader can take the sentence to cover both.

**Ledger-side (low).** G5-1's item text *"Five conjuncts"* — 20 has five conditions, six conjuncts. G1-2's *"before anything quantifies over it"* — true only in the dependence order. G3-9's *"file 20 does not fix one"* is harsher than the text: Part IV does fix a general *requirement* (*"a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that eliminated at least one member of \(\mathcal T\)"*); what is not fixed is the criterion for a given system.

## 9. Where the parts came from

- **The ledger's verdicts are fitted** — produced by reading two texts. They are held exactly where they were checked (here, by this audit) and nowhere else. The check found no false *Done*, but fitting cannot certify more than the pair of texts read.
- **20's repairs are mostly built** — worked out with the theory in view, committing to unmet changes and catchable on them. The clearest signatures: 20 states its own attack points before anyone else supplies them (*"A protective contract the adequacy condition admits"*), names its concessions (*"That is a stated limit"*), and downgrades its own example (*"This is a scenario, not a consistency witness"*).
- **One is asserted and knows it**: the analysis-of-kinds conjecture. 10 *asserted* it as a corollary (*"its elimination loses no case"*); 20 keeps the assertion but relabels it — *"which this document does not claim to have proved."* That is the correct repair for an asserted part: not silence, but a stated status.
- **One is fitted-and-unheld**: Derivation 3's closure hypothesis (N6). It was tuned to repair an overclaim, and no case in the document satisfies it.

## 10. What would tighten it most

1. **Give condition 4 an unconditional form, or state what anchoring amounts to without a history.** Either drop the "where the module supplies a history" qualifier and require active-route anchors whenever \(D\) is a physical organization, or say plainly that without a history the anchored grade is world-kind-plus-decomposition and that the decoy of attack (F) is then unexcluded. Either closes N1; the first also removes N2's tension if Grievance 12 is scoped to \((E)\) at the same time.
2. **Add the correlation to Part II's list of families, with the footprint condition that makes Grievance 1 derivable** — a causal component's footprint determines its output port only, a correlational component's footprint contains both. Then Grievance 1 cites a signature instead of constructing one, and S4 closes.
3. **One sentence tying "explanatory jobs" to questions** in Part VI — *"where each \(f\) is a question and the jobs are its obligations"* — which is what the typing of \(\operatorname{Account}\) already forces.

## 11. What this does not show

This audit does not show that 20's theory is true, that the anchored grade is adequate, or that the analysis-of-kinds conjecture holds. It shows that 20a's *Done* rows are accurate, that 20 repaired a long list of real defects in 10, and that the residual and new defects are concentrated in one place: the anchored grade's fourth condition and its interaction with Grievance 12. Hard-to-vary is not truth. A document can pass every check here and still be wrong; 20 says something close to that about itself — *"Whether the defined predicates capture explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, and this derivation says nothing about it."*

## 12. One next step

Run the poke that 20 invites but does not run: write out a decoy organization that shares a forward kind with a genuine mechanism on every edit of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), supply no history for \(D\), and check whether the anchored grade admits it. If it does, condition 4's active-route clause must become unconditional or the decoy must be excluded some other way; if it does not, attack (F) is closed and N1 falls to low severity. 20's own Part XV names this test; nothing in either document has run it.

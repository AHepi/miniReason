# Story‑critique of *Claude Fable Semantics*, attack aimed at Part IV's selection witness

I read the whole document from the page. Its Parts are the sequences, its definitions and witnesses are the characters, and the brief's five questions are the writer's own diagnosis, checked below against the text before anything else is said. Terms used in the skill's sense: a *witness* is a set of conditions that must be satisfiable from the definitions; a clause is *held* when a named job fails without it, *free* when a near neighbour does the same job, *idle* when nothing fails without it, and *two routes* when two clauses each do one job that neither does alone.

---

## 1. Corrections first

**Correction 1 – the brief over-reads one clause, and the over-reading makes the attack stronger.** The brief says the witness "now requires … blindness defined as absence of an itemised defect representation with scalar summaries allowed." On the page, blindness is **defined**, not listed among the conditions Sel "holds when there are". That list ends at causal responsibility ("and \(t\) is a member that survived, with the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it"); non-triviality is introduced in the next sentence as a condition; blindness is introduced in the sentence after that, ending "This is the only definition of selection; Part XII says what makes it a physical claim." Nothing states that a transport failing the blindness definition fails \(\operatorname{Sel}\). So the first question to the writer is whether blindness is a sixth conjunct or vocabulary. Both readings keep the attack alive: as a conjunct it is unquantified (Note 5); as vocabulary it has no consumer (Note 6).

**Correction 2 – there are two non-trivialities, not one.** The brief names "non-triviality over the events". The page has one on the *pair* — "a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)" — and one on the *events* — "Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of \(\Sigma_h\): no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition." The first is redundant (Note 1); the second is ill-typed (Note 4).

**Correction 3 – "does 'causally responsible' reintroduce a primitive?" is already answered in prose, twice, and the real question is narrower.** Grievance 9 says selection is "a population with more than one member, a variation operator that was applied, a survival condition that eliminated at least one candidate, and a sequence of events causally responsible for the survivor's presence. Whether it occurred is a claim about the physical module, fallible but checkable in principle." Part XII repeats it: "The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\)." So the question is not whether it is primitive but whether \(\Theta\)'s stated inventory contains it. It does not (Note 3).

**A correction to the brief's implied map of the document.** Of the five questions, only two are on the document's own attack list: Q4 is Part XV's "Construction reduced to selection", and Q3 is closest to Part XV's "A protective contract…" family. Q1, Q2 and Q5 are not on the list, and they are the three that are genuinely open.

## 2. Scene‑by‑scene notes, by sequence

### Sequence A — the filtering clauses of the selection witness (Part IV)

**Note 1. The discriminating pair is implied by the elimination clause; it is not a second route.** *On the page:* "non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)" and, two clauses later, "a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)." *Job:* bar the reading in which everything counts as selected. A survival condition that requires fidelity on \(H\) eliminates a member exactly when that member fails fidelity on \(H\), at a pair in \(H\) — so the discriminating pair follows. Remove the discriminating clause alone and nothing stops being done; remove the elimination clause alone and the trivial reading returns, because the discriminating clause does not require that anyone was filtered out. So these are not two routes to one job; one is subsumed. The document already knows which of the two carries the weight: "Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation." *Better version, using the document's own reasoning:* Part II excludes baseline pairs on purpose — "The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**" — so require the discriminating pair to be non‑baseline and answer‑changing. Then it earns its place.

**Note 2. The witness is not trivially vacuous, but it is cheap, and the concretely cheapest case is easy to write.** *On the page:* a population "of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)"; \(\mu\) "physically applied at least once"; \(H\) non‑empty; an eliminating survival condition; the survivor; causal responsibility. *The cheapest witness:* take any transport \(t:D\to E\) faithful on a contract \(C\) containing a non‑baseline pair \((a,b)\); form \(t'\) by one alteration of \(E\) at \((a,b)\) so that (F1) fails there and nothing else changes; let the variation operator be the admitted process that produced that alteration, let \(H=\{(a,b)\}\), and let the survival condition be fidelity on \(H\). Every clause is satisfied. *Why this is not quite a fault:* it is a hypothesis, checkable in principle, and the writer said so. *Why it still matters:* the whole force of "this transport was selected" then rests on the two clauses that cannot be checked — causal responsibility and blindness — so the cheapness is not repaired where it could be. Swapping \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\) for \(|\mathcal T|\ge 3\) leaves every job intact, so the *number* is free and the *requirement of a plurality* is held.

**Note 3. "Causally responsible" is a relation the semantics neither supplies nor defines (the brief's Q2).** *On the page:* "with the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it." *Job:* bar the witness in which the filtering merely co‑occurred, and so turn "selected" from a stipulation into a hypothesis. *Why it does not do that job as written:* the semantics' own causal vocabulary is an edit‑signature — "a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits" — and a transport's presence in a history is not a component with a signature, so (K) does not reach it. Part XII's inventory of \(\Theta\) is: "substrate state spaces and attributes; the admitted processes and, for each occurrence, the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\) …; … the **intervention repertoire** \(\mathcal I\subseteq A_\Theta\) …; resources and accuracy grades; the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) …; the process‑dependency order \(\prec\) used by histories; and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\)." Responsibility is not in the list, and the document asserts the packages are complete ("It claims that they are all listed"). *Two fixes, both already on the page.* (a) List the relation in Part XII. (b) Reduce it to Part IX, which already has the right tool: "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, … which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts. A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route." Read the clause as "the filtering and \(t\)'s occurrence lie on one active route in \(h\)" and it becomes checkable, needs no new primitive, and stops speaking a language the rest of the document replaced. The anchored grade already speaks that way, so the vocabulary is present exactly where Part IV needs it.

**Note 4. The event‑level non‑triviality clause is ill‑typed at "a faithful transport to \(t\)".** *On the page:* "no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition." *Job:* bar sighted selection. *Why it fails:* a transport is \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\), whose second argument is an organization, or at (R) a content; \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition as written is a condition, not an organization. The three things named are not the kind of thing a transport can be *to*. The reader can reconstruct the intention ("no event occurrence carries an organization held by a faithful transport to the target of \(t\), or to \(H\), or to the survival condition as an organization"), but the clause is the one meant to keep selection blind, and it cannot be applied as written.

**Note 5. Blindness is a relation to an unquantified defect, so it is either vacuous or undecidable.** *On the page:* "A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation." *Job:* state exactly the difference from construction, and allow a real filter to keep working on "a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly)". *Why it fails:* "blind with respect to a defect" binds no defect. For any process and any defect not on its variation route, blindness holds by definition, so the predicate is true of everything until someone says which defect. If the intended defect is *the* failure the survival condition acted on, the clause must say so and must say whether it is one or all; if it is a class, the class needs a closure. *Better version:* quantify over the failures encountered in \(\Sigma_h\), or over any whole class of them, and say whether the clause requires all or one.

**Note 6. Blindness is defined and never consumed.** No predicate in Parts II–XVI depends on it, the dependence order of Part XIV does not name it, and the sentence that is supposed to be held by it — "a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation" — is a claim about evidence the definitions do not gather. On the reading where blindness *is* a conjunct, Note 5 applies; on the reading where it is vocabulary, this note applies. Either way the same hole, which is the honest answer to the brief's Q1: the witness is not trivially satisfiable in the empty sense (Note 8), but the clauses that would raise its cost are the two that do not check.

**Note 7. Blindness and non‑triviality are two routes to one job, and they can undo each other.** Both keep selection from being sighted: the event clause forbids events that are faithful transports to the target, the history or the survival condition; the blindness clause forbids an itemised record of the defect on the route to the variation. Each has a second job — the event clause is wider (any representation of the target, not only the defect), the blindness clause carries the licence for scalar summaries — so *keep both*. But they are not ranked, and they pull apart: a scalar summary of the survival condition's own performance is "explicitly permitted" by the blindness clause, and the event clause as written would forbid an event that is precisely that. The "does an added detail undo another's work" test fires here.

**Note 8. What holds in this sequence and should be protected.** The elimination clause does the anti‑trivialisation work on its own: a population of two identical realized transports cannot satisfy Sel, because the survival condition has nothing to eliminate. This is the clause that makes Part XV's exclusion — "A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded" — true. Do not touch it.

### Sequence B — the line between constructed and selected

**Note 9. The sentence that draws the line appears twice and is enforced nowhere (the brief's Q4 and Q5).** *On the page, twice:* "What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both" (Part IV), and "Construction is not selection. A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both" (Part X). *Job:* make the disjunction a partition so the precedence rule has two disjoint cases. *Why it fails:* of the two features, exactly one is formalised, and on one side only. The target feature is Con — "there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\)" — so construction requires it and selection does not forbid it. The defect feature is the blindness definition, which is neither a conjunct of Sel (Note 6) nor a conjunct of Con or of Build: Part X's witness is "A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization." So the only formal asymmetry runs opposite to the prose: **Con is strictly stronger than Sel by one clause and weaker by none.**

**Note 10. The rewrite questions therefore get partial answers, and both directions hang on clauses the document does not nominate.** *Construction into selection:* a construction episode that is also a physical history with a plurality of variants and one eliminated variant satisfies every listed clause of Sel, unless blindness is a conjunct (Note 6) — in which case it satisfies Sel exactly if the defect is not itemised, which is the very thing the prose says construction does have. *Selection into construction:* Build asks for "an owned subhistory of \(h\), delimited at \(e\), prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**, and is not a composition of content‑preserving transfers", with "nontrivial" glossed as "neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)", and "required for the use" as "deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for \(c\) on its contract". A variation that changed a component relation is not content‑preserving, so it is not a \(\equiv_\ell\) image of the carrier; and deleting the anchor of an active component breaks (F1) by (F1)'s own content, since (F1) *is* the equality of anchored relation and component relation. So three of Build's clauses are satisfied by such a selection history. *The one clause that might guard, and is never named as a guard:* ownership — "A subhistory is **owned** by \(s\) when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability \(s\) possesses in the sense of Part XII (\(\operatorname{Can}\)), under \(s\)'s continuity and resource contract." A variation‑and‑survival history contains occurrences of the eliminated members, which are not executions of the survivor's capabilities. If the writer intends ownership to be what keeps the two apart, the two "what keeps the two apart" sentences do not say so. **The page does not decide here, and I am not going to pick a side for you (see §4b, Note 29).**

**Note 11. The clause Part IV relies on is asserted in three places and formalised in none.** Part XV describes the same attack as "a demonstration that every construction witness — an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation — can be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV." So "an itemised defect representation" is asserted as part of the construction witness in Part IV prose, Part X prose and Part XV, and appears in neither \(\operatorname{Sel}\) nor \(\operatorname{Con}\) nor \(\operatorname{Build}\) as a conjunct. A promise made three times and paid nowhere is the hardest kind to see, because each mention makes a reader think the other place defined it.

**Note 12. What holds here, and the date it rests on.** "Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance" does a real job no other clause does: it bars a later construction episode from retroactively converting a selected transport into a creative one. But "origination" appears once in the document, here, and is never defined. Without it the guard cannot be applied, and a reader cannot tell whether the construction witness that triggers the precedence rule must be the same episode that originated the transport. Either define origination as the episode delimited at the \(e\) of the first witness that holds, or replace the sentence with the condition it is standing in for — that the construction witness's episode contains the transport's preparation, not merely a later repair of it.

### Sequence C — the precedence rule and its consumers

**Note 13. The precedence rule is held, but its load is far smaller than its position suggests, and one of the two places it bites is the one the brief worries about.** "with the following precedence: it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*." *Job:* one provenance per transport when several histories can be argued. *Where it is consumed:* (R) takes the disjunction — "\(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t)\)"; Deploy takes the disjunction — "by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance"; Part V deliberately hands provenance away — "(E) carries **no provenance condition**: any modelled candidate, including one whose transport is declared, can be assessed for account‑hood, because a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history." So flips of the rule leave (R), Deploy and Account unchanged. *Where it does bite:* exactly one positive use — Part III — "A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) with the witness" — plus the negative use in Part XV's refutation conditions. So the rule matters where it is least defined, which is the recipe for the brief's Q3. Fix the clauses, not the rule.

**Note 14. The third provenance, declared, is excluded consistently, and this works.** "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does." The exclusion is carried through (R), Deploy and Part XV without exception, and the modelling convenience is kept. Remove the third provenance and the doctrine survives but the document becomes unusable as a model of anybody's practice; so it is held by two jobs at once — the modelling job and the contrast job — and this is a place to leave alone.

### Sequence D — what the rest of the document does with Part IV

**Note 15 (chain of questions). The chain is well formed, and it is why the gap propagates.** Part IV narrows to one question — is provenance a matter of physical history? — and that question becomes the opening of everything after it. Provenance re‑enters only at (R), at Deploy and at (EK). The document's arrangement is honest and, unusually, means the fix is *local*: nothing else can carry the load Part IV drops.

**Note 16 (planted and never collected).** Three plants with no collection: the itemised defect representation (Notes 9, 11); "origination" (Note 12); blindness itself (Note 6). No collection without a plant was found — Part 0's attacks (A) to (F) are all collected one by one in Part XV, which is worth saying because it is rare and it means the review can be brief about everything except provenance.

**Note 17 (cause versus label).** Test the three turns in this material. *Turn:* "Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected." Shown cause: the elimination clause. Remove it: the turn does not happen. Cause, not label. *Turn:* "with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation." Shown cause: the physical‑module claim that the history is checkable. Remove it: the clause is a word. Cause, not label — but it is exactly the clause whose content is missing (Note 3). *Turn:* the precedence rule's decision. Shown cause: the existence of a construction witness. Remove it: the transport is selected. Cause, not label — but the witness it names is half‑undefined (Note 10).

**Note 18 (small before big, and the explaining scene).** The document's only worked case for provenance is Derivation 10, and it is quarantined: "This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it", and "If (F1) and (F2) hold for \(t_1\) on the extended contract — which would have to be checked by writing out \(P\), \(S_1\) and the relations, and is not checked here — then…". So the rule is never shown small. That is not a fault in itself — the candour is a virtue — but it is *why* Q1 cannot be settled from the page by anyone, including the author. One worked selection witness, three lines long, would settle Notes 1, 2, 5 and 6.

### Pass 3 — people and choices

**Note 19 (the choice test on the rule).** At the decision the rule governs, the options for a transport with both witnesses are "constructed" and "selected", and the rule makes it a forced move. That is a decision procedure, not a choice, and that is correct design; but it means the rule contains nothing of its own, and all its content sits in the clauses. This is the structural reason Notes 9–11 are not a detail.

**Note 20 (the cost test).** The brief's tests about felt price transfer weakly to a formal document; the nearest carrier is the worked case, and here the price is stated and not shown in the very place the brief's Q2 and Q5 live. "\(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport that is typically selected: the population is the variants of the system's tracking machinery, the history is the sensory changes encountered, and the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held." That is an assertion where a witness is needed, and "typically" is doing a witness's work.

**Note 21 (the unquestioned first case).** The first appearance of the pattern is the field‑to‑primitive transport, and it is the case nobody in the document questions: "that is the expected arrangement, not a requirement." Part XV's attack list does not include it. So the one place where the theme could be planted by showing a correspondence that was *found and not made*, and what it cost, is passed over — and it is the one place where the light burden of Note 2 would be visible.

### Pass 4 — theme as a pattern

**Note 22 (the theme as one question, and the count).** The theme, as a question about how to act: *how much of a correspondence is made rather than found, and how do you tell?*

| Who | Answer | Faces whom |
|---|---|---|
| Sel | Found, by variation and survival, blind, target not available as a target | Con, in the precedence rule |
| Con | Made, target available before the variation, binding constructed | Sel |
| Dec | Asserted by the modeller, neither made nor found | Nobody: excluded by (R) |
| Field‑to‑primitive transport | "Typically selected" | Nobody: no witness exhibited |
| Simulation layer, occlusion (Derivation 10) | Construction does what selection cannot | Its own selection response, left unstipulated |

Two answers are left out of the ending: Dec is excluded by fiat rather than faced, and the field‑to‑primitive transport is settled by "typically". Neither is a fault alone; both matter because they are where the count is asserted instead of shown.

**Note 23 (the fair mirror test — the sharpest form of the brief's Q3).** Lay the two acts side by side as the document does. Did both have options? Yes. Did both know the cost? The selection side is *defined* as blind, and the construction side is asked nothing about blindness — the mirror is asymmetric here, and the asymmetry runs the wrong way: the side the prose calls sighted has one extra requirement (target instantiated before \(e\)) and no extra exclusion, while the side the prose calls blind faces (at best) an unquantified exclusion. Did both gain? The construction witness gains the precedence rule. What did each do afterwards? Nothing is stated — the same silences appear in Part IV's prose and Part X's prose, and the true fault there, the missing itemised defect representation, is the one that lets a construction witness off cheaply. *So: yes, there are cases where a plainly selected transport can be counted constructed, not because the rule is gerrymandered, but because the witness it prefers is the one whose defining feature is nowhere required.*

**Note 24 (working open question, or hole — and I do not pick a winner).** Part XV treats the collapse as open: "A demonstration that every construction witness … can be rewritten as a selection witness … without loss. This collapses the two provenances and removes creativity from the semantics." Two readings are held on the page. *The provenances are distinct* is held by the two prose sentences (Note 9) and by Part 0's "Creativity lives in construction. Selection produces the raw material construction works on." *They collapse* is held by the absence of the defect clause from every formal definition, and by the fact that three of Build's four content clauses are satisfied by any selection history whose variation touched an anchor binding. Nothing on the page settles between them, so I do not. What the writer should be told is what is holding each side, so that a rewording for other reasons does not quietly close it: exactly one clause, ownership, holds the collapse shut in one direction, and exactly one clause, a quantified blindness, holds it shut in the other. If either is removed by accident, the question is answered without anyone deciding it.

### Pass 5 — how it fits

**Note 25 (meeting points).** Part IV is the strongest meeting point in the document: the precedence rule is at once a technical rule about labelling transports and the exact place where "creativity lives in construction" is enforced. The world‑rule/test‑theme meeting is also real: the admitted edits of Part II are the theme, because "what something is" is settled by what changes it. Few theory documents have either. Both should be protected.

**Note 26 (medium check, and the vanishing test).** The medium is a formal argument, and in that medium a witness must be checkable from the definitions. Against that yardstick: the plurality clause passes; the μ clause passes; the discriminating‑pair clause passes but is implied; the elimination clause passes; **the causal‑responsibility clause fails** (no definition, no supplied relation); **the blindness clause fails** (unquantified defect); **the event non‑triviality clause fails** (ill‑typed). Four of the clauses of the one witness that carries the document's distinction between finding and making are not readable as an instruction. In prose, a vague clause is carried by the reader's charity; in this document, where the whole claim is that conditions are on supplied relations, it is not. The vanishing test finds the same list: the itemised defect representation vanishes from the formal clauses, "origination" vanishes after one sentence, and the blindness clause vanishes after Part IV.

**Note 27 (what a reviser or adapter must protect).** The dependence order of Part XIV; the deliberate absence of a provenance condition from (E); the elimination clause; the maintenance clause, once dated; the precedence rule itself; and the consistent exclusion of declaration from (R), Deploy and (EK).

## 3. How it fits together

The plot (definitions, witnesses, derivations), the people (Sel, Con, Dec and their choices) and the theme (made versus found) meet at exactly one point, and the document knows it: the precedence rule, held up by Part 0's "Creativity lives in construction" and by (EK)'s Deploy condition. Everything else in the document is either upstream of that point or downstream of it, and Part V is a deliberate act of emptying the middle so that provenance lands only where it must. That is good structural design, and it is why a fault at that point is expensive and a fault anywhere else in the document would have been cheap. The mirror test and the medium test both land on the same clause, from different directions, which is the strongest evidence available from the page that the clause is the place to work.

## 4. The faults that matter most

**1. The clause that separates construction from selection is nowhere a conjunct.** The itemised defect representation is asserted as part of the construction witness three times — "What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect …; a construction witness contains both" (Part IV), the same sentence again in Part X, and "an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation" in Part XV — and appears in neither \(\operatorname{Sel}\), \(\operatorname{Con}\) nor \(\operatorname{Build}\). The one formal asymmetry runs the other way: Con adds "the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\)" and forbids nothing. *Fix:* make blindness a named conjunct of Sel with its defect quantified over the failures encountered in \(\Sigma_h\), and add the mirrored requirement to \(\operatorname{Con}/\operatorname{Build}\) — or, if the writer prefers, reduce the separation to the target clause and strike the defect clause from both prose sentences so the document stops promising it. (Notes 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 23, 24; answers the brief's Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5.)

**2. "Causally responsible" is a relation the semantics does not supply and cannot check.** *Fix:* either list it in Part XII's inventory, or reduce it to Part IX's active route, in the exact form that paragraph already uses. The document has already chosen one of these in spirit, at the anchored grade; Part IV should follow it. (Note 3; answers Q2.)

**3. The three clauses that would raise the cost of selection are the three that do not check.** The discriminating pair is implied by the elimination clause (Note 1); the blindness clause is defect‑unquantified (Note 5) and unconsumed (Note 6); the event non‑triviality clause is ill‑typed (Note 4). *Fix:* tie the discriminating pair to Part II's baseline exclusion; quantify the defect; re‑type the event clause; and write one worked selection witness three lines long. (Notes 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 18; answers Q1.)

**The brief's five questions, in one line each.**

- **Q1, trivially satisfiable?** Not in the vacuous sense — the elimination clause excludes the singleton and empty readings, and Part XV says so correctly — but cheaply: a two‑member population, one applied variation, one failed pair, one elimination satisfies the whole list, and the clauses that would raise the cost are the ones that do not check.
- **Q2, does "causally responsible" reintroduce a primitive?** Not if it is \(\Theta\)'s; but \(\Theta\)'s inventory does not contain it, and no definition reduces it. As written it is either an unlisted item inside a package the document says is complete, or idle.
- **Q3, does the precedence rule make a plainly selected transport constructed?** The rule is not the problem and does not need changing. The problem is its basis: construction is preferred while the feature the prose says defines construction is required of nobody, so the preferred side can be claimed on evidence that also fits selection.
- **Q4, can the witnesses be rewritten into each other?** Construction into selection: yes, on every formal clause, unless blindness is a conjunct. Selection into construction: yes on three of Build's four content clauses, and undecided on ownership. The page does not settle either direction, and I am not going to settle it for you.
- **Q5, is the blindness definition consistent with Part X's construction witness?** It does not contradict it, and that is the whole of the good news. It is not joined to it: Part X's witness is "the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization", with no defect record; the nearest thing to one lives in the separate Episodes paragraph ("A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content‑sensitive response"), and Part IV's Con cites "an episode (Part X)", not specifically a complete critical one. Three hops the document does not take.

### 4b. Testing my own notes

**Note 28 (swap the story).** Would "your two provenance witnesses are not disjoint" fit any theory with two similar predicates? Not this note as written: it names the two clauses, quotes the sentence in its three appearances, and cites the two definitions that omit the clause. It would not fit a document where the separating clause is a conjunct of both witnesses, or where one witness is defined by the negation of the other. Held.

**Note 29 (flip the scene).** Had the document made blindness a quantified conjunct of Sel and a required feature of Con, would I have faulted that? I would have faulted the defect's quantification and the ownership clause — Notes 5 and 10 — which stand whichever way the separation is drawn. So the note is not coming from me: I would have had something to say either way, and it is the same something. This is also why I refuse to pick a winner in Note 24.

**Note 30 (where the note came from).** Built, not fitted. The document set itself the job in two sentences, and the note measures its definitions against its own sentences. One fitted element is marked as such in Note 26: "a formal semantics should have checkable witnesses" is an expectation about the medium, not a claim about this document, and I have labelled it there so the writer can weigh it.

## 5. What I am unsure of

Whether blindness is meant as a sixth conjunct or as vocabulary — Correction 1 — changes which of Notes 5 and 6 is the live one, and the answer is not on the page.

Whether ownership is the intended guard that keeps selection out of construction — Note 10 — is not on the page either, and it is the single clause that holds one direction of Q4 shut. If the writer says it is, the collapse question is in much better shape than it looks and my Note 10 softens to a request for one sentence.

Whether "causally responsible" is meant to be usable as an active route — Note 3 — I cannot tell, because Part IV does not cite Part IX and Part IX does not cite Part IV.

# Hard-to-vary audit of `20_semantics_revised`

A note on sources before I start. The skill's own word list says it was built from the source theory "Claude Fable Semantics", and this document is a revision of that same theory. So where I use the document's terms — contract, transport, Inst, admitted edit, forward kind, historical kind, hard to vary — those are the document's terms, not independent evidence. The word-list mapping cannot vouch for the revision. I have taken the document's words as the object of the audit, not as proof of anything. The one place the document itself uses "hard to vary" is Part VI ("**Hard-to-vary.** For explanatory jobs F ⊆ F′, Pres(F′) ⊆ Pres(F)…"), so I use the phrase because it is the document's, not because the word list endorses it.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is asked.** The document claims: "The semantics has two [primitive packages]… 1. The **physical module** Θ… 2. **The normative relation** 𝒩, when a question invokes one" (Part XIV); a list of **declared parameters**; and "Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module (with the components listed in Part XII), the normative relation where invoked, and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV, by the dependence order there" (Derivation 6, Part XVI). It further claims: "No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters" (Part XIV). The frozen question is an audit of *that* claim: (1) predicates used before or without definition; (2) definitions that quietly use "represents", "explains", "is a cause", "relevant", "begs the question", or a judgement word; (3) places where Inst or A_Θ is asked to do more than the module is said to supply; (4) the dependence order, clause by clause, and any cycle, especially through (R), Con, Build, Deploy and Attempt.

**Range.** Parts II–XIV as written, with the definitions of (R), Sel, Con, Dec, Build, Deploy, Attempt, (G), (EK), and the two grades of Account. **What is asked.** Not "is the semantics true", but "is this bookkeeping claim true of its own text". **Kind.** A derivability/consistency question — "what counts as what under this rule", and "does the graph of definitions bottom out". **Frozen.** The document as written. Any repair I suggest is a new claim, and I mark it as one.

## 4. Whole-document checks

### 4.1 The ten tests, run on the audit question

**Remove.** Remove Inst: (R) fails, since it needs Inst_ℓ(o,D); Inst is load-bearing. Remove Con from (R): constructions would no longer represent, and the document's own commitment "Creativity lives in construction" (Part 0) fails; Con is held by a job. Remove the repertoire clause from Build: a relabelling would count as a construction; the clause does work — but it is exactly what imports (R), so the part is held and the holding part is another definition, not a basis. Remove Con's "episode (Part X) whose construction witness" and Con is undefined. **Verdict:** the load-bearing parts are not idle, but one (the repertoire) is loaded from the thing it is meant to stratify.

**Swap.** Put a near neighbour in Build's "for explanatory use of c" — say "for predictive use of c" — and nothing else in Build's conditions changes, because nothing else in Build refers to explanation; if the swap works, "explanatory" is colour (F6). Same test for Deploy's "problem-directed activity", replaced by "any activity" (F7), and for (EK)'s "a correct account", replaced by "an account" (F10). Each swap works, so each word is loose.

**Flip the outcome.** If the document had defined representation by Sel alone, and left Con out of (R), the rest of the structure would read as well and the stratification claim would be easier to keep. The choice to include Con is a design choice, not forced by anything on the change list; it is allowed to be free, but then it should be marked free, not presented as forced by "Creativity lives in construction".

**Poke.** For each loose word, name a change that should alter the verdict and one that should not. "Explanatory use": no change is specified that would distinguish explanatory from non-explanatory use in Build; the word is a label until a poke is supplied. Same for "relevant difficulty", "correct", "definable", "operative result", "problem-directed", "meaning" of a K pair.

**Reverse.** The dependence is not running backwards overall — (O) and (Q) are genuine starting schemata — but one condition runs backwards: anchored condition 3 excludes "a component whose relation is, on C, definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it". It excludes the explanandum by naming it, rather than by a property of the component's own signature. That is a reverse move: the target is written into the ban (F11).

**Hunt the answer in the starting points.** This is the audit's central result. The conclusion "every other predicate is defined from the two packages and the declared parameters" is partly imported under other names: "explanatory use", "problem-directed activity", "correct", "relevant", "meaning", "definable", "status", "recognized", "content-sensitive". Each carries the target notion inside the definitions that are supposed to produce it. Findings F6–F11, F19, F24, F28.

**Add a job.** The document's own job for the dependence order is acyclicity: "the only cycle that could arise… is broken". Add that job and test the structure against it. It fails: F1. The new job rules out the document's stated version, so it held something new in place.

**Build the best rival.** Rival reading A: "construction witness" in Con is a *different* notion from Build's witness. Then the cycle through Build is broken — but Con is undefined, and Part IV only points to "an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t", where the only construction witness specification is Build's. Rival reading B: the repertoire is a *declared* parameter (like "the restriction operation's named background"), not the defined set of deployable contents. Then the cycle is broken and the analysis-of-kinds story survives — but the text says "The repertoire R_{β,ℓ}(s,ξ) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect", which is the defined notion. The two readings differ on a change: whether the prior repertoire is data or definition. The document must choose; as written it is reading B in Part XIV's spirit and reading A's text. This is the strongest rival available and it tells the two apart.

**Check the patches.** Patches appear at exactly the joints: Part IV's "(available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below)" blocks one path from Con to (R); it does not block the repertoire path. Part XIV's "the stratification of Part IV … is what keeps this order well-founded" is a patch that *asserts* the result rather than showing it. Part XV's concessions narrow claims quietly (mathematical explanation "falls under a declared admission relation and not under the physical quantifier of this attack") — a legitimate narrowing, but it removes a case from the purview of the audit and should be recorded as such.

**Look inside.** Two structures could give the same output list ("two primitives, everything else derived") by different routes: one where the order is acyclic, one where the cycle is hidden in a clause. The audit finds the second. Matching the list of primitives is not matching the derivation.

### 4.2 The dependence order, clause by clause

- "(O) and (Q) are schemata over the supplied organization and query." — Accepted.
- "(K) depends on (O), a grain, and a contract." — (K) is "sig_C(j) = {(a,b,L_j(a,b)) : (a,b) ∈ C}"; it depends on L from (O) and on C. Grain enters only through the *kind* definition ("of one forward kind on (ℓ,C)"), not through (K) itself. Minor looseness.
- "(F1), (F2), (A) depend on (O), (Q), (K)." — (F1) never mentions signatures: it is "proj_{V_k}[Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b)] = L_k(τ(a),σ(b))". Derivation 1 *derives* a (K) fact from (F1), so (F1) does not depend on (K). **F5.**
- "(E) depends on those and on C_adm for adequacy; anchoring depends on (E), (K) on C_adm, and histories." — Accepted, but anchoring also reaches (R): condition 4 says "each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX)", and active route is defined with "a represented input" and "the represented distinction" (F15).
- "(S), (B), (D) depend on (E)." — Accepted.
- "(R) depends on (F1)–(F2), Inst and physical provenance; provenance depends on Θ and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on Inst and ≡_ℓ but not on (R)…" — **False as written: F1 and F2 below.**
- "(K1) depends on (E)." — Accepted: "Bearing(c,z,p_δ) ⟺ Account(E_c,p_δ)."
- "Deploy depends on (R) and (CT1)." — Accepted.
- "Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E)." — Missing the repertoire clause: "the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire". **F1.**
- "(N), (G) depend on Deploy and Build." — New also uses ≡_ℓ and the repertoire; Origin also uses Attempt, and Attempt's "active route" uses (R). **F3, F5.**
- "(P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy, ProducedBy." — Also Result, Repair, CreativeCriticalEpisode, ProducesVia, and the obligation predicates. **F10.**
- "(RC), (U1)–(U3) depend on all of the above." — Also Enable, question-begging, capability, barriers. **F26, F40.**
- "No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters" — contradicted; see the findings below.
- "whether the defined predicates *capture* explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, not a theorem." — This is the escape hatch, and the document is honest to keep it. The audit does not attack it. But it cannot rescue the *bookkeeping* claim (Derivation 6), which is what is being tested.

### 4.3 The cycle

**F1 (the cycle).** As written, the definitions form a cycle: **(R) → Con → construction witness → Build → nontriviality → repertoire → Deploy → (R)**. The clause-by-clause path is:

- (R): "Rep_ℓ(o,c) ⟺ ∃D ∃t [Inst_ℓ(o,D) ∧ Faithful_{C_c}(t:c→D) ∧ (Sel(t) ∨ Con(t))]";
- Con: "Con(t;h,e) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t";
- Build: "contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**"; and "a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ";
- repertoire: "The repertoire R_{β,ℓ}(s,ξ) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect";
- Deploy: "holds when s at ξ holds a representation of c — by (R)…".

So the construction witness is not (R)-free: its nontriviality clause quantifies over the repertoire, and the repertoire is the set of contents that are deployable, and Deploy is defined by (R). The cycle can be entered at Deploy/Build as well: Deploy → (R) → Con → Build, and Build → repertoire → Deploy. It is escapable per instance when the transport in question is *selected* rather than constructed — but it binds exactly in the case the document cares about, "Creativity lives in construction", and it is a cycle in the graph of definitions regardless.

**F2.** The sentence "provenance depends on Θ and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on Inst and ≡_ℓ but not on (R) — the stratification of Part IV … is what keeps this order well-founded" is therefore false as written. The Part IV parenthetical blocks one route, not this one.

**F3.** The order omits from the graph: capability (Can), ownership, binding, ≡_ℓ, active route, ProducedBy, ProducesVia, Result, Lic/Scope/Live, Enable, question-begging, and the artistic predicates. The document says these are "given where they are first used", but a predicate "given where it is first used" is exactly a predicate not placed in the order — so the claim "by the dependence order there" cannot be checked for them, and a cycle through one of them is invisible.

**F4.** Possible second cycle or overload: **Can** "requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one under the same continuity and resource contract"; **Ownership** "A subhistory is **owned** by s when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability s possesses in the sense of Part XII (Can)"; and **Build** requires "an owned subhistory". If "construction" in Can is the document's own Construction/Build, then Can → Build → ownership → Can. If it is ordinary construction of a retained realization, the word "construction" is used in two senses and neither is marked; and Can does not appear in the dependence order at all.

**F5.** Two further edges are unlisted: Attempt depends on active route and hence on (R) ("on an active route"), and New depends on ≡_ℓ and the repertoire in addition to Deploy. This is what makes the audit question's "(R), Con, Build, Deploy **and Attempt**" correct as a list of nodes: Attempt is entangled with the same cycle even though it is not a node in it.

### 4.4 Predicates used before or without definition

**F6.** **"for explanatory use of c"** (Build) is undefined. Account is the intended carrier of "explains" — Part V: "An **explanatory candidate** for question p is an organization E…" — but Build does not cite Account, a query, or a use task for this phrase. (Deploy, by contrast, cites "the declared use task U"; Build does not.) The word "explanatory" is doing work in the predicate that generates (G) and (EK).

**F7.** **"integrated into problem-directed activity"** (Deploy) is undefined. Nothing in the document says what makes activity problem-directed, and Deploy is a conjunct of (N) and (EK).

**F8.** **"some nontrivial use respect"** in "the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect" is undefined, and it is the definition of the repertoire used by Build (F1).

**F9.** **"the relevant difficulty"** (Attempt) is undefined, and the passage also uses "fails" of an obligation without defining obligation-failure in Part X (Part XI uses o(ξ) as a predicate, but Attempt does not say this).

**F10.** **"a correct account"** (Part XI preamble to CreateEK: "An epistemic obligation requires a correct account, or the correction of a use through one, to be deployable") is undefined; "correct" is exactly the truth-like predicate the document elsewhere disclaims, and "the correction of a use" is undefined.

**F11.** **"definable"** in anchored condition 3 — "No active component's relation is, on C, definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it" — is undefined: definable in what language, from what vocabulary? With no specification, the condition is a label, and the document's own "the one-component candidate whose single relation is Sol_D… fails conditions 1 and 3" depends on it.

**F12.** **"the discrimination requirement of Part IV"** is used in Part III — "A **selected** question is the surviving member of a population of questions under a variation-and-survival history satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV" — but Part IV never gives a requirement that name. What it gives is the non-triviality clause ("no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to t, to H, or to the survival condition") and the blindness clause ("no itemised representation of the defect…"). The named requirement is a dangling reference.

**F13.** **Historical kind** is "a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance." Part IV requires witnesses only for Sel, Con and Dec. "Descended from" and "copied from" have no witness, so historical kinds are not fully defined from the stated base.

**F14.** **"itemised representation of a defect"** ("a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held") is a *second* sense of "representation": it is a record, not Rep_ℓ. It appears in Sel's blindness clause and in the Con/Sel contrast ("a construction witness contains both"). The document does not mark the overload, and if a reader takes it in the (R) sense the cycle (F1) becomes direct.

**F15.** **Active route** is "a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts." Undefined here: "represented input", "operative result", "applicable relations", "represented distinction", "declared contrasts" (and "declared contrasts" is not in the Part XIV declared list). It also uses representation, and active route is used by Attempt, ProducedBy, ProducesVia and the anchored grade.

**F16.** Part IV says of content identity at the primitive layer: "it does not use the representation predicate below, which is defined in terms of it." But (R) as written uses Inst, Faithful and provenance, not content identity. Either the sentence is false or (R) is incomplete.

**F17.** Part III's coherence clause is malformed: "It is **coherently posed** when Sol_D(1,b_0)≠∅, Q is defined on every pair in C, and S and K are disjoint from each other's complements as stated." "S and K are disjoint from each other's complements" can only mean S ⊆ K and K ⊆ S, i.e. S = K, which contradicts "disjoint". The intended condition (probably "S and K are disjoint") is not what is written.

**F18.** Part V says "Account(E,p,t,Γ) holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold", then gives (F1), (F2), (A), NonCircular, NonVacuous, Adequate — and the formal statement is six conjuncts: "Account(E,p,t,Γ) ⟺ (F1)∧(F2)∧(A)∧NonCircular∧NonVacuous∧Adequate." Either (F1)+(F2) are one condition in the prose, or the count is wrong.

**F19.** The work of K runs through "each with a stated meaning" and "a pair in K enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning". "Meaning" is undefined, and K is how the document asks about impossibility — so an undefined semantic object carries a load-bearing case (the document concedes this in Part XV: "handled only through a declared counterfactual set whose meaning is stipulated").

**F20.** Non-circular dependence: "A boundary condition b is **independent** of the answer when no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports." "Carries the answer ports into the boundary ports" is undefined (which transport, over what organization, along what route?), and "an unanalysed boundary input" is undefined. The independence condition therefore rests on a relation the document has not defined.

**F21.** Anchored condition 2: "Whenever two components of D can be separately varied by pairs in C_adm with distinct effects on Ans_p, they lie in distinct anchors." "Separately varied" is undefined; the phrase is doing the work the condition needs.

**F22.** "Ans_E" and "Ans_S" are used as if defined — (A): "Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b)) = Ans_p(a,b)"; Part IV: "the expectation is Ans_S(τ(a),σ(b))" — but only Ans_p is defined, in (Q): "Ans_p(a,b)=Q(D,a,b)".

**F23.** A history is "a set of occurrences with the acyclic precedence ≺_h supplied by the physical module… and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated." The second component is an extra supply not listed in Part XII, which lists "the process-dependency order ≺ used by histories" but not a physical interpretation, its ports, or the actual connections.

**F24.** K2 uses "For argument application u with essential premises Prem(u)" and "the form of u". Neither "essential premises", "argument applications", nor "argument forms" is on the Part XIV declared list (which lists only "an appraiser's license set, contract and withdrawal log"). "Reason use" is prose without a predicate and uses "the operative deliberative rule", "role bindings", "the represented objection"; "Bearing" uses "a connection". "Receipts" uses "a reference to an event with an interpreted claim" — "interpreted claim" undefined.

**F25.** The aesthetic section uses "An effect organization 𝓡 ⊆ A×K×F; a purpose G ⊆ K×F; achievement 𝓡[a,k]≠∅∧𝓡[a,k]⊆G[k] (AR); a normative relation 𝒩 ⊆ A×K×Rsn×𝒱_A". A, K and F were earlier the edit set, the counterfactual set, and the sensory field; Rsn and 𝒱_A are new and undefined. This is a notation collision inside a section the document says it does not derive.

**F26.** Universality: "𝔈_Θ the explanatory contents and 𝔓^adv_Θ the coherently posed advanceable challenges" — "explanatory contents" and "advanceable" undefined; barriers: "An explanatory barrier is an independently characterized domain … for which every admitted, non-question-begging enabling condition leaves the capability required for the domain's contents unavailable" — "independently characterized", "available", and "explanatory" undefined.

**F27.** The layer definitions use ordinary notions without defining them: "whose components are whatever regularities hold among readings" (F); "persistent things with boundaries and identity" (P); "dependencies among things and whose queries are predictions" (S). These are the layers the document's headline scenario (Derivation 10) rests on.

**F28.** Constitutive rules: "A rule relation C_r ⊆ Z×S answers 'which status under this rule?' by its fibre"; "Whether the rule governs a practice, and whether it should, are separate questions." "Status" and "governs a practice" are undefined, and this is one of the places the document says it is *replacing* a judgement.

**F29.** Observation edits are defined with representational language: "an edit that alters the relation reporting the port without altering what it reports". "Reporting" is not defined.

**F30.** Part II's output role uses "determined by": "A port is an **output** of component j when its value is determined by L_j given the other ports of V_j across B." But components impose relations ("L_j(a,b) ⊆ ∏_{v∈V_j} X_v"), and the document insists "Several solutions remain several." A relational component whose fibre is not a singleton determines no unique value; "output" is therefore undefined for such components, or silently restricted to functional ones.

### 4.5 Inst and A_Θ asked to do more than the module supplies

**F31.** Admissible grains: "A grain is **admissible** for an occurrence o when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain (Part IV, Inst) and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes". Signatures are sets of triples "(a,b,L_j(a,b))"; what it is for an *edit to realize a signature* is not defined, and neither is "a component no admitted change can touch" beyond the gloss. The module is asked to license grains and to have signatures realized by edits, without a stated criterion.

**F32.** Inst is "a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain", and the document says "Every definition that needs 'the organization o instantiates' quantifies over Inst_ℓ(o,·)." But several definitions use definite descriptions: Con's "the organization t targets is instantiated", Build's "the built organization", admissibility's "every component of that organization", the anchored grade's "the target". The document's quantifier gloss is a patch over the fact that these clauses read as if a unique organization were supplied.

**F33.** Con requires "an occurrence in the episode's history" for the target. Mathematical organizations have only "a **declared admission relation** stated with the question" (Part III) and are explicitly said not to fall under the physical quantifier (Part VII: "there is no physical module here"). So Con — and therefore (R), and therefore Deploy — is unavailable for mathematical contents unless their transport happened to be selected by physical variation. Either Inst must cover mathematical occurrences (more than the module is said to supply) or the construction of mathematical explanations is unrepresentable in the very framework that claims to represent construction.

**F34.** A_Θ is "stated as a set", but Part II's A is "closed under a partial associative composition with identity 1", which is what makes "τ(1)=1, τ(a_2a_1)=τ(a_2)τ(a_1)" in (F2) meaningful. Closure, partial composition and identity are not in the module's stated supply.

**F35.** Part IV says Inst returns "the admitted edits A_Θ restricted to the occurrence". "Restricted to the occurrence" is an operation on the edit set that the module's description does not mention.

**F36.** Type slip: Part III says "C_adm ⊆ A×B is the set of pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization, the physically admitted edits A_Θ (Part XII)". A_Θ(o) is a set of edits (⊆ A), while C_adm is a set of edit–boundary pairs (⊆ A×B). Identifying them conflates the two, and B is not in Part XII's inventory — yet C_adm and the anchored grade's world-kind condition both quantify over A×B pairs.

**F37.** Anchored condition 4 says "fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair **the module admits**." For a mathematical or modelled organization there is no physical module; the declared admission relation is the intended stand-in, but the wording of the condition still says "the module".

### 4.6 "Represents", "explains", "is a cause", "relevant", "begs the question", judgement words

**F38.** "Represents/representation" is used informally before (R): Part 0 ("Correspondence between a representation and what it represents is not a primitive"), Part IV (F14, F16), Part IX (F15). The formal (R) does not itself use "represents"; the leak is the other way — the definitions of Con, Sel, active route and content identity use representational words whose relation to (R) is not fixed.

**F39.** "Explains/explanatory/relevant": Part 0 "An explanation is a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**"; Part 0 "a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing"; Build "for explanatory use of c"; Part XIII "the explanatory contents"; Part VI "explanatory jobs". None of these is defined; Account is the intended carrier, but the predicates that generate Origin and (EK) use the word directly. "Relevant" survives in "question-relevant" and "the relevant difficulty".

**F40.** "Begs the question": Part XIII replaces the judgement with a structural condition — "An enabling condition χ for the use of content c is **question-begging** when χ contains, as a component at the declared grain, a deployment of c or an organization held to c by a faithful transport: the capability is then supplied, not enabled. This is a structural condition on χ, and it replaces any judgement about what 'begs the question'." The replacement is structural only relative to Deploy and (R), which is where F1 and F7–F8 sit. It also says a deployment is "a component at the declared grain", which is a category slip: deployment is a state/capability, not a component of an organization.

**F41 (negative finding).** I did not find a smuggled "is a cause" predicate. The document gives causal assignments, measurements and rules as signature patterns and says so: "These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data. The semantics never asks whether a component 'is' a cause." The audit finds nothing to add here.

**F42.** Part XIV's membership says "The base class: interpretations supplying these data with correct typing" — "correct typing" is used as a condition but no type system is defined.

**F43.** Derivation 5's construction uses "one port u_Q with domain the set of queries admissible on D" — "queries admissible on D" is undefined — and "one port u_(a,b) for each (a,b) ∈ A×B of the target", which is a port per pair without a finiteness condition on A×B.

## 5. Where each part came from

- **Fitted.** Sel is fitted: the document says so — "a population 𝒯 of realized transports…, a variation operator μ… that was physically applied at least once", "a survival condition requiring fidelity on H that eliminated at least one member". Sel is held only where the history ran.

- **Borrowed.** Θ (physics), 𝒩 (normative theory), "explanation" (Part 0's target notion), "truth value" in Part VIII ("its truth value at its own index does not change") and "correct account" in (EK), ordinary "thing", "identity", "regularity", "status", "meaning", "record". Each of these rests on some other explanation that the document does not test here.
- **Unknown.** "definable" in anchored condition 3; "a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes"; whether "construction" in Can is Build; whether the repertoire is declared or defined; whether Con applies to mathematical organizations.

## 6. What would make it harder to vary — the changes that would tighten it most

1. **Break the cycle at the repertoire.** Replace "neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire" with a declared prior-repertoire parameter R_{<e} fed in as data, and add it to the Part XIV declared list — the same move the document already makes for "the restriction operation's named background". That makes the stratification sentence in Derivation 6 true and removes the load from Deploy→(R)→Con→Build.
2. **Define "explanatory use" in Build by a declared use task**, as Deploy already does with U, or delete the phrase and let the required use be "U_c".
3. **Give Inst's returns a stated uniqueness convention** (a set of organizations at a grain, plus what "the built organization" selects), and state as module obligations that A_Θ carries identity, partial composition and the restriction used in Part IV.
4. **Place the omitted predicates in the order** — Can, ownership, binding, ≡_ℓ, active route, ProducedBy, ProducesVia, Result, Lic/Scope/Live, Enable, question-begging.
5. **Fix the malformed coherence clause** (F17) and the count in Part V (F18), which are cheap and remove two avoidable doubts.

## 7. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not a truth-meter. This audit has not tested whether Account captures explanation, whether (R) captures representation, or whether any theorem fails. It has tested one bookkeeping claim — that the predicate graph is defined from two packages, the declared parameters, and an acyclic order — and has found loose and undefined parts, an omitted set of order clauses, and a live cycle through (R), Con, Build and Deploy. It did not find a smuggled "is a cause" predicate (F41), and it did not find (R) using the word "represents" in its own definition; the leaks are in Con, Sel, active route and Build. Passing this audit would not have shown the semantics true; failing it does not show the semantics false. And nothing here bears on the constitutive conjecture, which the document itself leaves open.

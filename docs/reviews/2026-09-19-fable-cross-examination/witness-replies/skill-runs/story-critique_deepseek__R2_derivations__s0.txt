## 1. Corrections first

Five of the six items you name are real and I confirm them from the page. One needs correcting, and the correction moves the D6 note somewhere else. You write that Derivation 6 "claims that the Rep/Con cycle is broken". On the text, the cycle it names **is** broken, exactly as the proof says: (R) requires `Sel(t) ∨ Con(t)`, and `Con(t;h,e)` requires that "the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\)" — `Inst`, not `Rep`. So the edge runs Rep → Con and not back. The trouble is a *second* loop on the document's own words, which the proof's phrase "the only cycle that could arise" rules out without checking (notes 11–12). One addition, which reframes your Derivation 5 question: Part III's coherence clause is ill-formed, so "are its solutions exactly the coherently posed questions?" cannot even be tested until that clause is fixed (note 9).

**How the five passes map.** Pass 1 transfers: a derivation is a scene, its job is the claim it supports, and remove/swap apply to its hypotheses. Pass 2 transfers: the chain of questions is the "what this does not show" paragraph that follows each derivation. Pass 3's apparatus (people, wants, cost in a body) does not transfer — no characters here — but its **choice test** does: for each definitional decision, are two real options on the page? Pass 4 transfers as a pattern count over one question: *is explanation fully characterized by organization, transport, provenance and realization?* Pass 5's **medium check** transfers once restated: the "medium" of a claim here is its court of appeal — `C`, `C_adm`, or the history — and a claim must be filed in the court that can see it.

## 2. Scene-by-scene notes, grouped by sequence

### Sequence A — the two preservation lemmas (D1, D2)

**1. Derivation 1's injectivity hypothesis does bookkeeping, not the work its title names.** On the page: "Let \(t\) satisfy (F1) on \(C\), and let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\)." The job, from the derivation's own title, is *kind preservation*. But the third-coordinate correspondence is (F1) alone, and injectivity appears only in the last sentence: "injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates." Remove the hypothesis: the claim's content survives (each \((a,b)\in C\) still carries \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)) = \operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\); where two pairs collapse, (F1) forces the two projections to agree). What is lost is only the bijection of *index sets*. So the hypothesis is a true but mislabelled condition: it is sufficient, not necessary, and it is not what holds the result in place. Better version: state the identity pair by pair for any \((\tau,\sigma)\), and add the bijection clause separately, where `τ[C] = C` is needed.

**2. Derivation 1 proves a claim indexed on `τ[C]`; the sentence that reports it says "on C".** The Claim: "every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\)". The follow-up: "a faithful component is of one forward kind, **on \(C\)**, with its anchor subnetwork." Those are different statements whenever `τ[C] ≠ C`, and `τ` need not be invertible — Part VII has `τ` undefined at the intervention on \(H\). Part II defines "of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)" only between "two components \(j,j'\)" of one organization on one contract, so the cross-organization comparison of an `E`-component with a `D`-subnetwork needs a stated transport-relative extension. That gap is then inherited by Part V's condition 4, by Part VII's "its one component is not of one forward kind with the assignment of \(L\)", and by Derivation 10. **What to protect:** the cross-references themselves are careful — Part V says "on the translated contract", Part XV(C) says Derivation 1 "only shows fidelity preserves signatures". I found no place where Derivation 1 is cited as stronger than what it proves. Fix: state the extension once in Part II, and notes 2, 10 and 20 all close.

**3. Derivation 1 stipulates the anchor's visible ports, which Part II calls declared.** The proof says "the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\)." Part II says a subnetwork's "**visible ports** are a declared subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) of its ports, the rest being hidden." Deriving the declaration from `λ` is reasonable and probably intended, but it is added data at the exact point where (F1)'s left side (a projection to `V_k`) is equated with an `E`-side relation. Say that `λ` carries the visible-port declaration.

**4. Derivation 2's definition of account-equivalence compares `D`-side relations, so its own first clause is automatic.** The definition: "are **account-equivalent on \(C\)** when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide." Since the words "anchored relations" name `D`-side projections, the Claim's hypothesis "have the same anchor map up to that bijection" already gives that clause, whatever (F1) says. The proof's "By (F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence" slides between the two: (F1) is what identifies each side with an `E`-side component relation, not what makes the two candidates' `D`-side relations coincide. The Claim is true — with the natural reading of "pulled back" as "at the same \((a,b)\in C\)", (A) for both candidates does give the profile clause — but the proof credits the wrong premise.

**5. (F2) is carried in Derivation 2's hypotheses and used nowhere.** The Claim requires that both candidates "satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\)"; the proof names (F1) and (A). Either state the job (F2) is doing, or drop it.

**6. Account-equivalence has no consumer, so the Claim is idle.** I can find no other use of the relation or the term in the document. By the remove test, cutting the Claim costs nothing: the paragraph that does the work is "What it does not say" — "Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on \(C\) with *different* decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account." Better version: keep the definition, replace the Claim with the statement actually used (shared anchor map plus (A) at the same source pairs), or give account-equivalence a job in Part XV's attack (C), where it would earn its keep.

### Sequence B — selection and its limits (D3, D4)

**7. Derivation 3's population is not the population its definition defines.** The Claim: "from a population \(\mathcal T\) of organization–transport pairs". Part IV: "a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)". Since the derivation alters component relations — that is, alters the *organization* — it must work with pairs, and it must say so and say how Part IV's `T` relates. As written the hypothesis is not stated in the vocabulary of the definition it cites.

**8. The 'admissible class' makes the closure hypothesis nearly the conclusion.** On the page, closure requires that for "each relation \(L'\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) in the admissible class (**the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)**)", `T` "contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) everywhere except that \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L'\)". If the admissible class is what some member of `T` realizes, closure says `T` is complete under coordinatewise substitution — that is, it stipulates in advance that the off-`H` variants the conclusion names are already members. Two further points. First, the proof's key sentence, "Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\)", needs the survival condition to *be* fidelity on `H`; Part IV's wording, "a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that eliminated at least one member", also permits a condition that requires more, under which the proof fails. State which reading is meant. Second, the conclusion's force is then a negative one — "survival on `H` is compatible with arbitrary off-`H` behaviour" — which is worth having, but the fix is to state the hypothesis non-circuitously (a parametric family, or "the variation operator generates every pointwise variant") and to let the *negative* claim carry the derivation. Part XV's attack (D) already states it in the fenced form: "in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off \(H\)".

**9. Derivation 3's Consequence is stronger than its Claim.** The Claim's conclusion is conditional: "whenever some admissible \(L'\) changes that solution set". The Consequence drops the caveat and says "the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\)". What the proof establishes is that the *survival condition on `H`*, plus closure, does not determine it. The selection data of Part IV also include the events of `Σ_h` and "the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence"; nothing in the proof rules out that those determine the survivor. **What to protect:** Derivation 3's own hedges are exemplary — "It does not follow that the survivor *is* unfaithful there", "Where the population is parametric and not closed under pointwise alteration, the survivor may be determined off \(H\) by its parametrisation, and this derivation is silent", "fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts." Part 0's grievance 3 and Part XV's attack (D) both repeat the population hypothesis. No stronger citation exists elsewhere.

**10. Derivation 4 is correctly labelled and correctly self-described.** Surprise is "a violation at \((a,b)\notin H\)", a violation is a fidelity failure, and Part IV already adds that "a change outside \(C\) altogether can neither violate nor surprise". So a system with no transport is never surprised, and one whose history exhausts its contract is never surprised within that contract; both are unpackings. Its claim "nothing else in this document cites it as support" holds: Derivation 10 says "surprise by definition". No fault. (I note only that Part XV's counterexample list — "Derivations 1–3 and 8" — omits 5, 6, 7 and 9 as well; see note 13.)

### Sequence C — question-finding (D5)

**11. Derivation 5's solutions are not exactly the coherently posed questions; the set is too large, and two of the three coherence clauses have no counterpart.** The Claim to check is "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\)." Part III says: "It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated."

- *First clause.* `D` and `b_0` are the construction's boundary: "Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed." The only component named is "the baseline port reads 'in'" — membership of the baseline in the contract, not solvability of the target's baseline. Nothing reads `Sol_D(1,b_0)`. If it is empty, the set of coherently posed questions on `D` is empty while `D_p` still has solutions; the two sets therefore cannot be equal.
- *Second clause.* The query port has "domain the set of queries admissible on \(D\)" — a term used nowhere else in the document, and not Part III's "defined on every pair in \(C\)". Either say the domain is the queries defined on `A×B`, or add a component tying `u_Q` to the "in" ports.
- *Third clause.* The construction defers: "\(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III." Read naturally (complement in `A×B`), "\(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements" gives `S ⊆ K` and `K ⊆ S`, hence `S = K`. But Part III also says `S` is "the declared subset of admitted pairs" and `K` is "a declared set of counterfactual edits — pairs the module does not admit". So the clause the construction points at contradicts the definitions of `S` and `K`, and cannot serve as a constraint. Fix that clause to `S ⊆ C_adm, K ∩ C_adm = ∅` (or whatever was meant), and the third gap closes.

Two smaller items. The Claim also asserts "It is a \(D_p\) in the sense of (O)", which needs the edit set to be "closed under a partial associative composition with identity \(1\)"; the construction lists "toggle a pair; alter \(\mathcal Q\); alter \(S\); add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning" and never says what composes with what. And "the closure conditions —" introduces three items where the claim needs the complete list, so even the direction "every coherent question is a solution" is asserted rather than shown.

**What to protect:** the port set itself. One port per pair of `A×B` with domain {in, out}, plus `u_Q`, `u_S`, `u_K`, is the right shape; the job the derivation actually does — carrying a question as a content for (G), (N) and `≡_ℓ`, with "provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization" — survives the defects. And "What the construction cannot do is add a port to \(D\)" is stated consistently with Part X's "What is not represented" and with Part XV's attack (E). Also a one-line fix: Part X says "(G) ... where \(p\) is a coherently posed question that \(s\) instantiates before \(e\)" — `Inst` relates occurrences to organizations, so this should say the system instantiates `D_p`, which Derivation 5 supplies.

### Sequence E — invariance (D8)

**17. **18. That is worth having (it licenses `≡_ℓ`), but it is a modest invariance result, and the honest caveat — "The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, lost event identities, or maps that alter admission" — belongs in the Claim, not after the proof.

## 3. How it fits together

The derivations where a claim is also structural work are Derivation 1 (which licenses Part V's condition 4 and all cross-organization kind talk), Derivation 5 (which carries the merit condition and attack (E)), Derivation 6 (which carries the completeness claim) and Derivation 8 (which licenses `≡_ℓ`). The unquestioned first case — the pattern's first instance, which no one in the document questions — is Derivation 1's stipulation that the anchor's visible ports are `λ`'s translates of `V_k`: the cross-organization identification that Part V's condition 4, Part VII and Derivation 10 all then rely on. Read as a medium check: Derivation 1's kind claim is filed in `C` when it is visible only on `τ[C]`; Derivation 3's Consequence is filed as a claim about "the selection data" when it is a claim about the survival condition; Derivation 5's "exactly" is filed as a theorem when the closure conditions are a list. Filing, not arithmetic, is where most of these faults live.

## 4. The three faults that matter most

1. **Derivation 5's "exactly", resting on Part III's ill-formed coherence clause.** This is the bridge to the merit condition, to attack (E), and to Derivation 7. The solution set over-generates, the target's baseline solvability is never read, and the `S`/`K` clause the construction defers to contradicts Part III's own definitions of `S` and `K`. Fix Part III's clause and add the missing closure conditions before anything downstream is re-read.
2. **Derivation 6's "the only cycle that could arise".** The document's strongest structural claim ("No residual predicate is left undefined") rests on an assertion, and there is a written path through the repertoire and (R) that the proof does not consider. Add the Part XV defeater item at the same time.
3. **Derivation 3's hypothesis and consequence.** The population is not the object Part IV defines, the admissible class presupposes what the proof concludes, the survival condition's reading is unstated, and the Consequence drops the Claim's own caveat. It is the result Part 0's grievance 3 and Part XV's attack (D) both lean on, so it should be exact.

An honourable fourth: Derivation 8's object list, which must be widened or `≡_ℓ` must stop citing it. **Protect:** Part V's scrupulous citation of Derivation 1; Derivation 3's self-imposed caveats; Derivation 5's stated limit on new dimensions; Derivation 4's self-description; Derivation 10's self-labelling; and Part IV's stratification of *instantiated* versus *represented* in the construction witness, which is the right mechanism and is what makes Derivation 6's first half true.

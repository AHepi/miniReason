# Hard-to-vary referee report on `20_semantics_revised`

Attack (A) sufficiency, anchored grade of Account.

**A note on vocabulary.** The skill's `word-list.md` says the skill's plain words were mapped from a source theory, and Part III, §"Where the sources are" of that file names the source as "Claude Fable Semantics — a structural class of explanatory creativity, with selected and constructed correspondence", supplied as `10_Claude_Fable_Semantics_-_standalone_theory.md`. The document I am examining is a revision of that same theory, so terms like **contract**, **transport**, **provenance**, **forward kind**, **account**, **anchored account**, **active route**, **selection / construction / declaration** are the document's *own* terms, quoted from it below. I have not used the word-list as evidence about the document.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being attacked.** The sufficiency of the anchored grade of Account.

**What is asked, in the document's own words** (Part 0, "Where to attack this"):

> "**(A) Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing."

And (Part XV, first item):

> "**A faithful, anchored transport that does not explain.** A candidate meeting the anchored grade of (E) on an admitted contract with a non-declared transport, which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency."

**What has to be met, decomposed.** The candidate must satisfy, jointly:

- Six conjuncts of (E) (Part V): F1, F2, (A), NonCircular, NonVacuous, Adequate.
- Four anchored conditions (Part V): properness, resolution, no-global-relation, world-kind (with the active-route clause).
- A **non-declared** transport (Part IV, precedence rule): provenance is `Sel` or `Con`; `Dec` is excluded.
- "Plainly" explains nothing.

**Range.** On an admitted contract, with D attributed by the physical module at an admissible grain.

**Kind of question.** Mixed. Predominantly *Why can it not happen?* (the sufficiency claim says no such candidate exists), tested by attempting the construction; plus *Does it achieve its purpose?* for the second part of the attack — do the four anchored conditions exclude any genuine mechanism?

**The document's own verdict, before I start.** Part XV: "A candidate meeting (E) alone and explaining nothing is already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of Sol_D — and is why the second grade is stated; it does not refute the class, because the class's sufficiency claim is for the anchored grade." So the burden is entirely on the anchored grade.

---

## 2. The explanation in parts (the document's sufficiency claim)

The document's argument is short and I give it back first. (E) is:

> "$\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}$" (Part V).

(E) is stated with **no provenance condition** — "any modelled candidate … can be assessed for account-hood" (Part V). The stronger grade in Part V adds four conditions stated "in the same vocabulary":

> "1. **Properness.** Every anchor λ(k) is a proper subnetwork of D, and the anchors of the active components refine a cover of D's components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of D that some pair in C_adm touches lies outside every anchor."
> "2. **Resolution.** Whenever two components of D can be separately varied by pairs in C_adm with distinct effects on Ans_p, they lie in distinct anchors. The decomposition of E is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about."
> "3. **No global relation.** No active component's relation is, on C, definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name."
> "4. **World-kind.** Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ, C_adm), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits. Where the physical module supplies a history for D, each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically."

The document's pitch for the grade (Part V, "Why the anchoring condition is stated the way it is"):

> "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose, and it is what 'the model is about the actual mechanism' means here."

The document's own summary of what each condition rules out (Part V):

> "The one-component candidate whose single relation is Sol_D satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3; the reversed calculation of Part VII is an account at the identification contract and fails condition 4 on the production edits; a decoy with the same signature on C but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4."

### The four attempts the user asked me to try

I tried each of the four named candidates. I give the block for each.

---

**Finding 1 — Attempt 1: the one-component repackaging of Sol_D is blocked by condition 1 (properness), and by condition 3 (no global relation) on the narrow reading.**

Set D with ports V_D, and E with a single active component k whose anchor λ(k) = D and whose relation L_k(τ(a),σ(b)) = Sol_D(a,b). Then (F1) holds vacuously (projecting Sol_D to V_k = V_D gives Sol_D), (F2) holds, (A) holds, NonCircular is witnessed by removing k — except for the caveat in Finding 4 — and NonVacuous and Adequate hold. Condition 1 fails: "no anchor is the whole target". Condition 3 fails: "a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name" (Part V). The document says this in advance (Part V, "What (E) excludes"): "The one-component candidate that repackages Sol_D satisfies (E). It is an account at C and not anchored."

This is not an attack. It is the case the second grade was invented for.

---

**Finding 2 — Attempt 2 (the two-component split): satisfies all six conjuncts of (E) and all four anchored conditions. It is blocked only by the non-declared requirement, which is a clause of the attack, not one of the four conditions.**

I built it explicitly. Take a two-component target D on ports {I, M, O}, with component d1 imposing M = f(I) and d2 imposing O = g(M). Let the admitted edit set A admit at least: set_I, set_M, set_O, drop_d1, drop_d2. Let the contract be C = (C_adm ∩ S) ∪ K with S the whole admitted set, K = ∅. Let E be the organization with the same ports {I, M, O}, two components k1 imposing M = f(I) and k2 imposing O = g(M), and the transport t : D → E be the identity on ports, edits and boundaries, with λ(k1) = {d1}, λ(k2) = {d2}.

**Check (E):**

- (F1): for k1, `proj_{{I,M}}[Sol_{d1}(a,b)] = {(I, M) | M = f(I)} = L_{k1}(τ(a),σ(b))`. Same for k2. Holds.
- (F2): π[Sol_D(a,b)] = Sol_E(τ(a),σ(b)) because E and D are the same organization up to relabelling. Holds.
- (A): Ans_E = Ans_p. Holds.
- NonCircular: Γ = {k1, k2}. Remove the proper sub-block B = {k1}: k2 then imposes the full relation on {M, O}, and the answer profile changes. Holds.
- NonVacuous: Sol_D(1,b_0) ≠ ∅. Holds.
- Adequate: no admitted pair in C_adm∖C; C is not protective. Holds.

**Check the four anchored conditions:**

- Properness: λ(k1) = {d1} and λ(k2) = {d2} are both proper subnetworks; the anchors cover {d1, d2}. Holds.
- Resolution: d1 and d2 can be separately varied by set_I and set_O with distinct effects on Ans_p; they lie in distinct anchors. Holds.
- No global relation: on the *narrow* reading (only "the target's own solution set") this holds, because each L_k is a relation on a proper subset of D's ports, not Sol_D itself. On the *broad* reading it fails — see Finding 16.
- World-kind: k1 vs d1, k2 vs d2; they respond identically to every edit in C_adm. Holds.
- Active route: if the physical module supplies a history, d1 and d2 are the actual occurrences on the active route. Holds.

**Where it is blocked.** The transport t is the identity up to relabelling. Its provenance is decided by Part IV's precedence rule: it is `Con` only if a construction witness exists, else `Sel` only if a selection witness exists, else `Dec`.

- On the `Con` path, Part X requires "a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**", and says the binding is nontrivial only when "neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ". The identity map is a relabelling. So `Con` fails.
- On the `Sel` path, Part IV requires "a finite history H ⊆ C … containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of T fails it); a survival condition requiring fidelity on H that **eliminated at least one member**". This is contingently satisfiable, but Part XVI's Derivation 3 explicitly says the survivor "may be determined off H by its parametrisation" and on that reading the survivor is not `Sel` for C_adm-fidelity; and its provenance is not automatically `Con`.
- Fallback: neither witness exists, so t is `Dec` by Part IV's precedence rule.

**Consequence.** The two-component split fails the attack's own requirement of a non-declared transport. That clause — not any of the four anchored conditions — is what blocks it. That is worth stating plainly: the four anchored conditions *alone* are not what carries sufficiency here for this candidate; the attack requires the transport to be selected or constructed, and the anchored grade says nothing about that.

**Note on "plainly explains nothing".** A critic can reasonably say E = D relabelled is a restatement of the target and so explains nothing. But the document denies this on its own terms: Part V, "What (E) excludes": "A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account." The document therefore agrees that a faithful decomposition *is* an account. So even granting a non-declared transport, this candidate would not be conceded as "plainly explaining nothing".

**Finding 3 — Attempt 3 (a lookup with a proper decomposition): blocked by (F1), or by condition 3, or by condition 1, depending on how it is drawn.**

Three ways to draw it, each blocked:

- *One component whose relation is the target's answer for every (a,b) — a table indexed by the contract.* Fails (F1) on any contract containing an intervention, as the document says: "A table of observed answers has no component whose relation is replaced by an intervention; it fails (F1) under any contract containing one" (Part V). The component has no response to a paired change; it is a label under the skill's own test.
- *One component whose relation is Sol_D, anchored to the whole target.* Blocked by condition 1 (properness), as Finding 1; the document lists it in the same place.
- *Split lookup — an "input reader" k1 with a proper anchor and an "answer looker" k2 with a proper anchor.* For (F1) to hold on k2, `proj_{V_{k2}}[Sol_{λ(k2)}(a,b)]` must equal L_{k2}(τ(a),σ(b)). If L_{k2} merely returns Ans_p, then the only λ(k2) that satisfies this is one whose local solution set already determines the answer — which is the mechanism's answer-determining subnetwork, not a table. If instead the relation on k2 is written as `proj_{V_{k2}}[Sol_D(a,b)]` (the projection of the target's whole solution set onto k2's footprint), condition 3 excludes it: "a projection of it" (Part V, condition 3). Either way the lookup disappears into the mechanism or is caught.

The document states the position directly (Part V, "What (E) excludes"): "A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account."

---

**Finding 4 — Attempt 4 (a decoy with the same world-kind): blocked by condition 4 or by the corollary that matching on C_adm makes the two the same account at that grain.**

For a "decoy" D′ to satisfy condition 4, each of its components must be of one forward kind with its anchor on (ℓ, C_adm). By Part II, a forward kind on (ℓ, C_adm) is an equivalence class of components whose signatures coincide on `C_adm ∖ baseline`. If D′ and D have the same forward kinds on C_adm, they agree on every admitted edit, and Part XIII's "same explanation at this level" principle (which the document elsewhere calls Derivation 2, in Part XVI) says they are one account at that grain: "Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on C *with different decompositions*, or different active commitments, are not one account, and no result here says they are." Conversely, a candidate whose components are not of one forward kind with their anchors fails condition 4 — exactly the case the document names: "a decoy with the same signature on C but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4."

So there is no candidate that is both a decoy and satisfies condition 4: at the anchor level, "decoy with the same world-kind" is not a coherent description. The finding is that this is not a *cheat* to be caught — it is a notational impossibility.

### The four anchored conditions and genuine mechanisms

**Finding 5 — I could not find a genuine mechanism that properness excludes, but I can name one cost that the document does not state.**

Part 0 already concedes: "an **anchored account** is an account at a contract whose anchors are a proper decomposition of the target at the resolution the question needs". Where the target D has one component under the physical module, no proper subnetwork exists, and properness is unsatisfiable. The condition then says: no account of a single-component target is anchored. The document's defence is that the module decomposes (Part XIV: "It is a package, and the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle"). Given that package, this is consistent; but it shows the anchored grade is a claim as much about the module as about the candidate.

**Finding 6 — The document's own accounting of the one-component repackaging is a self-admission, quoted exactly.**

Part V: "The one-component candidate that repackages Sol_D satisfies (E). It is an account at C and not anchored. That is the honest statement of what (E) alone can do, and why the second grade exists." Part XV: "A candidate meeting (E) alone and explaining nothing is already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of Sol_D — and is why the second grade is stated." So (E) alone is properly called loose in the skill's sense — the repackaging is an alternative version that survives it — and this is not hidden.

**Finding 7 — The four anchored conditions exclude *coarse* genuine mechanisms only where the module licenses a finer decomposition of D, and in that case the exclusion is arguably correct.**

Resolution: "Whenever two components of D can be separately varied by pairs in C_adm with distinct effects on Ans_p, they lie in distinct anchors." If a genuine mechanism treats two module-distinguishable subcomponents as one bundle, the mechanism must still reproduce their separate effects — otherwise (F2) will not hold. So resolution here is doing a job (F1, F2 need components that respond to the separated variations), not cutting a genuine mechanism.

**Finding 8 — A likely over-reach: condition 3 read broadly excludes *any* faithful decomposition of a target whose components do not share hidden constraints.**

Part V, condition 3: "No active component's relation is, on C, definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it." On the broad reading, the D–E pair I built in Finding 2 (M = f(I) on {I, M}, O = g(M) on {M, O}) fails, because `L_{k1}(a,b) = proj_{{I,M}}[Sol_D(a,b)]` and `L_{k2}(a,b) = proj_{{M,O}}[Sol_D(a,b)]`. Yet the pair is a genuinely decomposed mechanism. So on the broad reading, the condition excludes a trivial but genuine mechanism. The narrow reading — that only the whole relation Sol_D is excluded — preserves this pair and preserves the negative case the document names ("a component whose relation is the target's own solution set"). The document does not say which reading is intended. This is the loose bolt I would point to first.

**Finding 9 — A possible over-reach: world-kind on the full C_adm.**

Condition 4: "Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ, C_adm), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits." For a higher-level explanation (say, of a chemical regularity), the anchor subnetwork exists at the module's grain and responds to every physically admitted edit reachable at that grain. The component must respond as the anchor does, on the same set of edits. If the module's admitted edit set contains edits that reach below the mechanism's descriptive level, then the coarse-grained mechanism's forward kind is being tested against responses it does not have — and the coarse-grained claim is unanchored. The document has a reply (Part II, "Admissible grains": "a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed"), but the reply speaks to the *admissibility of the grain*; it does not discharge the fact that C_adm is fixed by the occurrence, not by the grain. I mark this **unknown** rather than **excluded**, because whether it bites depends on the module chosen — a test that would settle it: name a physical module with a coarse A_Θ at the coarse grain and check whether the coarse mechanism can be anchored there.

**Finding 10 — The active-route clause is not an over-reach, but it is load-bearing in a way the document does not spell out.**

Condition 4's second clause: "each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically." This is stated, and it is the clause that distinguishes "the mechanism that actually fired" from "a mechanism that could have, and gives the same answer." The document is explicit that this is a difference in *h* rather than in *signature* (Part IX). So a reader who counts "the mechanism that could have fired" as explanatory loses that candidate, but the loss is by design and is stated. Not over-reach; a stated preference.

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

**Finding 11 — Reverse: the direction test is run, not evaded.**
Part VII works the pole-and-shadow example both ways and reports which direction holds where: "The reversed calculation `H = L tan θ` is not [faithful]: intervening on `H` changes the target's `L` but not the calculation's `H` (here τ sends the intervention on `H` to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning `H`, which the reversed organization has none of, so τ is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair)." Held.

**Finding 12 — Hunt the answer in the starting points.**
NonCircular is the condition: "The target's answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component; moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named 'law' does not discharge this." Part V's list of exclusions adds: "'p because p' fails non-circular dependence." Held, with the caveat in Finding 4.

**Finding 13 — Flip.**
Had the one-component repackaging turned out to be anchored, the second grade would be idle. It is not: "it is excluded by properness if it is one component" (Part V). So the anchored grade is held in place by the need to block the repackaging. This is one confirming check; the document does not rest on it alone.

**Finding 14 — Rivals.**
The strongest rival to the document's sufficiency claim is the attack candidate itself, and I could not construct one that survives all clauses. The document names one rival as already known — the one-component repackaging — and rules it out; it names none other among its own negative examples. The document \*does\* concede that the second grade may be too strong in the other direction: "(F) The anchored grade. Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors (Part V) either exclude a genuine mechanism or admit a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change." That is exactly what I have been testing, and I found loose bolts (Findings 8, 9, 10) rather than a clean instance of either failure.

**Finding 15 — Change list.**
The contract is `C=(C_adm∩S)∪K` (Part III). The document is explicit that omissions must be stated: "every admitted pair excluded from C is excluded by the stated scope S, not silently" (Part V), and the adequacy condition ranges over `C_adm∖C` to catch quiet narrowing. Held. Where it is loose is that `C_adm` itself is a primitive the module supplies. Part XIV lists that as a primitive package ("The physical module Θ: substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set A_Θ …"), so the change list is only as rigorous as the module.

**Finding 16 — The world-kind condition versus a "backup".**
The condition already excludes the case of a candidate whose anchor is a backup that "would have responded identically". The document's concession, worked into Part VII for the case of "explanations that remove structure", is that the anchor may be to a *deleted* subnetwork, "whose relation is full by (O)". That is a standing exception inside the anchored grade, not outside it, and it is stated. So world-kind is not, on inspection, univocal across all target types.

**Finding 17 — Where the parts came from: fitted.**

- The one-component repackaging is a case the conditions were fitted *to*: Part V: "a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name." The document says it "is already known to exist" (Part XV).
- Derivation 3 ("selection guarantees nothing off its history") is fitted to a specific population hypothesis: "suppose T is **closed under pointwise alteration off H**". The document discloses the fit: "Where the population is parametric and not closed under pointwise alteration, the survivor may be determined off H by its parametrisation, and this derivation is silent."

**Finding 18 — Where the parts came from: built.**

The three provenance witnesses (Part IV, Sel/Con/Dec; Part X, Build; Part X, Origin) are worked out with the target in view and are set up so they can be caught out. They commit to definite claims about unencountered cases (e.g., the definition of "blind" with respect to a defect, or the "nontrivial binding" clause), which is what makes a targeted swap possible for a later critic. This is the strongest part of the document by the skill's own test.

**Finding 19 — Where the parts came from: asserted.**

- The constitutive conjecture (Part 0) is stated as a conjecture: "explanatory creativity is fully characterized by … (i) … (ii) … (iii) … (iv)." Asserted, and not derived.
- The claim that the anchored grade "is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose" (Part V) is asserted; it is the very claim under attack (A), and it is not proved in the document.
- "Plainly explains nothing" — the criterion of the attack — is not defined anywhere in the document. The attack assumes a reader will know it when they see it. The four negative cases the document names (one-component repackaging, reversed calculation, "p because p", scope drawn to protect a candidate) are being used *as* the operational reading of that phrase. So the criterion of sufficiency is itself partly constructed from the negative cases the document is trying to rule out — a small circularity in the framing of (A), though not in the conditions themselves.

**Finding 20 — Where the parts came from: borrowed.**

- The physical module (Part XIV, "It is a package"; Part XII, "What the module supplies"). Its `Inst_ℓ` is explicitly "a decomposition oracle". The sufficiency claim bottoms out here.
- The notion of a "mechanism" the anchored grade is *about* ("the actual mechanism", Part V) is not defined as a primitive anywhere. Anchor to an active route is the closest the document comes, and that is a condition on the anchor, not a definition of the mechanism.

## 5. What would make it harder to vary

**Finding 21 — The one change that would tighten the anchored grade most is to say which reading of condition 3 is intended.**

As it stands (Part V), "or a projection of it" is open. On the broad reading, condition 3 excludes the concrete two-component candidate I built in Finding 2, which is a genuine if trivial mechanism; on the narrow reading it does not. The document is silent on the reading, and Finding 2's candidate is a perfectly ordinary worked target — the sort of thing the document itself works at Part VI and elsewhere. Making the reading explicit would remove the ambiguity Finding 8 names.

**Finding 22 — The second change is to state whether the non-declared requirement is doing the work, or the anchored grade alone.**

Part V claims the anchored grade "is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose". The attack asks for a candidate *with a non-declared transport*, i.e., with `Sel∨Con` provenance. The document is silent on whether the four conditions alone are claimed sufficient, or only the four conditions plus a provenance witness. Finding 2's candidate tells the reader *which* clause is doing the work — the non-declared clause is — and the section title "Why the anchoring condition is stated the way it is" would be tighter if that were spelled out. Two possibilities, both defensible, and the reader cannot tell which is the document's.

**Finding 23 — One further poke that would sharpen the whole discussion.**
Part V, "Why the anchoring condition …": take a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade on a physically admitted contract with a non-declared transport, and add a second anchor to the same subnetwork — an exact duplicate of an existing component, with the same anchor, same relation, same signature. Removing the duplicate alone leaves every job done (the original still carries it). Does the anchored grade still hold? It should, and the document's own redundancy discussion (Part VI) allows it. But the duplicate has no other job: if `Account` still holds under its removal, part by part the second component is idle. This is a small internal looseness in the *parsing* of "active" in condition 4 — the document does not say active commitments must be non-redundant.

## 6. What this does not show

**Finding 24.** Passing or failing this test does not prove the sufficiency claim true or false. It locates the loose bolts and names what would tighten them. I could not construct a candidate satisfying all four anchored conditions with a non-declared transport that plainly explains nothing, but the four conditions do have looseness (Findings 8, 9, 10, 23), and the sense in which "plainly explains nothing" is to be judged is defined only by the document's own negative examples (Finding 19). A future candidate could pass all the conditions and be a genuine over-reach; my failure to construct one is a report about this attempt, not a certificate.

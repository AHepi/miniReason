## Frozen question

**Question, frozen.** Audit `20a_revision_ledger` against `10_semantics_original` and `20_semantics_revised`. For each `20a` row marked **Done**, decide whether the repair is actually present in 20 and whether it does what the row says. Find any defect of 10 that survives in 20 under new wording. Find any new defect 20 introduces which 10 did not have. Rank by severity. Quote, not summarise.

**Kind of question.** This is a backward/evidence question — “what can we tell from what we see?” — with a purpose check on the ledger. The ledger is the candidate explanation of the revision; the two texts are the evidence.

**Jobs.** (1) For every Done row: the claimed change exists in 20. (2) It repairs the defect named or implied. (3) It does what the row says, at the stated location, without relocating the defect. (4) It does not create a new defect. (5) The ledger’s own status labels survive cross-examination. (6) Surviving and new defects are ranked.

## The ten tests on 20a

### Remove

Remove each claimed repair. For `G2.3`, remove Contract adequacy from `(E)`. Then 20 has no condition that checks an excluded admitted change at all. The remaining non-vacuity only says exclusions are “excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently”:

> “every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently.”

So the job “catch a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change” fails.

For `G5.3`, remove the row. The sentence “A contract consisting only of relabelings…” is still absent from 20. The removal changes nothing in 20. That part is idle.

### Swap

Swap “Done” for “Partly” in `G2.3`. Does 20 change? No. 20’s Contract adequacy still has the same wording. The status label is loose.

Swap “five conditions” for “six conditions” in 20 Part V. The equation is already six conjuncts. The text count is loose.

### Flip the outcome

Had 20 not added Contract adequacy, would 20a still say Done? The row says:

> “Done — note it is now a sixth conjunct of (E), which is a stronger move than the list asked for”

The ledger could have said Done for the addition of a condition even if the condition did not work. The status is easy to vary.

### Poke

For `A_Θ`, the poke is: change Part III from `A_Θ` to `A_Θ(o)`. The mismatch is visible. For Contract adequacy, the poke is: supply an admitted pair where both target and candidate differ from baseline but differ from each other. The condition says nothing. For “World-kind”, the poke is: supply a component of `E` and a subnetwork anchor of `D`; the definition of forward kind does not cover the pair.

### Reverse

Is 20a running backwards from the desired result? Yes in places. The clearest is `G5.3`:

> “| Non-vacuity's last sentence moved | Part V | Done |”

The sentence is not in Part V. The ledger reports the intended move, not the text.

So the ledger’s “Done” cannot rest on a cross-examination. It rests on the revision list.

## What this does not show

This audit does not show that 20 is false, or that 10 is true. It shows which parts of 20a are held in place by the text, which are loose, and which are idle. A false explanation can be hard to vary. The marks are about the ledger and the revision, not about the truth of the semantics.

## 4. Findings

### Derivation 1

**1.** The injectivity hypothesis is doing real work and is not removable. Claim: *"Let t satisfy (F1) on C, and let (τ,σ) be injective on C. Then every active component k of E has, on τ[C], the signature that its anchor subnetwork λ(k) has on C, up to the port translation."* Proof step: *"injectivity of (τ,σ) on C makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates."* If (τ,σ) merges (a,b) ≠ (a′,b′) in C, then F1 forces the two anchor relations to agree, while `sig_{τ[C]}(k)` contains a single triple at the collapsed pair and `sig_C(λ(k))` contains two — so the sets differ and the conclusion fails as stated. **Held by the injectivity hypothesis**; the hypothesis should be *called* load-bearing.

**2.** The conclusion presupposes a reading of (F1) that (F1) does not state. (F1) is *"proj_{V_k}[Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b)] = L_k(τ(a),σ(b))"* — a projection onto E's ports applied to a solution set defined on the anchor's ports. Part II says only that a subnetwork's *"visible ports are a declared subset V_U^vis of its ports, the rest being hidden"*, while the transport supplies *"a subnetwork of D with a port translation"*. The proof then *asserts* *"the visible ports of the anchor being those λ translates to V_k"*. So the hypothesis carries an unstated condition on λ and on the anchor's declared visible ports. **Loose; one line fixes it.**

**3.** D1 states its result as *"a faithful component is of one forward kind, on C, with its anchor subnetwork"*, but Part II defines that relation only inside one organization: *"Two components j,j′ are of one forward kind on (ℓ,C) when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on C\{(1,b):b∈B}"*. Across organizations, with a **subnetwork** in place of a component, the notion is neither defined nor given a footprint. Part V's world-kind condition inherits this: *"Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor."* **Loose.**

**4.** D1 compares on all of C, baseline pairs included; the kind relation excludes them on purpose (*"The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its state"*). D1 is therefore stronger, which is harmless, but the two relations should be distinguished in the text to avoid the reader taking the "state" exclusion to be in force.

**5.** τ's status is contradictory in the document, and D1 and D3 quantify over a notion the document has not fixed. Part IV: *"τ:A_D→A_E translates edits"* (total; only π is qualified "on the stated scope"), yet Part VII: *"so τ is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair"*. D1 says "injective on C" and D3's closure speaks of *"L_j(τ(a),σ(b))"* for (a,b) ∈ C, which requires τ defined there. (F2)'s quantifier and the scope of *"τ(1)=1, τ(a_2a_1)=τ(a_2)τ(a_1)"* are also left implicit. **Borrowed and loose: a transport's scope must be defined before either proof means anything.**

### Derivation 2

**6.** The definition of account-equivalence is a condition on the *projections*, not on the anchors: *"under which, for every (a,b) ∈ C, the anchored relations proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide."* By (F1) those projections **are** the component relations L_k(τ(a),σ(b)); so two candidates with *different* anchor maps, agreeing component relations and translations, are account-equivalent by the definition — while the item's own closing paragraph says *"Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on C with *different* decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account."* The claim's hypothesis ("the same anchor map up to that bijection") shows which reading the author intends; the definition should say it. **Loose, and self-conflicting.**

**7.** (F2) is in the claim's hypothesis and is never used: the proof runs *"(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles."* **Idle hypothesis (removable).**

**8.** The claim is nearly the definition restated, and its consequence is thin. The stronger gloss — *"Two candidates that no listed change can separate are one account at that grain"* — appears in the skill's word list attached to Derivation 2, but the revision states the converse-ish (a sufficient condition with *stronger* hypotheses) and explicitly declines the converse. Per your instruction I do not use the word list as evidence; I record only that the revision does not prove that stronger claim, and that if the revision needs it, D2 cannot supply it.

**9.** Nothing else in the revision cites D2 (no other occurrence of "account-equivalent" or of a cross-reference to it). As a part of the document it is **idle**, unless the author intends it to bear the analysis-of-kinds conjecture — which the document pointedly does not do, citing D1 for that.

### Derivation 3

**10.** The proof needs the same injectivity the claim never states. *"Survival on H constrains only the relations at pairs in H. The altered pair agrees with (E,t) on H, hence survives."* If (τ,σ) is not injective on C, a pair (a,b) ∈ C\H can translate to the same (τ(a),σ(b)) as a pair in H; the altered member then violates (F1) at an H-pair and the step fails. **Hypotheses insufficient as written.**

**11.** The qualifier is doing the real work, and the citations drop it. Claim: *"…whenever some admissible L′ changes that solution set."* Citations: Part 0 grievance 3 — *"There is a result below (Derivation 3) that selection provides no guarantee of fidelity off the history it was selected on, under a stated hypothesis about the population"*; Part 0 (D); Part XV — *"A selection history H⊊C, in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off H, whose survivor is determined on C\H. This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis."* At a pair where no single-component alteration of E's relation changes the solution set, D3 concludes nothing, so Part XV's refuter is not excluded by D3 as stated: Part XV is either over-stated or ambiguous between "the survivor is *determined*" (which would contradict D3) and "the survivor is *faithful*" (which D3 explicitly allows). **Cited as stronger than proved.**

**12.** "Admissible class" is defined by the population under test: *"each relation L′ ⊆ Π_{v∈V_j}X_v in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of T)"*. Consequences: (a) it is not checkable independently of T, so the hypothesis is a property of the very population whose informativeness is at issue; (b) for a realized finite population the closure demands a member for every realized relation at every off-H slot, so the population must be enormous, and the document flags this only as *"a stated hypothesis about the population"*; (c) the label collides with the document's other uses of admissibility (*"A grain is admissible for an occurrence o when the physical module licenses…"*, "admitted edits", "physically admitted"), so a reader may take it to mean the module's permitted relations, which is not what the parenthetical says; (d) the closure is applied one slot at a time, so *"pointwise"* overstates it. **Loose, with a cheap fix (name it "the relations realized in T", and define it inside the claim).**

**13.** The closing sentence does not follow: *"Where the population is parametric and not closed under pointwise alteration, the survivor may be determined off H by its parametrisation, and this derivation is silent. That is why the primitive layer is *not guaranteed*; fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts."* The derivation is silent exactly in the case the document says prevails (a parametric population), so it cannot support the "not guaranteed" claim there; scope the sentence to the closure case. **Loose consequence.**

**14.** The first proof step is misworded: it should say the *translated images* of the pairs in H, and it should state that the altered member agrees with (E,t) in π, τ, σ, λ and every other component relation — which the claim's wording does ("agreeing with (E,t) everywhere except that…"). **Loose wording only.**

### Derivation 4

Part IV: *"That a system with no transport cannot be surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract cannot be surprised, follow from them by unpacking and are not results (Derivation 4 says so)."* The remark's two claims do follow by unpacking, and its statement *"nothing else in this document cites it as support"* is accurate — Part IV cites it only to point at it. **Held by Part IV's definitions; idle as a derivation, and correctly declared so.**

### Derivation 5

**16.** *"Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on D"* fails as constructed, in five ways. Part III: *"It is coherently posed when Sol_D(1,b_0) ≠ ∅, Q is defined on every pair in C, and S and K are disjoint from each other's complements as stated."* (a) Condition (i) is **not encoded**: the baseline component only forces the baseline pair to be "in", and *"Boundary: the target D and b_0, held fixed"* leaves the emptiness of the baseline solution set unconstrained, so a question with an empty baseline is still a solution. (b) Condition (ii) is **not encoded**: nothing relates u_Q's value to the "in" ports. (c) Condition (iii) as printed in Part III is unreadable — literally "S and K are disjoint from each other's complements" is S = K — and D5 defers to it (*"u_S and u_K are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III"*), so the construction inherits a garbled clause. (d) D5 **adds** a condition Part III does not require — *"if u_{(a_2a_1,b)} reads 'in' then so do its factors where the composition is defined"* — which excludes questions Part III allows (a question may range over a composite edit without its factors). (e) **O_p and ρ_p are not represented**, though *"A question is p=(D, C, b_0, Q, O_p, ρ_p)"*. So the solutions are both a superset (incoherent questions admitted) and a subset (factor-closed questions only) of the coherently posed ones. **Loose — the exactness claim is false as written.**

**17.** It is not shown to be an organization in the sense of (O): (O) requires the interpretation to supply L_j(a,b) *"for each j, edit a, and boundary b"*; the construction gives a port list and prose components (*"Components: the closure conditions — the baseline port reads 'in'…"*) but no relation tables, and none at all for its four declared edits (*"toggle a pair; alter Q; alter S; add a pair to K with a meaning"*). So *"It is a D_p in the sense of (O)"* — and with it *"Inst, Deploy, Build and New apply to occurrences that carry it; provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization"* — is **asserted, not established.**

**18.** The citation load is heavy. Part III makes the construction load-bearing by definition (*"A constructed question is the result of an episode with a construction witness whose result is the question-organization of Derivation 5"*); Part X's merit condition and (G)'s Attempt (*"p is a coherently posed question that s instantiates before e"*) lean on it; Part 0 (E) lists question-finding as load-bearing. Part XIV's dependence order **omits D_p entirely**. **Loose, and it propagates.**

**19.** Reverse test: the construction is tailored to produce the desired solution set and then asserts it; and Part III already contains the conclusion by definition. The aim is partly in the starting points. **Loose.**

### Derivation 6

**20.** The named cycle is genuinely broken. Part IV: *"Con(t;h,e) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t, and in which the organization t targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before e — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below."* Since (R) is *"…∧(Sel(t) ∨ Con(t))"*, that clause really does keep (R) out of that provenance witness. **Held, by that sentence.**

**21.** But *"the only cycle that could arise"* is false. A second loop runs through the repertoire: (R) → Con (*"an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t"*) → Build → the binding nontriviality clause (*"neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ"*) → the repertoire (*"The repertoire R_{β,ℓ}(s,ξ) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect"*) → Deploy (*"holds when s at ξ holds a representation of c — by (R)…"*) → (R). Part XIV's assurance (*"provenance depends on Θ and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on Inst and ≡_ℓ but not on (R)"*) is therefore unestablished.

**22.** If the escape is that "the repertoire" in the nontriviality clause is a repertoire of *bindings*, that predicate is nowhere defined — contradicting the item's own consequence, *"No residual predicate is left undefined."* **Either horn is a defect.**

**23.** A third loop runs through ownership and capability: Part X — *"A subhistory is owned by s when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability s possesses in the sense of Part XII (Can)"*; Part XII — *"Can_{Ω,β}(ξ,T;χ) requires an owned retained realization…"*; and Part XIV lists ownership as derived. Either the "owned" in Can is the same notion (a circle) or it is a second, undeclared notion (an undefined predicate). **Loose.**

**24.** The proof method cannot deliver the claim: *"Each definition names its inputs at first use"* is exactly what a circular definition also does. The step needs an actual dependency audit, which the item does not perform. **Loose.**

**25.** Hunt the answer in the starting points: the conclusion is already asserted in Part XIV — *"No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters"* — and Part 0 adds *"It claims that they are all listed."* The "derivation" adds no check over the assertion. **Idle as a derivation.**

**26.** Counterexamples to the consequence, from Parts II–XIII: Part XII's *"Grades. Cap^{q,r}_Ω(ξ) ⊆ Admit^{q,r}_Θ…"* uses Cap^{q,r} and Admit^{q,r}, defined nowhere in the document; Part XI's *"An effect organization R ⊆ A×K×F; a purpose G ⊆ K×F; …a normative relation N ⊆ A×K×Rsn×V_A"* uses K, F, Rsn, V_A undefined (and K collides with Part III's counterfactual set and the (K) tag); Part XIII's *"the coherently posed advanceable challenges"* uses "advanceable" undefined; plus findings 21–23. **The completeness claim is refuted as the document stands.**

**27.** Part XIV's dependence order also omits D_p, which (G) needs. **Loose.**

### Derivation 7

**28.** Valid and held, given Part III (*"An assessment is an event at a fixed (C,Q); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another, and the record must show the boundary"*) and Part VIII (*"A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change"*), and conditional on D5 for the question-organization with C′. **Borrowed from Part VIII and D5.**

**29.** The fixity premise needs a scope the document does not state: "an account on C" includes adequacy, which quantifies over C_adm — *"there is no admitted pair (a,b) ∈ C_adm\C at which Ans_p differs…"* — and C_adm is *"the set of pairs the module in force admits"*. If the episode's later theory changes the module (or a non-physical question's declared admission relation), the quantifier changes while C does not, and Part VIII does not secure fixity of the old claim's truth value. **Loose: the index must include the module, or the derivation must hold it fixed and say so.**

**30.** The Consequence says *"the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded."* Part III and Part 0 divide the job differently: the recording requirement is non-vacuity (*"every admitted pair excluded from C is excluded by the stated scope S, not silently"*) and Part 0 grievance 4 says *"caught by the requirement that exclusions be stated, and then by the adequacy condition."* D7's sentence can be read as making the record irrelevant. **Loose wording, internal inconsistency.**

### Derivation 8

**31.** The class of permitted φ is not defined and the preserved list cannot carry the conclusion. The claim: *"Let φ be a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps λ), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module's admission (A_Θ, Inst, ≺), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and N. Transporting all data along φ preserves (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK)."* Needed but not listed: the organizations' data (ports, domains, components, footprints, boundaries, and A with its composition and identity — required for (F2)'s τ(a₂a₁) = τ(a₂)τ(a₁)); the query Q (required for (A)); the module's Exec and the resource/accuracy grades (required for (CT1), hence Deploy, hence (EK)); the repertoire and Part VI's named background and edit family (required for (N) and Part VI). The proof's *"each of its inputs is carried along"* is therefore an assertion, not a check. **Loose.**

**32.** The exclusion of the pole→shadow recoding is asserted, not derived: *"because λ is among the transported data, a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted φ, since it does not preserve D's structure."* The first clause is true by stipulation (λ is carried); the second clause appeals to a structure-preservation condition on D that is not in the enumerated list. If the list is read as exhaustive, a bijection relabelling ports while carrying the component relations is permitted, and the example would be permitted too. **Loose: say "isomorphism of the (O)-data".**

**33.** The restriction appended after the proof (*"The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, lost event identities, or maps that alter admission"*) is a list of examples, not a boundary. Bijectivity already excludes coarsenings and the hypothesis excludes admission-altering maps; the boundary should be stated first (bijective, structure-preserving, admission-preserving), then the examples. **Loose.**

**34.** Citation mismatch. Part X defines ≡_ℓ as *"the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents"*, but D8's φ additionally carries the physical module's admission, the stated scopes, the counterfactual meanings and N — none of which a recoding of two contents need preserve (contents can be mathematical or modelled) — and D8's result is preservation of (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK), not an equivalence relation on contents. This matters because ≡_ℓ is used in (N) and in the binding-nontriviality clause — precisely where the loop of finding 21 lives. **Cited as a different, stronger notion than proved.**

## 7. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not truth. These are derivation-level defects: D5, D6 and D8 do not carry their stated consequences as written, D1's citation and D3's proof need a hypothesis the document states only in one place, and D2 and D9 do no work. That says nothing about whether the semantics is right, and the document's own principle governs here — *"Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false"* (K2), and *"an established ¬O yields ¬(T∧B∧I) and nothing narrower"* (K3). Where I say a claim is unproved, the support is removed, not the claim.

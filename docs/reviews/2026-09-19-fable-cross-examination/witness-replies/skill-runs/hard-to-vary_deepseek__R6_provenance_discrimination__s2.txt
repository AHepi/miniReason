# 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained.** The provenance classification in Part IV: how the revised selection witness works, how it relates to the construction witness of Part X, and whether the precedence rule and blindness clause do the work claimed for them.

**Over what range.** Part IV, Part X, Part XII’s selection paragraph, Part XIV’s primitive-package list, Part XV’s attack (D) and “Construction reduced to selection”.

**What is asked.** Six questions, kept frozen:
1. Is the revised selection witness still trivially satisfiable by some construction?
2. Does “causally responsible” reintroduce a primitive?
3. Does the precedence rule let a plainly selected transport count as constructed because some construction witness can be gerrymandered?
4. Can every construction witness now be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying these clauses, or vice versa?
5. Is the blindness definition consistent with Part X’s construction witness?
6. What is held, loose, idle, borrowed or unknown, and by what?

**Kind of question.** Mixed. The selection/construction witnesses are claims about what produces a provenance (“what produces it?”). The precedence and blindness clauses are partly rule-status questions (“what counts as what under this rule?”).

In the document’s own words, the revised selection witness has these parts:

- **P1 Population.** “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)”
- **P2 Variation.** “a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\)”
- **P3 History.** “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)”
- **P4 Survival elimination.** “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)”

- **P6 Causal responsibility.** “with the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it”

- **P8 Blindness.** “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation; a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.”
- **P9 Construction witness in Part IV.** “\(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module’s sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below.”
- **P10 Build in Part X.** “\(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an owned subhistory of \(h\), delimited at \(e\), prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers.”
- **P11 Construction witness items.** “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization.”
- **P12 Separation sentence.** “What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”
- **P13 Precedence.** “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.”
- **P14 Part XII selection claim.** “The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\)”
- **P15 Primitive packages and derived provenance.** “The semantics has two. 1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\), the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), the process order \(\prec\), and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\). … 2. The **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\)” and “**Derived.** … Provenance, from physical history (Part IV, XII).”

# 4. Findings

## Part-by-part findings

**Finding 1 — The population clause does its narrow job, but not the anti-triviality job.**
Mark: **held** for excluding singleton population; **loose** against a manufactured decoy.
Quote: “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)”.
Why: it blocks the singleton reading named in Part XV: “A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded.” But it does not block a population of \(\{t,\text{decoy}\}\), where the decoy is built to fail \(H\).

**Finding 2 — The variation operator is underconstrained.**
Mark: **loose**.
Quote: “a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\)”.
Why: the clause does not require that \(\mu\) changed any member, produced a novelty, or generated \(t\). A null operator or a label-swap operator applied once satisfies the wording.

**Finding 3 — The history clause blocks empty history, but not a decoy.**
Mark: **held** for non-vacuity; **loose** against a decoy.
Quote: “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)”.
Why: it needs only “some member” to fail, not \(t\) to face a serious rival.

**Finding 4 — The elimination clause blocks no-elimination, but not trivial elimination.**
Mark: **held** for no-elimination; **loose** against a decoy.
Quote: “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)”.
Why: eliminating a manufactured decoy satisfies it.

**Finding 5 — The survivor clause is definitional.**
Mark: **held** / definitional.
Quote: “\(t\) is a member that survived”.
Why: it identifies the candidate whose provenance is at issue. It does no independent work.

**Finding 6 — “Causally responsible” reintroduces an unlisted primitive unless it is defined from \(\Theta\).**
Mark: **borrowed / unknown**.
Quote: “with the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it”.
Why: the document says the primitive packages are two. Quote: “**Primitive packages.** The semantics has two. 1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\), the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), the process order \(\prec\), and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\). … 2. The **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\)”. “Causally responsible” is not in that list. Part XII repeats it but does not define it: “The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\) … This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV’s is the only one.” Part XII’s list of what \(\Theta\) supplies includes “the process-dependency order \(\prec\) used by histories” but not a causal-responsibility predicate. So either “causally responsible” is a third primitive, or the document owes a derivation from \(\prec\) plus the intervention repertoire. As written, it is borrowed from an external account of causation.

**Finding 7 — Non-triviality over events is vague.**
Mark: **unknown**.
Quote: “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition”.
Why: a mechanical filter can be causally responsible for eliminating a decoy without being a faithful transport to \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition in any checkable sense. The clause does not say what would count as a violation or how to test it.

**Finding 8 — Blindness is defined in a way that permits a blind binding construction.**
Mark: **loose / unknown**.
Quote: “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation; a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.”
Why: a learner that uses only a scalar error signal to build a nontrivial binding is blind by this definition. That matters for the separation from construction, because Part X’s Build clause does not require an itemised defect representation. Quote: “\(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an owned subhistory of \(h\), delimited at \(e\), prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers.” So a blind, scalar-guided binding construction can satisfy both P8 and P10 in principle.

**Finding 9 — Con is underdefined about the construction witness’s defect representation.**
Mark: **loose / unknown**.
Quote: “\(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module’s sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below.”
Why: it requires the target to be instantiated before \(e\), but it does not itself require the construction witness to contain an itemised representation of a defect. That requirement appears only in the separation sentence of Part IV and Part X.

**Finding 10 — Build is held for relay exclusion, loose for the defect-representation separation.**
Mark: **held** for “relay is not construction”; **loose** for defect representation.
Quote: “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.”
Why: the listed witness items do not include “an itemised representation of a defect”. Yet Part IV says: “a construction witness contains both.”

**Finding 11 — The construction witness item list is idle for the separation job.**
Mark: **idle** for the separation job.
Quote: “A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization.”
Why: those items do not by themselves distinguish construction from selection. The distinguishing item — itemised defect representation — is asserted elsewhere, not listed here.

**Finding 12 — The separation sentence is inconsistent with the Build clause as written.**
Mark: **loose / inconsistent**.
Quote: “What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.”
Why: Part X’s Build clause requires an owned subhistory, explanatory use, a nontrivial binding required for that use, and not being a composition of content-preserving transfers. It does not require the itemised defect representation. The same inconsistency appears in Part X: “A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.” Part XV then repeats the stronger reading: “A demonstration that every construction witness — an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation — can be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV, without loss.”

**Finding 13 — Precedence is a free convention that can reclassify by existential witness.**
Mark: **loose / free**.
Quote: “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.”
Why: “if a construction witness exists” is existential. If a later episode reconstructs a transport structurally identical to an earlier selected transport, the rule as written can classify the later transport as constructed. That is intended for reconstruction — “Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not” — but the rule does not state a transport-identity criterion or tie the witness to the origination event. The same sentence tries to protect origination: “Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.” But a reconstruction is not merely maintenance, and the document does not say when a reconstruction is a new token rather than the old one.

**Finding 14 — The maintenance clause is held for maintenance, loose for origination identity.**
Mark: **held** for maintenance; **loose** for origination identity.
Quote: “Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.”
Why: it does not define “origination” for a transport, nor say whether a transport is individuated by its tuple \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\) or by its physical occurrence. Part IV says “A transport from an organization \(D\) to an organization \(E\) is \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\)”, which reads like a type, but the surrounding talk of “history” and “origination” reads like a token.

**Finding 15 — Part XII’s selection paragraph is borrowed, not a definition.**
Mark: **borrowed**.
Quote: “The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\) … This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV’s is the only one.”
Why: it names “causal responsibility” as part of the claim but does not reduce it to \(\Theta\)’s listed supplies.

**Finding 16 — Part XIV’s primitive list omits causal responsibility.**
Mark: **held** for the two-package claim; **loose** for the claim that nothing else is primitive.
Quote: “**Primitive packages.** The semantics has two. 1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\), the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), the process order \(\prec\), and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\). … 2. The **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\), when a question invokes one.”
Why: if “causally responsible” is not definable from those, the list is incomplete.

**Finding 17 — The derived-provenance claim is not fully supported.**
Mark: **loose**.
Quote: “**Derived.** … Provenance, from physical history (Part IV, XII).”
Why: provenance depends on selection, and selection depends on “causally responsible”, which is not derived in the document’s dependence order. Part XIV says “(R) depends on (F1)–(F2), \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and physical provenance; provenance depends on \(\Theta\) and on Part X’s witnesses”. If \(\Theta\) does not supply causal responsibility, the derivation has a gap.

Why: removal returns the singleton reading that Part XV says is excluded: “A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded.”

Why: removal would allow a static population with differential survival. But because the clause does not require \(\mu\) to have changed anything, it is weak even when present.

**Finding 20 — Remove non-empty non-trivial \(H\).**

Why: it does the job of excluding “merely consistent”, but the document does not define the predicate doing that job.

**Finding 23 — Remove non-triviality over events.**
Mark: **held** for the intelligent-selection exclusion.
Quote: “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition”.
Why: removal would allow an event that faithfully represents the target or the survival condition to count inside selection.

**Finding 24 — Remove blindness.**
Mark: **held** for the itemised-defect exclusion.
Quote: “no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation”.
Why: removal would permit a sighted variation process to count as selection.

**Finding 25 — Remove “nontrivial binding construction required for that use”.**
Mark: **held**.
Quote: “contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**”.
Why: removal would let relay or mere recoding count as construction. The document says: “Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.”

**Finding 26 — Remove “target instantiated before \(e\)”.**
Mark: **held** in Con; **idle** in Build.
Quote: “the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\)”.
Why: it separates construction from a blind variation process that never has the target available. But it is not in the Build clause; Build has “for explanatory use of \(c\)” instead.

### Swap

**Finding 27 — Swap realized transports for descriptions of transports.**
Mark: **held**.
Quote: “a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports”.
Why: swapping in descriptions breaks the physical-module requirement. Part XII says the selection witness is “a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\): realized transports, physically admitted variation, a survival condition enacted by the environment.”

**Finding 28 — Swap “causally responsible” for “consistent with”.**
Mark: **unknown**.
Quote: “causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it”.
Why: the document explicitly forbids the swap, but gives no test that would tell the two apart. A poke would be: intervene on the filtering and see whether \(t\)’s presence changes. The document does not supply that.

**Finding 29 — Swap constructed and selected in the precedence rule.**
Mark: **loose / free**.
Quote: “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*”.
Why: the precedence is a convention for sorting cases where both witnesses exist. Nothing in fidelity forces constructed to outrank selected. If the order were reversed, a consulted case could be classified differently. The document’s reason is creative: “Construction is not selection.” But that reason does not make the ordering itself held by a change.

**Finding 30 — Swap itemised representation for scalar summary.**
Mark: **held** by the definition, but it weakens the separation.
Quote: “a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted.”
Why: a scalar-guided process remains blind. This is exactly the route by which a blind binding construction can satisfy both blindness and Build (see Finding 8).

**Finding 31 — Swap explanatory use for any use in Build.**
Mark: **held** if explanatory use is doing work; **loose** because Con does not echo it.
Quote: “prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for explanatory use of \(c\)”.
Why: Con says only “whose construction witness prepares \(t\)”. It does not repeat “explanatory use”. If any use sufficed, Build would be easier to satisfy.

### Flip

**Finding 32 — Flip elimination.**
Mark: **held**.
Quote: “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)”.
Why: if no member had been eliminated, the witness would fail. The clause is outcome-specific.

**Finding 33 — Flip construction binding.**
Mark: **held**.
Quote: “a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\); it is **required for the use** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for \(c\) on its contract.”
Why: if the binding were a relabelling already in the repertoire, Build would fail. This clause is doing real work.

### Poke

**Finding 34 — Poke causal responsibility.**
Mark: **unknown**.
Quote: “the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence”.
Why: the needed poke is: remove or alter the filtering; \(t\) should disappear or change. The document does not state this test. Without it, “causally responsible” remains a label.

**Finding 35 — Poke \(\mu\).**
Mark: **loose**.
Quote: “a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once”.
Why: applying \(\mu\) should alter selection status; not applying it should not. But if \(\mu\) is the identity, applying it changes nothing. The clause does not exclude this.

**Finding 36 — Poke blindness.**
Mark: **held** in definition; **unknown** because “defect” is underdefined.
Quote: “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation”.
Why: inserting an itemised defect representation should break blindness. Changing a scalar summary should not. That pair is stated. But the selection witness does not say which defect is at issue — a failed pair, a failed member, or a failed relation.

**Finding 37 — Poke construction binding.**
Mark: **held**.
Quote: “it is **required for the use** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for \(c\) on its contract.”
Why: this gives the poke. Deleting the binding should break the account; if it does not, the binding is idle.

### Reverse

**Finding 38 — The witness can be reconstructed after the survivor is known.**

Quote: “a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered” and “\(t\) is a member that survived”.
Why: the document does not require \(H\), \(\mathcal T\), or the survival condition to be fixed before \(t\)’s survival is observed. A witness written after the fact can choose a decoy and a \(H\) that make \(t\) the survivor.

**Finding 39 — Causal responsibility is meant to block the reverse reading, but has no test.**
Mark: **unknown**.
Quote: “with the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it”.
Why: this is the clause that would distinguish selection from post hoc story. But it is not operationalised.

### Build the best rival

**Finding 45 — Rival to the selection/construction separation: scalar-guided binding construction.**
Mark: **critical**.
Quote: “a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted” plus “contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**”.
Why: a process that varies bindings under a scalar error signal, has a target instantiated, and builds a nontrivial binding required for explanatory use is blind by Part IV’s definition and has a construction witness by Part X’s Build clause. The separation sentence says construction requires itemised defect representation, but Build does not. This is the best rival because it satisfies both definitions without gerrymandering.

**Finding 46 — Rival: gerrymandered construction after selection.**
Mark: **loose**.
Quote: “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists”.
Why: if a later episode reconstructs a transport structurally identical to an earlier selected one, the rule can call the resulting transport constructed. The document says “Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not”, so this is sometimes intended. The danger is when the same token is reclassified because a witness can be written for a copy.

**Finding 47 — If no change tells selected from constructed for a given token, ask a finer question.**
Mark: **unknown**.
Quote: “The three are distinguished by their histories, not their outputs.”
Why: the needed finer question is: what is the origination event of this transport token? The document does not define transport identity. Record that as a new question.

# 5. Whole-explanation checks

Finding 10, 44.

4. **Define transport identity and tie precedence to the origination event.** Otherwise “constructed if a construction witness exists” can reclassify a selected token through a later reconstruction. Finding 13, 46, 56.

# Hard-to-vary report on `20_semantics_revised`

**A note on vocabulary, before anything else.** The words I am about to use — *contract*, *query*, *transport*, *parts/components*, *jobs*, *change list*, *held / loose / idle / borrowed / unknown*, *fitted / built / asserted* — are the skill's plain words for this document's own terms (the skill's word list maps "the change list" to the document's "contract", "jobs" to "explanatory jobs (F)", "fitted/built/asserted" to the document's "selected/constructed/declared"). The skill was built from a source version of *this* theory. So where I use those words I am speaking the document's language, not bringing in outside evidence, and I will not cite the word list against the document.

## 1. The question, frozen

Attack (A) as the document itself states it:

> "(A) **Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing." (Part 0, "Where to attack this")

Frozen sub-questions from the user's message: (i) can such a candidate be built, and if not, which of the six conjuncts of (E) and which of the four anchored conditions blocks each of the four named attempts — the one-component repackaging, a two-component split, a lookup with a proper decomposition, a decoy with the same world-kind; (ii) do the four anchored conditions exclude a genuine mechanism?

Range: the anchored grade of Account, on contracts whose admitted set is the physical module's (`C_adm`), with a transport whose provenance is selected or constructed. Excluded from the question by the document itself: mathematical organisations under a declared admission relation, and the invention of a new dimension of variation.

---

## 2. The jobs

Each as a contrast (why this and not that):

1. **Admit every faithful re-decomposition, admit no restatement.** The grade must pass a mechanism and block the one-component repackaging of `Sol_D`.
2. **Separate a mechanism from a decoy that coincides on the contract someone chose** — but not from one that coincides on *every* admitted change.
3. **Answer the question asked**, not a neighbour.
4. **Not hide the answer in the starting points.**
5. **Not be satisfiable by a contract drawn to exclude the embarrassing change.**
6. **Do all of this on the full admitted contract, not just the selected one.**
7. **Do it without inspecting a label.** "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes. None inspects a label." (Part V)

---

## 3. The explanation in parts (the document's own words)

The claim under test is: *the anchored grade plus a non-declared transport is enough — no candidate satisfies it and explains nothing.*

Its parts, named by the document:

- **(F1) Component fidelity.** "For every active component k of E with anchor subnetwork λ(k)⊆D, and every (a,b)∈C, the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of λ(k) and projecting away its hidden ports equals the component relation of k under the translated edit."
- **(F2) Assembly fidelity.** "π[Sol_D(a,b)]=Sol_E(τ(a),σ(b))".
- **(A) Question fidelity.** "Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b))=Ans_p(a,b)".
- **Non-circular dependence.** With the witness: "there exists an edit τ(a) on E … that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block B⊊Γ … under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way. Removing all of Γ changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing."
- **Non-vacuity.** "Sol_D(1,b₀)≠∅" plus a contract "in the form of Part III".
- **Contract adequacy.** "C is not **protective** for ℰ".

Then the four anchored conditions:

1. **Properness.** "Every anchor λ(k) is a proper subnetwork of D, and the anchors of the active components refine a cover of D's components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of D that some pair in C_adm touches lies outside every anchor."
2. **Resolution.** "Whenever two components of D can be separately varied by pairs in C_adm with distinct effects on Ans_p, they lie in distinct anchors."
3. **No global relation.** "No active component's relation is, on C, definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name."
4. **World-kind.** "Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor… Where the physical module supplies a history for D, each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically."

And the summary claim: "Anchoring is therefore **component** fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label. It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose, and it is what 'the model is about the actual mechanism' means here."

## 4. The change list

What the claim ranges over: different targets `D` and their module-supplied decompositions; different `C_adm`; different transcriptions `τ,σ,π,λ`; different provenances (selected/constructed/declared); different queries.

What it leaves out, and where that is said: mathematical organisations under a *declared* admission relation (stated, Part VII); "the invention of a new dimension of variation — a port the target organization did not have" (stated, Part X and XV); explanation by symmetry and explanation of an effect as a selection artefact, with "no construction here" (stated, Part XV). Honest exclusions.

What it does **not** say up front is that the claim has never been run: "Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment; the experiments that have been run test proxies" (Part 0).

## 5. The ten tests

### Remove

**Finding 1 — the one-component repackaging is blocked twice, and the document also blocks it a third way without noticing.** Conditions 1 and 3 both kill it; the document says so: "The one-component candidate whose single relation is Sol_D satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3." Conditions 1 and 3 are therefore **two routes to one job** (killing that candidate); either alone does it. But the document's claim that it "satisfies (E) on every contract" is in tension with its own non-circular witness. With Γ of size one, the witness demands "a nonempty **proper** sub-block B⊊Γ" — and a set of size one has no nonempty proper sub-block, while "Removing all of Γ … witnesses nothing." On the literal reading, non-circular dependence is *unwitnessable* for a one-component candidate, so it fails (E), not merely the anchored grade. Either the document's sentence is wrong, or "sub-block" means a subnetwork of E rather than a subset of Γ — and a one-component E has no proper subnetwork either. The "already known to exist" counterexample to (E) alone (Part XV) may therefore not exist.

### Swap

**Finding 2 — "or a projection of it" is load-bearing, and only for one case.** Swap condition 3's phrase "definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it" for plain "Sol_D(a,b)" and a component whose relation is a projection of the target's own solution set survives — possible exactly when its anchor is a *proper* subnetwork that is redundant (D's other components add nothing on those ports), so that proj Sol_λ = proj Sol_D and (F1) holds. That clause is what stops the projection being parked in a component anchored to a proper but redundant subnetwork. **Finding 3 — Condition 2 (resolution) is the one of the four for which the document exhibits no case at all.** Neither a positive case nor a failing candidate is given; the pokes offered are condition 4's ("a decoy with the same signature on C but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4") and condition 3's. So conditions 1 and 3 are held by the one-component case, condition 4 by the decoy case, and condition 2 by nothing exhibited.

### Flip the outcome

**Finding 4 — the sufficiency claim flips honestly.** Had the attack succeeded, the document says the anchored grade would fall: "This would show change-fidelity plus proper anchoring is not enough" (Part 0), and "A candidate meeting the anchored grade of (E) on an admitted contract with a non-declared transport, which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency" (Part XV). The claim is stated so it can be caught out. Not a defect.

### Poke

**Finding 5 — Condition 3's poke is a form-test, not a change-test, and that is where a genuine mechanism is most likely to be excluded.** For each anchored condition, ask for a change that should alter the verdict and one that should not. Condition 1: replacing D by a one-component target should flip it (and does); renaming components should not. Condition 4: an edit in `C_adm` on which the candidate and its anchor part company should flip it (and does, "fails 4"); a purely historical difference should not (and does not, where no history is supplied). Condition 3 has no such pair: it says a relation is out "by name" if it *is* Sol_D or a projection. But a variational principle — "the trajectory is the one that minimises the action" — is a relation on the whole target whose *functional* can be poked (change the Lagrangian and the selected trajectory moves). It has a change-response, so the change-fidelity machinery should want to keep it; condition 3 discards it on its *shape*. That is the sharpest candidate for "excludes a genuine mechanism," and it is the one condition of the four that is not a change test — while the document advertises that "None inspects a label."

### Reverse

**Finding 6 — nothing in the grade runs backwards.** (F1) is component-forward; (A) compares profiles forward; condition 4 extends (F1) to `C_adm`. The direction result of Part VII is separate and correct as far as it goes.

### Hunt the answer in the starting points

**Finding 7 — the grade's slogan is the conclusion restated as a premise, and the document marks the restatement only in part.** "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy … and it is what 'the model is about the actual mechanism' means here." But world-kind *defines* "about the mechanism" as "of one forward kind with its anchor on `C_adm`", and the anchor is a piece of the module-supplied decomposition. So "the account is about the mechanism" is "the account's parts behave like the target's parts". The document marks the *residue* — "What remains after it — a label that discriminates where no admitted change and no history does — is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV, which this document does not claim to have proved" — and the wider identification is marked once, in Part 0, as "the constitutive conjecture … A genuine explanatory achievement that these four cannot represent counts against the conjecture. A candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too." It is asserted, not hidden; but it is asserted.

### Add a job

**Finding 8 — the one job that would tighten it most is the one it cannot do.** Add: *the account must not be satisfiable by the target itself.* Build the photocopy: E with the same ports, components and relations as D, Γ all of E, and the identity transport (λ(k) = the k-th component of D). Then (F1) holds by construction, (F2) holds with π=id, (A) holds, non-circularity is witnessed by deleting any component, and conditions 1–4 hold whenever D has two or more components, all of them on the active route. The photocopy *is* D restated as its own explanation, and the grade passes it. What blocks the attack here is not a condition but provenance: by the precedence rule, "it is constructed if a construction witness exists; otherwise selected if a selection witness exists; otherwise declared" — the identity transport has neither witness, so it is **declared**, and the attack requires non-declared. Give it a selection witness (a population of anchor-maps tried, one surviving on an encountered history, blind to the defect) and the candidate stands. **This is the closest I can come to the counterexample, and it converts attack (A) into the constitutive conjecture**: the photocopy is, by the document's own lights, a faithful decomposition of the target, hence an account — so it is not "plainly explaining nothing," unless D's module-supplied decomposition is not the mechanism's decomposition, which is the module's business, not the grade's.

### Build the best rival

**Finding 9 — for this question the rival and the claim are one explanation.** Rival: "the anchored grade certifies a faithful re-decomposition of D at the question's resolution, not explanation." I could find no candidate on the change list that the two claims treat differently. Per the skill, say so: at this level they are the same claim, and the finer question — *is a faithful re-decomposition of the module's own decomposition of D an explanation?* — is a new question, and it is the constitutive conjecture.

### Check the patches

**Finding 10 — the anchoring patch is stated, not quiet, but one of its four parts has no other job shown.** (E) alone failed on the one-component candidate, and the document says so openly: "The one-component candidate that repackages Sol_D satisfies (E). It is an account at C and not anchored. That is the honest statement of what (E) alone can do, and why the second grade exists." The patch narrows what counts as an account, loudly. But of the four added conditions, condition 2 (resolution) has no exhibited case and no other job in the document — the skill's warning about "a part added with no other job" attaches to it, weakly.

### Look inside

**Finding 11 — it does look inside, but only half-way.** (F1) is component-level: "prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition." But the anchored grade extends only (F1) to `C_adm`, not (F2): "Anchoring is therefore **component** fidelity on the full admitted contract." So a candidate whose components each track their anchors on every admitted change while its *assembly* misfits on an admitted change outside C is anchored. Condition 4's summary sentence — "fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits" — is true of components only.

### The four named attempts, and what blocks each

**Finding 12 — the four named attempts, decided.**

- **One-component repackaging** — blocked by condition 1 ("no anchor is the whole target") and by condition 3 ("excluded by name"), and, on the literal reading of the non-circular witness, by non-circular dependence as well (Finding 1).
- **Two-component split** — blocked by (F1), which forces each piece to be the projected solution set of an *actual* proper subnetwork of D, and by condition 2 wherever the pieces are separately variable with distinct answer-effects. The escape route — a piece whose relation is a proper subnetwork's projection, strictly looser than any projection of Sol_D — is not caught by condition 3, but such a piece is a faithful piece of its anchor, not a decoy. No counterexample.
- **Lookup with a proper decomposition** — blocked by **nothing**, and the document concedes it: "A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account." So this attempt fails as a counterexample only because the document *identifies* faithful decomposition with account. That identification is the conjecture.
- **Decoy with the same world-kind** — deliberately not excluded. If it shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every admitted change, then "two components no admitted change separates are one forward kind there" (Part 0), and by Derivation 2 it is the same account at that grain; a decoy that differs on any admitted change fails condition 4. The only surviving gap is a difference of *history* alone — and condition 4 closes that only conditionally, "Where the physical module supplies a history for D."

**Finding 13 — the four conditions against a genuine mechanism.** Three exclusions I can name.

(i) Condition 3 excludes every explanation whose form is a global selection principle (least action, maximal entropy, equilibrium, a conserved functional). It does this by shape, not by change-response (Finding 5). The document's defence — such a component "is the explanandum repackaged as a law" — is asserted, not argued, and is exactly what a variationalist denies.

(ii) Condition 1's coverage clause — "no component of D that some pair in C_adm touches lies outside every anchor" — forces the account to cover answer-irrelevant parts of the target. A genuine *lean* mechanism, one that cites only what the answer depends on, fails condition 1; only the fattened version passes.

(iii) Condition 4's active-route clause conflicts with Part VI's redundant routes: "Redundant routes. Γ={a,b}, S={{a},{b},{a,b}}: each is contributory, neither indispensable." A mechanism whose explanation includes a genuine backup route fails condition 4 ("not a backup that would have responded identically"), and condition 1 forbids dropping the backup's component from coverage. Where a history is supplied and a component of D is touched but never active, conditions 1 and 4 can be jointly unsatisfiable. This is a direct internal tension, not a philosophical one.

**Finding 14 — condition 4's central phrase is ill-defined as stated.** "Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor" compares signatures under the translated edits, so it presupposes (τ,σ) injective on `C_adm`. Derivation 1 states that assumption — "let (τ,σ) be injective on C" — and condition 4 does not restate it.

**Finding 15 — bookkeeping.** (E) "holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold" and then lists six conjuncts; "(A)" names both Question fidelity (Part V) and the sufficiency attack (Part 0, Part XV).

## 6. Where the parts came from

- **(F1), (F2), (A), non-vacuity, adequacy — fitted.** They are stated to exclude a named list: "The classic attempts — lookup tables, reversed calculations, conclusion-as-premise, and the one-component candidate whose single relation is the target's own solution set — each fail a named condition." Fitted parts are held only where they were tested; ask what they have never met.
- **Condition 3 — fitted, and fitted by name.** "it is excluded by name."
- **Condition 4 — built.** It commits to what happens on changes nobody has tried: "not only the ones the contract selected … every pair the module admits." That is the mark of a part that can be caught out — and the decoy case is exactly such a catch.
- **Condition 1 — built, but partly asserted.** Properness is a structural condition; the coverage clause is an addition whose job (Finding 13ii) may be to fatten accounts rather than to explain.
- **Condition 2 — unknown.** No case, no witness, no other job shown.
- **The active-route clause — borrowed.** It rests on Part IX's histories and Part XII's process order, neither of which is tested here.
- **"The one-component candidate … satisfies (E) on every contract" — asserted,** and in tension with the non-circular witness (Finding 1).
- **"It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy … and it is what 'the model is about the actual mechanism' means here" — asserted** (the constitutive conjecture), and **borrowed** from the physical module, whose instantiation relation "is a decomposition oracle: that is what 'physics says what a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden."

## 9. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Extend (F2) to `C_adm`.** Right now only components are checked off the contract (Finding 11), so an assembly may misfit an admitted change and still be anchored. This is the single cut that would tighten it most.
2. **Exhibit one case that fails condition 2, or drop condition 2.** As it stands one of the four conditions does no shown work (Finding 3).
3. **State (τ,σ) injective on `C_adm` in condition 4** (Finding 14), and **say whether coverage is meant to reach answer-irrelevant and inactive components** (Finding 13ii–iii) — that is where a genuine mechanism is squeezed.

## 10. What this does not show

I could not build the candidate. That does **not** show the anchored grade is sufficient: it shows I could not find the change that separates "faithful re-decomposition of the target" from "explains." The closest candidate I can construct — the photocopy with a selection witness — is refused by the document on provenance grounds, and if it were given a non-declared provenance it would be, by the document's own identification, an account. Passing this test is not truth, and failing it would not have been falsehood. The sufficiency claim remains a conjecture, and the document says so itself.

The document's own worked example, Derivation 10, is the natural target, and it is explicitly not usable as it stands: "This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it."

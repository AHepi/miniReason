# Step 5 — The ten tests, and the findings

**Verdict on your eight candidates, first, so you can route:** J1 real but narrower than stated (a role/letter clash, not a direction contradiction); J2 real (Part VI and (EK)/(R), not (K1)); J3 real, plus an omission; J4 real, one clause of it sharper than you put it; J5 real, plus a dropped hypothesis; J6 real; J7 real; J8 real. ### Finding 1 (hunt / reverse). The one-component candidate cannot satisfy (E).

NonCircular requires "a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)". A candidate "whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\)" has, on the natural reading, \(\Gamma\) consisting of that one commitment, which has no nonempty proper sub-block — so the existential fails, so (E) fails. But Part V says twice: "The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3"; "The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored." The escape route — that "one-component" describes \(J\) while \(\Gamma\) names more than that component — contradicts "whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\)" as the total content of the candidate. This is load-bearing: Part XV's sufficiency argument needs it — "A candidate meeting (E) alone and explaining nothing is already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) — and is why the second grade is stated."

### Finding 2 (remove one conjunct / count). "Five" against six, and one summary drops the sixth.

Part V: "holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold", then lists Component fidelity (containing (F1) *and* (F2)), Question fidelity, Non-circular, Non-vacuity, Contract adequacy. (E) then writes six conjuncts. Worse than bookkeeping: Part 0's own account of the first grade is "An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated." That is F1, F2, A, NonCircular, NonVacuous — **adequacy is missing**, and adequacy is the conjunct Part V calls "the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change" and Part XV makes an attack of.

### Finding 3 (remove each of four). The anchored grade is described as four, three and two conditions in four places.

Part V: "adds four conditions on the anchor map" (properness, resolution, no global relation, world-kind). Part 0: "whose anchors are a proper decomposition of the target at the resolution the question needs, and whose components are of one forward kind with their anchors under every change the physical module admits" — **condition 3 dropped**. Part I: "Anchoring is fidelity on the full admitted contract with a proper decomposition" — **conditions 2 and 3 dropped**. Part V itself: "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution" — **condition 3 dropped**. Condition 3 is not decorative: the same paragraph says the one-component candidate "fails conditions 1 and 3".

### Finding 4 (swap / poke). Anchored condition 4 is weaker than (F1) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), yet Part V says anchoring *is* fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

Condition 4 requires "of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor"; (K) defines that by signatures coinciding "on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)"; (F1) requires the anchored relation to equal the component relation "for every \((a,b)\in C\)", baselines included. So condition 4 permits a component and its anchor to differ in state at the baseline, which (F1) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) forbids. The bridge Part V lays is: "By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract" — but Derivation 1 is stated "let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\)", and neither Part V's citation nor the anchored grade requires injectivity. So the bridge is stated without its hypothesis, and it proves a strictly stronger thing (signature on all of \(\tau[C]\), baselines included) than the kind-claim it is used to support. Derivation 1's own gloss — "a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork" — inherits both problems.

### Finding 5 (reverse). (E) is not "what can be assessed within a question".

Part 0: "The first grade is what can be assessed within a question." Part I: "Account at a contract is what a question can assess." But (E)'s adequacy conjunct is "there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse" — a quantifier over the admitted set *outside* the contract. Part 0 grievance 4 admits this: "'Faithful on C' is a fact within C. 'C is not protective' is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes." Either the first grade is not assessable within a question, or "within a question" silently includes \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which the assessment event's index does not (next finding).

### Finding 6 (flip the index). Part III indexes an assessment to \((C,\mathcal Q)\); Part VIII and Derivation 7 index a claim to \(C\).

Part III: "An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another." Part VIII: "A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity… its truth value at its own index does not change." Derivation 7: "The assessment is indexed to \(C\); its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index (Part VIII)." So a change to \(\mathcal Q\) alone ends the assessment under Part III while leaving the Part VIII index untouched; and the truth of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\) depends on more than \(C\) — on \(D\), \(b_0\), \(\mathcal Q\), and on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) through adequacy and condition 4. Derivation 7's "may both be true" therefore needs an index wider than the one it states.

### Finding 7 (reverse / poke). "stopped representing… stopped deploying" does not follow.

The full sentence: "surprise at \((a,b)\in C\) is a failure of fidelity on \(C\), so on that contract the system has, by (R), stopped representing the content, and by Part X stopped deploying it." But (R) is an existential — "\(\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con})]\)" — and Inst is a relation: "one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain (a mechanical one, a thermodynamic one, a computational one)". Failing one transport at one pair does not negate the existential. And Deploy is a disposition over a repertoire: "holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\)… integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task \(U\) as a retained capability". Poke: name a change that should restore Deploy and one that should not. The document's own selection response is the first — "the transport is re-tuned within the population" — yet provenance is fixed: "Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance."

### Finding 8 (swap near neighbours). Selection and construction are made exclusive, then given a precedence rule; "non-trivial" carries two senses; re-tuning contradicts provenance fixity.

- "with the following precedence: it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected*…" — but "What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both." If each excludes the other by definition, precedence never operates.
- "non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)" versus "Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of \(\Sigma_h\)" — one word, two conditions, in one Part.
- "Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance." versus "A **selection response** extends \(H\) and lets \(\mu\) act: the transport is re-tuned within the population." If \(\mu\) is applied and a member is eliminated, the definition of Sel is met again, which is a new selection event and so a new transport; if it is maintenance, the phrase "the transport is re-tuned" needs the survivor to be the same bearer throughout.

### Finding 11 (swap). The "one orientation" is one direction but two letterings.

The direction is used consistently: Part IV, Part V and (R) all put the answered-to organization at the tail. The clash is in names. In Part IV/Part V, \(D\) is the target ("an explanation \(E\) is held to its target \(D\) by \(t:D\to E\)"); in (R), \(D\) is the carrier and \(c\) is the target. So (F1)'s "active component \(k\) of \(E\) with anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\)", instantiated inside (R), would anchor the carrier's components in *content* subnetworks, and neither (F1) nor (F2) is written out for that direction. The sentence "This one orientation is used everywhere" is then a promise the notation does not keep for its own flagship formula. Also, Derivation 8's exclusion argument — "a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted \(\phi\), since it does not preserve \(D\)'s structure" — uses as its reason the very thing it has already required of \(\phi\): "that preserves the physical module's admission (\(A_\Theta\), Inst, \(\prec\))". The equivariance claim for anchoring is close to an assumption.

### Finding 12 (remove the module). Grain: declared parameter against module-relative admissibility.

Part XIV lists "the indices — grain \(\ell\) (constrained by admissibility), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\)" as declared parameters ("Neither derived nor primitive; supplied with each use"). Part II makes admissibility depend on the module: "when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain (Part IV, Inst) and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes". Three consequences the document does not separate: (i) for "a mathematical or modelled organization, the data are stipulated" (Part II), there is no Inst and no physically admitted edit, so admissibility is undefined while the rescue rule is unconditional — "Claims can be rescued by moving to a grain only if the new grain is admissible"; (ii) Part 0 and Part I state the anchored grade as fidelity "under every change the physical module admits", while condition 4 is stated on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), and for non-physical organizations \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is "a **declared admission relation** stated with the question" (Part III); (iii) the admissibility clause mixes a signature — which is indexed to a *contract* \(C\subseteq A\times B\) in (K) — with "some physically admitted edit", an element of \(A_\Theta\) alone. The clause as written compares objects of different types.

### Finding 13 (literal read). Part III's coherence clause is unsatisfiable as written.

If the intent is \(S\cap K=\varnothing\), the wording does not say it. Coherence is used downstream: "\(\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)\)… where \(p\) is a coherently posed question".

### Finding 18 (look inside). Part VII's reversed-calculation gloss is self-undermining, and "observed" is used in two senses.

"intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\) (here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), **which the reversed organization has none of**, so \(\tau\) is undefined…)". Separately: "The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\)" — but Part II's observation edit "alters the relation reporting the port without altering what it reports", and Part III says an identification question has "a \(C\) containing edits to the observed value". "Alter the observed value" and "alter the relation reporting the port without altering what it reports" are not the same condition, and both are called observation.

### Finding 19 (poke). "Anchored to themselves" is not the anchoring relation.

Part VII: "the background components (arithmetic, the determinant) are anchored to themselves under the identity translation, which is stated rather than assumed." Anchoring in Part IV is "\(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\)", and condition 1 is "Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\)". This is the one worked mathematical example of the anchored grade.

### Finding 20 (swap). Question-begging fires on transports that (R) says represent nothing.

Part XIII: "An enabling condition \(\chi\) for the use of content \(c\) is **question-begging** when \(\chi\) contains, as a component at the declared grain, a deployment of \(c\) or an organization held to \(c\) by a faithful transport". So a merely declared faithful transport inside \(\chi\) makes \(\chi\) question-begging, while by (R) it cannot be a deployment and cannot represent \(c\) at all. The guard fires on cases the semantics treats as empty.

### Finding 21 (hunt). The merit condition uses (P) for something (P) is not defined over, and is named two ways.

The same condition is called "one condition under (P)" in Part 0 and "the merit condition of Part X" in Part 0's attack (E), Part X and Part XV.

# Report §4 — Whole-explanation checks

- **Flip.** Would the document work if the opposite of each finding were written? For three it would *not*, so those are not free choices: Part XV's sufficiency argument needs the one-component candidate to be an (E)-account (Finding 1); Part XIV's classes need (G) and (EK) as defined (Findings 16, 17); (R) needs a transport whose source is a content (Finding 10). Those sentences are held by the document's own argumentative jobs. Where the flip *is* cheap — the count word, the three summaries of the anchored grade, the "stated rather than hidden" family — the reversed version works as well, so those sentences do no work.
- **Direction.** Several repairs run result-first: "It is what 'the model is about the actual mechanism' means here" (Part V) names the target rather than deriving the condition; the merit condition is "the only merit condition in the semantics" and (P) "does not rank alternatives" — results stated, then guarded. By the poke test the *substance* survives: for condition 4 the document gives both halves (a decoy "with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4"; "A less elegant account satisfies (E) as fully as a more elegant one"). But no poke distinguishes the three summaries of anchoring from one another — which is why Finding 3 is a finding.
- **Answer hidden in the starting points.** "What (R) takes from outside is \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\)"; the module "must say how a lump decomposes" (Part 0); "the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle" (Part XII). So the pivotal question — what counts as the same part — is answered by a declared primitive. That is stated, not hidden. But it gives a single escape route to Findings 4, 12 and 19, and attack (C) — "a case in which a declared kind-label does discriminating work that no admitted change… and no history can do" — can be answered by a module that supplies a finer decomposition, which the document does not undertake to bound.
- **The change list, and what it leaves out.** The exclusions are unusually well stated: the new dimension of variation; explanation by symmetry; explanation of an effect as a selection artefact; aesthetics; no experiment; no class membership. The document even says of one of them, "That is the stated limit." Left out *silently*, as far as I can find: any criterion for the record of an index change; any format for the meaning of a counterfactual pair (Part III: "each with a stated meaning" — the meaning's form is never given); any definition of "correct typing"; and any statement of how (R)'s \(C_c\) relates to a question's \(C\).
- **Build the best rival.** *Rival reading:* the revision is the source theory plus (i) a second grade with a new bridge to (F1), and (ii) a content/organization vocabulary, and the tensions are the seams. What would tell it apart from the document's own reading? Write out (F1) and (F2) explicitly for (R)'s direction with the roles re-lettered, and state whether the anchored grade requires fidelity at the baseline or only kind-coincidence off it. If both go through with no amendment, the rival reading is refuted for Findings 4, 10 and 11. Note the support for the rival: the findings do cluster — Findings 1, 3, 4, 10, 11, 15, 16, 20 sit exactly where the new vocabulary meets the old. That clustering is evidence about where the patches are, not proof that the patches fail.
- **Where the parts came from.** See Step 6. The short version: the mathematics is built and can be caught; the scope sentences are asserted and are protected by "the document says so"; the module is borrowed and declared.

# Report §5 — What would make it harder to vary

Two cuts, in order of how much they close.

1. **State the anchored grade once, and write the representation-direction fidelity explicitly.** One paragraph that (a) fixes the four conditions and references them by number everywhere else; (b) says whether the baseline pairs are inside the anchored condition or outside it; (c) carries Derivation 1's injectivity hypothesis, or drops the sentence that cites it; (d) writes (F1) and (F2) out for \(t:c\to D\) with the letters re-rolled. That single paragraph closes Findings 1, 3, 4, 5, 10, 11 and 19, and turns Finding 2 into a deletion.
2. **Write one instance out, with a job list.** The document refuses to instantiate (EK) for a principled reason, and Derivation 10 disclaims itself. A single instance of (G) alone — an organization, its target, the binding, the edit that deletes it, the obligation with before and after values, and the active route — would force the merit condition, the record, and "explanatory jobs" into a form that can fail. That closes Findings 14, 16, 17, 20, 21 and 24's record item.

Both cuts are cuts of *looseness*, not of content. Neither adds a new mechanism, so neither can rescue the document by adding a part with no other job.

# Report §6 — What this does not show

Hard to vary is not true. This run finds which passages are held, which are loose, which are idle, which are borrowed and which are unknown. It does **not** show that the semantics is false, that any claim about the world fails, that any class defined in Part XIV is empty, or that the revision departs from the file it revises. I did not have `10_Claude_Fable_Semantics_-_standalone_theory.md`; where the skill's word list summarises "Part III", "Part V" and "Part VI" of the source, I treated those as the source's words, not the revision's, and did not use them as evidence. Passing does not certify; failing does not convict. Several findings here may be answerable by a sentence the document has that I did not find — I have quoted where I looked.

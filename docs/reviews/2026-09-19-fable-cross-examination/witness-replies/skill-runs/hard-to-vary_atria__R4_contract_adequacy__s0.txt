# Hard-to-vary report: the contract-adequacy conjunct

**A note on vocabulary.** The skill's word-list maps its plain words ("contract", "admitted edit", "account", "the change list") onto a source file, `10_Claude_Fable_Semantics`. The document under examination, `20_semantics_revised`, is a revision of that same theory, so those terms are *the document's own*. I use them as its vocabulary, not as independent support. No claim below rests on the word-list.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being attacked:** the revision's promotion of *contract adequacy* from an external objectivity condition into a conjunct of `Account` itself.

**Exact passage under attack (Part V, "Contract adequacy"):**

> \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse. This is the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change; a stated exclusion is not thereby a legitimate one.

**And its placement (Part V, eq. E):**

> \[\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}. \tag{E}\]

**Kind of question:** mixed — *what counts as what under this rule* (is this condition well-defined and of the right strength?) together with *does it achieve its purpose* (does it do the jobs the document assigns it?). Both are held fixed for the whole run.

**Range:** all questions \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) with \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), physical and declared-admission alike.

## 3. The explanation in parts

In the document's own words, the working parts of the condition:

- **P1 — the domain.** "there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)"
- **P2 — the target-side test.** "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value"
- **P3 — the candidate-side test.** "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not"
- **P4 — the mirror clause.** "or the reverse"
- **P5 — the strength of the test.** Movement from baseline, not value equality, between target and candidate.
- **P6 — the placement.** Adequate is the sixth conjunct of (E).
- **P7 — the gloss.** "catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change; a stated exclusion is not thereby a legitimate one."
- **P8 — the surrounding claim.** "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes."

**Fair version, strengthened before testing.** The strongest reading of the revision is: adequacy is *deliberately* a question-level, movement-only check over the excluded admitted pairs, complementing (F1)/(F2)/(A) inside \(C\) and the anchored grade's condition 4 over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); together these partition \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) so that no admitted pair is unchecked. That is the version tested.

## 5. Part by part

| Part | Mark | Held by / what would replace it |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** domain \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) | **Held**, with an **unknown** edge | Held by the partition of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\): inside \(C\), fidelity (F1)/(F2)/(A) governs; outside, adequacy governs. Since \(K\) is disjoint from \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) ("pairs the module does not admit"), \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S^{c}\) cleanly. *Unknown edge:* the domain contains pairs where \(\tau\), \(\sigma\) or \(\mathcal Q\) is undefined (finding 3). |
| **P2** "differs from its baseline value" | **Loose** | Replaceable by "differs from \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b)\)". Part III defines exactly one baseline, "\((1,b_0)\)", so the literal reading compares across boundaries; the near neighbour does the job without the spurious failures of finding 5. |
| **P3** \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) | **Unknown** | Part IV types \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) as a function, but Part VII says "\(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair". The condition's truth value at such a pair is undetermined, and it decides a load-bearing example (finding 4). |
| **P4** "or the reverse" | **Held** | Held by J1: a scope can equally hide a *spurious* candidate dependence the target lacks. Removing it leaves a mirror-image hole. |
| **P5** movement, not value equality | **Held** | Held by J2/J4 against the only stronger swap (finding 8). Any stronger version collapses the two grades. |
| **P6** placement as a conjunct of (E) | **Loose** | Replaceable by an external objectivity predicate \(\operatorname{Obj}(E,p)=\operatorname{Faithful}_C\land\lnot\operatorname{Protective}\) that serves J5–J7 identically while keeping J2/J4 (finding 9). Nothing in the document stops the swap. |
| **P7** the gloss | **Loose / overstated** | The formula catches only *movement-discordant* exclusions. A decoy that moves when the target moves, to a different value, is not caught (finding 7). |
| **P8** "every conjunct is a condition … under changes" | **Loose** | Literally true but conceals that adequacy is the one conjunct not assessable from the question alone — it needs \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) (finding 10). |
| **\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) itself** | **Borrowed** | Rests on the physical module's \(A_\Theta\) (Part XII: "the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta(o)\)") or, for non-physical organizations, on "a **declared admission relation** stated with the question" (Part III). Not tested here. |

## 6. Whole-explanation checks

### Remove

**Finding 1 — the *condition* is load-bearing; the *placement* is not.** Remove adequacy from (E) and put it in a two-place objectivity predicate. J5 still holds ("the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics" — now truly a separate conjunct, matching Grievance 4's "Two claims, two indices" exactly). J6 still holds: the objectivity predicate still "catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded". J7 needs rewording but its point survives — (EK) would simply require the objectivity predicate alongside `Account`. Meanwhile J2 and J4, which the placement *breaks* (findings 9, 10), are restored. No job is unique to the conjunct placement.

**Finding 2 — removing P4 alone breaks J1.** Without "or the reverse", a scope may freely hide a candidate dependence the target lacks. The mirror clause is doing real work.

### Swap

**Finding 3 — the baseline swap is unaddressed and it bites.** Swap "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value" for "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b)\)". J1 is unaffected — a protective scope is still caught. So the \(b_0\)-baseline reading is loose. Under the literal reading, any \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) with \(b\neq b_0\) fires the condition whenever the target's answer depends on the boundary and the candidate's does not track it identically — a boundary mismatch, not scope protection.

**Finding 4 — the undefined-\(\tau\) swap decides Part VII.** Part VII states of the reversed calculation:

> here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair

and then:

> The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\): **it is an account at that contract**, and not anchored as a production account, because on the production edits — admitted, and outside the identification contract — its one component is not of one forward kind with the assignment of \(L\).

The production edits are admitted, outside the identification contract's \(C\), hence inside \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\). At those pairs the target's answer moves and \(\tau\) is undefined. So:

- If undefined counts as "does not differ", adequacy **fails** and Part VII's "it is an account at that contract" is **false**.
- If undefined makes the condition **silent**, Part VII is saved — but then adequacy says nothing about a candidate that has *no response at all* to an excluded change, which is the most protective-scope-like case there is.

The document is silently committed to the second, weaker reading. Either way, this is not a nitpick: it is the joint in the theory.

### Flip

**Finding 5 — the flip confirms the condition's necessity but not the placement's.** Had the revision *not* added the conjunct, Grievance 4's "the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V)" would name a condition that does not exist. So the *condition* is needed. Had the movement criterion been value-concordance instead, no legitimately scoped question could have an account (finding 8), so the movement criterion is needed too. **Nothing flips on the placement at all** — the signature of an idle part.

### Poke

**Finding 6 — the poke that should matter most does not register.** For P3, the change that should matter is: the candidate has no translated response at an excluded admitted pair (Part VII's own case). The formula is silent or mis-evaluated there. The change that should not matter — a \(K\)-pair, since "a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning" and adequacy "ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)" — is correctly silent. So the poke pair exists, but the load-bearing half of it is exactly the half the condition does not cover.

**Finding 7 — the too-weak poke.** Construct a decoy faithful on \(C\), and at some \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) *and* \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\neq\operatorname{Ans}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\) — both move, to different values. Adequacy passes. This is the document's own conceded attack, but the concession understates it. Part XV says:

> **A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target. This shows the adequacy condition is too weak…

"Separate" is *value*-level; the formula tests only *movement*. Such a scope does not pass by luck — it passes by construction. Correspondingly, Part V's exclusion table overstates:

> An account whose contract excludes an admitted change that would tell it apart from the target fails adequacy.

"Tell it apart" is value-level; the formula delivers movement-level only.

### Reverse

**Finding 8 — direction is right in Derivation 7, backwards in (EK).** Derivation 7 assesses \(C\) by what it excludes (\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)), not the reverse; narrowing \(S\) enlarges the checked set, so a protective narrowing is caught structurally. Good. But (EK)'s guard names a rescue that cannot work as described:

> A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK).

Since \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S^{c}\), narrowing \(S\) makes adequacy *harder*. The only rescues are widening \(S\) (which then subjects the pair to full fidelity — harder still), changing \(\mathcal Q\) or \(\tau\), or shrinking \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) itself. So "narrowing the contract" must mean narrowing the *admission relation*. For a physical question that is unavailable — \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is fixed by the module's \(A_\Theta\). It is available only where the admission relation is declared, i.e. mathematical questions (Grievance 7: "'physically admitted' is not defined for a mathematical organization, so a mathematical question must *declare* its admission relation"). **The (EK) guard is therefore vacuous for physical questions and live only for declared-admission ones.** The document does not note the asymmetry.

### Hunt the answer in the starting points

**Finding 9 — not circular, but the gloss hides the limit.** Faithful-on-\(C\) does not entail non-protective; a candidate can be faithful on \(C\) and discordant outside it. The condition adds genuine content. But the promise "a stated exclusion is not thereby a legitimate one" is only half delivered: a *stated* exclusion of a value-separating change is legitimate by the formula. The sorting of that case is pushed to the anchored grade's condition 4 ("fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits"), which is a condition, not a corollary — as Part V itself says.

### Add a job

**Finding 10 — adequacy's one non-redundant job, and the cost of where it sits.** Add the job "catch assembly-level discordance outside \(C\)". (F1)/(F2)/(A) are required only on \(C\); the anchored grade's condition 4 is *per-component* signature identity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). A decoy whose anchors are right component-by-component on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) but which has lost a shared constraint could still move its answer discordantly at an excluded pair. **Adequacy catches that; nothing else does.** This is its unique contribution, and it is (F2)-shaped at question level over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

But that is precisely the problem for the placement: (E) now contains a \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)-level requirement while Part 0 still says of the first grade:

> An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated.

Four properties, no adequacy — and:

> The first grade is what can be assessed within a question.

Adequacy cannot be assessed within a question; it requires the module's \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). The revision adds a conjunct to (E) and leaves Part 0's characterisation of the grade, and the sentence "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes", un-updated. The two-grade architecture the document wants — (E) within the question, anchoring over the admitted set — is blurred at exactly the seam the revision welded.

### Build the best rival

**Finding 11 — the rival that does the same jobs and keeps J2/J4.** Rival R: \(\operatorname{Obj}(E,p)\equiv\operatorname{Faithful}_{C}(E,p)\land\lnot\operatorname{Protective}(E,p)\), with `Account` restored to the pre-revision five conjuncts. R serves J1, J5, J6, J7 and preserves J2 and J4. The change that tells the revision from R: a legitimately scoped question whose target has an out-of-scope dependence. Concretely, from Part VII's own materials — "Stipulate \(H:=U_H,\ \theta:=U_\theta,\ L:=H\cot\theta\). Under \(A\) containing interventions on \(H\) and \(\theta\), these ports are inputs and \(L\) is an output" — let \(S\) exclude interventions on \(\theta\). The target's \(L\) moves when \(\theta\) is intervened on; a candidate built for fixed \(\theta\) does not. Under the revision, no account exists for a legitimate scoped question. Under R, an account exists and the objectivity predicate is simply false, which is the honest report. Since a change on the list separates them and the document's own Part 0 favours R, the placement is the easier-to-vary version.

**Finding 12 — the rival that must be rejected, confirming P5.** Rival R′: "no admitted pair outside \(C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\neq\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)". This is (A) on all of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); it collapses the two grades (J4) and forbids scoping (J2). P5's movement criterion is *held in place* by rejecting R′. Credit where it is due: the weakest-thing-that-doesn't-collapse design is a real, defensible choice, visible in the document's two-grade story.

### Check the patches

**Finding 13 — the patches carry the formula's work.** Four patches attach jobs to adequacy: Part 0's silence about it; Part V's exclusion-table sentence ("…fails adequacy"); Derivation 7's consequence ("…catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded"); (EK)'s guard ("A later narrowing … to rescue adequacy"). Checked against the formula: the exclusion-table sentence overstates (finding 7); Derivation 7's job is only movement-level and only where defined (findings 4, 6); (EK)'s named rescue is mis-described and physically vacuous (finding 8). Part XV's entry is the one honest patch — it is stated openly in advance as an attack, which is proper — but it frames the weakness as incidental when the formula makes it structural.

### Look inside

**Finding 14 — adequacy is an output-level check outside \(C\), and the document's own Derivation 9 forbids that move.** Derivation 9:

> If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both.

Adequacy is a function of two answer-profile projections on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\). Two candidates with concordant movement there but different mechanisms are indistinguishable to it — and by Derivation 9, no function of those projections could distinguish them. So adequacy cannot, by the document's own lights, do the mechanism-sorting its gloss claims outside \(C\); that requires the anchored grade. The honest restatement: adequacy catches *movement-discordant* exclusions; mechanism-sorting outside \(C\) is condition 4's job.

---

## 7. Where the parts came from

- **The condition (non-protectiveness): built.** It is worked out with the target in view, commits to every excluded admitted pair whether or not anyone performs the change ("whether or not anyone supplies the change", Grievance 4), and can be caught out — Part XV lists the catch as an open attack. This is the exposure that makes it hard to vary.
- **P5, the movement criterion: built.** It is the weakest test that does J1 without collapsing into (A) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) — a design reason, visible in the two-grade architecture, not a fitted constant.
- **P2, "its baseline value": asserted.** Nobody derived the choice of comparator; Part III fixes only \((1,b_0)\), and the alternative in finding 3 is equally consistent with the text.
- **P3's silence at undefined pairs: fitted.** It is held in place by exactly one thing — Part VII's requirement that the reversed calculation be "an account at that contract". Held only where it was tested, silent everywhere else; Derivation 3's moral applies to it.
- **P6, the placement: asserted.** Nothing derives it. Part 0's summary of the grade omits it, and the rival in finding 11 serves every job it serves.
- **\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\): borrowed** from the physical module or from a declared admission relation.

---

## 8. What would make it harder to vary

**Tighten these two and most of the above closes.**

1. **State the definedness convention for the quantifier.** Either restrict P1 to pairs where \(\tau\), \(\sigma\) and \(\mathcal Q\) are all defined, or add an explicit clause that a pair where \(\tau\) is undefined while \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) moves counts as protective. Then re-run Part VII's reversed calculation against the fixed condition and report whether "it is an account at that contract" survives. This single fix decides the load-bearing example (finding 4) and closes the biggest hole (finding 6).
2. **Fix the comparator and split the gloss from the formula.** Replace "its baseline value" with "its value at \((1,b)\)" (finding 3). Then either keep movement-concordance and rewrite the gloss to say exactly what it does — *adequacy catches movement-discordant exclusions; value-discordant exclusions are the anchored grade's province* — or, if the gloss is intended, strengthen the condition to value-concordance and accept that restricted contracts can no longer have accounts, which requires restating Part 0's first grade rather than leaving it as it stands (finding 10).

A third, optional: move adequacy out of (E) into the objectivity predicate Grievance 4 already describes as a conjunction. That restores the two-grade seam at no cost in jobs (findings 1, 11) — unless finding 10's unique assembly-level job is wanted *inside* account-hood, in which case say so and amend Part 0.

---

## 9. What this does not show

- **It does not show the condition is false**, or that protective scopes are uncatchable. The partition of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) between fidelity-on-\(C\), adequacy-on-\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), and condition 4 is a real structure, and narrowing \(S\) genuinely cannot rescue a candidate.
- **It does not show the revision is wrong about objectivity.** Grievance 4's substantive reply — that non-protectiveness is a condition, not a hope — survives every finding here; the dispute is about *where the condition sits*, not whether it exists.
- **It does not touch the anchored grade**, which is where the document's real sorting work is done and which is not under test.
- **Hard to vary is not truth.** A well-placed, well-defined adequacy conjunct could still fail to capture objectivity; a loose one could still be right. What the marks show is which parts are held by the thing being explained and which could be swapped at no cost.

## 10. One next step

Run the fixed condition on Part VII's two worked examples and report the results. For the odd-order skew-symmetric case, the document says "Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation" — with the three presentation alterations all inside \(C\), \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) is empty and adequacy is vacuous, which is legitimate but should be said. For the reversed calculation under the identification contract, the outcome turns entirely on fix 1, and either answer changes the theory: silence concedes the too-weak hole; failure retracts "it is an account at that contract". That is the single test most worth running.

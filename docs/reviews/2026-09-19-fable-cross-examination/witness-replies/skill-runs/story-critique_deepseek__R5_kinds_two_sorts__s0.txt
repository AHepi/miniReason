## 1. Corrections first

**1.** Your phrase "excluding the baseline pairs from *separating changes*" points at a real seam, but not quite where you put it. ### Sequence B — Parts I–III (organizations, contracts, kinds)

**7. The baseline exclusion: what it costs, what it does not, and the missing case. (your first question)** Four findings, because the answer splits.

*(a) Constants are not lost.* (K) makes the third coordinate the **relation**, not a response: *"sig_C(j) = {(a,b,L_j(a,b)) : (a,b) ∈ C}"*. A constant baked into a relation ("F = 3x" against "F = 4x") differs at every pair in C, baseline or not. The exclusion cannot swallow a constant that lives in a relation.

*(b) Initial conditions and state are merged on purpose, and safely.* Part III says the contract *"contains the baseline (1,b_0)"*, and (F1) is quantified over *"every (a,b) ∈ C"*. So two accounts differing in baseline behaviour are still separated by fidelity; the exclusion bears on kind-**identity** only. That deserves one sentence in Part II — it defuses half your question by itself.

*(c) One narrow class of real distinctness is merged, and the stated reason does not describe it.* *"The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states."* The page manufactures the counter-case itself: *"An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port."* A component replaced by every non-baseline edit in C imposes the full relation at those pairs (*"A deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports"*), so two assigners of different constants are *of one forward kind on (ℓ,C)* — and these are not "two things at different positions … in different states"; they are two different assignment **rules**. Here the reason given is false for the very class the exclusion merges.

*(d) No case on the page is a case for the exclusion.* Compare every neighbouring condition, each of which names its quarry: *"(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition"*; *"This is the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change"*; *"removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block … Removing all of Γ changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing."* The exclusion has one half-sentence and nothing else.

**Not a fault, and do not cut it:** the flip test clears it. Had the page included baseline pairs, I would fault that — kinds would be state-indexed and one kind could not appear twice at different states, which the anchored grade compares across occurrences. So the exclusion is **held**, by the job "let one kind appear at two positions". Better version that keeps that job and closes (c): exclude the baseline pair only *on the ports the component does not assign*, and keep it on the ports it does. Also fold in the contradiction from note 1 — "no pair of the form (1,b) separates two components" — and add the missing case: one sentence, a coarse grain, and the swapping pair of Part XVI.10.

**8. "Of one forward kind … with its anchor" has no definition to appeal to.** *(your second question, definitional half)* Part II defines the relation between *"Two components j,j'"*; Part IV says *"λ assigns each component of E a subnetwork of D with a port translation"*; Part V's condition 4 and Part 0's anchored-account sentence both then say *"Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor."* Derivation 1 patches this inside a proof (*"up to the port translation"*) and adds a stipulation the transport data does not carry (*"the visible ports of the anchor being those λ translates to V_k"*). This is **held**, not optional: the resolution condition *requires* E to be at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about, so anchors must be subnetworks. One sentence in Part II extending the definition to a subnetwork whose visible ports biject with the footprint closes it, and λ's port translation should be required to land on V_k.

**9. Admissible grains: a stated constraint with no instance.** *"a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed."* This is the sentence that stops a claim being rescued by coarsening, and it is exactly the example the kinds section lacks. One inadmissible coarsening, with the dead component shown, would do double duty with note 7.

### Sequence C — Parts IV, IX, X (layers, provenances, active route, construction)

**10. **11. The active-route definition does not contain the clause that does the work. (your third question, first half)** *"An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts."* A redundant but actual backup path satisfies every clause of that sentence — being actual, connected, relation-satisfying and identically responsive is what a redundant path **is**. And the page gives away that it is a historical condition: *"the difference between the two is a difference in **h**, not in signature."* Fix: put the actuality clause into the definition (for instance, each component of the route lies on the precedence order's causal path for the result in the occurrence), and say in one line why Part VI's `S`-set cannot do this job — in its own example *"each is contributory, neither indispensable"*, so a counterfactual support notion cannot pick the live path.

### Sequence F — Parts XIV–XVI (collection, defeat list, derivations)

**20. The historical kind is required by the document and equipped by nothing. (your second question in full)** *"A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance."*

- *Demand is real, so it cannot be deleted.* Derivation 2 names it as the remedy for underdetermination (*"The remedies are a finer contract — a new question — or a history, which the semantics represents as a historical kind"*), and condition 4's actuality clause uses a component-level history (note 11).

**21. The analysis-of-kinds conjecture: three statements, two undefined terms, and a quantifier the refutation condition cannot meet. (your fourth question)**

## 3. How it fits together

The document is organised around one cut — signature against history — and all four of your questions are questions about that cut. Part II places the cut at the identity-edit pairs and argues for it in half a sentence (note 7). Part V's condition 4 crosses the cut and does not say so, while Part 0 and Part V describe the grade as if it did not (notes 11–12). Part IV equips the historical side for transports; the kinds section claims it for components without the witness (note 20). And the conjecture is the claim that the cut is exhaustive, stated three ways and refutable by none (note 21). The pattern count on the central problem ("when two things are indistinguishable under every change the question admits, what justifies treating them as different?") is genuinely good — forward kinds, historical kinds, transport provenance, the actuality clause, the declared counterfactual set K, Derivation 3's silence, and the swap case's *"identity, at this grain and contract, is exhausted by trajectory"* are six different answers to one question, and that is why the document is worth attacking rather than dismissing. Two of those answers are never made to face each other: the fidelity-only answer of Grievance 12 and the actuality answer of condition 4 sit in different Parts and never meet in a scene. That is the loose thread, and it is the same thread as your third question. The fidelity half of the document is well built, well illustrated and well audited (I sampled Derivation 6's dependence order at Bearing → Account, Deploy → (R), Build → (F1)/(A), and at the cycle-breaking clause *"a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness"* — the edges hold).

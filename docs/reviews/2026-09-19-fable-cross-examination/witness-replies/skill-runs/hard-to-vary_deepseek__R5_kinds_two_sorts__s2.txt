## 0. Note on vocabulary, and what I am not doing

The skill's plain words are **this document's** words: its "change list" is the document's *contract*, its "parts" are *components*, its "match-up" is a *transport*, and *provenance*, *forward kind* and *anchored grade* come straight from the file. Where I use one of them I am using the document's term, not an outside standard, and I am not treating the skill's word-list as evidence about this revision. This is a criticism, not a truth-meter; loose is not wrong.

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained, over what range, asking what.** The revision's account of kinds and of the anchored grade, over: Part II's (K) clause and the forward/historical kind definitions; Grievance 12; Part V's condition 4 and the "Anchoring is therefore…" paragraph; Part IX's active route; Part XIV's "Derived"; Part XV's attack (C) and "A discriminating kind".

**The four sub-questions, as asked:** (i) does excluding the baseline pairs lose real distinctions; (ii) is a historical kind well-defined without the Part IV witnesses; (iii) does the actuality-of-route condition smuggle history into fidelity; (iv) is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture now refutable.

**Kind of question:** mostly *what counts as what under this rule* (the kinds), with one *does it achieve its purpose* (does condition 4 do what its gloss claims), and one hidden *what produces it* (the active-route condition runs on the target's history).

**Short answers.** (i) No loss for fidelity or adequacy — the question's contract always contains its baseline and fidelity ranges over all of it — but there *is* a loss at condition 4's gloss, and the exclusion is wider than its stated reason. (ii) No: the witness it names does not exist for components, two of its four provenances are undefined, one of Part IV's three is missing. (iii) Not into fidelity — every conjunct of (E) is change-only — but yes into the *anchored grade*, which two sentences describe as pure fidelity on a larger contract. (iv) It is stated in refutable *form*; three terms in the refuting case cannot be run, so as it stands it is not refutable in practice.

## 3. The explanation, taken apart in its own words

Stated back, strongest version: *kinds of components are edit-signatures on a contract, with the identity-edit pairs dropped because the relation there is the component's state; a second kind is a predicate on the producing history; transports are individuated by history while components are individuated by signature; the anchored grade is component fidelity on the whole admitted contract with a proper decomposition; and if any label discriminates where no admitted change and no history does, the conjecture dies and correspondence is primitive again.* That is the version I test.

## 5. The ten tests, run

**Remove.** Remove the historical kind: no predicate of Parts V–XI breaks; only the conjecture's second disjunct and Grievance 12 need rewording. Remove condition 4's active-route sentence: the anchored grade becomes what Part 0 says it is, and the document loses its *only* verdict that needs `h` — nothing else covers a cause-versus-backup pair that no admitted edit separates. So the two additions do not support each other — **F12**.

**Swap.** Swap "a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component" for "the provenance of the transport that fixes the component's anchor": the active-route case is lost, because which subnetwork in `h` is live is not how any carrier came to be. **F6**.

**Flip.** Suppose someone produces the discriminating kind Part XV asks for. The revision absorbs it by the same sentence: "if their histories differ, that is a *historical* kind" (Grievance 1) — and any two occurrences' histories differ. The explanation covers the refutation and the confirmation equally well. **F8**.

**Poke.** For the exclusion: change nothing but *where* a candidate's one difference sits — move it from a non-baseline admitted pair (the document says such a decoy "fails 4") to an identity-edit pair at a different boundary, and it passes. One change that should not matter: rename the components. **F3**.

**Reverse.** Does the anchored grade run backwards? Its first three conditions are forward (candidate responses against the target's). Its fourth, in the active-route sentence, runs from the target's history to a verdict about the candidate — a direction the document's other conditions do not have. **F9**.

**Hunt the answer in the starting points.** The conjecture's conclusion — labels are not primitive — is pre-installed the moment "historical kind" means any predicate on the history: the label "this one is the cause" tracks the actual causal path, so it is already a historical kind. **F8**.

**Add a job.** If the revision is right, a question must be able to ask *which of two signature-identical components is the operative one*, and get different answers. It can — through the active-route clause. That kills the rival "the semantics cannot represent a difference no admitted change separates". But it also shows the resource doing the work is `h`, not the historical kind. **F12**.

**Build the best rival.** The rival is Part 0's own attack (C): a kind-label whose discrimination is primitive. No change on the list separates the rival from the revision, because "no history distinguishes" is only as tight as "historical kind". At this grain they are one account; the finer question — *which* histories count as kinds — is the one still owed.

**Check the patches.** The revision adds a second kind exactly where signature-individuation could not reach (a difference no admitted edit detects). The added part has no other formal job, and its definition is the unconstrained one, so it buys the conjecture's truth at the price of its refutability — **F12**, **F8**.

**Look inside.** Forward kinds have workings: signature, footprint bijection, contract, grain. Historical kinds have only a verdict-word, "history". **F5**.

## 6. Findings, numbered

**F1. The exclusion costs nothing in fidelity.** Part III: "Fidelity conditions range over all of `C`", and "it contains the baseline `(1,b_0)`". (F1) is stated "for every `(a,b) ∈ C`". So a wrong constant, a wrong initial condition, or a wrong state at the contract's baseline still fails the account. Held.

**F2. The exclusion is wider than its reason.** "The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**" — singular, and about *state* — while the formula drops `(1,b)` for **every** `b ∈ B`. The document never says whether the identity edit at a boundary other than `b_0` is an admitted pair. Consequence for the document's own Grievance 2, "the question needs either a finer contract or a history": for a difference that appears only at identity-edit pairs, no finer contract can ever separate them *as kinds*, because the exclusion lives in the kind relation, not in a contract. Only fidelity (on a contract that names that pair) or a history remains.

**F3. Condition 4's gloss is not what its formalisation checks.** Condition 4: "Each active component of `E` is of one forward kind, on `(ℓ,C_adm)`, with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits." But "of one forward kind on `(ℓ,C_adm)`" discards `C_adm`'s own baseline pairs. So a candidate differing from its anchor only at an admitted identity-edit pair outside the contract is not caught — although the document says "a decoy with the same signature on `C` but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4" and although adequacy compares answers, not relations, so it does not catch it either. This is the one place where the attack's first question bites.

**F4. Two clauses count changes differently.** (K) ranges over all of `C`, including baseline pairs; the kind relation drops them. Admissibility uses the first ("every component … has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes"), condition 4 uses the second. The document does not say which governs when they disagree.

**F5. The named witness does not exist for components.** "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance." But Part IV's witnesses are `Sel(t;T,μ,H,Σ_h)`, `Con(t;h,e)`, `Dec(t)` for *transports*, and "This is the only definition of selection". Part XIV repeats the promise: "historical kinds, from provenance witnesses". There is no component-level witness anywhere.

**F6. The list is neither Part IV's three nor defined.** Part IV gives "constructed … otherwise selected … otherwise declared". The historical-kind list drops *declared* and adds "descended from, copied from", which appear nowhere else; the nearest the document comes is "Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not", which leaves copying without a provenance.

**F7. "An occurrence of a component" is a juxtaposition the document keeps apart.** "An occurrence is a physically located carrier" versus "A component is a slot in an organization"; and `Inst_ℓ(o,D)` relates occurrences to whole organizations. So the phrase presumes a bridge the document does not define.

**F8. The conjecture's second disjunct is automatic on the broad reading.** Grievance 1: "if their histories differ, that is a *historical* kind". So any label that tracks any historical fact is already a historical kind, and the only refuting case left needs two components with *identical* histories. On the narrow reading (one of the four relations) the definition is broken (F5, F6). Broken or trivial: either way the disjunct cannot do discriminating work.

**F9. The asymmetry holds for fidelity and fails for the anchored grade.** Grievance 12: "it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change" — true; no conjunct of (E) consults history. But Part 0 says the anchored grade "is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive", and Part V says "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition". Condition 4 adds: "each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) … and not a backup that would have responded identically", and Part IX states the difference plainly: "the difference between the two is a difference in `h`, not in signature". No contract, however large, carries that difference. The honest statement of the anchored grade is: fidelity on `C_adm`, plus a proper decomposition, plus — where the module supplies a history — actuality of route. So the answer to the third attack question is: it smuggles history into the anchored grade, not into fidelity, and the two summary sentences are wrong rather than the guard in Grievance 12.

**F10. The active-route clause needs a component→occurrence map that is not supplied.** Part XII lists what the module supplies — state spaces, admitted processes, `A_Θ(o)`, controlled actions, resources and accuracy grades, `Inst_ℓ`, `≺`, `Exec` — and no map from a component of `D` to the occurrences realizing it; Part IV says what the module returns is "a full organization … and that is a decomposition". So an anchor (`λ(k) ⊆ D`) cannot be checked against "a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences". Either the module owes an unstated piece, or the clause is not evaluable.

**F11. The refuting case has three terms that cannot be run.** Part XV: "A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in `C_adm` distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work." (a) "no history distinguishes" is only as tight as F8. (b) "the distinction does explanatory work" appears nowhere else in the document; the nearest predicates are Account, anchoring, `Pres`, `CriticalBlock`, and if it means "changes Account status", the case is possible in principle and should be said so. (c) "no change in `C_adm`" is narrower than the semantics' own practice: the non-circularity witness is "an edit `τ(a)` on `E`, translated from some `(a,b) ∈ C`", and `C = (C_adm ∩ S) ∪ K` where "`K` is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit"; and condition 3 ("No active component's relation is, on `C`, definable as `Sol_D(a,b)` or a projection of it") is a definability condition, not a change condition at all. A label like "law" is already eliminated by the document using a change that may sit in `K`. The clause should quantify over `C`, counterfactuals and stated meanings included.

**F12. The patch and the gap.** The revision absorbs the case signature-individuation cannot reach — two components the same under every admitted change but only one on the operative path — by adding a historical kind. But that kind is consulted by no defined predicate, and the verdict that needs `h` is stated directly on `h`, in a different condition, with a different subject (an anchor, not a component). So the two additions are unjoined: the historical kind buys the conjecture its breadth, the active-route clause does the work.

**F13. Attack (F) does not cover condition 4's second sentence.** Part XV offers "Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors … either exclude a genuine mechanism or admit a decoy", leaving the actuality clause — the one condition that can exclude a real mechanism whose anchor is not the live route — untested by name.

## 7. Where the parts came from

- **(K), forward kind, admissibility guard:** **built** — worked out from the organization formalism, committing to grains and contracts never used. It can be caught out (F3).
- **The baseline exclusion:** **asserted** — "excluded on purpose", one line of reason, no case; and its one cost shows up in the sentence that glosses condition 4.
- **Historical kind:** **asserted**, and not even in Part IV's declared sense — no elimination has occurred. The document says as much: "it is a conjecture", "which this document does not claim to have proved".
- **Asymmetry claim (Grievance 12):** **asserted**; true of fidelity, silent about the anchored grade.
- **Active-route clause:** **borrowed** from Part IX's histories and the module's `≺`, with part of the loan unpaid (F10).
- **The conjecture:** **declared** — a modelling convenience on the document's own ledger, unaccompanied by the discriminating case that would select it.

## 8. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Give the historical kind a criterion and a witness.** Say exactly which predicates on a component's history count, and either state the component-level witness (population, variation, elimination, causal responsibility, for components) or define the historical kind as inherited from the anchoring transport's provenance and say so. F5–F8, F12.
2. **Say whether identity-edit pairs at boundaries other than `b_0` are admitted, and then either extend condition 4 to them or drop them from the exclusion — and correct the sentence "fidelity holds … on every pair the module admits".** F2, F3.
3. **Restate the anchored grade as what it is:** fidelity on `C_adm`, proper decomposition, and, where the module supplies a history, actuality of route; and drop Part 0's and Part V's "same fidelity relation on a larger contract". F9, F10.
4. **Restate the refuting case over `C` (counterfactuals included) with "explanatory work" cashed as a named predicate.** F11.

## 9. What this does not show

It does not show the semantics is wrong, nor that any of the four claims is false — the anchored grade may be exactly what it should be, and Ockham is not on the list. It shows which parts are held (the signature, fidelity over `C`, the disclaimer in Derivation 1), which are loose (the exclusion's rationale, the historical kind, the two summary sentences), which is idle in the formalism (the historical kind; the "identity edit separates nothing" clause), which is borrowed (the active-route clause, on an unstated piece of the module), and which is unknown (whether condition 4 is meant to cover baseline pairs of `C_adm`; which histories count). Passing would not have proved anything either.

## 10. One next step

Take a candidate `E` that agrees with its anchor on every pair of the question's contract and differs only at an admitted identity-edit pair `(1,b′)` with `b′ ≠ b_0`, where `b′` is a boundary the contract never names, and ask the revision: anchored or not? **Condition 4 as written says anchored** (kind-coincidence discards `(1,b)` for every `b`, and adequacy compares answers, not relations); **condition 4's own gloss says not anchored** ("on every pair the module admits"). One of the two sentences is wrong, and the answer settles attack question (i). The second step, if wanted, is the same poke for the historical kind: two components of one forward kind on `C_adm` whose carriers differ only in which of two identically-responding routes was actually traversed — name the witness that makes them different historical kinds.

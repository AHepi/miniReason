## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained:** that this document has exactly two primitive packages (the physical module Θ, the normative relation 𝒩) plus the declared parameters listed in Part XIV, and that every other predicate in Parts II–XIII is *defined* from those, in the order Part XIV states, with no cycle.

**Range:** Parts II–XIII's predicates, at the wording they are given, read against Parts 0, XIV, XVI and XII. **What is asked:** a *what produces it?* and *does it achieve its purpose?* pair — which supplier introduces each predicate, and whether the stated order is well-founded. Nothing here is about whether the predicates capture explanation; the document concedes that separately: "whether the defined predicates *capture* explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, not a theorem" (Part XIV). I test the strongest version: Part 0's "It claims that they are all listed", not the weaker economy claim it explicitly disclaims.

## 2. The jobs

- **J1.** Every predicate *used* must be introduced by Θ, by 𝒩, by a listed parameter, or by a definition. Contrast: one used and unlisted.
- **J2.** The order must be well-founded. Contrast: a predicate that reaches back to itself.
- **J3.** Each use of `Inst` must stay inside "a decomposition oracle … listed rather than hidden" (Part XIV).
- **J4.** Each use of `A_Θ` must stay inside Part XII's "the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\) — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits".
- **J5.** Items listed as *derived* must actually be defined. Contrast: one merely named.
- **J6.** No definition may quietly use *represents*, *explains*, *is a cause*, *relevant*, *begs the question*, or a judgement word. Contrast: one that does.

## 3. The claim in parts (owner's words)

(a) the two-package sentence and the declared list (Part XIV); (b) the derived list; (c) the dependence order; (d) Derivation 6's claim and proof; (e) `Inst` and `A_Θ` as module items and every place they are put to work; (f) each predicate in Parts II–XIII as defined or not.

## 4. Change list for this audit

Changes that could bear: substitute a term inside a definitional clause; delete a clause; exhibit a situation the definitions must cover (a selection with no representation anywhere in it; a construction whose target is not represented; a found question; an account assessed off its contract; a module that licenses several decompositions of one lump; a boundary set chosen by the modeller); re-index a claim (C vs C_adm vs A×B). Left out on purpose, and said up front: Part XVI's mathematics, and whether the predicates capture their targets.

## 5. The ten tests

**Remove.** Take `Inst` out of Θ: (R) fails, as the document says — "What (R) takes from outside is \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\)." Take `A_Θ` out: (E)'s adequacy and the anchored grade collapse. Both are *held*. Take "the repertoire" out of Build's nontriviality clause and the clause has no referent — so it is doing work, and doing it with the wrong thing (F8).

**Swap.** *(i)* Swap "the repertoire" in Build for "a declared binding store": the job ("rule out a relabelling") is done just as well. **F8 (loose, and mis-typed).** Build says a binding is nontrivial "when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)", while the only repertoire defined is "the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect" — a set of contents, not of bindings. *(ii)* Swap "a represented input" in active route for "an input occurrence": the clause's stated job — ruling out "a backup that would have responded identically" (Part V, condition 4) — still gets done. **F1 (loose, and the sharper fault below).**

**Flip.** Suppose every predicate had a named supplier. The document would read identically; suppose none do (our case) — still identical. Nothing in Parts II–XIV is arranged to be caught by an unlisted predicate: Derivation 6's proof checks the order against itself. **F31.** The completeness claim is not held in place by any arrangement of the text; only an outside reading of clause and definition can catch it.

**Poke.** *Should matter:* an alteration the module permits that is absent from the organization's declared edit set A. *Should not matter:* the modeller's choice of boundary conditions. Stated as (F22–F23): world-kind requires fidelity "on every pair the module admits", where pairs live in "\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\)" and B is a declared parameter — so the strongest grade moves when a modelling choice moves, and nothing in the text says A_Θ ⊆ A.

**Reverse.** Direction is fine — a claim about definitions is checked by reading definitions. What is missing is the poke: nothing in the document reads each definition for a term with no supplier. Part XII's "since every contract, every grain-admissibility claim and every world-kind claim quantifies over it" is a starting point that already asserts the result (F22).

**Hunt the answer in the starting points.** Yes, partly. `Inst` is a decomposition oracle; the document names that cost. But (R) existentially quantifies — "some organization \(o\) instantiates at \(\ell\) is held to \(c\)" — over a relation that "may instantiate several organizations at one grain". A permissive module therefore makes representation almost free, so the module must supply a *discipline* on licit decompositions, which Part XII's list does not mention. **F24 (loose; this is `Inst` doing more than stated).**

**Add a job.** New job: no definition may need a predicate that needs it back. The document does not survive (F27–F30). This new job is what rules out the "bookkeeping only" verdict on everything above — see §7.

**Build the best rival.** Rival: the same formalism with a longer, honest base list (binding store; causal responsibility; argument forms and essential premises; correctness of accounts; the distribution of explanatory contents; the distinguished represented distinction; the contrasts; visible ports). What tells them apart? An occurrence in my F-list. At the level of "which predicates are defined from what", the two differ only in that list; so ask the finer question — *for each unlisted item, is it eliminable in favour of a listed one?* — and record it as new. The document has no place where that question is answered.

**Check the patches.** Mostly honest: the anchored grade is an openly declared second grade ("That is the honest statement of what (E) alone can do, and why the second grade exists"); Derivation 3 is narrowed to a stated closure hypothesis and says it "is silent" otherwise; Part XV concedes the adequacy condition may be too weak; the analysis-of-kinds conjecture is marked a conjecture. Uneven in one place: Derivation 4 is demoted — "This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation" — while Derivation 9, which is the same kind of unpacking, keeps its proof ("Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs"). **F34.** By the document's own standard the bar is applied unevenly. And Derivation 6's one-cycle argument reads as a patch aimed at the *named* worry (representation ↔ construction) while leaving the unnamed ones untouched (F27–F30).

**Look inside.** The order is an output description — it says what is on the base, not how each definition was read. Derivation 9 says exactly why that cannot settle an account claim: "If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both." Applied to Derivation 6: two texts sharing the order's shape can differ on the claim. **F31 again.**

## 7. `Inst` and `A_Θ` doing more than the module supplies

- **F22 (loose; the strongest of the two).** Condition 4 requires fidelity "on every pair the module admits". Pairs are "\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\)", and B is declared ("boundary \(\beta\)"). So world-kind — the document's gloss for "the model is about the actual mechanism" — is not a module-only claim, and Part XII's "every … world-kind claim quantifies over it [\(A_\Theta\)]" omits the boundary coordinate.
- **F23 (unknown).** The relation between the organization's A and the module's A_Θ is never stated; "\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\)" needs A_Θ ⊆ A. Settled by exhibiting one module-admitted alteration absent from A.
- **F25 (loose).** Admissible grains need "every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit **realizes**" — what it is for an edit to realize a signature is not said.
- **F26 (loose, low).** \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\) is atemporal, but Con needs the target "instantiated … **before** \(e\)"; the ordering comes from ≺, not cited there.

## 8. Cycles (J2) — through (R), Con, Build, Deploy, Attempt

- **F27 (the finding; a strict predicate-level cycle).** (R) requires Sel ∨ Con. Con requires "an episode (Part X) whose **construction witness** prepares \(t\)" — and the construction witness is Build's. Build's nontriviality needs the binding not to be "a relabelling of a binding already in **the repertoire**". The repertoire is "the set of contents **deployable** in some nontrivial use respect", i.e. Deploy. Deploy requires "a representation of \(c\) — **by (R)**". So **Deploy → (R) → Con → Build → Deploy.** The document asserts the opposite edge-set — "Deploy depends on (R) and (CT1). Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E)" — so the cycle is visible between its own order and its own words.
- **F28 (unknown).** Build also needs the subhistory to prepare an organization "for **explanatory use** of \(c\)". If that means Deploy, the same loop closes without the repertoire; the term is undefined, and the cycle question turns on the one word.
- **F29 (loose).** Ownership is "every process occurrence in it is an execution of a **capability** \(s\) possesses in the sense of Part XII (\(\operatorname{Can}\))", and Can "requires an **owned** retained realization or an **owned**, physically admitted, finite **construction** of one". Ownership ↔ Can, and Build depends on ownership. The same word "construction" also spans Part X's content-construction and Part XII's building of a realization.
- **F30 (loose/unknown).** (R) → Sel → "no **itemised representation** of the defect" → (R), if that phrase carries (R)'s sense. Note where the disclaimer sits: Con says "not in the sense of the representation predicate below", and Sel — the clause that actually uses the word — has no such disclaimer.
- **F32 (order clause errors).** "(F1), (F2), (A) depend on (O), (Q), (K)" — (F1) and (F2) cite no signature. "(N), (G) depend on Deploy and Build" — \(\operatorname{Attempt}\), a conjunct of (G), is never placed, and it needs active route, hence (R). (EK)'s other inputs (Result, CreativeCriticalEpisode, Origin, ProducesVia) are unlisted. Ownership and Can are unplaced. Anchoring's (R) edge (F20) is unlisted.
- **F31.** Derivation 6's proof — "the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV" — names one loop, and its premise "(R) is not used inside any provenance witness" is at risk inside Sel (F30). It does not reach the repertoire loop, which is not about targets at all. With F27–F30, J2 fails; J3/J4 fail on F22–F26; J1/J5/J6 fail on F1–F21.

## 10. Where the parts came from

**Asserted:** both packages as packages; "causally responsible"; "blind"; "itemised representation"; the precedence rule for provenance; 𝔈_Θ and 𝔓^adv_Θ; "correct account"; "essential premises"; and the completeness claim itself.

**Fitted:** nothing in the audited claim — there is no history of cases here, and the document says so ("Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment"). That matters for the verdict: the failures above are not failures of fit but of a bookkeeping claim that was never run over its own text.

## 11. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Cut the repertoire out of Build.** Replace it with a declared binding store \(K_{\beta,\ell}\) with its own index (one line), then check that Con no longer reaches Deploy. If it still does through "explanatory use", decide that clause too, and record the decision. This is the one cut that closes F27/F28.
2. **Split the completeness claim in two.** One claim: "every predicate is supplied by Θ, 𝒩, or a listed parameter" — checkable by a table of one row per predicate *occurrence* in Parts II–XIII. Second claim: "the order is acyclic" — checkable by arrows only. The document now fuses them, and only the first can be settled by reading.

## 12. What this does not show

Not that the semantics is false, and not that its predicates fail to capture explanation — the constitutive conjecture is untouched. Not that the definitions are idle: (F1), (F2), (A), adequacy and the finite monotone theorem each rule rivals out, one clause (whose answer is hidden in the starting points, F22–F24) excepted. Not that the faults are equal: most of F1–F21 are cured by adding a line to Part XIV ("the change is in the list, not in the theory"); **F27 and F29 are not** — they cost surgery. And I have not audited Part XVI's or Part VII's mathematics; a counterexample to (I2), (O1), (T2) or (CT2) is a different change list, and Part XV already names it.

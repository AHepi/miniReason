## 1. Corrections first

Your four statements check out against the page with three amendments, and one of them changes your figure. (i) Forward kinds: correct, but the exclusion is written for **all** baseline pairs — "their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)" — not just the actual baseline \((1,b_0)\); the prose's reason names "the baseline" in the singular. (ii) Historical kinds: the page adds a witness requirement and an enumeration you left out — "a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — **with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance**" — and two of those four provenance types appear nowhere else in the document. (iii) Grievance 12: the page states the asymmetry, but the claim it actually denies is narrower than "components by signature": "What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to **fidelity** on a contract." That narrower claim is the one that breaks. (iv) "Now" implies a revision history I cannot check: the document carries no version label, and says so itself — "None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label." I judge this text only.

On your first attack I disagree in part: constants are not lost, as I argue in note 2. On the other three you are right, and the page convicts itself in each case.

## 2. Notes, by sequence

### Sequence A — Part II's kinds clause (the exclusion)

**Note 1. The exclusion is written more widely than its stated reason reaches.** Page: "Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\). The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: **the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states. A change separates two components when their signatures differ at it; the identity edit separates nothing." Job of the exclusion: keep position out of kind. Why the reason does not carry it: (K) says the signature's third coordinate is the relation — "\(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\)" — so a *position* is never in a component's signature to begin with, and excluding \((1,b_0)\) is not needed for that job. What the exclusion actually removes is the component's own *relation* at the identity edit. The reason given fits only a component whose baseline relation is a singleton, i.e. a held value; the sentence that does the real work is the next one, "the identity edit separates nothing". Swap test: replace \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\) with \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\). The stated job — "two things at different positions are one kind in different states" — is still done, for the actual state. So either the all-\(b\) version is free (and should be narrowed to keep real distinctions), or there is a second job the document never names: a component's disposition under a *non-actual* boundary is also a state, not a kind. Name it or narrow it.

**Note 2. Constants survive; held values are lost on purpose; the answer grade catches the ones that matter.** Your worry, tested. A constitutive constant is not confined to the baseline: \(L_j(a,b)\) is the component's relation at every pair in \(C\), so a component imposing \(y=2x\) and one imposing \(y=4x\) differ at every pair where the component survives — any contract separates them. What is lost is a difference that lives *only* at the identity edit, e.g. a held port value represented as \(\{(3)\}\) against \(\{(5)\}\) where some admitted edit overwrites it; on that contract "A changed rule is a changed component" plus the replacement rule leaves only the baseline entry, which is discarded. That loss is answer-checked elsewhere: (A) requires "For every \((a,b)\in C\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)", and C "contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)". So any baseline difference the question reads fails account-hood anyway. The exclusion costs the *kind* classification only, and only where the question is blind. That is defensible, and the document should say this, because the reason it gives is not the reason that works.

**Note 3. Condition 4's gloss overstates its reach.** Page: "fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits." With the baseline pairs discarded, the reach is \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\), so this sentence is false as written. The document already has the correct phrasing four paragraphs later — "a kind-claim about the world is a claim about every change the module admits" — so the fix is to use its own words: every admitted *change*.

**Note 4. Who benefits.** The exclusion loosens the anchored grade in favour of the candidate under assessment: baseline-only differences no longer bear on "is of one forward kind ... with its anchor". That runs against condition 4's stated purpose, "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy", and it is worth a sentence in Part V saying so.

### Sequence B — the historical kind (Part II, Part IV, Part XII, Part XIV)

**Note 5. The historical kind carries no index, while the document promises every kind-claim does.** Grievance 2: "it is indexed to the physical module's decomposition, the grain, and the contract, and every kind-claim carries that index." The historical kind is indexed to nothing stated — not to a grain, not to \(h\), not to the occurrence. Which history? The occurrence's, the population's, \(h\)'s? Relative to what contract? This breaks the standard the document sets for its own kind-claims.

**Note 6. Your second attack is right: no provenance witness exists for components.** Page: "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance." Part IV requires witnesses only for transports, and only two of them: "A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has a provenance determined by its history in the physical module, with the following precedence: it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*." Both witnesses are transport-shaped: \(\operatorname{Sel}\) needs "a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)", a variation operator "on \(\mathcal T\)", a survival condition that "**eliminated at least one member**" of \(\mathcal T\); \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) needs an episode "whose construction witness prepares \(t\)". So for a component there is no witness, no precedence rule, and no definition of "an occurrence of a component" — Part IV defines an occurrence as "a physically located carrier". Worse, two of the four provenance types the sentence lists, "descended from, copied from", have no witness *anywhere* in the document. Job of the definition: give Grievance 12 something to point at, and give attack (C) its second disjunct. It does neither. Better version: state for a component the same five items Part IV requires of a transport — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — plus the construction alternative, plus a precedence rule, plus a sentence fixing what "occurrence" means at component level.

**Note 7. No supplier either.** Part XII lists what the module must return — "substrate state spaces and attributes; the admitted processes and, for each occurrence, the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\); the interpretation of some processes as controlled actions ...; resources and accuracy grades; the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) ...; the process-dependency order \(\prec\) ...; and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\)" — and component provenance is not on the list. So the historical kind has neither an internal witness nor an external supplier. Unlike your attack on the baseline pairs, this one has no counter-argument on the page.

**Note 8. Two devices for one fact, and neither knows the other.** The same fact — a component realized in matter came to be there a particular way — is handled twice. Part IX: "A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature, and it is what the anchored grade's fourth condition uses." That is a component-history fact, and it is placed inside a *fidelity* condition — but it is never called a historical kind, and the historical kind is never used by any condition. Count the citations: the historical kind is named in Part 0 ("as a second kind of kind"), Part II, Grievance 12, Part XIV ("historical kinds, from provenance witnesses"), Derivation 2 ("The remedies are a finer contract — a new question — or a history, which the semantics represents as a historical kind") and Part XV, and used by nothing. Two routes to one job is allowed when each has a second job; here the kind-route has no job at all, and it is the route attack (C) was given.

**Note 9. The scenario concedes the loss, which is the one place the cost is paid on the page.** Derivation 10: "A claim that 'component 1 is *really* thing 1' is either a claim about a finer contract or a claim about history; on this contract, neither is available, and the correct report is that identity, at this grain and contract, is exhausted by trajectory." This is honest and specific, and it is the document charging itself for the cost of its own apparatus — protection-worthy. But "on this contract" mislocates the reason: history is not contract-relative in this document, so a contract cannot make history unavailable. The reason must be that no component-level witness has been supplied, which is note 6.

**Note 10. The conditional is a lucky exit.** Condition 4 reads "**Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\)**, each anchor is an active route ... and not a backup that would have responded identically." Luck that helps the hero needs a reason. Whether the anchored grade tests history is made contingent on the module, and the module is *required* elsewhere to supply \(\prec\) and \(\operatorname{Exec}\) — so the reader cannot tell whether the history is absent because the module has none or because its supply was not asked for.

### Sequence C — the anchored grade and Grievance 12 (your third attack)

**Note 11. The condition is two conditions in one numbered slot.** Part V condition 4 pairs a signature clause and a history clause: "Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor ... Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX)". The paragraph that introduces the four conditions then calls the whole grade fidelity: "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution." And it names the grade's purpose as making the distinction your third attack is about: "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose." So the grade that separates mechanism from decoy uses a difference that is, in Part IX's own words, "not in signature". Better version, and it costs one paragraph: split condition 4 into **world-kind** (signature, on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)) and **actuality of route** (history, \(h\)), number them separately, and describe the anchored grade as signature plus actuality rather than as fidelity.

**Note 12. Your third attack lands: yes, history is smuggled into fidelity, at exactly one place — and the asymmetry survives only where it was never in danger.** Grievance 12: "Provenance matters to *representation* because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change." That denial is true of (F1), (F2), (A) and so of (E) — and it has a real job there worth protecting, stated by the document: "(E) carries **no provenance condition**: any modelled candidate, including one whose transport is declared, can be assessed for account-hood, because a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history." Held at (E); broken at the anchored grade, by the second half of condition 4. So the honest statement is: history is irrelevant to the two fidelity equations and to account-hood at a contract, and relevant to the anchored grade's fourth condition. Grievance 12's last sentence should be scoped to (E), or the actuality clause should move out of the numbered anchoring conditions into its own named condition, with Grievance 12 amended to match.

**Note 13. Attack (F) under-covers the thing it is meant to test.** "**The anchored grade.** Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors (Part V) either exclude a genuine mechanism or admit a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change." That names conditions 1 and 2 and part of 4 — and never the actuality clause, which is the only place history enters, and which is not describable as "shares a world-kind ... on every physically admitted change". A referee working from this list will not test the asymmetry at all. Add the clause, with the question it invites: is a backup that would have responded identically a different mechanism or the same one?

**Note 14. The fair mirror test does not come out equal.** Lay the two histories side by side as the document treats them. Transport: "it came to hold by selection, construction, or declaration, and its provenance is a fact about events", load-bearing in (R), in Deploy, in (EK). Component: "A component realized in matter also has a history" — load-bearing only in the unnumbered half of condition 4, and disowned in the same breath. Both had a history; the document charges one and credits the other, while using both. That is the unequal mirror your third attack names, and it is not a matter of wording alone, since the anchored grade's power is stated to be the thing the disowned history supplies.

### Sequence D — the conjecture and Part XV's refutation conditions (your fourth attack)

**Note 15. The conjecture cannot be refuted as stated, and not for the reason a reader might expect.** Page, Part II: "The analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV) is that every discriminating use of a kind-label is one or the other." Part XV: "a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work." Derivation 1 already refuses to shield it — "It does not show that a kind-label can do no work, because the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component, because the contract may not be the admitted one, and because histories are not signatures" — so the document is not the obstacle. Three defects are. (a) "or a historical kind" has no criterion (notes 6 and 7), so the second disjunct is a name. (b) Read charitably as "any predicate on the producing history", it satisfies the conjecture for any two components whose producing histories differ, since some predicate separates any two distinct histories; the disjunction then does no work and the conjecture loses content. The conjecture's whole load sits on a class of admissible historical-kind predicates that the document never fixes. (c) The first disjunct says "a forward kind on **some** admissible grain and admitted contract". "Some" lets a defender shop for the index, and nothing ties the index to the contracts of the two accounts under test. Compare the precision of the neighbouring attack (D): "A selection history \(H\subsetneq C\), in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off \(H\), whose survivor is determined on \(C\setminus H\)." Better version, in that style: fix a question \(p\) with its contract; two candidates satisfying (E) and the anchored grade; a stated class of admissible historical-kind predicates; and require the label's discriminating use to change an answer profile or an account verdict at every admissible grain and every recorded contract, with no predicate in the class distinguishing them. Then (C) becomes a thing to be shown rather than a posture.

**Note 16. The two statements of the same attack differ, and one of them is stronger.** Part 0: "Produce a case in which a **declared** kind-label does discriminating work that no admitted change under **the physically admitted contract** and no history can do." Part XV's version quantifies over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which for a mathematical or modelled organization is a "declared admission relation" (Part III). So Part XV's attack is available against a mathematical counterexample and Part 0's is not, and "declared kind-label" against "kind-label" is a second unstated difference. Pick one and use it in both places.

**Note 17. The chain of questions breaks where the second kind is handed on.** Part II closes on the conjecture; Part XV's (C) opens on it; but the middle link — what a history would have to do — is never supplied, so the reader who was holding "is history a kind of kind?" is asked to drop it. Alongside: planted and never collected, "descended from, copied from", "the witness that Part IV requires for provenance" at component level, and "a backup that would have responded identically" (the document does not say whether such a backup is a counterfactual occurrence, an alternative realization, or a hypothetical; \(\operatorname{Inst}\) is a relation, so several organizations sit on one occurrence, and it is not obvious what it means for a "backup" to be off the route). The pattern count, as Pass 4 asks for it:

**Testing my own notes.** *Origin.* Notes 1–3, 5, 11–13, 15–17 are built from the document's own sentences and jobs; note 2 is fitted from the ordinary treatment of state in dynamical systems and needed the page's (A) and baseline rule to hold; note 14 is borrowed from the mirror test as restated in this method. *Swap the story.* Would these notes fit any formal document? "Define your terms", "state your index" would; notes 1, 3, 6, 11, 15 are tied to named sentences and named jobs and would not. *Flip the scene.* Had the document excluded no baseline pair, I would have faulted it for making state a kind — so note 1 must be read as it is written: a scope-and-justification note, not an advice to include the pairs. Had Grievance 12 made the wider claim that history is irrelevant everywhere, the same paragraph would have contradicted itself; the note is confined to the narrower claim the page actually makes.

## 3. How it fits together

The document's central question is where a difference may live, and its answer is: in a change the module admits, or in a history. The formal machinery delivers the first half and, at one point, the second — condition 4's actuality clause — which is a plot event in this document's terms and also its theme's hardest case, since it is the place where a mechanism is told apart from a decoy that responds identically. The named device for the second half, the historical kind, is never used by any condition, so the theme is carried by the undeclared route rather than the declared one.

## 4. The faults that matter most

**Fault A (fix this with Fault B).** The historical kind has no witness for components and no supplier in the physical module, so the second kind of kind is a name; the fault propagates into the conjecture's second disjunct and into attack (C), which cannot be applied as written. Notes 6, 7, 15.

**Fault B.** The anchored grade contains a history condition inside what the document calls fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and Part IX says in terms that the difference is "not in signature", so the one place component history does real work sits on the wrong side of the line drawn in Grievance 12 and repeated in Part V's "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract". Notes 8, 11, 12.

**Fault C.** The baseline exclusion is right in what it is for and wrong in what it says and how far it goes: the reason given fits a held value, not a relation; the exclusion is written for every boundary; and condition 4's "every pair the module admits" overstates its reach. Notes 1, 3. Your version of this one is half-right: constants are not lost, held values are lost by design and caught by (A).

What to protect: Derivation 1's disclaimer, which declines to shield the conjecture with its own theorem, and Derivation 10's admission that in the worked case identity is exhausted by trajectory. Both are the document charging itself for its own costs, which is rare and is what makes the repairs above cheap.

## 5. What I am unsure of

Whether "the witness that Part IV requires for provenance" is meant to import \(\operatorname{Sel}\) and \(\operatorname{Con}\) to components by analogy, or whether a component-level witness exists in a file I was not shown. Whether "descended from, copied from" are deliberate shorthand for a lineage vocabulary that will get its own witnesses, or are labels. Whether the all-\(b\) exclusion is deliberate (the notation) or incidental (the prose says "the baseline"). Whether the actuality clause is meant as a fifth kind of kind or as a condition of the anchored grade — the page reads both ways, and the repair differs, so I would ask rather than fix. And most of the document asserts nothing that has been run: "Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment", so I judged definitions against definitions, not a theory against results.

## 6. One next step

Write the component-provenance witness that Grievance 12, Part II and Part XIV all point at: for a component, the same five items Part IV requires of a transport, plus the construction alternative and a precedence rule, with "an occurrence of a component" defined. That single addition makes attack (C) decidable, gives the second disjunct of the conjecture content, and forces the actuality clause in condition 4 to declare whether it belongs to that witness or to the anchored grade — which is Fault B's repair arriving in the same edit.

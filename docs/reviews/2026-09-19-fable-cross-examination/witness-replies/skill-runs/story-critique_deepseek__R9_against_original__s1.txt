## Corrections first

Two corrections, both about the brief, and the second one changes how much this audit can settle.

**C1. 20a contains no diagnosis.** The brief says to "take that as the writer's own diagnosis," but 20a is not a diagnosis. Its own first line says what it is: "the mapping from the consolidated revision list (file 15 §G, as amended by §F and §E3) to the sections of file 20 that carry it." It reports presence and location, never a fault. The only self-critical rows in it are already marked "Partly", "Not done", and the section "What was not done," which reads: "Any check of file 20 by the witnesses. It has not been cross-examined." So the writer's diagnosis here is not a list of defects to check. It is a set of *Done* claims to falsify.

**C2. I do not have file 15.** The row "Completeness of the enumeration" cannot be tested: the ledger is a map from a list I cannot read, and its own parenthetical "as amended by §F and §E3" refers to amendments that are not in either document I hold. Every finding below is therefore either (i) a *Done* row whose repair is not on the page of 20, (ii) a *Done* row whose repair is on the page but does not do what the row says, or (iii) a defect that exists in 10 and still exists in 20. I cannot say whether a defect once listed in 15 was dropped from 20a; I can only say whether what 20a claims 20 does, 20 does.

**One row contradicts itself, and it is a calibration failure.** In G1 the item list row says all of ProducedBy, ProducesVia, Result, Lic, Scope, Live, Enable, ≡_ℓ, Exec, restriction, observation edit, independent boundary, "nontrivial binding construction," content-preserving recoding, owned, relevant, non-question-begging and ≺_h are "Done" — offered together with a stated action: "Define or count as primitive". That the list is presented at "Done" while its own companion row is "Partly" is the ledger's only internal contradiction, and it is the same fault as row G5's (below): a claim of completeness carried by a colophon.

**A phrase with no row.** The list above does not contain "repertoire". The predicate is load-bearing where it is used:

> "The repertoire \(R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect."

and it enters (EK)'s last conjuncts, which require "\(c\in\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\)" with `Deploy(s, c, ξ′; U_c)`. Result is claimed defined; repertoire is not in the list of predicates to define or count, and no definition of it appears. Also absent is "Enabled": U1/U2 spell out `Enable` as a predicate, and Part XIII supplies the *condition* χ, not the predicate:

"Under which \(s\) has Can" is not itself a rule for deciding the formula. The ledger's G1 row was the promise that nothing of this kind would be left "under which"; as written it is not kept.

## 3. Defects of 10 that survive in 20 under new wording

**Survivor 1 (the largest). The sufficiency doctrine is still carried by a grade whose separation job is not shown to be done.**

10:
> "**Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies all four conditions of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract… and that plainly explains nothing. This would show change-fidelity is not enough."

20:
> "**(A) Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing."

and twenty pages later, the same paragraph:

> "A candidate meeting (E) alone and explaining nothing is already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) — and is why the second grade is stated; it does not refute the class, because the class's sufficiency claim is for the anchored grade."

The one-component candidate is also written into Part V twice, once positively — "It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored. That is the honest statement of what (E) alone can do" — and once negatively — "A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change… is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account."

Now lay the page against the ledger's own list of what the grade adds, the set I quoted in §2 under G4. Every one of the four conditions is a condition on the *anchor map*: on anchors, on their relation to separately-variable components of \(D\), on the anchor's kind. Nothing in the list is a condition on \(C\). So the sufficiency attack has not been answered; it has been renamed. To show the grade separates mechanism from decoy, 20 would have to show a decoy that satisfies (E) on \(C\) must fail one of the four. It instead says there is one, in the honest sentence about the table:

> "It is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account."

That sentence concedes the case I mean. A faithful decomposition that is one kind on \(C\) but the wrong kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is excluded by condition 4 only if some admitted change distinguishes it — which is exactly what an adversarial decoy will not offer. 20 has no argument that it must. The ledger does not flag this row; G4's row is "Done".

Let me put the test to it directly, as Pass 1 asks. **Swap** the two grades: had 10's answer to sufficiency been to *strengthen (E)* rather than *add a grade*, would a fault have appeared in that too? Yes — (E)'s whole purpose is to be a condition assessable inside a question, and the ledger's own defense of the added conjunct is that it makes (E) more attackable. So the note "sufficiency is unanswered" is not coming from me; it is held by the page in both directions.

**Survivor 2. The objection from the experiments, and the module's burden.**

10:
> "It does not derive reference from uninterpreted matter. It derives reference from matter **plus a selection or construction history**."

20:
> "It derives reference from matter **plus a selection or construction history**, given a physical module that says which organizations a lump of matter can instantiate at a grain. That module is a primitive here… because it must say how a lump decomposes."

The wording changes; the exposure does not. The question 10 was answering — whether representation needs more than matter plus history — is answered in 20 by saying only that the module supplies the decomposition relation and that this is "the module's burden":

> "how a lump of matter decomposes is the module's burden, and Part XII says what the module is."

Part XII then requires "a full organization… and every component relation… and that is a decomposition, not a reading of a meter." This is the same primitive doing the whole job it did in 10; naming it a *package* whose relation is "a decomposition oracle":

> "the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle: that is what 'physics says what a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden",

is the same claim, better dressed. Part XIV's "It is a package… listed rather than hidden" is a promise; the surviving defect is that the price was never paid. **Pass 1's remove test:** remove the decomposition oracle from the module and every antecedent below Part IV stops getting done — (R)'s left side, admissible grains, condition 4, the selection witness, derivation 6. So it is held. It is held *as a primitive*, and 10's objection 2 is not answered by relabelling it "the module's burden"; 10's own words were that the semantics "does not pretend to derive reference from uninterpreted matter" — 20 still doesn't derive it, and now says so in the same sentence it claims the cost is "listed".

**Survivor 3. Grievance 1's cause/correlation distinction is still not the formal signature.**

10:
> "Intervening on the input port of a causal assignment replaces the component that assigns that port and leaves the causal component's own relation in force"

20:
> "Intervening on the input port of a causal assignment replaces the component that assigns that port and leaves the causal component's own relation in force; the output moves through it. Intervening on the same port breaks a correlational component…"

But Part II's formal definition is unchanged from 10 except for the word "forward":

> "a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits"

and it is followed by the disclaimer "These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data." This is the misalignment 20a's G4 row claims to have fixed: "Grievance 1 aligned with the formal causal signature". It is not aligned. The formal signature condition for a causal assignment says nothing about what an intervention on the *input* port does to the *candidate's own relation*; it says a causal assignment is *invariant under observation edits*. The grievance's argument needs the stronger claim about causal assignments (the input intervention leaves the relation in force) and the argument that a correlation lacks it. (K) supplies neither: "invariant under observation edits" does not by itself date a component's history, and "changes under intervention on its output port" is a condition on a different port. The row is Done; the page says otherwise.

**Survivor 4. The ledger's own word, honestly reported, and worse than reported.**

20a:
> "Derivation 10 was downgraded from a consistency witness to a scenario, and the document no longer claims any relative-consistency witness for the class. That is a loss the list did not price."

10's Derivation 10 ended: "This episode is a relative-consistency witness for the class." 20's Derivation 10 now says: "This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it."

The loss is larger than the words "consistency witness" imply. Derivation 10 was the only place in either document where the machinery met a case: it is where 10's (F1),(F2),(A) were applied, where "no one declared them to be 'objects'" was tested, and where (G), (P), (EK) were said to hold jointly. 20a's own not-done list admits "any witness for concept creation" but does not say that *no* case in either file has been run at all. That the document now says so plainly is to its credit; that no row of the ledger prices it as a defect of the *system* rather than of the *file* is the surviving defect. Registered as a defect of the same kind as the skill would call "a promise of its type": the class's whole claim is that these definitions carve creativity, and not one instance of any definition has been checked. **What holds it in place:** every derived predicate in Part XIV now bottom-depends on a checked example only through \(\operatorname{Inst}\), \(\Theta\), and \(\equiv_\ell\), none of which has a worked case.

**Survivor 5. Arithmetic in (T2) is inherited unexamined.** 10 and 20 both state "\(e_n\le\varepsilon\sum_{k<n}L^k\)" (T2). The induction in the same document's "Functional transport" uses indices \(0..n\) with a base term, and the sum here has the base case \(k=0\) present as \(L^0=1\), so the sum is well-formed for \(n\ge 1\) and the statement is not false; but nothing in 20 labels the base case or the convention that \(n\) is the step index rather than the position index, and Part XV keeps (T2) on its falsification list unchanged: "A counterexample to the finite monotone theorem, (I2), (O1), (T2), (CT2)". Same as 10, so the surviving fault is only that the revision round touched (I4)→(I3) and the balance example but not this. Free of the big four; included because the ledger's G5 row claims the "identification example corrected" among the same row family and gives the impression the arithmetic was swept.

## 4. New defects introduced by 20

**New 1. Contract adequacy is a conjunct of (E) that does not answer to the changes (E) is defined over.**

20's own sentence that fixes what (E) is:

> "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes. None inspects a label."

and the conjunct:

> "**Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse."

\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) is by construction *not* in \(C\). So this conjunct is a condition on behaviour at changes outside the changes (E) is defined over. 10 had no such conjunct: its objectivity condition was "caught by any criticism that supplies the excluded change," which is a claim about future criticism, not a condition inside (E). The 20a row names this as "a stronger move than the list asked for"; it does not name the stronger consequence, which is that (E)'s parenthetical is now false of one of its six conjuncts, and that 10's answer to objection 4 is replaced by a condition (E) cannot evaluate inside its own contract.

**New 2. Fidelity now ranges over counterfactual edits the module does not admit.**

20's contract is now a union:

> "\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\). \(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit… Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\)."

and (F1) is stated over all of \(C\):

> "For every active component \(k\) of \(E\)… and every \((a,b)\in C\)…"

and τ is defined on the source. So (F1) demands of a *declared* relation that it satisfy an equation with a term on the right produced by an edit the physical module does not admit, using a τ that need only be given "on the stated scope" ("\(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) on the stated scope, τ translates edits"). For a physically attributed organization, 10's version quantifier was over admitted changes only; 10 had no \(K\) and no non-admitted pairs at all. 20's own caption says physical realization is "at every attribution":

> "Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physical module."

So the revision introduces a class of pairs on which fidelity is required and physical realizability is withheld by construction, and says so only as a scope note: "a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning." It enters (F1) and (F2) without any such note. This is a new defect, and it is not in 20a's row for G2, because the row lists only the union form and the objectivity split.

**New 3. (E)'s count is internally inconsistent, and the row that says it was fixed does not fix it.** Quoted in §2. 10 said "four conditions" over four formula conjuncts. 20 says "five" over six conjuncts, in the same paragraph in which it also says "each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\)" — which the sixth is not (New 1). One correction, two counts, no drawing.

**New 4. The constitutive conjecture's four items no longer name what carries sufficiency.**

20's Part 0:

> "The **constitutive conjecture** is this: explanatory creativity is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports between organizations checked by change-fidelity, (iii) the provenance of those transports and of the questions they serve, and (iv) their physical realization. A genuine explanatory achievement that these four cannot represent counts against the conjecture."

20 has since added the anchored grade as the grade at which sufficiency is claimed ("the class's sufficiency claim is for the anchored grade", Part XV), and has added the adequacy condition to the first grade. Neither is named among (i)–(iv). (i) could be read to cover properness and resolution, (ii) to cover "world-kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)", and the ledger lists all four conditions as Done under the anchoring row — but the conjecture is the one paragraph a reader takes away when they do not read Part V, and it enumerates four things, none of which is the device the document now says carries the claim. This is a 20-introduced gap, because 10's four items *did* coincide with its four formula conjuncts. Part 0's remedy sentence elsewhere is precise about exactly one of the additions and not the other:

> "A candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too."

**New 5. Vocabulary introduced late carries load.** (E)'s own second-grade conditions use properness and resolution in the same paragraph in which they are named and no lemma connects them to (F1). The ledger's G4 row claims "λ a proper decomposition (ROM and global-anchor cases)" is Done in "Part V anchored grade, conditions 1 and 3." A pairing is *defined*, and (F1) is stated over it separately; the ledger's word "proper decomposition" is also used, in its own line, for what 20 calls properness — "λ a proper decomposition (ROM and global-anchor cases)" — and specifically for the anti-repackaging job:

> "A component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name."

"Definable as" and "is" are two different conditions, and only the second is excluded by name. A component whose relation is a *projection* of Sol_D, recoded through a bijection, has a relation that is definable as a projection but is not stated to be excluded. This is what lets the repackaging survive in recoded clothing. **Held by:** the row's job is "no global relation", and the page's sentence holds only the narrower job. This note is held, not asserted: swap the recoding for the identical single component and the exclusion works, so the note is held by the exclusion's *own* wording.

**New 6 (highest severity). A "decomposition" is not the same relation as a "projection," and only the projection is (F1)-tested.** (F1)'s left-hand side takes the full solution set of the anchor subnetwork and projects it to ≤ |V_k| ports. F2 says "the assembled organization agrees" and requires the *projection of D's solution set onto E's port set* to equal E's. If the anchors are only proper subnetworks — not all of D's components — the composition of the anchor projections need not reconstruct "the assembled organization"; the shared constraints imposed by components of D that lie in no anchor are lost. Compare with Derivation 2 of 20, which is explicit about exactly this:

> "Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on \(C\) with *different* decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account, and no result here says they are."

If two different decompositions can both satisfy (F1), condition 1's last clause is the only thing on the page standing between a sound account and a repackaged one, and that clause is a clause about the anchor map, not about the solution set. Condition 3 excludes the identical single component. It does not exclude a two-component *factorisation* whose anchors together leave a shared constraint of \(D\) outside both, provided each factor's relation matches its own anchor's projection. 20 has no worked case that would show such a factorisation failing — the one case it does have, Derivation 10's, is now "a scenario… no condition is checked on it." So this is partly a consequence of Survivor 4, but the anchor/projection mismatch is *new*: 10 never claimed (F1) delivered the assembled organization, because 10 never had (F1)'s left side defined over subnetworks with visible ports. Part V of 20 introduces that definition and then runs (F1) on one side of it.

This is the fault I rank first, and I hold it for the reason 20 itself gives me: the ledger lists the anchored grade's job as "ROM and global-anchor cases," Part V says the one-component repackaging "satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3," and (E) alone is the grade "the class's sufficiency claim is for the anchored grade." If the anchor's solution set need not reconstruct D's, then a decoy can satisfy condition 1 by choosing anchors that each match their own subregion and condition 4 by having every active component of one forward kind with its anchor — because anchors and components move together. (A) and (F2) are the only counters on the page, and (F2)'s exact port set is not stated. If the union of anchors covers every component of D, F2 is enforceable; condition 1's "no component of \(D\) that some pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches lies outside every anchor" speaks of components *touched*, not of coverage. The union is not stated, and no lemma states it.

## 5. Ranked list (the faults that matter most, in order of what falls if they are not fixed)

1. **The decoy that is not yet excluded.** The anchored grade is stated and asserted to exclude; the page nowhere shows that it does, and its one worked counter-candidate ("the one-component repackaging") is now said to be "an account at \(C\) and not anchored." If an anchored decoy exists, the constitutive conjecture fails at its sufficiency limb and the whole two-grade structure was an unmoved label. Where to look first: the anchor-coverage clause of Part V condition 1, and whether (F2)'s agreeing projection is stated over \(\bigcup\) of anchors. Held by: "the class's sufficiency claim is for the anchored grade."

2. **Contract adequacy is the one conjunct of (E) that does not satisfy "(E)'s own account of itself."** Either the sentence "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes" is false, or adequacy is not a conjunct of (E) and 10's objection 4 is unanswered. Held by: "there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)" set against "In this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism."

3. **Sufficiency, in its old form.** Survives from 10; 20 answers it by relocation. Strengthened by New 6.

4. **(F1) now ranges over non-admitted pairs.** New. Held by: "Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\)" against "Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physical module." A theory that requires fidelity on changes that by its own account cannot physically occur has bought a scope note with a contradiction.

5. **The case has been withdrawn and not replaced.** Derivation 10 as a scenario; no instance of (EK), (G) or (P) checked anywhere. Held by: "no condition is checked on it."

6. **The constitutive conjecture no longer lists what carries sufficiency.** Held by the four-item enumeration itself; cheap to fix, expensive to leave, because it is the paragraph a reader quotes.

7. **Grievance 1 not aligned with (K).** Held by the incompatibility of "invariant under observation edits" with the causal-versus-correlational argument as stated.

8. **(E)'s count.** Cosmetic next to 1–7; listed because 20a claims it done.

9. **Repertoire, Enabled, occurrences-of-Δ left unpriced.** Held by the ledger's own "Define or count as primitive."

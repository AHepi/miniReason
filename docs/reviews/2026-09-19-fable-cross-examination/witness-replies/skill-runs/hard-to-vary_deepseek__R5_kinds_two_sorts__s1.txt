## 1. The question, frozen

- **Q1 (baseline).** Does excluding the baseline pairs from the separating changes of (K) lose real distinctions — constants, initial conditions, things differing only in state?
- **Q2 (history).** Is a historical kind well-defined when the provenance witnesses are not available for components?
- **Q3 (asymmetry).** Does the transport/component asymmetry of Grievance 12 hold up, or does the actuality-of-route condition in the anchored grade smuggle history into fidelity after all?
- **Q4 (conjecture).** Is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture in Part XV now stated so it could be refuted?

Range: the revision as it stands, Part II + Grievance 12 + Part V condition 4 + Part XV (C). Asked as four questions of kind *what counts as what under this rule*, with one *what produces it* clause inside Q3. **Finding 2.** The question is frozen; several tests below are pokes at the world of the semantics (add an edit to the contract, change the module, supply or withhold a witness), not objections to the prose.

## 2. The jobs this part has to do — put as contrasts

- **J1.** Sort components by their response to changes, and *not* by a label. (Grievance 11, Grievance 1.)
- **J2.** Sort components (or their occurrences) by history where forward signatures coincide, and *not* collapse history into signature.
- **J3.** Keep the semantics from being "a thing is what you can do to it" — i.e. show every kind-claim carries its index — and *not* pretend "one kind here" is a fact about the world.
- **J4.** Let a cause be told from a rule and from a correlation by a separating change where one exists.
- **J5.** Represent the difference between two things that no admitted change separates but whose origins differ.
- **J6.** Keep the two grades of adequacy coherent: account at a contract vs anchored; anchored must do the work "is about the actual mechanism" without importing a new primitive.
- **J7.** State the analysis-of-kinds conjecture as a conjecture, with a refuting case named.
- **J8.** Do it without forbidding state from mattering — i.e. initial conditions and positions must not split kinds.

## 3. The explanation in parts, in the document's own words

- **P1. Signature.** "The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}.\) (K)"
- **P2. Forward kind, with the exclusion.** "Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)."
- **P3. Why the exclusion.** "The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states."
- **P4. Identity edit.** "A change separates two components when their signatures differ at it; the identity edit separates nothing."
- **P5. Index.** "A forward kind is an equivalence class under this relation, and it is relative to the grain (which fixes \(J\)) and the contract (which fixes the changes). A coarser contract identifies more components; a coarser grain has fewer to identify."
- **P6. Historical kind.** "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance."
- **P7. The conjecture, stated.** "The analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV) is that every discriminating use of a kind-label is one or the other."
- **P8. The asymmetry, defended.** "A transport is an occurrence in a physical history ... A component is a slot in an organization: what it does under changes is its forward kind ... What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract."
- **P9. Fidelity vs representation on the same distinction.** "Provenance matters to *representation* because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change."
- **P10. The anchored grade, world-kind.** "**World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor."
- **P11. The same condition, second half.** "Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically."
- **P12. The refuting case, Part XV (C).** "A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work."

## 4. The change list for the claim under attack

What could be changed about the semantics-situation, and the claim says what happens:

- **W1.** Add to the contract an edit that touches only components whose relations differ at the baseline pair. (Changes whether the excluded pair was doing work.)
- **W2.** Move to a different grain. (P5 says kind identity may shift; Part II's admissibility rule constrains it: "a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed.")

## 5. The ten tests

### Remove
Remove P2's exclusion clause, so forward kind compares full signatures including baseline. Answer under the revision's own machinery: initial conditions and positions split kinds — the case P3 was fitted on — and every "thing" in different states becomes a different kind, which breaks the grain-admissibility rule and J8. **Finding 3.** The exclusion is not idle; it carries J8. Remove P6 instead, exactly as written: nothing in the fidelity conditions reads the historical kind (P8, P9), so **Finding 4.** the historical kind is idle **for fidelity** at every contract — the document says so itself — and its only billed job is J5, representation ("Provenance matters to *representation*").
Remove in a group: P6 together with the (C) attack in Part XV. What is lost? The conjecture loses its second disjunct, and with it the promised way of representing differences of origin. That costs J5 and J7 together, so the pair is a **critical block in the document's own sense** (Part VI, "(B)"). Remove P4. "The identity edit separates nothing" is doing a small job: without it, comparing two components whose footprints are related only by the identity edit at the baseline would either trivially coincide or trivially fail depending on the bijection. Keeping it is harmless; call it a stated convention.

### Swap
Swap "the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**" for a near neighbour: "the relation a component imposes at the baseline is part of its **kind** when no admitted edit moves it." Does the argument still run? The revision's defence of the exclusion uses "state" and "different positions" — exactly the cases where the exclusion is wanted. The swapped version explains the wanted cases by condition (no admitted edit moves it) rather than by naming the baseline relation "state". **Finding 5.** The swap is *better* on the document's own terms, which is the mark of a loose part: "state" here is standing in for the condition that would have to be checked, so what holds the exclusion in place is not yet named. Swap "descended from, copied from" for two other relations — "originated in", "transmitted by". The predicate is unchanged in content because no content was given. **Finding 6.** Descended-from and copied-from are placeholders; nothing in the document fixes what facts about a history would make one true.

### Flip the outcome
Suppose the revision had *included* the baseline pairs. What would it have said? That initial conditions and positions split kinds — and it would then have had to do all of J8's work elsewhere (putting state into the organization's initial data rather than the component relation, say). **Finding 7.** The revision could have told the same overall story both ways; the choice of exclusion is a design choice of the fitted kind, defensible but not forced by the other parts. That does not make it false — state genuinely matters — but the document has not produced the change that forces *this* line rather than the other.

### Poke
For the exclusion (P2/P3): a change that *should* matter — add an admitted edit to the contract that varies a component's constant (a coefficient of proportionality that no other edit moves). Under the revision's rule as written, two components differing only in that constant are "of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)" for every \(C\) that has no such edit, and the added edit, once in \(C\), changes the verdict. So the pair is pokeable, and the hole is stated: **Finding 8.** the exclusion loses a real distinction exactly when the contract affords no other edit that reaches the differing relation — the case of a baseline-only constant. A change that should *not* matter: the numbering of the components, the names in \(J\) — that is a genuine "would not" half and the document is right to be insensitive to it.
For the historical kind (P6): ask for the change that should alter a component's historical kind. **Finding 9.** None is supplied. "Selected, constructed, descended from, copied from" names four relations and fixes none of their events; the witness is deferred to Part IV, which defines provenance "**of a transport**" ("A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has a provenance determined by its history"), not of a component. A part with no such pair is a label in this skill's sense.

### Reverse
Does part of the revision run backwards from the result it wants? Test the asymmetry defence (P8). It argues from the sort of thing a component is — "a slot in an organization" — to the conclusion that history does not matter to fidelity. That argument would equally show that a historical kind *cannot exist* (a slot has no history), yet P6 grants one. The reply has to be that the historical kind attaches to "an occurrence of a component," i.e. to the realization, not the slot. **Finding 10.** The revision moves between the component-as-slot and the component-as-realized-occurrence without an edit that marks the crossing; that is running a small argument backwards from the wanted conclusion, since the wanted conclusion (no history in fidelity) is available only for the slot reading.
Now poke the anchored grade directly. Fix a physical target and an anchor with a perfect signature. Present two backups whose relations differ from the operative component's *off* the contract but not on it, or present the route that actually occurred versus a backup that "would have responded identically" (P11). Holding signature fixed, W5 changes the anchored verdict and does not change forward kind. **Finding 11.** In physical cases, anchoring is sensitive to facts of the sort a historical kind records, so the claim that history "does not matter to *fidelity*" is false for the second grade as stated, unless one reads "fidelity" narrowly to mean (F1)–(F2) alone — which the anchored grade does not do, because condition 4 is *inside* it ("adds four conditions", of which 4 is world-kind plus active route).

### Hunt the answer in the starting points
For the exclusion: the thing to be explained is "why are state-differing things the same kind?" The answer is "the baseline relation is state." **Finding 12.** The conclusion is sitting in the starting point under the name "state." For the conjecture: the thing to be evaluated is "can a kind-label do work that no signature and no history does?" The refuting case (P12) ends "and the distinction does explanatory work," and "explanatory work" is precisely the notion the conjecture governs. **Finding 13.** The refutation criterion contains the conclusion under another name; it needs an account of "does explanatory work" independent of the conjecture, or it will be satisfied and defeated by stipulation.

### Add a job
Add: **different components of a kind have different constants and are treated as different for building.** Where would we see it? In a contract containing an edit on the constant (recovers), in a contract without one (lost), or in the module's own admitted edits (W3, recovers if the module admits it). **Finding 14.** The new job does *not* rule out any rival version of the exclusion as it stands; it only locates the case. So it added a location, not a holding. Add a second job instead: **a historical kind must make a difference we can see.** Where would we see it? In (R)'s requirement that the transport be selected or constructed; in Deploy; in (EK). Note that all three require the provenance **of the transport**, not of the component. **Finding 15.** The added job shows the historical kind has no site where it alone does work — its billed site (representation) is already staffed by transport provenance.

### Build the best rival
The strongest rival at this level: **drop the historical kind entirely.** Say: a component's kind is its signature on the admitted contract; provenance attaches to transports, and where a component's lineage matters, the lineage is a fact about the *transports* that produced and maintain it, not a second kind of kind. What could we change that would make this rival and the revision give different answers? **Finding 16.** Nothing on the change list separates them, because every job the historical kind is given is discharged by transport provenance in `Sel`/`Con`/`(R)`/`Deploy`/`(EK)`. At this level they are one account. If the revision wants them apart, it owes a case with a discriminating change — a case where two components of one forward kind, with identical transport provenance, differ in a way that does explanatory work. That is close to but not identical with the (C) attack, and it is a finer question: **record it as new**: *"Can two components differ in historical kind while sharing every transport provenance fact, and does the difference do any work?"* **Finding 17.** Calling this a new question, per the skill's "if no change on the list can tell them apart, they are the same explanation at this level," is the honest report, not a failure to decide.

### Check the patches
Has the revision been caught out before, and how was it rescued? The Grievance 2 defence ("not operationalism") reads like a patch to "kinds are edit-signatures, full stop": it adds the index, the module, and the admission that the question may need "either a finer contract or a history." **Finding 18.** The patch narrows the claim (kinds are relative to grain and contract) and, in the same sentence, widens it (history will do when the contract will not) without recording that the claim changed. Part XV then adds a second patch — the conjecture — whose only purpose is to absorb the case the first patch could not: a label that discriminates where no change and no history does. A part added only to absorb a failure is exactly what the skill calls a patch with no other job. Grievance 12's honest self-labelling ("It is an asymmetry, and it is stated rather than hidden") is good practice and does not by itself settle whether the asymmetry is *held*; it settles only that it is not concealed. **Finding 19.**

### Look inside
Does the revision explain how kind-sorting *works*, or only what comes *out*? For the forward kind, it looks inside: signatures are per-component relations, and Derivation 1 carries the claim ("every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has ... the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\)"). For the historical kind, it looks only at outputs — "a predicate on the history," with no account of which predicates, no account of their events, and no witness conditions. **Finding 20.** Could something built quite differently inside give the same reports? Yes: any predicate on histories at all gives the revise's reports, because none is excluded. The change that would show which one we have is the specification of the predicate class and a component-level witness.

## 6. Where each part came from

- **P2/P3 (baseline exclusion): *fitted*.** It is tuned on the cases in P3 — positions, states — and presented as a general principle. "Different positions" is one family; constants, coefficients, and rule parameters are not on the fitted set, and the revision is silent about them. Ask what it has never met: a baseline-only constant, a boundary condition that no admitted edit moves, a rule constant the module cannot vary.
- **P1, P4, P5 (signature, identity edit, index): *built*.** Derivation 1 works the consequence out with the target in view and can be caught out (it says exactly where it is silent: "because the contract may not be the admitted one, and because histories are not signatures").
- **P6 (historical kind): *asserted*.** No witness conditions for components, no predicate class, two of four relations undefined.
- **P7, P12 (conjecture and its refutation): *asserted*.** Stated as a conjecture, which is honest, but a conjecture is not yet a claim with truth conditions until its two key terms are fixed.
- **P8/P9 (asymmetry): *built*, then broken by P11.** The asymmetry is argued from the sorts; the world-kind/active-route condition gives the anchor an index that includes history, so the built part and the applied part disagree.
- **P10 (world-kind clause): *built*.** It does real work — it is what makes the anchored grade range over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and not just \(C\).
- **P11 (active route): *borrowed*.** It rests on Part IX's "active route," whose criterial content ("under the declared contrasts") lives in the criticism/use machinery that is not being tested here. Name it as borrowed and say so.
- **The availability half of P8 ("in principle"):** borrowed from the physical module, which Part XIV lists as primitive — "the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle." So the availability of the provenance witnesses is a module-dependent fact, not something the semantics can settle.

## 8. Answers to the four questions, stated plainly

- **Q1. Yes, in one scoped case, and the revision does not record it.** State and initial conditions are recovered (the question can ask about them; they are also why the exclusion was fitted). Constants and rule parameters are lost exactly when the contract affords no other edit that reaches the differing relation, so the loss is real but not universal. The exclusion is a fitted choice, defended by the very fact it excludes; a small test would locate it: *for every baseline difference, is there some admitted edit that moves the component?* Where yes, nothing is lost; where no, the revision has ruled the difference state by name. **Finding 21.**
- **Q2. No, not as written.** The historical kind is a placeholder: four relations named, none defined, and its witness is deferred to a definition that is "of a transport," not of a component. **Finding 22.** Two components of one forward kind are said to "may be of different historical kinds" — but nothing in the document would let anyone check that claim on a pair of components.
- **Q3. The asymmetry holds when comparing fidelity as (F1)–(F2); it does not hold when comparing fidelity as the document uses it in its second grade.** The actuality-of-route condition does smuggle history into the anchored verdict, and the smuggling is hidden by the word "fidelity." Two repairs are honest: either move the active-route requirement out of condition 4 into a separate, named anchor condition on the instance (then anchored accounts come in two flavours, signature-anchored and route-anchored), or say plainly that in physical cases anchoring is also historical, so the asymmetry is between *fidelity* and *representation* at the first grade and between *kinds of anchoring* at the second. **Finding 23.**
- **Q4. The conjecture is stated in refutable form, but with two terms unfixed, so it is not yet refutable in practice.** Give the predicate class a bound and separate "does explanatory work" from "is a discriminating kind-label"; otherwise the first and the second will be the same thing under two names, and either refutation or defence can be had by stipulation. **Finding 24.**

## 9. Whole-explanation checks

- **Direction.** Part II answers "what counts as what under this rule" (forward kind, historical kind), not "what produces it." The one production-shaped claim, the asymmetry defence, runs from the sort of a component to a conclusion about what may matter to fidelity. That direction is defensible; it is not evidence, and it does not by itself fix the component's own historical kind, whose direction of fit runs from history facts the document has not specified.
- **Answer hidden in the starting points.** "State" (P3) and "does explanatory work" (P12) both carry what they are asked to explain.
- **Rivals.** The best rival is "drop the historical kind; lineage attaches to transports only." No change on the revision's change list separates them. That is the single most useful negative result here — and it is a question for the revision to answer with a case, not an accusation.
- **Where the parts came from.** Fitted: the baseline exclusion. Built: signature machinery, the world-kind clause, Derivation 1. Asserted: the historical kind, the conjecture and its refutation. Borrowed: "state," "active route," the availability of the witnesses (the module).
- **Whole-document check.** Nothing in Part XIV's dependence order is broken by the historical kind — it sits beside (K) and reads histories, which are prior. So the revision's internal wiring is sound; the problem is not inconsistency but that two of the parts do not do the work they are billed for.

## 10. What would make it harder to vary

Two cuts and one poke, in order:

1. **Give a rule, not a name, for the baseline pairs.** For each pair the contract leaves untouched, say whether it is state and why; state that where no admitted edit reaches a relation, the exclusion loses a distinction and the claim is narrower by that much. Or else admit the baseline pair as a separating change when stated conditions hold. Either version is harder to vary than the present one, because a rival can no longer swap "state" for something else and get the same coverage. **Finding 25.**
2. **Bound the historical kind or drop it.** Either specify the predicate class and the component-level witness, and produce the case where two components of one forward kind, with the same transport provenance, differ in historical kind and it does work — or fold it into transport provenance and say so. **Finding 26.**
3. **Poke the anchored grade to locate the route condition.** Take a physical target with two occurrences agreeing in signature: one on the actual route, one a backup that would have responded identically. Ask which condition in the anchored grade changes sides, and name it in the statement of that grade. **Finding 27.**

---

## 11. What this does not show

Passing any of these tests would not show the revision true, and failing them does not show it false. Part II may be correct as a theory of kinds. What the tests show is which parts are doing work: the forward kind and the world-kind clause are held by named jobs; the baseline exclusion is loose and its loss is unrecorded; the historical kind is idle for fidelity, unheld for representation; the asymmetry holds at the first grade and is broken by the fourth condition of the second; and the conjecture is stated as a conjecture, which is to the revision's credit, but with two terms unbound. None of this rules on whether the source theory's overall constitutive conjecture is right, and the revision itself says no test of it has been run.

## 12. One next step

Write the two missing sentences. First, beside the baseline exclusion, a sentence fixing when a baseline difference is state and stating what is lost when it is not. Second, beside the historical kind, one line that says which predicates count and what a component-level witness would contain. If that second line cannot be written without borrowing the transport witness, the honest move is to drop the historical kind and let provenance attach where the document already puts it.

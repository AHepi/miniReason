- **(P8) The limit on concept creation** (Part X): "The invention of a new dimension of variation — a port the target organization did not have, a respect in which nothing was previously varied — is not a question found in the sense above, because the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing \(A\times B\). ## 3. Findings

**Finding 1 — The merit condition is not trivially satisfied, given a nontrivial \(O\).** Take a random re-parameterisation of an old question, say a change of one bit of \(S\). Does it enter (P1) as a found question? Only if "a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P), with \(o\) failing at the state before \(e\)." For a bit-flip in \(S\), the account of \(p'\) is the old account under a re-parameterisation; (P) requires "there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\)" *and* "\(o\) failing at the state before \(e\)" (Part XI: "\(\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\)"). Unless the bit-flip is aimed at a failing obligation, (P1) rejects. So the condition is not automatic. The user's triviality worry does not land on the definition as stated.

**Finding 2 — Its strength is borrowed from the declared \(O\).** Part XIV lists as declared parameters "the obligation sets \(O,P,O_p\)". It is the declared \(O\) that gives (P1) whatever discriminating power it has. On the document's own stance this is not a hole; every claim is relative to declared parameters. But it means the class membership of "the found questions" is not a formal property of the semantics; it is a property of the \(O\) supplied. The merit condition's reach is therefore *borrowed*, in the skill's sense. This is the honest form of the user's "trivial" worry.

**Finding 3 — The merit condition is stated counterfactually, where the document's closely related machinery requires actuality.** (P1) says "a deployable account of \(p'\) **would** repair \(o\) under (P)". Deployment, actual, appears elsewhere: (EK) requires "\(\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\land\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\)". So a coincidental repair that was never deployed, and whose causal route was never on an active route, satisfies (P1) but not (EK). The document does not say whether this gap is intended. Loose; the tightening is available in the document's own vocabulary — a clause of the form "the repair lies on an active route that passes through a deployment of the account of \(p'\)", mirroring ProducesVia. **Finding.**

**Finding 4 — Derivation 5's edit set does not state the composition rules that Part II requires of it.** Part II: "\(A\) is a set of admitted edits, closed under a partial associative composition with identity \(1\)." Derivation 5 gives four edit names and no composition of them. Whether "toggle a pair" composes with "alter \(\mathcal Q\)", and what identity and partial composition these have, is not stated. Part VI's (D) uses "a declared family \(\mathcal V\) of organization edits" and compares pairs \(v,w\) of them, so composition matters downstream. Loose. **Finding.**

**Finding 5 — "Add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning" conflates the edit with the datum.** Part III attaches meanings to the *pairs in \(K\)*: "\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit — each with a stated meaning". The meaning is data carried by the pair, not a part of any edit's identity in (O). If the meaning belongs to the datum, "with a meaning" is redundant in the name of the edit, and the edit is simply a set-edit of \(u_K\). If the meaning is meant to be part of edit-identity, then \(D_p\)'s \(A\) is a set of edit-plus-meaning pairs, which is not the shape of \(A\) in (O). The document should say which. As stated, the fourth edit is *either* a plain set-edit with a redundant name *or* not a Part II edit. **Finding.**

**Finding 6 — \(K\) is represented only monolithically, while \(S\) is represented bit-by-bit.** Part III restricts \(K\) to "pairs the module does not admit." Pairs the module does not admit are not in the target's \(A\times B\) — so they have no \(u_{(a,b)}\)-port. \(K\)'s content can therefore only be the value of the single port \(u_K\). \(S\), by contrast, prints its membership on the \(u_{(a,b)}\)-bits. This is coherent, but it means that the port-list does not surface \(K\)'s structure, and any closure condition tying \(u_K\) to "the 'in' ports" (P4) has to be stated as a constraint on the *value* of \(u_K\), not as a constraint on other ports. This is a design move, not a fault; it should be said rather than left to the reader. **Held, but noted.**

**Finding 7 — (P6) is over-broad.** The question-tuple is "\(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\)." The port-list of Derivation 5 supplies \(u_{(a,b)}\), \(u_{\mathcal Q}\), \(u_S\), \(u_K\), and the boundary "the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed." No port or boundary item corresponds to \(O_p\), and \(\rho_p\) is provenance ("attaches to the whole tuple \(p\)"). Two questions that agree on \(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q\) and differ only in \(O_p\) are one \(D_p\) by this construction. So "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\)" should read "modulo \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\)", or a \(u_{O_p}\)-port should be added. **Finding.**

**Finding 8 — Baseline precision.** Derivation 5's boundary is "the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed." Since a question fixes its baseline (Part III: "The **contract** \(C\) ... contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)"), a different \(b_0\) is a different \(D_p\). This is consistent with Part III ("An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\)"), but the phrase "questions on \(D\)" should read "questions on \(D\) with baseline \(b_0\)" for precision. Small. **Finding.**

**Finding 9 — (P8) holds for its narrow reading.** The limit says a new port "is a change to the target organization itself". That is right: \(D\)'s ports are \(V\), "\(V\) is a set of ports, each with a nonempty value domain \(X_v\)", and a claim about the same \(D\) with a new port would require a change of \(V\), which \(D_p\)'s boundary refuses to admit. For the case where the new port is *not* licensed by Inst at any admissible grain, no order of Inst-plus-new-grain supplies it (Part II: "every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes"). **Held.**

**Finding 10 — But the evidence for (P8) is narrower than its wording.** (P8) is stated as a class-level claim: "this document has no witness for it." Derivation 5 supplies only the construct-level evidence: *this* construction is built over "an existing \(A\times B\)." Part IV says "one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain (a mechanical one, a thermodynamic one, a computational one)", and Part II adds that "Claims can be rescued by moving to a grain only if the new grain is admissible, and the move is a new claim at a new index." Under that reading, the "new dimension" can be a *different target* \(D'\) that Inst already licenses for the same occurrence. A question about \(D'\) is fully representable as a \(D_{p'}\), and its construction is a finding *for \(D'\)*. What remains unrepresentable is the *passage* from \(D\) to \(D'\) as an episode's originative content. So (P8) is correct as a claim about *this construction*, but it is stated as a claim about the *class*. The document should either say which reading it intends, or say that the limit is a limit of Derivation 5's device, not of the semantics. **Loose at the class level, held at the device level.**

**Finding 11 — Part XV's description of case (i) is too strong.** Part XV: "(i) elimination of a physically impossible rival, handled only through a declared counterfactual set whose meaning is stipulated." Part VII handles *two* sub-cases and says so: "Two cases. Where the introducing edit is admitted, an account is faithful when introducing those components changes the answer in \(E\) as it does in \(D\)... That is the semantics' treatment of eliminative explanation, offered as **adequate for the first case and as scoped for the second**." The first sub-case uses no \(K\); only the second does. Part XV's "only through a declared counterfactual set" therefore covers the second sub-case and misses the first. **Finding.**

**Finding 12 — Case (ii) is genuinely open, and the reason is structural.** (F1) is a condition on each component against its anchor ("the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\) and projecting away its hidden ports equals the component relation of \(k\) under the translated edit"); (F2) is a condition on the assembled solution sets ("\(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)"). Neither says anything about invariance of \(E\)'s solution set under a *group* acting on \(D\)'s. A symmetry *preserves* solutions; a Part II edit *changes* them, and the identity edit "separates nothing" (Part II). So (K)'s signature is blind to symmetries by construction. Closing (ii) would require a new invariance condition or a new family of "automorphism edits" in \(A\) — neither is in the document. **Held as open.**

**Finding 13 — Case (iii) is genuinely open, and the reason is also structural.** The selection witness is a claim about events, not an organization: "\(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) holds when there are: a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\)...; a finite history \(H\subseteq C\)...; a survival condition... that **eliminated at least one member**." A selection-artefact explanation must represent how the data would look under the selection process, which needs the process as an organization with a transport. The document treats selection only as a witness. So (iii) cannot be closed without a new device. **Held as open.**

**Finding 14 — Case (iv) is not open; it is mislabelled.** Part XV says of (iv): "which falls under a declared admission relation and not under the physical quantifier of this attack." That sentence concedes the case is not a challenge to the class. Part VII works it: "This is a mathematical organization, so the admission relation is declared... The expansion is an account at that contract." So a mathematical explanation has a worked treatment on a declared admission relation, and attack (B)'s quantifier is "under *any* admitted contract" *with the physical module in force*. Case (iv) should be listed under the scope of (B) rather than under its cases, or Part XV should say that (iv) is closed by clarifying (B). **Borrowed** from the general declared-parameter stance of Part XIV. **Finding.**

**Finding 15 — Best rival to the merit condition, unaddressed.** A rival criterion is available in the document's own vocabulary: "a constructed question whose construction witness contains a nontrivial binding for \(D_p\)". Part X defines nontrivial binding: "a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)." Whether a re-parameterisation that repairs no obligation could still require a nontrivial binding for \(D_p\) — and whether, conversely, a question that repairs an obligation always does — is a question the document does not answer. Whether (P1) and the binding-rival are one criterion at this level is **unknown**; the document should say whether they separate, or name the case that tells them apart.

**Finding 16 — "Found" is not the answer hidden in the starting points.** (P1) uses (P), which is a defined relation over state values and active routes, and the merit refers to the state before \(e\). The parts do not presuppose "found" under another name. **Held.**

**Finding 17 — The counterfactual in (P1) is a poke the document does not run.** The skill's poke requires both halves. Half 1: deploy the account and observe whether the obligation's value moves — should alter the answer. Under the counterfactual form of (P1) this half is not what the criterion reads; the document does not say whether the counterfactual tracks the actual. Half 2: restate the question in different grammar — should not alter the answer, and does not, because (N) collapses recodings by \(\equiv_\ell\). Half 1 is the poke the document should run if it means (P1) to be discriminating beyond the case of actual deployment.

**Finding 18 — Direction is not reversed.** Both Derivation 5 and the merit condition run forward from the episode's history: (G) requires "\(\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\)", and the merit condition adds an obligation-failure at the state before \(e\). Poking the cause (the episode) should move the effect (the attribution), and poking the effect (calling a question "found") should not conjure the episode. That direction holds. **Held.**

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

- **Flip.** Had the merit condition been omitted, a random new contract would count as a found question, and Part XV's line "as the line between finding and relabelling" would have no subject. So (P2) does the job it names. Under the flip, the *limit* (P8) would also lose its point, since there would be no line for the invented dimension of variation to fall outside of. Both stand.
- **Reverse.** See finding 18. No backwards reading.
- **Hunt the answer in the starting points.** See finding 16. No label in the place of a part. But the strength of (P1) is carried by the declared \(O\) — a *declaration*, in the document's own sense, not a label. That is a different matter, and finding 2 covers it.
- **Add a job.** A new job for (P1): that the found question not merely *would* repair an obligation but *did*. (P1) does not survive this added job unchanged. Part XV already anticipates an added job ("a re-parameterisation it wrongly admits") — and Part XV does not say which rival versions that new job rules out either, so the added job in the document's own listing is currently idle. **Held as needing pairing.**
- **Build the best rival.** See finding 15. The rival is not separated.
- **Check the patches.** The merit condition has no recorded failure. Part XV does name the direction in which it *could* fail ("A question found that the merit condition wrongly rejects, or a re-parameterisation it wrongly admits"). No patch is recorded; the document is honest about the exposure.
- **Look inside.** (P1) is a success predicate on an outcome ("repairs \(o\) under (P)"). Two episodes with the same repair outcome can differ in whether the repair was on an active route through the deployment of the account — the difference the document elsewhere preserves with ProducesVia. So (P1) matches output where (EK) matches workings. Finding 3.

## 5. What would make it harder to vary

Three cuts, in order of what holds the most:

1. Give the merit condition a **provenance route clause** in the document's own vocabulary — the repair must lie on an active route that passes through a deployment of the account of \(p'\), mirroring "\(\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\)" in (EK). That closes finding 3 and gives (P1) a "would not" half: a coincidental repair, however deep, would not count.
2. State the **composition and identity** of Derivation 5's admitted edits, as Part II's \(A\) requires and Part VI's (D) uses. That closes finding 4 and makes (P5) a set of edits rather than a list of names.
3. Split Part X's limit into two claims — a claim about *this construction* (Derivation 5 is built over an existing \(A\times B\), so no witness here) and a claim about the *class* — and say which one is meant. That closes finding 10.

A fourth, smaller cut: add a \(u_{O_p}\)-port to \(D_p\), or weaken (P6) to "modulo \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\)" (finding 7).

---

## 6. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not the same as true. The findings above mark parts as held, loose, borrowed or unknown, and they do not show that the merit condition, Derivation 5, or the limit are false. A loose part may be correct; it just is not currently held in place by the changes that could have caught it out. Nothing here decides whether the analysis-of-kinds conjecture, the constitutive conjecture, or the anchored grade is right.

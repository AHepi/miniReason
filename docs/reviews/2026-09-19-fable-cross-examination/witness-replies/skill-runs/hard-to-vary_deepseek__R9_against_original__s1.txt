## 1. The question, frozen

The frozen question is 20a’s own: “the mapping from the consolidated revision list (file 15 §G, as amended by §F and §E3) to the sections of file 20 that carry it, so that file 20 can stay a standalone theory with no history in it while every change remains traceable. ‘Not done’ means exactly that.”

I freeze it there. I do **not** change it to “is 20 good?” except where a row marked “Done” claims a repair that is not present, or is present but does not do the job the row says.

## 2. The explanation in parts

The ledger is a set of repair claims. Its parts are the rows. The jobs are:

- locate the repair in 20;
- check the repair is present;
- check it does what the row says;
- find defects of 10 surviving in 20 under new wording;
- find new defects 20 introduces;
- rank by severity.

20a says its statuses are honest: “‘Not done’ means exactly that.”

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

### Remove

Remove the ledger’s “Done” mark from any row and the text often still has the wording. But remove the **proper sub-block** clause and 20’s one-component example becomes consistent again. Remove the **ownership** definition and the Can/owned cycle disappears. Remove the **merit condition** and the Part XIV dependence order is closer to true.

### Swap

Swap “admitted edit–boundary pairs” in Part III for “pairs the module does not admit” and 20’s own contract definition breaks: 20 says “The **contract** \(C\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs” and then “\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit.” That swap is not a test; it is already in the text. See F3.

### Flip

Flip the ledger claim “Non-circular dependence witness: proper sub-block” against 20’s own example: 20 says “The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored.” If \(\Gamma\) has one member, the required witness is “a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\),” which does not exist. The flip exposes F1.

### Poke

For each repair row, one poke that should change the verdict: if 20 contains the passage, the mark is Held; if the passage is absent or contradicts another passage, Loose/Borrowed. The largest poke is the one-component example: it is supposed to be the known candidate meeting (E), but 20’s non-circular condition cannot be met by it.

### Reverse

Reverse from the ledger’s “Done” back to the text. Several rows work backwards from the desired repair. Example: “Define or count as primitive: … relevant …” is marked Done, but 20 still says “the relevant difficulty” without defining it. Example: “Θ's admitted edit set stated explicitly before anything quantifies over it” is marked Done, but 20 Part II says “some physically admitted edit realizes” before Part XII’s \(A_\Theta(o)\).

### Hunt the answer in the starting points

The ledger puts the conclusion “Done” in the starting point. Several rows are true only if one reads the target passage charitably. The clearest is “Five conjuncts, not ‘four conditions’”: 20 says “five conditions” but prints six conjuncts, and 20a itself says “now six, with adequacy.”

### Add a job

Add the job “the repair must not contradict another repair.” Then:
- G2.1 (“C = …” with \(K\)) fails against G1.2 (“admitted edit set”).
- G5.2 (“proper sub-block”) fails against the one-component candidate.
- G1.5 (“owned” defined) fails against Part XII’s “owned retained realization.”
- G6.11 (“merit condition under (P)”) fails against Part XIV’s “(P), (EK) depend on (G)”.

### Build the best rival

Best rival: 20 is a genuine improvement over 10 on many rows, especially G3 provenance and G6 Derivation 3. But its own new conditions—proper sub-block, adequacy, anchored grade, merit condition—introduce new internal contradictions. That rival is stronger than “20a is simply false.” It says: many rows are Held, but the Done mark is not globally reliable.

### Check the patches

20’s patches are visible. The patch “proper sub-block” was added to stop removing all \(\Gamma\) counting as a witness. It breaks the one-component example. The patch “Contract adequacy” was added to catch protective contracts. It makes \(S\) nearly vacuous for target-sensitive changes and leaves the Part XV attack ambiguous. The patch “ownership” was added to define a primitive. It creates a cycle with Can.

### Look inside

Matching outputs is not matching workings. 20a maps ledger rows to section names. That is output matching. Looking inside shows:
- F1: one-component contradiction.
- F2: ownership/Can cycle.
- F3: contract type contradiction.
- F4: Derivation 1 injectivity omitted in Part V.
- F5: (P)/(G) dependence cycle.
- F6: reversed calculation has undefined \(\tau\) but is called an account.

## 5. Findings, ranked by severity

### Critical

**F1. 20’s one-component example contradicts its own proper-sub-block condition.**  
20a: “Non-circular dependence witness: proper sub-block, translated edit on E” — “Done.”  
20: “removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions.”  
20: “The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored.”  
If \(\Gamma\) has one active component, there is no nonempty proper sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\). So the one-component candidate cannot satisfy non-circular dependence. Yet 20 says it satisfies (E). New defect introduced by 20’s patch.

**F2. “owned” and Can are defined in a circle.**  
20: “A subhistory is **owned** by \(s\) when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability \(s\) possesses in the sense of Part XII (\(\operatorname{Can}\)).”  
20: “\(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one.”  
Owned depends on Can; Can depends on owned. New defect or unfixed defect disguised as a definition. Ledger G1 marks this “Done.”

**F3. Contract \(C\) is both “admitted” and allowed to contain non-admitted pairs.**  
20: “The **contract** \(C\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over.”  
20: “\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit.”  
20: “\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\).”  
The same object cannot be “the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs” and contain “pairs the module does not admit.” New defect introduced by 20.

**F4. Part V cites Derivation 1 without its injectivity hypothesis.**  
20 Part V: “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract.”  
20 Derivation 1: “Let \(t\) satisfy (F1) on \(C\), and let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\). Then…”  
The injectivity condition is absent from Part V’s appeal. New defect introduced by 20, because 20 added the hypothesis and then omitted it where used.

**F5. The dependence order cycles (P)/(G).**  
20 Part XIV: “(P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy, ProducedBy.”  
20 Part X: “subject to the **merit condition**: … would repair \(o\) under (P).”  
But (P) in Part XI does not use (G); it uses \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}\). So (G) depends on (P), while Part XIV says (P) depends on (G). New defect introduced by 20’s merit condition and unchanged dependence order.

**F6. Reversed calculation is called an account after \(\tau\) is said to be undefined.**  
20: “\(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair.”  
20: “Under the identification contract it is an account at that contract.”  
20 Part IV: “\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits.”  
If \(\tau\) is undefined on a production edit, \(t\) is not a total transport; if it is not a transport, it cannot be an account at the identification contract either. New defect introduced by 20’s attempted repair.

### High

**F7. Non-vacuity’s scope honesty is now tautological.**  
20: “\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\).”  
20: “every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently.”  
Since \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S\), the “excluded by \(S\)” clause is true by the form of \(C\). 10 had a substantive clause: “A contract consisting only of relabelings, or excluding every change under which the active commitments could matter to \(\mathcal Q\), does not satisfy non-circular dependence.” 20 removed it from non-vacuity and only partially moved it. Surviving defect under new wording.

**F8. Properness can exclude genuine single-component targets.**  
20: “Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\) … no anchor is the whole target.”  
If \(D\) has one component, or if the only workable anchor is the whole target, the anchored grade cannot be met. This may be intended against the one-component decoy, but it is stated as a universal properness condition. New defect introduced by 20.

**F9. Contract adequacy is both too weak and too strong.**  
20: “there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.”  
It compares both to baseline, not to each other; two different non-baseline responses pass. It also makes \(S\) unable to exclude any admitted change that alters the target, so “stated scope” becomes nearly vacuous for target-sensitive physical questions. New defect introduced by 20’s sixth conjunct.

**F10. The anchored grade imports a history condition into a fidelity grade.**  
20: “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.”  
20: “Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution.”  
Actuality of route is not component fidelity under change. 20a itself flags “this is a history condition inside an adequacy grade, and it is the place where the transport/component asymmetry of Grievance 12 is least clean.”

**F11. “relevant” survives undefined despite being listed as defined.**  
20a: “Define or count as primitive: … relevant …” — “Done.”  
20: “for which the relevant difficulty is that some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\).”  
10 had “contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use.” 20 replaces “relevant” in Build with “required for that use,” but reintroduces “relevant” in Origin. Surviving defect under new wording.

**F12. \(A_\Theta\) is not stated before it is used.**  
20a: “Θ's admitted edit set stated explicitly before anything quantifies over it” — “Done.”  
20 Part II: “every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes.”  
20 Part XII: “the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta(o)\) — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits.”  
The explicit set appears in Part XII, after Part II already quantifies over “physically admitted edit.” Surviving defect.

**F13. Bearing still ignores grounds and connection.**  
20a: “Bearing parameters” — “Done.”  
20: “A criticism \(c\) has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection. Let \(p_\delta\) be the question … Then \(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)\).”  
\(g\) and the connection are named but not in the predicate. 10 had the same problem: “A criticism has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection. … Then \(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p)\iff\operatorname{Account}(\mathcal E_c,p_\delta)\).” Surviving defect.

**F14. “\(s\) instantiates a question” is a category error.**  
20: “where \(p\) is a coherently posed question that \(s\) instantiates before \(e\).”  
A system instantiates an organization, not a question tuple. Derivation 5 builds a question-organization \(D_p\). New wording defect.

**F15. \(K\)’s type is left open.**  
20: “\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit.”  
If \(K\nsubseteq A\times B\), then (F1) and (A), which quantify over \((a,b)\in C\) and use \(\tau(a)\), are not well-typed. The ledger marks the contract form “Done.” New defect.

**F16. The count of conditions is inconsistent.**  
20a: “Five conjuncts, not ‘four conditions’” — “Done”; but the same cell says “Part V (now six, with adequacy).”  
20: “the following **five** conditions hold”; the displayed formula has six conjuncts: \(\text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}\). New/ledger internal defect.

**F17. The constitutive conjecture still names four items while the revised semantics adds more.**  
20: “explanatory creativity is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports between organizations checked by change-fidelity, (iii) the provenance of those transports and of the questions they serve, and (iv) their physical realization.”  
20 then adds contract adequacy, anchored grade, merit condition. Either the conjecture is no longer the conjecture, or the four items silently absorb the additions. New defect.

**F18. Derivation 10 was downgraded and the loss is unpriced.**  
20a: “Derivation 10 was downgraded from a consistency witness to a scenario, and the document no longer claims any relative-consistency witness for the class. That is a loss the list did not price.”  
20: “This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it.”  
This is not a repair; it is a regression recorded in the ledger.

## 6. Where each part came from

- **Fitted:** most G3 provenance rows and G6 Derivation 3 are fitted to the stated defects in 10 and survive the text check.
- **Built:** G6.1, G6.3, G6.8 are built with the target in view and are stated in a way that can be caught out.
- **Asserted:** several “Done” rows are asserted. F11 (“relevant”), F12 (\(A_\Theta\)), F13 (Bearing), F19 (non-vacuity) are asserted as Done but not held by the text.
- **Borrowed:** F2 borrows “owned” from Can and Can from “owned.” F5 borrows the dependence order and the merit condition from each other. F6 borrows transport totality while declaring \(\tau\) undefined.

## 7. What would make it harder to vary

The two jobs that would tighten it most:

1. **Resolve F1 and F3 together.** Either restore a nonempty block in non-circular dependence, or show a one-component candidate that has a proper sub-block; and decide whether \(C\) is admitted pairs or a set including non-admitted counterfactuals.
2. **Resolve F2 and F5 together.** Either make “owned” primitive, or define it without Can; and either remove the merit condition’s dependence on (P) or correct Part XIV’s dependence order.

## 8. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not true. This audit does not show 20 is false. It shows which rows are Held, which are Loose, which are Borrowed, and which new defects 20 introduces.

# Corrections first

Your description of the page is accurate. Part V does make contract adequacy a conjunct of Account, and in the shape you quote: “**Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.” It is the sixth conjunct of (E).

Three corrections, and one of them makes your case weaker and two make it stronger.

- **Your “too weak” bullet is the document’s own listed defeat.** Part XV already says: “**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V’s adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target.” So this is conceded ground, not a new attack. My job is only to say whether the definition nevertheless closes it. It does not — but not for the reason the wording of the attack suggests, and the sharper reason is elsewhere (note 7).
- **“Too strong” is not what the condition does as written.** As written it fires only on *asymmetry*: the target moves and the candidate does not, or the reverse. A target with out-of-scope dependence that the candidate mirrors in direction passes. Your own paraphrase carries the same asymmetry (“the target’s answer moves while the candidate’s does not”), so the defect is invisible from your diagnosis too.
- I have only the revised text. That this conjunct was *moved into* Account by the revision I take on your word; what the page shows is that the document’s headline description of the first grade was not updated for it. Part 0 says an account at a contract is “faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated” — four requirements, no adequacy — and then, “The first grade is what can be assessed within a question. The second is what ‘the explanation is about the actual mechanism’ means, and it is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract.” A conjunct that ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) is a condition on the larger contract, sitting in the grade that is defined as not being one.

# Scene-by-scene notes

## Sequence A — the machinery adequacy quantifies over (Part III)

**1. The scope really is the only place adequacy can look, and it really is closed there.** \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), and \(K\) is “pairs the module does not admit”, so \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\) and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S\). Job: stop a scope hiding the embarrassing change. It does exactly that. This is the load-bearing half of the condition and it is sound — protect it. (I use it in notes 7 and 10.)

**2. The quantifier domain is where the question’s coherence guarantees nothing.** Part III: “It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)”. Only on \(C\). Adequacy then evaluates \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) at pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\). So the condition’s quantifier ranges over exactly the region where nothing on the page requires \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) — or, if \(\tau\) is partial, \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) — to exist. The same gap as “\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)” is the reason the condition cannot be evaluated there. *Fault.* Better version: extend coherence to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), or restrict adequacy to pairs at which both profiles are defined **and** make the account declare the set where they are not.

**3. The clause tying \(S\) to \(K\) is unreadable.** Part III requires “\(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other’s complements as stated”. Read literally that is \(S\subseteq K\) and \(K\subseteq S\), i.e. \(S=K\), which contradicts both glosses given two sentences earlier. If it is a typo for \(S\cap K=\varnothing\), say so; adequacy’s meaning depends on where \(K\) sits relative to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and today nothing on the page forbids a modeller parking a separating pair in \(K\) (note 4).

**4. \(K\)’s “stated meaning” is given a job no conjunct performs.** Part III: “a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning.” But Non-vacuity as stated is “\(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\)” plus a bookkeeping clause, and never touches a meaning. Part V’s own exclusion table goes further: “An account whose only substantive contrast is one that no admitted edit realizes and that is not declared in \(K\) with a stated meaning fails non-vacuity” — a verdict no conjunct delivers. *Fault (planted, never collected).* Note the consequence for your fourth question, below.

## Sequence B — the conjuncts of Part V

**5. Which baseline — the page fixes half of one.** The target’s baseline is fixed: \(C\) “contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)”. But “differs from its baseline value” at a pair \((a,b)\) with \(b\neq b_0\) is ambiguous between \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) and \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b)\); the second is the counterfactual shape the semantics uses everywhere else, and the two give different verdicts. Worse, the clause “or the reverse” needs a baseline **for \(E\)**, and none is supplied: (F2) fixes \(\tau(1)=1\) and says nothing about \(\sigma(b_0)\). Grievance 4’s statement of the same condition (“would alter the target’s answer and not the candidate’s”) has no baseline at all — so the condition is stated twice, differently, and completely in neither place. *Fault.* Fix: add \(\sigma(b_0)=b_0^E\) to (F2), beside \(\tau(1)=1\), and write “differs from \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b)\)”.

**6. \(\tau\) is undefined at the pair, and the document’s own worked verdict depends on which way you read that.** Part IV types \(\tau\) as a map: “\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits”. Part VII then says of the reversed calculation: “here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation’s component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair” — and in the same paragraph, “it is an account at that contract”. The production edit is admitted and outside the identification contract, i.e. squarely in adequacy’s domain. So either (i) undefined counts as “does not [differ]”, adequacy passes, and Part VII’s verdict stands — and then a candidate dodges adequacy by having no counterpart for the edit, which is the dodge the condition exists to stop; or (ii) undefined counts as failure, adequacy fails, and Part VII’s stated verdict is wrong. *Fault, and the decisive one: the page’s only worked case where a contrasted contract is at issue cannot be decided by the definition.* Note this is not a matter of taste: the document needs a clause, and the two available clauses have opposite consequences.

**7. Movement is tested, not agreement — the loophole is exactly the move the condition exists to close.** Adequacy fires when \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) moves and \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) does not, or the reverse. If **both** move — to different values — it does not fire. Inside \(C\), (A) is a value test: “\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)”. Outside \(C\), adequacy silently drops to a direction test. Combine with note 1: an exclusion where only the target moves is caught, so the *only* exclusion that both hides the embarrassing change and passes is one at which both answers move. Your “too weak” question therefore has an exact answer: yes, a scope can exclude exactly the change at which the candidate’s answer differs from the target’s, provided both answers move there. Better version: state adequacy in (A)’s own form on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), i.e. \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\). The fix is safe on the document’s own test case — the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) still passes it (“satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored”), because equality holds everywhere; so the fix does not accidentally move the anti-repackaging work from condition 3 into grade 1.

**8. Remove it — and the job is unique inside its grade.** Deleting adequacy costs grade 1 its only \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)-level condition: (F1), (F2), (A) are all on \(C\); Non-circuit and Non-vacuity are not about scope. The job *is* done for grade 2, and done better, by world-kind (“fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits”) and by Resolution (“Whenever two components of \(D\) can be separately varied by pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with distinct effects on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), they lie in distinct anchors”). These are not two routes to one job — one is per-component, one is at the answer profile — so the real question is whether grade 1 needs the net at all. That is the architectural crux, and note 10 says why.

**9. Swap it — and the swap names the term the index forgets.** Replace \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with \(C\) and the quantification empties: the condition becomes true by arithmetic and its whole job dies. So \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is *held* by the job. But \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is precisely the term no index fixes for declared organizations.

**10. Not too strong as written — but the escape from the weak reading is not free, and this is the good news.** Widening \(S\) until nothing is excluded makes adequacy vacuous, but it makes (F1), (F2) and (A) range over all of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) — the grade-2 burden. So the weak reading is only profitable for a candidate that excludes. Combined with note 7, the loophole is narrow and exact rather than a general failure, and the condition cannot currently be shown either too strong or useless. What it *is* is half a condition: strong enough to be worth stating, worded so that it cannot be checked.

**11. Adjacent, unranked: condition 3 over-reaches against its own gloss.** “No active component’s relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target’s own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law.” For a target whose components do not constrain each other’s ports, a faithful copy of one component satisfies \(L_k=\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_D\), so the anchored grade would exclude a genuine mechanism — a name the gloss itself does not reach. Not part of your attack, but found while testing adequacy’s neighbours in the same conjunction.

## Sequence C — the surrounding claims

**12. Derivation 7’s index is one clause short, and its last sentence over-claims.** Derivation 7: “The assessment is indexed to \(C\); its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index.” But the truth value of “\(E\) is an account” at a fixed \(C\) depends on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which is not recovered from \(C\) (Part III allows the same set \(C\) from different \((C_{\mathrm{adm}},S,K)\); Part XIV lists the declared admission relation separately). So two assessments with the same \((C,\mathcal Q)\) — Part III: “An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\)” — can disagree on Account, and the disagreement *is* adequacy. The index must be \((C,C_{\mathrm{adm}},\mathcal Q)\). The final sentence, “the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded”, is then half true: it catches a change to \(S\); by construction it cannot catch a change to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), because it takes \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) as given. And (EK) already knows this is a move: “A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index.”

**13. Grievance 4 states the second index unqualified; Part VII qualifies it.** Grievance 4: “a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target’s answer and not the candidate’s … the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism.” Part VII: “Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation.” Both are on the page; they are not in the same place, and the guarantee is stated where the qualifier is not. *Fault-lite.* The repair is the document’s own device, used twice already (“characterized by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed”): require that a declared admission relation be fixed by a record before the candidate is assessed, or the analogy with Part XIII’s independently specified challenges fails. Then the fair mirror is fair again.

**14. No worked case lets adequacy be the deciding conjunct, so its price is never felt.** The odd-order example passes because it excludes nothing: “Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation.” The reversed calculation is decided by note 6’s unstated clause. The occlusion scenario is “a scenario, not a consistency witness”. So the cost test on this condition comes back empty: the document asserts what the condition catches and lists the attack on it, but never exhibits a candidate that adequacy, and nothing else, rejects. *Fault.* Add the case (this is also the cheapest way to settle note 6).

## Passes 3–5, where they transfer

**15. Pass 3.** There are no persons, so the flaw/want/opponent battery does not transfer. The choice test does, on the candidate-versus-target pairing: at each admitted pair outside \(C\) the candidate either moves or does not, and at a \(\tau\)-undefined pair neither option is on the page — a forced move, which by the skill’s own rule shows nothing and carries nothing. Flipping the condition (agreement instead of asymmetry) would make grade 1 non-question-internal; that cost is real, and it is why note 7 asks for a decision rather than a correction. The cost test is note 14.

**16. Pass 4 — the pattern count, and an unfair mirror.** The document’s central question about how to act is: what stops a contract from hiding the change that separates a candidate from its target? Sites: Grievance 2, Grievance 4, Grievance 10, Part III (\(S\) with reasons; \(K\) with meanings), Part V (Non-vacuity’s statedness, adequacy), the anchored grade (world-kind, Resolution), Part VII (declared admission), Part VIII (historical index), (EK) (later narrowing is a new index), Derivation 7, Part XV. Two answers, not one: where the physical module supplies \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), the change cannot be hidden; where the question declares its admission relation, the second index is a declaration — Part VII owns this, Grievance 4 and Derivation 7 do not. The fair mirror test on the pair: did both have options, did both know the cost, what came after? The physical case’s \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is supplied by \(\Theta\), so there is no option; the declared case’s is chosen by the same act that chooses \(S\), so adequacy can be made vacuous by the declaration that makes it evaluable. Silence afterwards is the true fault here: the qualifier exists and the guarantee is published without it.

**17. Pass 5.** Adequacy is the one place in Part V where a formal condition is also the objectivity claim, so it is a genuine meeting point of plot and theme — and the world’s rules do express the theme, since \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}=A_\Theta(o)\) is what makes the honesty condition non-negotiable. Medium check: it is a prose-only conjunct standing beside three formulas, in a document that gives formulas for its other pivotal conditions, and it is the conjunct whose quantifier is hardest to read; that is a real cost in this medium. Vanishing: \(b_0\) is defined once, used in Non-vacuity and in adequacy’s baseline, and never related to \(\sigma\) (note 5).

# How it fits together

Adequacy is a sound anti-gaming condition bolted into a grade whose description forbids exactly that kind of condition. The bolt is what creates three of the four problems you name — the index collision with Derivation 7, the undefined domain, and the missing baseline for \(E\) — and the fourth, the wording, is independent of the bolt. The document knows the middle is hard: it states the condition as a direction test rather than a value test, which is neither gameable in the ordinary way nor able to catch the case Part XV names. Grade 2 already holds the complete version of the job; the question is whether grade 1 can hold a fragment of it without ceasing to be question-internal.

# The two or three faults that matter most

**1. The condition cannot be evaluated in its own quantifier range, and the document’s one worked verdict depends on the missing clause (notes 2, 6).** Fix by stating the baseline for \(b\) and for \(E\), the definedness requirement on \(\mathcal Q\) and on \(\tau\) beyond \(C\), and the index as \((C,C_{\mathrm{adm}},\mathcal Q)\), and re-running Part VII’s identification example under it with the admission relation written down.

**2. Movement is tested where agreement is needed (note 7).** The only exclusion that passes and still does discriminating work is one at which both answers move — precisely the case the condition exists to catch. State it as the equality form of (A) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\); the check on the \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) repackaging shows this does not collapse the two grades.

**3. Legitimacy is checked at one of three levels (notes 1, 4, 13).** Scope exclusions are checked; admission-relation exclusions and counterfactual declarations are not, and \(K\)’s promised “stated meaning” is used by no conjunct. Add the requirement that a declared admission relation be fixed by a record before the candidate is assessed, and give \(K\)’s meaning a condition to live in.

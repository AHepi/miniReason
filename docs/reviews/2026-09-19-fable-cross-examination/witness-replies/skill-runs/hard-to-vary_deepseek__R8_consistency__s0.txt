# Referee report — `20_semantics_revised`

**A note on vocabulary first.** The words I use below — parts, a change to the world / a poke, the change list, the question, still explains, surprise, fitted / built / asserted, held / loose / idle / borrowed / unknown, hard to vary — are the skill's plain-word renderings of this theory's own terms. `word-list.md` maps them: "the change list" ← *contract*; "a poke" ← *admitted edit*; "fitted / built / asserted" ← *selected / constructed / declared* provenance; "hard to vary" ← the `Pres(F′) ⊆ Pres(F)` claim of Part VI. So I have not used the skill's phrasing as evidence about the document; wherever I say what the document holds, I quote the document.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What:** internal contradictions or tensions **between parts** of the revised document — two passages that cannot both be right as written, or a claim used in one place that the definition it cites does not license.

**Over what range:** all of Parts 0–XVI of the supplied file, including the derivations and the attack lists.

**What is asked:** for each tension, the exact passage, the part it collides with, and a mark. Not: whether the theory is true; not: whether it is elegant; not: whether it is hard to vary in Deutsch's sense. A "contradiction" here is an *internal* failure — the document against itself — so the strongest form of it is one where a candidate passes or fails a condition **by the document's own definition**, contradicting what the document says about that candidate.

The user's eight candidates are taken as the frozen attack; anything else I found is marked as such.

---

## 2. The jobs

Each put as a contrast — why this *and not that*.

1. One term, one type: why "D" is the target **and not** the carrier; why "c" is a content **and not** an organization, in every place it is an argument.
2. One count, one list: why (E) has **five** conjuncts and not six; why the anchored grade has "four conditions" and not two.
3. Definition and use agree: why the kind-relation applies to a component-and-subnetwork pair **and not** only to two components of one organization.
4. A claim about a candidate matches that candidate's verdict: why the one-component candidate **does** satisfy (E) **and not** fail Non-circular dependence.
5. A cited condition does the job claimed for it: why adequacy catches a protective scope **and not** only scopes where one side stays at baseline.
6. Fixity at an index survives what the index depends on: why "E is an account on C" **does not** change when the module changes and C does not.
7. Cross-references land: why "the discrimination requirement of Part IV" **is** in Part IV **and not** only invoked from Parts III and 0.
8. Two lists of defeaters list the same defeaters: why Part XV's list and Part 0's (A)–(F) agree, **and not** differ.
9. An asserted scenario and an asserted membership claim agree: why "then (G) holds" **and** "None is asserted" cannot both stand.
10. A summary matches the thing summarised: why "anchoring … is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract" covers a definability exclusion and a history condition.

## 4. The change list I used

Edits a referee can make to the *document* (the analogue here of pokes at the world): delete a conjunct; swap a symbol's referent; flip the arrow; replace a subnetwork anchor by a component; narrow or widen a contract (an index change); change the module while holding C fixed; substitute a candidate (one-component repackager, reversed calculation); move a claim's index silently; re-type the letter \(c\). **Left out by me openly:** I did not test the document against any fact outside itself (no claim about infants, physics, or mathematics is checked); the truth of the constitutive conjecture is not at issue; and I did not re-derive the source theory's omitted parts (capability, recursion, universality are out of scope per `word-list.md`'s own warning).

## 5. Findings

### Candidate 1 — orientation of transports (Part IV) vs (R), Account, Derivation 8

**F1. The letter \(D\) carries two roles in one sentence, and a third in Derivation 8.** Quote: "This one orientation is used everywhere: an explanation \(E\) is held to its target \(D\) by \(t:D\to E\); a carrier's organization \(D\), with \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\), is held to the content \(c\) it represents by \(t:c\to D\)." In (R) the same letter is the *carrier* ("\(\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land… t:c\to D]\)"), while in Part III "\(D\) is the target"; Derivation 8 then says "it does not preserve \(D\)'s structure". **Verdict:** the orientation *rule* is applied consistently (the arrow points at the thing held, so content→carrier is the same rule as target→explanation); only the naming is loose. **Mark: loose** — no job fails, but any reader checking (R) against Account will misread it.

**F2. "the organization \(t\) targets" has no fixed referent, and the asymmetry claim next to it contradicts the document's own paradigm.** Quotes: Constructed holds when "the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst"; and "a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both." On the reading forced by the orientation rule (target = what the constructed organization answers to), the selection paradigm of Part IV is a counter-case: "\(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport that is typically selected: the population is the variants of the system's tracking machinery, the history is the sensory changes encountered…" — here \(F\), the thing \(P\) answers to, is instantiated throughout the selection history. Nothing in the `Sel` definition forbids it either, so the clause "no target instantiated before the variation" is an **asserted addition** dressed as a definitional difference. **Mark: loose on the naming, unknown on the asymmetry claim** (one written-out example would settle it). Note that Derivation 6's stratification job — "a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness" — is done by the Constructed clause alone and does not need the over-strong separation sentence.

**F3. `Ans_S` is unbound.** Quote: "the **expectation** is \(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)." (Q) defines "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\)" for a *question* \(p\); Part V writes "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)" for an organization. The surprise paragraph never binds \(S\) to the transport's codomain, though \(S\) is elsewhere the simulation layer. **Mark: idle slip** (removable with no loss to any stated job).

*Checked and holding:* (R)'s direction, Account's use of \(t:D\to E\) with "\(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\)", (K1) as "\(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)\)", and Derivation 8's exclusion of "the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port". These four are consistent as written. **Mark: held** (by the orientation rule, which they all obey).

### Candidate 2 — the two-place Account vs (K1), (EK), Part VI

**F4. (EK) applies Account to a content, and patches the type afterwards.** Quote: "\(\land\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\land\) … Here \(c\) is a content whose organization is the \(E\) of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p_c)\)". But Part X says "The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a question." For a transport-valued or question-valued \(c\) — and Derivation 5 explicitly makes a question "the content \(c\) in (G)" — there is no "organization of \(c\)" for Account to take. **Mark: loose** — the gloss privately narrows \(c\) at the point of use.

**F5. Part VI's support sets are built on the two-place Account, which re-quantifies the very \(\Gamma\) they range over.** Quotes: "Define \(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\). (S)" and, immediately before, "The predicate is two-place on the organization and the question, with the transport and commitments existentially quantified". Since \(\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\) existentially picks its own \(t\) and \(\Gamma\), "\(W\)" need not be the active commitment set of \(E|W\); and the critical-block condition "\(W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\land W\setminus B\notin\mathsf S_{E,p}\)" then compares two candidates whose commitments are chosen independently. The four-place form the document also supplies — "\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\)" — is what (S) and (B) presuppose. **Mark: held-to-nothing** (the support machinery, the finite monotone theorem and the redundant-route/interference examples all rest on it).

**F6. "explanatory jobs \(F\)" is used once and never defined, and is passed where a question belongs.** Quote: "For explanatory jobs \(F\subseteq F'\), \(\operatorname{Pres}(F')\subseteq\operatorname{Pres}(F)\), where \(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\)." Account's second argument is a question \(p\) (Part III's tuple), and no definition of a "job" appears anywhere else in the document. **Mark: unknown** — a definition of *job*, or a replacement by \(O_p\)/\(\mathcal Q\), would settle it.

*Checked and holding:* (K1)'s use of the two-place Account matches the predicate as defined. **Mark: held.**

### Candidate 3 — the six conjuncts of (E) vs any sentence still counting them

**F7. The count does not match the list, and neither matches the gloss in Part 0.** Quote: "holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold" followed by five headings but the display "\(\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}. \tag{E}\)" — six conjuncts. Part 0 glosses the same grade with four items: "An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated." The gloss also drops Contract adequacy, and says "under every change the contract admits" although \(C\) contains \(K\), pairs "the module does not admit". **Mark: loose** — no job fails, but three texts give three counts and one of them omits a condition.

### Candidate 4 — grain admissibility vs the declared-index status of grain

**F8. The admissibility clause is ill-formed against its own gloss, and is stated only for occurrences.** Quote: "A grain \(\ell\) is a declared index, but not a free one. A grain is **admissible** for an occurrence \(o\) when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain (Part IV, Inst) and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes: a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed." A signature is a *set* of triples — "\(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\)" — and no single edit "realizes" one; the gloss that follows ("no admitted change can touch") is a different, single-edit condition. It is also indexed to an *occurrence*, while Part V condition 4 needs a grain for every candidate, including the mathematical one of Part VII where there is no module at all ("a mathematical question must *declare* its admission relation"). Relatedly, Part III defines "\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\) is the set of pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization, the physically admitted edits \(A_\Theta\)", but Part XII's \(A_\Theta\) is per-occurrence — "the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\)". **Mark: loose as written, unknown in effect** (one worked admissibility judgement would settle it).

**F9. Grievance 2 disclaims world-facts for kinds; Part V asserts one.** Quotes: "'One kind at that index' is a fact about the pairing of an organization with a contract, not a fact about the world"; and "The right court of appeal is not \(C\) but \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\): a kind-claim about the world is a claim about every change the module admits." The two are indexable apart (forward kind at \(C\) vs world-kind at \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)), so this is friction rather than contradiction — but the same word "kind" carries both, and condition 4 is then called "**World-kind**". **Mark: loose** (the contrast is available but never stated in grievance 2).

### Candidate 5 — baseline-pair exclusion vs Derivation 1's preservation on all of C

**F10. The kind-relation is defined for a fixed organization; its principal uses compare a component of \(E\) with an anchor subnetwork of \(D\).** Quotes: "Fix an organization \(D\) … The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is…"; "Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** …" — versus "Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor" (condition 4); "a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork" (Derivation 1); "then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things" (Derivation 10); "its one component is not of one forward kind with the assignment of \(L\)" (Part VII). The comparison across organizations needs a bijection of *change sets* (\(\tau,\sigma\)) as well as footprints, which (K) never supplies; and the anchor may be multi-component — a case (K) covers only "for a single component with all ports visible". The document itself names the gap, but only as a limit on the *conjecture*: "It does not show that a kind-label can do no work, because the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component…" **Mark: loose at its base** — the anchored grade, the reversibility verdict in Part VII and the Derivation-10 scenario all lean on the unlicensed comparison.

**F11. The baseline exclusion does no work in Derivation 1 and pushes against its own summary.** Quotes: kind-identity ignores baselines — "their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)" — with the stated reason "the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**"; but fidelity requires equality at every pair including baselines — "for every \((a,b)\in C\), the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\) … equals the component relation of \(k\)". So a component and its anchor differing only at baselines are *one kind* on (K) and *not faithful* on (F1), and the exclusion earns nothing. Worse for the summary: Derivation 1 says both "a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork" *and* "The anchored grade of Part V is where signature on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is required; it is a condition, not a corollary." If the first sentence holds at every contract, it holds at \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), making condition 4's kind clause a corollary — the document can keep the second sentence only by relying on F10's gap. **Mark: loose** (the exclusion is either a decoration or a defect, and the two sentences cannot both be exact).

### Candidate 6 — surprise on \(C\) entails loss of Deploy (Part IV) vs Part X

**F12. The inference ignores the existential in (R), which Part IV itself insists on.** Quotes: "surprise at \((a,b)\in C\) is a failure of fidelity on \(C\), so on that contract the system has, by (R), stopped representing the content, and by Part X stopped deploying it"; and (R) is "\(\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land…]\)"; and "It is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain … and nothing here assumes uniqueness." Failing *one* transport falsifies a conjunct for that pair, not the existential. Part X's side of the inference holds — "Deploy … holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance" — and so does the biconditional step from \(\neg\)Faithful to \(\neg\)Rep *for that \(D,t\)*; only "stopped representing" as an unqualified claim fails. **Mark: loose** (one word — "by that transport" — would repair it).

### Candidate 7 — the historical index (Part VIII) vs Derivation 7

**F13. Fixity at the index is contradicted by the index's own dependence on the module.** Quotes: "A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change"; versus "\(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse." \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is "the set of pairs the module in force admits". Add an admitted edit outside the stated scope \(S\), keep \(C\) as a set unchanged, and account-hood at that fixed \(C\) flips, because adequacy ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\). The same clause makes \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\) — "two-place on the organization and the question" — depend on an argument it does not have, the module in force. **Mark: held against it** (the adequacy conjunct does this job; the historical-index sentence as written does not survive it).

**F14. Adequacy is narrower than three passages claim, and Part XV concedes it.** Quotes: "This is the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change"; "A contract that quietly excludes such a change is caught by the requirement that exclusions be stated, and then by the adequacy condition, whether or not anyone supplies the change"; "the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded" (Derivation 7). Against: the condition only fires where one side *stays at baseline* — "differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) does not, or the reverse". A pair outside \(C\) at which both move, wrongly, escapes it; and Part XV lists exactly that: "**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target." Derivation 7's "whether or not it is recorded" also sits awkwardly with the record-keeping emphasised in Part III ("the record must show the boundary") and Part 0 ("the two ways are indexed so they cannot be confused"), since adequacy is a condition *at* a contract, not a detector of index changes. **Mark: loose** (the patch is narrower than the three claims made for it; the strongest statement of what it catches is Part XV's own concession).

### Candidate 8 — the scenario in Derivation 10 vs Part XIV's membership claims

**F15. "then (G) holds" vs "None is asserted".** Quotes: "If \(S_1\) was built by an owned subhistory containing a nontrivial binding construction required for the use … then (G) holds"; and "Whether (P) and (EK) hold depends on constituents the scenario does not supply: a recognized difficulty, a conjectural objection, a content-sensitive response, an epistemic obligation named with its before and after values, and the active routes of Part XI. None is asserted." But (G) is "\(\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\)", and Attempt is "the relation that \(c\) is actually used, **on an active route**, to address \(p\) — where \(p\) is a coherently posed question that \(s\) instantiates before \(e\), and for which the relevant difficulty is that **some obligation in \(O_p\) fails** at the state before \(e\)". The listed unsupplied constituents — the active routes, and a named failing obligation — are constituents of (G) itself. **Mark: held against it** (the document's own definitions of (G) and Attempt).

**F16. The same scenario grounds "one forward kind" twice, on two different footings, and is not a class member by its own statement.** Quotes: "then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind … with the continuity subnetworks of things" (fidelity route) versus "Under (K), with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap — and that is a claim from signature coincidence" (signature route); and "This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it." Against Part XIV: "**Membership.** … The creative-episode class: base interpretations with an instance of (G) connected to a critical episode", with the base class being "interpretations supplying these data with correct typing". **Mark: loose** (the scenario cannot carry a membership claim, and it uses both of F10's licit and illicit kind-comparisons in one passage).

### Other findings

**F17. The two lists of defeaters disagree.** Part 0's list is (A)–(F) — "**(F) The anchored grade.** Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors (Part V)…" — and Part XV's list contains eight items, of which two appear nowhere in Part 0 ("**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits**", "**A mathematical error**") and one of Part 0's (the (F) anchored-grade attack) appears nowhere in Part XV. Part 0's (A) also says of the classic candidates "each fail a named condition", while Part XV's first item needs one of them to "meet (E) alone". **Mark: loose** — a reader cannot tell which list is the load-bearing one.

**F18. The one-component candidate cannot satisfy (E), and three places say it does.** Quotes: Non-circular dependence's witness is "there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\), translated from some \((a,b)\in C\), that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way. Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing." For a candidate with one active commitment, \(\Gamma=\{k\}\), there is no nonempty proper sub-block, so the witness cannot exist and NonCircular fails. Yet: "The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3"; "The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored"; and Part XV's "A candidate meeting (E) alone and explaining nothing is already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\)". Two further frictions in the same clause: NonCircular requires \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\) (proper) while Part VI's removal is written "For nonempty \(B\subseteq W\)" — two different notions of removing a block; and Part 0's (A) says those candidates "each fail a named condition", agreeing with my reading against Part V's. **Mark: held against it** — this is the document contradicting itself on a candidate it uses twice as the reason the second grade exists.

**F19. \(C\) is called a set of admitted pairs but contains non-admitted ones, and this leaks into kind-identity.** Quotes: "The **contract** \(C\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over" — versus "\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit" and "\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\)." Two consequences the document does not note: "A change separates two components when their signatures differ at it" ranges over all of \(C\), so a pair in \(K\) — which is "not a possibility at all" — can separate two **forward** kinds, against Part 0's gloss "a *forward kind* is how a component responds to the changes that contract **admits**"; and Non-vacuity in Part V states the scope requirement without Part III's added burden, "with the reason for each exclusion recorded". **Mark: loose.**

**F20. The contract is given a provenance in Part XIV that Part III denies it.** Quotes: Part XIV's declared parameters include "the contract \(C\), in the form \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), with its provenance, the stated scope \(S\), and the counterfactual set \(K\) with meanings"; Part III: "\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\) attaches to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone, with the witness required by Part IV." **Mark: loose** (one word in Part XIV).

**F21. "the discrimination requirement of Part IV" is not in Part IV.** Quotes: Part III, "A **selected** question is the surviving member of a population of questions under a variation-and-survival history satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV"; Part 0, "or that construction reduces to selection under the discrimination requirement of Part IV". Part IV states non-triviality — "no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition" — and blindness — "A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation" — under neither name. **Mark: unknown** (the reference must be either renamed or defined).

**F22. The anchored grade's own summaries omit two of its four conditions.** Quotes, the four: "1. **Properness.** … 2. **Resolution.** … 3. **No global relation.** No active component's relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it… 4. **World-kind.** … Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX)… and not a backup that would have responded identically." The summaries: "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution"; and Part 0's "it is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive". Condition 3 is a definability exclusion scoped "on \(C\)" — not a fidelity condition on a larger contract; condition 4's second clause is a condition on histories. Since the one-component candidate is said to fail "conditions 1 and 3", condition 3 does work — so the summaries are the loose ones. **Mark: loose** (or F18's verdict is wrong instead; the two cannot both stand).

**F23. Derivation 2's hypothesis contains its conclusion, so (F2) does no work there.** Quotes: the definition — "**account-equivalent on \(C\)** when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide"; the claim — "If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\), have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and \(\Gamma\) corresponds to \(\Gamma'\), then they are account-equivalent on \(C\)." The anchored relations are fixed by \(\lambda\), \(D\) and \((a,b)\); "the same anchor map up to that bijection" is already two-thirds of the definition, and the profiles follow from (A). (F1) and (F2) are cited in the proof but not used. **Mark: idle** (the result stands with a shorter hypothesis; stating it that way would also remove the appearance of circularity).

**F24. Derivation 5's question-organization does not carry the whole question it claims to have as its solutions.** Quotes: "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\)"; against its own construction: "Ports: one port \(u_{(a,b)}\) for each \((a,b)\in A\times B\) of the target … one port \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) … one \(u_S\) … one \(u_K\) … Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\)"; and Part III's question is "\(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\)" with "coherently posed" requiring "\(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated". Nothing carries \(O_p\) or \(\rho_p\), so the solutions are coherent \((C,\mathcal Q)\)-pairs, not questions; and the closing line, "provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization", sits against Part III's \(\rho_p\) "attach[ing] to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone, with the witness required by Part IV". **Mark: loose** (the claim is broader than the construction).

## 6. The ten tests, run

- **Remove.** Cut Contract adequacy from (E): nothing in Derivation 2 or Part VI notices; what notices is Part XV's protective-contract attack and grievance 4's objectivity claim — so adequacy's job is *objectivity*, held by Part V's own sentence and weakened by F14. Cut (F2): Derivation 2 stands unchanged (F23), while Part VII's direction verdict ("\(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair") collapses — so (F2) is held by Part VII, idle in Derivation 2. Cut the baseline exclusion: Derivation 1's remark becomes exact (F11).
- **Swap.** Swap the letter \(D\)'s referent in (R) for the target's — (R) becomes unreadable (F1). Swap "five" for "six" — the list already does (F7). Swap adequacy's baseline comparison for "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\) at every \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)" — the document's own protective-contract attack disappears, which is how we know the writing is too weak (F14).
- **Flip the outcome.** Flip the historical index: does "E is an account on C" have to move when the module moves and \(C\) does not? Yes, by adequacy (F13).
- **Poke.** Two halves for adequacy: a change that should matter — add an admitted pair outside \(S\) where target and candidate both move wrongly; the document says nothing happens (F14's "should not" half fails). For (R): add a second instantiation with a faithful transport; Part IV's "stopped representing" must move although no history changed (F12).
- **Reverse.** Part IV runs expectation ← fidelity ← edit-pair; the direction is right (the cause is the world's edit, the effect the system's answer) — the failure is only the existential (F12), not the arrow.
- **Hunt the answer in the starting points.** The claim "(E) alone is too weak" is supported, in three places, by a candidate that fails (E)'s own NonCircular witness (F18): the conclusion is sitting in the starting points under the name "the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\)". Also "world-kind" is put in as a condition in Part V while Derivation 1's own remark derives it (F11).
- **Add a job.** Job: *the account grade must survive substitution of a declared transport for a selected one.* It does — "(E) carries **no provenance condition**" — and then Deploy/(R) restore the provenance requirement, consistently. Job: *kind-claims must be evaluable by admitted changes only.* It fails, because \(C\) may contain \(K\) (F19). Job: *the same claim must survive a module change at fixed \(C\).* It fails (F13).
- **Build the best rival.** A revision that (i) states kind-identity across organizations with a bijection of change sets, (ii) writes adequacy as \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p\) over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), (iii) defines \(\mathsf S_{E,p}\) with \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\), (iv) defines "explanatory jobs". Tell-apart change: the protective-contract poke. The document's own Part XV says the rival wins that poke; on every other change on my list the rival gives the same verdicts. So at this grain the document and the rival are not "the same explanation": they differ on one listed change, and the document concedes it.
- **Check the patches.** Four patches found: adequacy's baseline-restricted form (F14); "What keeps the two apart" for the selection/construction asymmetry (F2); "it is a condition, not a corollary" for the anchored grade (F11); and the F16/F17 hedges ("This is a scenario, not a consistency witness"; "None is asserted"; two different attack lists). Each patch is locally narrow and each is presented as a strengthening, not as a narrowing — none of them says out loud "the earlier, wider claim is withdrawn".
- **Look inside.** Two candidates can match on outputs and differ inside — the document says so itself ("Two systems with identical outputs and different internal routes are different organizations here"). The mirror case is here: three candidates match on *listed conditions* (Part V says the one-component candidate "satisfies (E) on every contract") while differing in workings, because the condition that would separate them (NonCircular's proper sub-block) is invisibly unsatisfiable at \(\lvert\Gamma\rvert=1\).

## 9. What would make it harder to vary

The two cuts that would do the most, each killing several findings at once:

1. **Say what a kind-claim compares, and where.** Either define \(\operatorname{sig}\) and "one forward kind" for a pair of (component, subnetwork) drawn from \(D\) and \(E\) with a bijection on change sets as well as footprints, or restate condition 4, Derivation 1's remark and Derivation 10's sentence as claims about \(E\) alone. This kills F10, F11 and half of F16, and it removes the need for the hedge that currently keeps condition 4 honest.
2. **Write adequacy at the width you claim for it** — \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\) at some \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) — or drop the three sentences that say it "catches" protective scopes. This kills F14, repairs F13's tension, and lets Part XV and Part 0 give one list.

One further cut, cheap and decisive: **write \(\Gamma\) out for the one-component candidate and produce its NonCircular witness, or withdraw "satisfies (E)"** (F18).

## 10. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not the same as true. This report does not show the theory false, nor any part of it uninteresting, nor that any rival theory is right. A tension can be repaired by one word in the right place — F12, F20 and F25(a) are that shape — and the document is unusual in naming several of its own weak points in advance (the protective-contract attack, the declared-admission limit for mathematics, the "not represented" of a new dimension of variation). Judging kinds, causes, or the constitutive conjecture is out of scope: nothing here tests whether \(\operatorname{Account}\) captures explanation. I also did not test the two borrowed primitives (the physical module, the normative relation), and I did not test the derivations' claims against the source theory's Part numbers, because I do not have that file.

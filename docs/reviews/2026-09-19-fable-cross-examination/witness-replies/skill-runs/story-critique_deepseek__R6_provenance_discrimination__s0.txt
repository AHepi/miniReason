## 1. Corrections first

Your inventory of the clause set is accurate, and I could check every item against the page. Two corrections, and both make your attack sharper.

**(a) Two of your seven items are not part of the predicate.** The witness is written `Sel(t;T,μ,H,Σ_h)` — five parameters. Causal responsibility ("the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it") and blindness ("A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation") are stated in the paragraph *around* the tuple. So anyone who writes `Sel(t;T,μ,H,Σ_h) holds` has asserted neither of the two clauses that do the anti-triviality work. That is the hinge of notes 1, 4 and 5.

**(b) The non-trivial pair is defined against the population, not against the survivor**: "containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)". Since survival already requires fidelity on all of H, any member failing H is eliminated by it — good. But the definition never ties *that* member to the member named by "a survival condition requiring fidelity on H that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\)". With \(|\mathcal T|\ge 3\) a witness satisfies every clause with the discriminating failure and the elimination falling on different members at different pairs. A witness need not show who was beaten by what.

Pass 3's choice test has no persons to run on here; its analogue is the modeller's classification, which the precedence rule makes a forced move, so I use flip-the-choice instead (note 8). Pass 5's medium check I run as a *notation* check: what a formal document can carry is what its tuples carry, and nothing else (notes 5 and 6).

## 2. Notes, by sequence

### Sequence A — the selection paragraph

**1. The clause that blocks trivial satisfaction is the one term the document does not reduce.** What is there: "with the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it." Its job is stated by the document itself: "Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation." Why it fails: the only causal test this semantics owns is contract-relative — "The difference is a difference in edit-response, and the semantics checks exactly that." — and the one history-level causal vocabulary it has is "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a *represented* input to an operative result", which runs through (R), which requires provenance. Using active-route talk inside Sel would break the order the document claims: "(R) depends on (F1)–(F2), \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and physical provenance; provenance depends on \(\Theta\) and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and \(\equiv_\ell\) but not on (R)". So "causally responsible" is either a further primitive — against "It claims that they are all listed" and "No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters" — or it breaks the dependence order. Better version, in the document's own vocabulary: say instead that the filtering occurrence has an admitted edit, in Part XII's sense of "the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta(o)\) — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits", under which \(t\) is not present. That keeps provenance a fact about \(\Theta\), needs no counterfactual set \(K\), and is exactly the move grievance 1 already makes for cause against correlation.

**2. The elimination clause names a cause that, removed, changes nothing about the survivor.** Run the skill's cause test on it: take a witness with a member eliminated, then remove that member's elimination from the history in your head. Would \(t\) be present anyway? Yes — \(t\) was always faithful on H, so the rival's death is not what put it there. The clause guarantees that *some other thing failed*, not that \(t\) was chosen. Its job ("not every transport counts as selected") survives, so it should be kept; but it is one of "two routes to one job" with clause 1, and the two routes are of very unequal cost. The cheap route is the one any writer will use, and it is the one that carries no test.

**3. The field-to-primitive example does not obviously meet the witness it is offered as.** What is there: "\(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport that is typically selected: the population is the variants of the system's tracking machinery, the history is the sensory changes encountered, and the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held." It uses the Party IV vocabulary exactly, so it reads as a witness. But "realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\)" plus "eliminated at least one member" asks for variants that existed and one that died — a lineage — while "the sensory changes encountered" is drawn from one system's life. Under a within-system reading the population is replacings, and the witness collapses to "the system once swapped a tracker", which is note 2. Swap test: the *example* is free — any example would do — and what is held is that some realized rival was eliminated, which is precisely what the sentence does not say. One clause naming where the population lives would earn the example.

**4. Trivially satisfiable? Yes at the tuple, and the paragraph's guards are the two undefined terms.** Answering your first question directly. Removing each clause:

| Remove this | What stops getting done | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| \(|\mathcal T|\ge2\) realized | no rival exists; "population" is decoration | held by "not every transport counts" |
| μ applied at least once | no history of change | held |
| non-empty H with a non-trivial pair | nothing to survive; the condition is vacuous | held; cheap (a single pair) |
| elimination of ≥1 member | everything counts as selected | held, by the document's own stated job |
| causal responsibility | the survivor's presence may be incidental | **held, unreduced (note 1)** |
| no event is a faithful transport to t, H, or the condition | a designer who already knows the answer counts as selection | held, prose only |
| blindness | a feedback process counts as selection | **held, route-relative, prose only (notes 5, 6)** |

The cheapest candidate that satisfies every clause in the tuple is: one population of two, one application of μ, one pair at which the rival fails and is discarded, and no causal work done by the discard — the "system once tried a variant and threw it away" witness. Nothing in the tuple blocks it. The paragraph blocks it, twice, with two terms it never defines. So: not trivially satisfiable *by the page*, and trivially satisfiable *by the predicate*, which is a gap only if someone cites the predicate.

### Sequence B — construction, declared, precedence

**5. Blindness is defined but is not a conjunct, and Part X states a different version of it.** Part IV's is route-relative and defect-relative, and it permits scalars. Part X states: "A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both." Two divergences from Part IV. (i) Route-relative against witness-absolute: an itemised defect record sitting off the route from defect to variation is blind under Part IV and banned by Part X's sentence. (ii) The second half — "no target instantiated before the variation" — is a *positive* conjunct of Con ("the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\)") and appears nowhere in Sel as its negation. The mirror fails too: "a construction witness contains both" is not built into Con either, nor into Build's witness list ("A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization" — no defect). So the separation rests on comparison prose, not on the two predicates. Better version: make blindness a conjunct of Sel and the target-negation with it, and put the itemised defect record into Build's witness list. Blindness is held by a real job — it is what Part XV names as "the discrimination requirement of Part IV" — so make it a conjunct rather than dropping it.

**6. The scalar-summary permission is idle, and it licenses the rewrite that attack (D) denies.** "a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted." Nothing else in the document uses it; no derivation cites it. Its cost is that a scalar can carry a defect's profile to any precision, so a process *fully informed about the defect* counts as blind — while Part X says containing the itemised defect representation is what construction is. A construction episode whose only feedback is a fidelity score therefore satisfies Sel and Con at once. That answers one half of your fourth question: yes, a construction witness can already be rewritten as a selection witness, without gerrymandering, and the document has blessed the licence. It does not collapse the labels, because precedence takes Con — which is why I rank it below notes 1 and 8. Fix: say what a scalar may summarise (an aggregate over pairs, with no pair-indexed component), or drop the permission, since nothing uses it.

**7. Precedence: whether a plainly selected transport can be pushed up is undecided by the page.** What is there: "it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance." That last sentence blocks the obvious route (a later episode reclassifying an earlier transport). The route that remains is that Con's second conjunct is nearly free when the target is ambient — the document's primary case has \(P\) and \(F\) as targets, instantiated by the system all along — and Con's first conjunct, "whose construction witness prepares \(t\)", has no identity condition. The witness names "the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization", not \(\pi,\tau,\sigma\). So a witness that fixes λ and E can be read as preparing t, absorbing the parts of t that no witness speaks for. Read strictly (the episode produces the occurrence t), there is no gerrymander. Read permissively (the episode produces an organization and bindings that t has), any transport rebuilt once in the system's life bears constructed however plainly its survival did the work, and creative attribution under Part 0 — "every creative attribution requires it" — gets cheaper. The page decides neither, so I will not pick one. Fix: give Con an identity clause — the controlled subhistory's end state is \(t\), or an occurrence \(\prec\)-between it and \(e\).

**8. The label has no formal consequence anywhere, so the gerrymander is harmless — and the direction that does have a consequence is the mirror one.** Swap test on the two provenances: at every use-site they are an ∨. (R): "whose provenance is selected or constructed"; (EK): "Deploy supplies the provenance condition (\(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\)) through (R)"; and (E) says outright, "(E) carries **no provenance condition**". The only condition that consults construction specifically is Origin's Build, which consults Build, not Con. So swap Sel and Con at every use-site and nothing downstream changes: precedence decides a label no condition reads. Flip-the-choice on the precedence rule gives the same answer. Where the three-way split bites is the third value. A transport produced by relay — "Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not" — has no nontrivial binding, so no Build, so no Con; no population and no elimination, so no Sel; so the rule sends it to declared, and "Declared provenance is a status in the model, not a physical history" plus "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything" mean a faithful, physically produced copy of a selected transport confers no representation. That is a false consequence, and it is the mirror image of the worry you brought. Fix: one clause — a transport produced by an \(\equiv_\ell\)-preserving transfer of a selected transport is selected, on that transport's history plus the transfer.

**9. The fairness test on the two witnesses.** Part IV presents them as equal in kind: "Two provenances, not one." Two provenances, yes; equal standards, no. Sel is an existence claim over a population (cheap, as notes 2 and 4 show); Con is an existence claim over an episode whose witness is defined against "the repertoire" — "it is nontrivial when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)" — so it is relative to the representing system. That sits against the document's own account of what a provenance is: "It is an asymmetry, and it is stated rather than hidden. A transport is an occurrence in a physical history: it came to hold by selection, construction, or declaration, and its provenance is a fact about events." A witness one of whose conditions is a fact about the system's repertoire is not a fact about events alone. This is the un-equal mirror: the side the document calls creative is the side whose conditions are the loosest, not the strictest.

### Sequence C — what works, and should not be touched

**10.** Three things are doing real work and a reviser must not break them. (i) The running examples: "the one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\)" is planted in Part V where the second grade is introduced and collected by name in Part XV ("already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) — and is why the second grade is stated"), carrying the same job both times. The reversed calculation is planted in Part V's exclusion list and collected in Part VII with the concrete failure ("\(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair"). (ii) The (F1)/(F2) split, "prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition" and "prevents a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint" — two named jobs, two distinct failure modes, and both later exhibited. (iii) "This is the only definition of selection; Part XII says what makes it a physical claim." The *intent* to have one definition is exactly right, and every fix I propose in notes 1, 5 and 6 is a way of honouring it, not of adding a second one.

## 4. How it fits together

The document's plot is fidelity under change; its theme is provenance and creativity; they meet in exactly one conjunct, at (R). Part V drops provenance deliberately and with a reason — "a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history" — and that is the right call. But the consequence is that everything Part 0 spends its rhetoric on ("Creativity lives in construction", three provenances, the blindness distinction) carries one ∨ at (R) and nothing else, while the actual separator between blind and creative work is elsewhere: Build's ownership and nontrivial binding, and the obligation-change clause of (EK), "every change of value of an obligation in \(O\) between them lies on an active route from occurrences of \(\Delta\)". So the document's stated separator — the histories — is not the operator that separates the two. That is the fair-mirror failure, and it explains why all five of your questions land on Part IV: it is where the load is claimed and where the load is lightest. Part IV also delivers more than three new rules at once — occurrences, Inst, three layers, transports, three provenances, blindness, the scalar permission, (R), surprise — which is where a reader stops being able to check, and the two undefined guards sit in exactly that paragraph.

## 5. The faults that matter most, ranked

1. **The causal-responsibility clause (note 1).** It is the only clause that blocks trivial satisfaction (note 4), it is the second thing "causally responsible" is claimed to be while not being one of the listed primitives, and the fix is already in the document's repertoire.
2. **The provenance label with no consequence, and the relay case that has one (notes 7, 8).** Your gerrymander exists only on one reading of "prepares \(t\)", which the page does not decide; and in either case nothing downstream reads the label. The harmful direction is the mirror: a faithful copy lands on declared and therefore represents nothing.
3. **Blindness outside the predicate, with the scalar permission loosening it (notes 5, 6).** Two versions of the same condition in Part IV and Part X, neither a conjunct, and a permission that admits the rewriting Part XV says would collapse the provenances.

Notes 2, 3 and 9 are smaller: an untested elimination clause, an example that does not earn its witness, and a witness whose conditions are partly about the system rather than the history.

## 6. What I am unsure of

Whether "causally responsible" was meant to be read as active-route talk — if so, the dependence order in Part XIV needs a stated carve-out, and I did not find one. Whether "prepares \(t\)" was meant strictly. Whether Part X's sentence is meant to replace Part IV's blindness definition or to describe it. Whether relay is meant to be absorbed into maintenance — the page does not say. Whether the scalar summary is meant to be pair-indexed — the parenthetical "how many failures, how badly" suggests not, but the text does not settle it. And one gap in my own view: no actual selection witness is written out anywhere in the document, so I cannot tell how the author reads "realized transports" in the field-to-primitive case. All of the above is judged from the page alone; I have not seen any background file, implementation, or proof.

## 7. One next step

Rewrite the selection witness as a single predicate that carries all of its own clauses — blindness as a conjunct with the scalar permission made pair-indexed, the target-negation with it, causal responsibility stated as an admitted edit in \(A_\Theta\), and an identity clause in Con — and then run the field-to-primitive example through the rewritten predicate to see whether it still yields a witness.

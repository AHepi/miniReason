### Direct answers to the four questions

**Q1 — does excluding the baseline pairs lose real distinctions?** Partly, and not in the way the document's reason says.

- **Constants are not lost (F2).** A constant is a component whose relation is fixed across \((a,b)\). Two constants with different values differ as *relations* at *every* pair in \(C\), baseline or not, so they are separated even with the exclusion. Removal test: remove the exclusion and constants are still separated. The exclusion does no work for constants.
- **Things that differ only in state are merged in *kind*, by design, and are not lost from the theory (F3).** The document says so: "two things at different positions are one kind in different states." What is easy to miss is that the baseline is still in the contract (Part III: "it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)") and fidelity ranges over all of \(C\) (F1: "for every \((a,b)\in C\)"). So a genuine state difference reappears as an **F1 failure at a pair that is in \(C\) but excluded from the kind comparison**. This matters for Q4 below: a "which state" label's discriminating work is a *fidelity* fact, not a *kind* fact, so it is not a counterexample to the conjecture.
- **The exclusion is broader than its reason (F1).** The excluded set is \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\) — the entire identity-edit slice. The stated reason ("the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its state") covers \((1,b_0)\). The second sentence ("the identity edit separates nothing") is, as written, false for \(b\neq b_0\): \(b\) is a coordinate of the pair, so varying the boundary at the identity edit is a change to the pair, and \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\) and \(L_j(a,b)\) can both respond to it. So the exclusion silently removes boundary/input response at zero edit as well as state — a distinction the document's own sentence ("a change separates two components when their signatures differ at it") would otherwise keep.

**Q2 — is a historical kind well-defined without component provenance witnesses? No (F5, F6, F7).** The historical kind names "the witness that Part IV requires for provenance," but Part IV's witnesses are \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\cdot)\) and \(\operatorname{Con}(t;\cdot)\) over **transports** — "a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports," "an episode whose construction witness prepares \(t\)." No relation "the history that produced a component" is defined anywhere, and a component is, in the document's own words, "a slot in an organization," not an occurrence. Part XIV nevertheless lists historical kinds as "Derived … from provenance witnesses." So the historical kind borrows a witness defined for a different sort of thing, and "descended from, copied from" have no witness at all. By the document's own label test ("name a change to the world under which this label and a different one would come apart," Part V), a historical kind has **no contract-court poke at all**: its only poke would be a change to \(h\), which is a different court (Part IX). It is not yet shown to be more than a label.

**Q3 — does the active-route condition smuggle history into fidelity? Not into *fidelity*; yes into *anchoring*, and the document's own summary language hides that (F9, F10, F11).**

- The clause lives on the anchor map \(\lambda\), which is transport data ("\(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\)", Part IV), and transports are individuated by history — so Grievance 12's denial, "a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract," is **not** contradicted by condition 4(ii). F1/F2 remain signature-only.
- But condition 4(ii) is *bundled under a condition named "World-kind,"* which is a signature idea. Two anchors that agree on every signature on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) — i.e., that no change separates — *are* separated by the anchored grade, on a history ground: "A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route… it is what the anchored grade's fourth condition uses" (Part IX). That is the "same at this level" phenomenon the document confines to transports (Derivation 2). So the accurate statement is: **history is excluded from fidelity, not from anchoring.**
- The glosses omit it. Part 0's definition of an anchored account lists only "a proper decomposition of the target at the resolution the question needs, and… components… of one forward kind with their anchors under every change the physical module admits" — no active-route clause. Part 0's "a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive" and Part V's "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution" are likewise not equivalent to the four conditions once 4(ii) is added, because 4(ii) is a condition on *which* subnetwork is the anchor, not on the fidelity relation.
- Worse, 4(ii) is **not provenance-free**: it requires the anchor to join "a represented input" (Part IX), and "represents" is (R), which requires \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\). So the anchored grade does carry a provenance condition, while Part V's inventory says "Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK)," and Part XIV's dependence order lists anchoring as depending on histories but not on (R). Either "represented input" means (R) — and both lists are incomplete — or it is used loosely, and 4(ii) is less definite than the rest of the semantics.
- 4(ii) is also conditional: "Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\)." Where no history is supplied, two accounts that agree on every signature are *not* separated by the anchored grade at all. That is a scope drawn openly, but it means the anchored grade's discriminating power depends on the module.

**Q4 — is the conjecture stated so that it could be refuted? In shape, yes; as written, not cleanly (F12, F13, F14).** The refutation names the case-type, the two courts (\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) changes; histories), and the further condition ("does explanatory work"), and the document explicitly calls it a conjecture and says Derivation 1 does not protect it. That is the right shape. But three defects:

- **The second conjunct is under-defined.** "No history distinguishes" inherits the Q2 gap: there is no defined notion of the history that produced a component. A would-be refuter can establish "no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes" but not "no history distinguishes" as cleanly. State it as *identity of producing histories*, or over a defined class of history-predicates.
- **The two statements differ in scope.** Part 0(C) says "no admitted change under the **physically** admitted contract," Part XV says "no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)." But Part III says \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is, "for a mathematical or modelled organization, a **declared admission relation** stated with the question." So it is ambiguous whether a mathematical discriminating kind-label refutes the conjecture — and Part VII's own example is a mathematical organization under a declared admission relation.
- **"Kind-label" is not defined formally** (the document uses "a declared kind-label" in Part 0(C) without a definition), so the refutation is an argued case, not a formal counterexample — acceptable for a conjecture, but it should be said. And "no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes" is only as definite as the module, which Part XIV lists as a per-use primitive.

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip the outcome.** Take each attacked part and suppose the opposite. No baseline exclusion: every state difference becomes a kind difference, and "cause/rule/measurement" kinds are swamped — the document's claim "two things at different positions are one kind in different states" is false. No historical kind: Grievance 12 and Derivation 2's remedy ("or a history, which the semantics represents as a historical kind") both fail. No active-route clause: the identically-responding backup is not excluded. So all three parts are **load-bearing**; the document has committed to definite outcomes and is not covering both.

**Direction.** Is the document running backwards from the result? For the forward kind, no: it is a *definition* ("What a piece of an explanation *is* — a cause, a rule, a measurement, a thing — is not asked of a label", Part 0), and Grievance 2 states the commitment. For the conjecture, no: it is declared a conjecture and Derivation 1 disclaims it. So the direction is honest. The one place where a result is *described* as a condition and is not one is condition 4(ii) (F9).

**Answer hidden in the starting points.** The forward kind's conclusion ("a thing is its signature") is not hidden — it is the definition (K) over the declared parameter \(L\). That is asserted, not circular. The historical kind's content is not hidden either — it is **absent**: no component-provenance relation exists in the starting points. That is a gap, not a circle. This is the document's usual practice (Part XV lists unhandled cases; Part X has a "What is not represented" section) — but here the gap is not listed among the stated limits (F15).

**The change list (contract) leaves out.** The excluded pairs are \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\) from the kind comparison, and the whole history dimension from the contract court. The first exclusion is stated; the second is stated as a *different court* rather than as an exclusion. What is left out silently is the notion of a **component-provenance event**, which the historical kind claims to represent. That exclusion is not stated as an open limit anywhere.

**Rivals.** Two rivals are live. R1: "kinds are world-kinds independent of any contract" — this is the document's own attack (C), and the two are distinguished only by the refutation case, which the document says it cannot yet run. R2: "the anchored grade is fidelity + properness + resolution; the active-route clause belongs to the transport's provenance, not to condition 4." The two are told apart by the identically-responding backup: keep the clause and the backup is excluded; drop it and the backup is anchored. So the clause is held by a real case, but R2 wins on bookkeeping — the clause should be filed as a provenance/history condition on the anchor map.

**Where the parts came from.**
- Forward kind (K): **asserted** (a definition over the declared parameter \((V,X,J,B,A,L)\)). It commits to unmet changes, so it behaves like a built part, but nothing was worked out and criticised to place it.
- Baseline exclusion, core: **asserted** with a reason — a reason that could be wrong, and part of which is wrong as stated (P2).
- Baseline exclusion, extent: **loose** — not fitted to cases, not built, not forced by the reason given.
- Historical kind: **asserted** and **borrowed** (from Part IV's transport provenance). Not fitted; not built.
- Active-route clause: **built** on the module's histories (it commits to the backup case) and **borrowed** from Part IX and, through "represented input," from (R).
- Analysis-of-kinds conjecture: **asserted**, explicitly and honourably.
- Derivation 1: **built** (proved), and honest about its reach.

**Patches.** The revision's patches are the baseline exclusion, the historical kind, the analysis-of-kinds conjecture, Grievance 12, and the active-route clause. The exclusion, the conjecture, and the active-route clause each do a stated job and each has a poke. The historical kind is the exception: it is the patch added to absorb the "kinds obviously exist" objection (Grievance 11) and the underdetermination of Derivation 2, it has **no defined witness** and **no contract-court poke**, and the gap is not recorded among the document's stated limits (F15).

**Look inside.** The forward kind has a full internal construction ((K) plus the equivalence relation), and the anchored grade's first three conditions do too. The active route has a definition (Part IX) and a condition (Part V). The historical kind has **no internal construction** — a name, a list of provenance types, and a reference to a witness defined elsewhere for a different sort of thing. Matching outputs is not matching workings, and the historical kind currently offers no workings to inspect.

## 5. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Define the component-provenance relation, or drop the historical kind to a one-line consequence of the occurrence's history and say how coarse that makes it** (F5, F6). This is the single change that most tightens both Q2 and Q4: it gives the historical kind a poke (two components of one forward kind on every admitted change, differing in producing history) and it makes "no history distinguishes" a checkable negative ("the two producing histories are the same").
2. **Split condition 4's two clauses and file the second as a provenance/history condition on the anchor map** (F9, F10). Then the glosses in Part 0 ("a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive"; the anchored-account definition) and Part V ("Anchoring is therefore component fidelity…") become true, and the provenance inventory becomes complete.
3. **Rewrite the exclusion as \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\), or say plainly that the whole identity-edit slice is excluded because at the identity edit nothing is edited while \(b\) may vary, and check that nothing in Parts V–VI changes** (F1). Either the extent is a slip or it is a commitment; the document currently gives the reason for the narrower version.

## 6. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not the same as true. My marks say which parts are held by a job, which are loose, and which are idle; they do not say the forward-kind analysis of cause, rule and measurement is correct, nor that the anchored grade is adequate. Two further cautions from the document itself apply here and I am passing them on, not overruling them: Part XV says "Derivation 1 does not protect the conjecture; it only shows fidelity preserves signatures," and Part 0 says the constitutive conjecture "is a conjecture." A loose historical kind may still be the right idea; what is missing is what would hold it in place. And the failure of an argument for the conjecture is not the failure of the conjecture.

## 7. One next step

Take two components of one forward kind on **every** admitted change (so no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), baseline included, separates them) that were produced by the **same** occurrence, and ask whether any label separates them that does explanatory work. If one does, the analysis-of-kinds conjecture is refuted by a case with no under-defined clause; if none does, state the component-provenance witness explicitly (a population of component-realizations, an applied variation operator, an eliminating survival condition, a causal-responsibility clause) and note that a component's historical kind is then as coarse as the occurrence that realizes it. Either outcome settles both Q2 and Q4 with one test.

## Numbered findings

**F1.** The baseline-pair exclusion is the whole slice \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\), not the single baseline; the document's reason "the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**" covers \((1,b_0)\) only, and its second sentence "the identity edit separates nothing" is inaccurate as written, because \(b\) is a coordinate of the pair and the answer profile can respond to it. Net: boundary/input response at zero edit is removed from the kind signature alongside state. Mark: core held, extent loose.

**F2.** Constants are not lost. Two components whose fixed relations differ differ at every pair in \(C\), so they are separated with or without the exclusion.

**F3.** "Things that differ only in state" are merged in *kind* by design, and are not lost from the theory: the baseline is in \(C\) ("it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)") and F1 ranges over all of \(C\), so a real state difference shows up as a fidelity failure at a pair excluded from the kind comparison. Consequence for Q4: a "which state" label's work is fidelity, not kind, so the state case does **not** refute the conjecture.

**F4.** The exclusion's core is held by a real job — without it every state difference would be a kind difference and the kind notion would be swamped.

**F5.** The historical kind is loose: the witness it names ("the witness that Part IV requires for provenance") is defined only for transports; no relation "the history that produced a component" is defined.

**F6.** The historical kind is borrowed: Part XIV says it is "Derived … from provenance witnesses," and the witnesses in hand are Part IV's, for "a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports," while a component is "a slot in an organization."

**F7.** "Descended from, copied from" are idle: named, no witness, no condition uses them.

**F8.** The historical kind has no contract-court poke; its poke is a history-change, a different court. By the document's own label test it is at risk of being a label until a component-provenance witness is defined. Derivation 2's remedy ("or a history, which the semantics represents as a historical kind") inherits the gap.

**F9.** The active-route clause does **not** put history into fidelity — it lives on the anchor map \(\lambda\), which is transport data — but it is mis-filed under "World-kind," and the document's glosses of anchoring (Part 0's anchored-account definition; Part 0's "a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive"; Part V's "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution") are not equivalent to the four numbered conditions once clause 4(ii) is included.

**F10.** The active-route clause is not provenance-free: it requires the anchor to join "a represented input" (Part IX), and "represents" is (R), which requires \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\). So Part V's inventory ("Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK)") should include the anchored grade, and Part XIV's dependence order should list (R) for anchoring — or "represented" should be tightened.

**F11.** Clause 4(ii) is conditional ("Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\)"), so where no history is supplied, two accounts agreeing on every signature are not separated by the anchored grade at all. Openly scoped, but it means the anchored grade's discriminating power depends on the module.

**F12.** The two statements of the analysis-of-kinds refutation differ in scope: Part 0(C) says "no admitted change under the physically admitted contract," Part XV says "no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)" — and Part III makes \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) declared, not physical, for mathematical or modelled organizations. So it is ambiguous whether a mathematical discriminating kind-label refutes.

**F13.** The refutation's second conjunct ("no history distinguishes") inherits F5's gap. State it as identity of producing histories, or over a defined class of history-predicates.

**F14.** The conjecture itself is asserted, not built — declared a conjecture, with Derivation 1 explicitly not protecting it, and I found no circular use (condition 4 relies on signatures on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), not on the conjecture). In shape it is stated so as to be refuted, subject to F12–F13.

**F15.** The document's practice elsewhere is to list unhandled cases (Part XV; Part X, "What is not represented"). The absence of a component-provenance witness — which makes the second court of the conjecture under-defined — is **not** listed among the stated limits.

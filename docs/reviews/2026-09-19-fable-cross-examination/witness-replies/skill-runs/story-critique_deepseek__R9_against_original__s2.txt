# New defects 20 introduces, and surviving defects of 10

**Note 48 — New defect: \(K\) creates a type error at the core of (O) and (E).**  
20 Part III: “\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\) … \(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit”.  
20 Part III: “Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\)”.  
20 Part II: “For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies \(L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\).”  
If \(a\notin A\), then \(L_j(a,b)\) is not defined. Yet (F1), (F2) and (A) are required to hold for all \((a,b)\in C\), including \(K\). This is new in 20; 10 had: “The **contract** \(C\subseteq A\times B\)”. Severity: high. Better version: either extend \(L\) to \(K\) by declared counterfactual relations, or restrict fidelity to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and treat \(K\) in a separate counterfactual layer.

**Note 49 — New defect: non-circular dependence fails for singleton \(\Gamma\).**  
20 Part V: “there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\), translated from some \((a,b)\in C\), that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions”.  
If \(\Gamma=\{d\}\), there is no nonempty proper sub-block. So no single-commitment account can satisfy non-circular dependence. 10 allowed removing the whole \(\Gamma\): “removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\)”. The 20 fix overcorrects. Severity: medium-high. Better version: allow replacing a singleton with a different relation, or require the proper sub-block condition only when \(\Gamma\) has more than one element.

**Note 50 — New defect: contract adequacy may be undecidable or outside the question.**  
20 Part 0: “The first grade is what can be assessed within a question.”  
20 Part V: “**Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.”  
This requires quantifying over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), which may not be fully known. 20a itself admits: “Contract adequacy became a conjunct of (E) rather than a separate objectivity claim; this makes (E) stronger and is the most attackable new move”. Severity: high. Better version: make adequacy a separate objectivity claim, or state that \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) must be fully specified for account-hood.

**Note 51 — New defect: historical kinds use undefined predicates.**  
20 Part II: “A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance.”  
Part IV defines selected, constructed, declared. It does not define “descended from” or “copied from”. 20 Derivation 6: “No residual predicate is left undefined.” Contradiction. Severity: medium. Better version: define descent and copying with witnesses, or remove them from historical kinds.

**Note 52 — New defect: Derivation 5’s question-organization may not exactly capture coherently posed questions.**  
20 Derivation 5: “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).”  
20 Part III: “It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other’s complements as stated.”  
The construction has ports for \(\mathcal Q\), \(S\), \(K\), but no component ensures \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), nor that \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from complements. Severity: medium. Better version: add components enforcing those conditions, or weaken the claim to “the solutions include the coherently posed questions”.

**Note 53 — New defect: observation edit definition may not capture “without altering what it reports”.**  
20 Part II: “an **observation edit**: an edit that alters the relation reporting the port without altering what it reports, that is, replaces the component whose footprint is \(\{v\}\cup V'\) for a reporting set \(V'\) while leaving every component not touching \(v\) unchanged.”  
The formal condition does not state that the value of \(v\) is unchanged. Severity: medium. Better version: add a condition that the projection of solutions onto \(v\) is unchanged by the edit.

**Note 54 — Surviving defect of 10: Grievance 1 not fully aligned with formal causal signature.**  
10 Part II: “a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits”.  
20 Part II keeps this.  
20 Grievance 1: “Intervening on the input port of a causal assignment replaces the component that assigns that port and leaves the causal component’s own relation in force”.  
The formal definition does not state invariance under input intervention, which is the distinguishing feature from correlation. So the defect of 10 survives under new wording. 20a says Done. Severity: medium. Better version: add “invariant under intervention on its input port” to the formal signature.

**Note 55 — New defect: loss of consistency witness.**  
10 Derivation 10: “This episode is a relative-consistency witness for the class.”  
20 Derivation 10: “This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it.”  
20a admits: “Derivation 10 was downgraded from a consistency witness to a scenario, and the document no longer claims any relative-consistency witness for the class. That is a loss the list did not price.”  
Severity: medium. Better version: either restore a checked witness or state clearly that the theory has no consistency witness.

**Note 56 — New defect: merit function for questions contradicts 10’s disclaimer.**  
10 Part 0: “It does not supply an objective aesthetics, a probability of truth, a merit function, or a ranking of thinkers.”  
20 Part 0: “It does not supply an objective aesthetics, a probability of truth, a ranking of thinkers, or — beyond one condition under (P) — a merit function for questions.”  
The exception is a new merit function. Severity: medium. Better version: if the merit condition is kept, update the disclaimer to say it supplies exactly one merit condition, not a general merit function; or drop the condition.

**Note 57 — Surviving defect of 10: the relabeling clause is not fully integrated.**  
10 Part V: “A contract consisting only of relabelings, or excluding every change under which the active commitments could matter to \(\mathcal Q\), does not satisfy non-circular dependence and is therefore not a contract on which an account can be claimed.”  
20 Part V non-vacuity no longer contains this; it is in “What (E) excludes”: “An account whose only substantive contrast is one that no admitted edit realizes and that is not declared in \(K\) with a stated meaning fails non-vacuity.”  
The new version only covers “no admitted edit realizes”; it does not cover “contract consisting only of relabelings” as a separate case. Severity: low. Better version: add “or consists only of relabelings” back.

**Note 58 — New defect: Part V anchored grade condition 4 mixes history and fidelity.**  
20 Part V: “4. **World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor … Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.”  
This makes anchoring depend on actual history, not just forward kind. 20a admits: “this is a history condition inside an adequacy grade, and it is the place where the transport/component asymmetry of Grievance 12 is least clean.” Severity: medium. Better version: separate the actuality condition from world-kind, or restrict anchoring to forward kind and put actuality in a separate grade.

# Pass 2 — Sequences and the chain of questions

**Note 59 — Chain of questions.**  
The theory’s opening question is Part 0: “A candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too.” The closing question is Part XV: “None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label.” The chain runs: Part 0 sets the conjecture; Parts I–IV build; Part V defines Account; Parts VI–VII apply; Parts VIII–XIII build out; Part XIV collects; Part XV lists defeats; Part XVI derivations. The chain mostly holds. The main break is the handoff from Part III’s \(K\) to Part V’s fidelity conditions: 20 Part III says “Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\)”, but 20 Part II only defines \(L_j(a,b)\) for edits in \(A\). That break is the K type error in Note 48. A second break: Part 0 promises question-finding is represented, while Part X says “What it does not represent is the invention of a new dimension of variation”. That is a qualified promise, not an uncollected plant, but it changes what the opening promised.

**Note 60 — Planted and never collected.**  
20 Part II plants historical kinds with “descended from, copied from”. No later definition collects them. 20 Part III plants \(K\) with “each with a stated meaning”. 20 Part V collects \(K\) in non-vacuity: “not declared in \(K\) with a stated meaning”. 20 Part VII collects \(K\) for eliminative explanation. 20 Part XV collects \(K\) for impossibility eliminations. So \(K\) is collected, but the collection fails because of the type error. Historical kinds are planted and not collected. Severity: medium.

**Note 61 — Cause versus label.**  
The main turn in 20 is the addition of the anchored grade. The shown cause is the one-component candidate: 20 Part V: “The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3”. Remove that cause: would the anchored grade still be needed? Yes, because of the decoy: 20 Part V: “a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4.” So the turn is caused by real cases, not a label.

# Pass 3 — People and choices

**Note 62 — The choice test does not transfer to characters; it transfers to design choices.**  
The document has no persons, so the choice test on characters does not transfer. One design choice that does transfer: whether to make contract adequacy a conjunct of (E) or a separate objectivity claim. 20a says: “Contract adequacy became a conjunct of (E) rather than a separate objectivity claim; this makes (E) stronger and is the most attackable new move”. The two options pull almost equally: conjunct makes (E) stronger; separate keeps (E) assessable within a question, as 20 Part 0 says: “The first grade is what can be assessed within a question.” The choice is held by the job “catch protective contracts”. But it may overcorrect. See Note 50.

**Note 63 — Flip the choice.**  
Had 20 kept adequacy separate, would we fault that? Yes, 10’s defect was that non-vacuity did not catch protective contracts. So the choice is not idle. But the chosen option introduces a new defect. The best version is a third option: state that adequacy is a separate objectivity claim, and that (E) alone is the within-question grade.

# Pass 4 — Theme as a pattern

**Note 64 — Central problem question.**  
The theory’s central problem, put as a question about how to act, is: “What may count as an explanation, and what must be shown to defeat one?” The pattern count: Part II answers with organizations and changes; Part III with questions and contracts; Part IV with transports and provenance; Part V with account and anchoring; Part X with understanding and construction; Part XI with knowledge and repair; Part XV with defeats. The answers are different, as required. The fair mirror test: when 20 treats 10’s defects as repaired, are they? For forward kinds, yes: 20 Part 0: “a forward kind is how a component responds to the changes that contract admits”. For historical kinds, no: 20 Part II introduces “descended from, copied from” without a witness. So the mirror is unequal: it fixed the overclaim about forward kinds but introduced a less rigorous historical kind. That is the fair mirror failure.

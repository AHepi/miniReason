## 2. Notes, grouped by ledger section

**G1–G2 — primitives, parameters, contracts.** These are the strongest repairs in the document, and each is held in place by several jobs at once. The replacement of 10's function-like \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) by a relation is the clearest: 10's (R) read "\(\exists t[\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t:\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\to c)\land\ldots]\)" and 10's Part IV conceded "The one thing (R) takes from outside is \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\)"; 20's version is "\(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\,\exists t[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\)" with "It is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain." **Remove it** and the uniqueness assumption returns unmarked; **swap it** back to a function and Part IV's "nothing here assumes uniqueness" becomes false. Held by two jobs. Protect it.

The contract form \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\) is the other load-bearing repair, and it is what makes the new adequacy conjunct statable at all. Note the ledger's own admission here is accurate and understated: the row says "as a conjunct of (E)", and 20 indeed writes "\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff\text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}\)", while the prose above it says "the following **five** conditions hold". Five headings, six conjuncts — the count is by headings, consistent with 10's "four conditions", so this is a reader trap, not a fault. The ledger row's own wording ("Five conjuncts") is the only imprecise cell I found.

**G3 — provenance and the cycle.** This is where the largest real defect of 10 was killed. 10's **Constructed** read "in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a **represented** target", while 10's (R) required \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\) — a cycle. 20 breaks it: "the organization \(t\) targets is **instantiated** by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below", and Derivation 6 confirms "the only cycle that could arise … is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness." I ran the dependence order in Part XIV as the chain of questions and it now closes. This repair is held by three jobs (stratification, Derivation 6's proof, Part XIV's well-foundedness claim) and should not be touched.

**1. The anchored grade imports (R) through the back door.** This is the sharpest new fault, and it sits in the document's central new construction. Part V's condition 4 says:

> "Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically."

But Part IX defines the term as:

> "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a **represented** input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts."

"Represented" is (R), and (R) is where \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\) enters. So the anchored grade requires provenance. That contradicts three places: Part 0's "it is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive"; Part V's "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label"; and Part V's "(E) carries **no provenance condition** … Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK)". It also leaves Part XIV's dependence order incomplete: "anchoring depends on (E), (K) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and histories" — no mention of (R). There are two readings, and the page does not decide between them: either only actuality is meant, in which case the term is misnamed and should read "an actual subnetwork"; or the full Part IX definition is meant, in which case anchoring is not a fidelity condition and the three quotations above are wrong. *Fix:* say "an actual route" and define actuality in Part IX as a subnetwork of occurrences in \(h\) under \(\prec\), dropping the represented-input clause for this use; or state plainly that the anchored grade requires the target's inputs to be represented and add (R) to its dependence line.

**G4–G5 — kinds, anchoring, account conditions.** The withdrawal of 10's strongest claim is genuine and well done. 10's Part 0 asserted "a *kind* is nothing over and above how a component responds to the changes that level admits"; 20's Part 0 offers "one forward kind there" plus "a second kind of kind", and 10's Derivation 1 corollary "The word 'kind' is therefore eliminable … and its elimination loses no case" is replaced by Derivation 1's "The claim that labels are eliminable in favour of signatures on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and histories is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV." The fair-mirror test passes here: 10 treated (F1) and a kind-condition as equivalent and called the second redundant; 20 makes the second a *condition* and opens an attack point on it. That is the unquestioned first case being questioned, and it is the document's largest structural gain.

**2. Grievance 1 still argues in values while (K) tracks relations.** This survives from 10 in new wording. 20's Grievance 1:

> "Intervening on the input port of a causal assignment replaces the component that assigns that port and leaves the causal component's own relation in force; the output moves through it. Intervening on the same port breaks a correlational component, because a correlation is a constraint on how two ports happen to co-vary."

"The output moves through it" and "breaks" are claims about solution sets and satisfiability. But the formal criterion is (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b))\}\), a sequence of *relations*, and Part II says the causal/correlational distinction is a "description of patterns in (K)". Under an intervention on the input port, the causal component's relation \(L_j\) is unchanged (the component is not replaced) and the correlational component's relation is also unchanged (it does not assign the port, so it is not replaced either). At the level of (K) both are invariant at that edit; the difference appears in \(\operatorname{Sol}\), not in \(L_j\). The grievance asserts the bridge — "The difference is a difference in edit-response, and the semantics checks exactly that" — without exhibiting it. *Fix:* either re-run the argument at the subnetwork signature of Part II (which is built from \(\operatorname{proj}\operatorname{Sol}\), and where the two cases do separate), or state that the discriminating edit is the intervention on the *output* port, where the causal component is replaced and the correlational one is not.

**3. The reversed-calculation parenthetical is ambiguous, and one reading is false.** New in 20; 10's version was terse and correct ("intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\)" with no parenthetical). 20 adds:

> "(here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair)"

The reversed calculation \(H=L\tan\theta\) *is* a component assigning \(H\); that is what makes it reversed. If "which" picks up "component", the clause is false. If it picks up "edit" — the reversed organization admits no edit that replaces its \(H\)-assigning component — it is true and is the point. "Has none of" reads more naturally with a component, and this is a load-bearing example (it is what condition 4's exclusion is illustrated with). *Fix:* "the reversed organization admits no edit that sets \(H\), \(H\) being its output, so \(\tau\) is undefined on that intervention and (F2) fails."

**4. Part III's coherence clause is garbled.** New in 20:

> "It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated."

Read literally, "disjoint from each other's complements" is \(S\cap K^{c}=\varnothing\) and \(K\cap S^{c}=\varnothing\), i.e. \(S=K\) — the opposite of the intended \(S\cap K=\varnothing\), which follows anyway from \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and \(K\) holding pairs the module does not admit. *Fix:* "and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint, as stated."

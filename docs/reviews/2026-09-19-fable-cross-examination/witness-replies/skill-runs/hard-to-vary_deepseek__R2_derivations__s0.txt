## 3. Part-by-part findings

### Derivation 1 — Kind preservation under fidelity

**Finding 1 — Held.** The claim is valid as written only with the injectivity hypothesis. The claim says:

> “Let \(t\) satisfy (F1) on \(C\), and let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\). Then every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.”

The proof uses exactly that condition:

> “injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates.”

Remove injectivity, and two different pairs in \(C\) can map to the same translated pair; then the signature sets can fail to correspond. The hypothesis is doing real work.

**Finding 2 — Loose.** The proof assumes a port-identification not stated in the cited definitions:

> “the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\).”

Part II says a subnetwork’s visible ports are “a declared subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) of its ports”. Part IV says \(\lambda\) assigns a subnetwork “with a port translation”. The proof treats the anchor’s visible ports as exactly the image of \(V_k\). That is plausible, but it is an extra reading of \(\lambda\), not a quoted condition. What would settle it: a stated condition that the port translation of \(\lambda(k)\) identifies \(V_k\) with \(V_{\lambda(k)}^{\mathrm{vis}}\).

**Finding 3 — Over-cited elsewhere.** Part V cites Derivation 1 without its injectivity hypothesis:

> “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract; whether that is enough to say what the component *is* is the question the anchored grade below answers.”

Derivation 1 needed injectivity on \(C\). Part V states the entailment from (F1) alone. That is stronger than what was proved.

**Finding 4 — Over-cited elsewhere.** Derivation 10 also cites Derivation 1 without injectivity:

> “If (F1) and (F2) hold for \(t_1\) on the extended contract — which would have to be checked by writing out \(P\), \(S_1\) and the relations, and is not checked here — then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things…”

The scenario does not state that \((\tau,\sigma)\) is injective on the contract. So the citation again omits a hypothesis of Derivation 1.

### Derivation 2 — Account-equivalence

**Finding 5 — Loose.** The definition is under-specified:

> “Two candidates \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) and \(\mathcal E'=(E',p,t',\Gamma')\) for the same \(p\) are **account-equivalent on \(C\)** when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide.”

Problems: (i) a bijection between active components is not yet a bijection between their ports/footprints, so “the anchored relations … coincide” is not defined across different port domains; (ii) “the pulled-back answer profiles” does not say what the pullback is along. A port bijection and a pullback map are needed. What would settle it: define account-equivalence with a port/footprint bijection and an explicit pullback of \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) and \(\operatorname{Ans}_{E'}(\tau'(a),\sigma'(b))\) to \(C\).

**Finding 6 — Idle.** The claim assumes (F2):

> “If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\), have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and \(\Gamma\) corresponds to \(\Gamma'\), then they are account-equivalent on \(C\).”

But the proof never uses (F2):

> “(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles.”

So (F2) is idle for this particular claim.

**Finding 7 — Held, with repair.** Given the definition repaired as in Finding 5, the claim follows: (F1) plus the same anchor map gives the anchored relations, and (A) gives both pulled-back profiles as \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\). The “What it does not say” paragraph correctly limits it:

> “Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on \(C\) with *different* decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account, and no result here says they are.”

No over-citation of Derivation 2 was found elsewhere.

### Derivation 3 — Selection guarantees nothing off its history

**Finding 8 — Held.** The closure hypothesis is the load-bearing part:

> “suppose \(\mathcal T\) is **closed under pointwise alteration off \(H\)**: for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\), each component \(j\) of \(E\), and each relation \(L'\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)), \(\mathcal T\) contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) everywhere except that \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L'\).”

Remove it, and the proof has no altered pair to exhibit. It is doing real work.

**Finding 9 — Loose.** “Admissible class” is defined only by the parenthetical:

> “the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\))”.

That is self-referential but readable: the class is the set of component-relations appearing in members of \(\mathcal T\). If the phrase is meant to invoke the physical module’s admitted edits instead, that is not what the parenthetical says. What would settle it: state the admissible class independently, or explicitly bind it to \(\mathcal T\) as the only definition.

**Finding 10 — Loose/unknown.** The proof treats “survives on \(H\)” as equivalent to fidelity on \(H\):

> “Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\). The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives; it differs at \((a,b)\) by hypothesis.”

But Part IV’s selection witness includes more than fidelity on \(H\):

> “a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\); and \(t\) is a member that survived, with the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it.”

The proof does not address elimination or causal responsibility. If “survives on \(H\)” is shorthand for “satisfies fidelity on \(H\)”, the proof is valid. If it means “satisfies the selection survival condition”, the proof is incomplete. What would settle it: define “survives on \(H\)” in the claim, or weaken the claim to H-fidelity.

**Finding 11 — Held.** The stated consequence is carefully scoped and matches Part XV:

> “Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\): selection provides no *guarantee* of fidelity where it did not test.”

Part XV correctly says:

> “This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis.”

No over-citation found.

### Derivation 4 — Surprise, as a remark

**Finding 12 — Held.** The remark is accurate and self-limiting:

That a system with no transport is never surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract is never surprised, follow from the definition by unpacking. This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation, and nothing else in this document cites it as support.”

This matches Part IV’s definition of surprise as “a violation at \((a,b)\notin H\)”. No over-citation found.

### Derivation 5 — Question-finding is representable

**Finding 13 — Loose.** The construction claims exactness:

> “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).”

But the definition of “coherently posed” in Part III requires three things:

> “It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other’s complements as stated.”

The construction does not impose \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). It says only:

> “Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed.”

Holding \(b_0\) fixed is not the same as requiring the baseline solution set to be nonempty. So the solutions are not exactly the coherently posed questions.

**Finding 14 — Loose.** The construction does not explicitly impose “\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\).” It supplies a port \(u_{\mathcal Q}\), but no component checks definition on \(C\). So again, not exactly.

**Finding 15 — Loose.** The construction’s ports are “for each \((a,b)\in A\times B\) of the target”. But Part III allows \(K\) to contain pairs the module does not admit:

> “\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit — each with a stated meaning”.

If a counterfactual pair is not in \(A\), it has no port in \(D_p\). So \(D_p\) cannot represent every coherently posed question with non-admitted counterfactuals.

**Finding 16 — Loose.** The construction adds a downward-closure condition not in Part III’s definition of coherence:

> “if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads ‘in’ then so do its factors where the composition is defined”.

Part III does not require a contract to be closed under factors of admitted composites. So the construction may exclude coherently posed questions, as well as include non-coherent ones.

**Finding 17 — Over-cited elsewhere.** Part III uses Derivation 5 as the general form for constructed questions:

> “A **constructed** question is the result of an episode with a construction witness whose result is the question-organization of Derivation 5.”

Part X also relies on it:

> “the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing \(A\times B\).”

Since Derivation 5’s “exactly” claim is not established, these citations are stronger than what the construction proves. What it does prove, at most, is that some question-related organization can be written in the (O) schema, not that its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions.

### Derivation 6 — Two primitive packages, and the rest declared or derived

**Finding 18 — Held, for the specific Rep/Con cycle.** The claimed cycle is broken as stated. Part IV says:

> “Con(t;h,e) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode’s history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module’s sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below.”

So Con requires an instantiated target, not a represented one. (R) uses Con or Sel. Thus Rep depends on Con, but Con does not depend on Rep. The cycle is broken for that pair.

**Finding 19 — Loose.** Derivation 6 claims:

> “Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module … and the declared parameters … by the dependence order there.”

and the proof says:

> “the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV”.

“The only cycle that could arise” is asserted, not proved. There are other possible dependencies. For example, Part IX’s active route uses “a represented input to an operative result”; anchoring condition 4 uses active routes; (G) uses New, which uses Deploy, which uses Rep. If “represented input” is read as the Rep predicate, a further cycle must be ruled out. The proof does not do that.

**Finding 20 — Loose/over-cited.** Part XIV states:

> “No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters”.

But the document contains undefined or informally used predicates, e.g. Part XIII’s “coherently posed advanceable challenges” — “advanceable” is not defined; Part X’s “explanatory use of \(c\)”; Part IX’s “operative result”. So the consequence of Derivation 6, “No residual predicate is left undefined”, is stronger than the proof supports.

### Derivation 7 — The frozen assessment and the moving question are consistent

**Finding 21 — Held, conditional on Derivation 5.** The claim and proof are straightforward:

> “An assessment event with contract \(C\) and an episode in which \(C\) is replaced by \(C'\) with a construction witness are both representable without contradiction.”

The proof’s use of “a question-organization with contract \(C'\) is built (Derivation 5)” depends on Derivation 5. If Derivation 5’s exactness is repaired, this is fine. If not, the proof still works for the narrower claim that some question-organization can be built, but the citation to Derivation 5 is loose.

**Finding 22 — Over-cited.** The consequence says:

> “Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change; the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded.”

Part V’s adequacy condition only ranges over contract exclusions:

> “\(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.”

But Part III says an assessment is fixed by both contract and query:

> “An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another”.

So if someone changes \(\mathcal Q\) without recording it, Part V’s adequacy condition does not catch that. Part X’s merit condition is the relevant check for question changes. Derivation 7 cites Part V as catching all protective goalpost-moving; it catches only contract-side protection.

### Derivation 8 — Equivariance under genuine recoding

**Finding 23 — Loose.** The restriction on \(\phi\) is insufficient for the stated preservation:

> “Let \(\phi\) be a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps \(\lambda\)), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module’s admission (\(A_\Theta\), Inst, \(\prec\)), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and \(\mathcal N\). Transporting all data along \(\phi\) preserves (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK).”

The list does not include populations, variation operators, survival conditions, capabilities, resources, episodes, difficulties, objections, responses, or the Can predicate. Yet (G) depends on New, Deploy, Rep, Sel/Con, and Sel depends on a population, variation operator, survival condition, and causal responsibility. (EK) depends on CreativeCriticalEpisode, Deploy, ProducesVia. Anchoring’s active-route condition depends on “represented input” and “operative result”. So preserving the listed data does not automatically preserve (G), (P), or (EK).

**Finding 24 — Loose.** The proof is too quick:

> “Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data, and each of its inputs is carried along”.

This assumes what it needs to prove — that every input used by (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK) is among the transported data. It is not enough to say “each of its inputs is carried along” unless \(\phi\) is defined to carry those inputs.

**Finding 25 — Held in part.** The specific exclusion of the pole/shadow recoding is correct if relations must be preserved:

> “a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted \(\phi\), since it does not preserve \(D\)’s structure.”

If \(\phi\) preserves the component relations of \(D\), the swap is not permitted. That part is sound.

**Finding 26 — Over-cited elsewhere.** Part X defines \(\equiv_\ell\) using:

> “the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents.”

If Derivation 8 is overbroad, this parenthetical analogy is loose. It would need either a narrower statement or an explicit list of the bijections that \(\equiv_\ell\) actually uses.

### Derivation 9 — Output descriptions do not determine accounts

**Finding 27 — Held.** The claim and proof are valid:

> “If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both. *Proof.* Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs. ∎”

The consequence follows immediately. No over-citation found.

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip.** Had \((\tau,\sigma)\) been non-injective, Derivation 1’s conclusion could fail; had Derivation 5 included all three coherence conditions, its “exactly” claim would be closer; had Derivation 8’s \(\phi\) preserved Can and the selection witness, its preservation claim would be harder to vary.

**Poke.** For Derivation 1: change \(C\) to contain two pairs mapping to one translated pair but with different anchor relations. For Derivation 3: alter a component relation off \(H\). For Derivation 5: ask for a question with \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)=\varnothing\). For Derivation 6: ask for the definition of “advanceable”. For Derivation 8: ask for a \(\phi\) that preserves admission but not Can.

**Hunt the answer in the starting points.** Derivation 1’s conclusion is partly hidden in the injectivity hypothesis. Derivation 3’s conclusion is hidden in the closure hypothesis. Derivation 5’s “exactly” is hidden in unstated coherence conditions. Derivation 6’s “no residual predicate” is hidden in Part XIV’s assertion.

**Add a job.** Derivation 5 must represent \(K\) pairs outside \(A\); it fails. Derivation 6 must define “advanceable”; it does not. Derivation 8 must preserve Can; the restriction does not list it.

**Build the best rival.** A repaired Derivation 1 would state injectivity in every citation. A repaired Derivation 5 would include \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\)-defined-on-\(C\), and ports for non-admitted \(K\). A repaired Derivation 6 would give an explicit dependency graph. A repaired Derivation 8 would define \(\phi\) to preserve all inputs of (G), (P), (EK).

**Check the patches.** Derivation 3’s closure hypothesis is a patch that makes selection fail off \(H\). Derivation 5’s exactness claim is a patch not matched by the construction. Derivation 6’s cycle-break is a patch that works for Rep/Con but does not cover all predicates. Derivation 7’s citation of Part V as catching all goalpost-moving is a patch that catches only contract changes.

**Look inside.** Derivation 1 looks at signatures, not outputs. Derivation 3 looks at population closure. Derivation 5 looks at port organization. Derivation 6 looks at dependence order. Derivation 8 looks at data transport. These are the right places to look; the looseness is in unstated conditions and over-citations, not in the general approach.

## 5. What would make it harder to vary

1. Carry Derivation 1’s injectivity hypothesis into every citation — Part V, Derivation 10 — or prove a non-injective version that says what happens when two pairs map to one.
2. Repair Derivation 2’s definition of account-equivalence with an explicit port/footprint bijection and an explicit pullback map.
3. In Derivation 3, state whether “survives on \(H\)” means H-fidelity or the full selection survival condition, and define “admissible class” independently or explicitly as relations in \(\mathcal T\).
4. In Derivation 5, either add the missing coherence conditions — \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) defined on every pair in \(C\), ports for non-admitted \(K\) — or weaken “exactly” to “a subset of”.
5. In Derivation 6, replace the survey with an explicit dependency graph, and define or remove undefined predicates such as “advanceable”.
6. In Derivation 7, narrow the consequence: Part V adequacy catches contract-side protection; question changes need the merit condition of Part X.
7. In Derivation 8, expand \(\phi\) to preserve populations, variation operators, survival conditions, Can, resources, episodes and responses, or weaken the preservation claim to the data actually listed.

## 6. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not truth. These findings locate loose bolts and over-citations. They do not show the document false, and a repaired version could still fail for reasons outside this audit. Conversely, a loose part may be true.

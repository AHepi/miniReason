## 3. Part by part

### Derivation 1 — kind preservation

> "**Claim.** Let \(t\) satisfy (F1) on \(C\), and let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\). Then every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation."

**Finding 1 — the hypothesis list is sufficient only if a port correspondence is added, and the proof supplies it by a parenthesis.** The proof reads "\(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\), the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\)." That clause is not in Part IV's definition of a transport, which says only that \(\lambda\) "assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation". Nothing there makes that port translation a bijection onto the anchor's visible ports, or matches the value domains. Without it, both (F1) and "up to the port translation" are ill-formed. **Mark: held, conditional — the condition is borrowed from an unstated convention and is not on the list of hypotheses.**

**Finding 2 — the remark under the proof is stronger than the claim.** The remark says: "a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork." Two problems. (i) Part II defines the relation only inside one organization at one grain and contract: "Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\)." A component of \(E\) and a subnetwork of \(D\) are not "two components" of one organization, so the definition does not cover the cross-organization case. (ii) It drops the injectivity hypothesis and changes "on \(\tau[C]\)" into "on \(C\)". **Mark: loose (could be otherwise — the definitions do not hold it in place).**

**Finding 3 — the anchored grade is cited more strongly than Derivation 1 proves.** Part V, condition 4: "**World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor". This needs the cross-organization reading of Finding 2 *and* needs \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); Derivation 1 requires injectivity as a hypothesis and says nothing about \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). **Mark: borrowed — it rests on Derivation 1 plus an unstated injectivity condition on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).** (Credit where due: Part XV is honest — "Derivation 1 does not rule this out; it shows only that fidelity preserves signatures." That one is accurate.)

**Finding 4 — on injectivity itself: used, and sufficient, and stronger than needed.** The proof does use it: "injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates." Poke that should matter: take two pairs in \(C\) with the same \((\tau,\sigma)\) image and different relations — the conclusion fails. Poke that should not matter: rename the ports — the conclusion stands. So the injectivity clause is a working hypothesis, not decoration. **Mark: held by the collapse-avoidance job.** It is stronger than needed (non-injective translations with coincident third coordinates would do), which is not a fault.

### Derivation 2 — account-equivalence

> "Two candidates \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) and \(\mathcal E'=(E',p,t',\Gamma')\) for the same \(p\) are **account-equivalent on \(C\)** when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide."

**Finding 5 — the definition cannot be evaluated as written.** "The anchored relations ... coincide" compares a relation on \(\prod_{v\in V_k}X_v\) with a relation on \(\prod_{v\in V_{k'}}X_v\). "A bijection between their active components" does not supply the port-and-domain correspondence needed to say the two relations are the same relation. **Mark: loose — a design choice (definitions are allowed to be free), but it must be stated fully before the claim resting on it can be checked.**

**Finding 6 — the proof is either superfluous or a non-sequitur.** The proof: "(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles." Read the definition at face value — the anchored relations are D-side objects determined by \(\lambda\) and \((a,b)\) alone, independent of \(\tau,\sigma\) — then with a shared anchor map they coincide *without* (F1), and the profile clause is exactly (A). Read it as coincidence of the candidates' component relations instead, and the proof fails, because it never shows \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L_{k'}(\tau'(a),\sigma'(b))\) — the two transports may translate differently. **Mark: the claim is nearly a restatement of its definition; (F1) and (F2) are idle hypotheses in it.**

**Finding 7 — the section is idle for the rest of the document.** Nothing else cites account-equivalence; the underdetermination point in "What it does not say" is already made in Part 0, grievance 2, without it. **Mark: idle — removable with no loss to the remaining derivations.**

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip.** Would these derivations read the same had the opposite been true? Derivation 6 would — its proof names one cycle and asserts the rest, so it reads identically for any list of definitions. Derivation 5 would — the construction is drawn from the phrase "coherently posed" and would be presented unchanged if coherence meant something else. Derivations 1, 3, 9 and 8 would not: drop injectivity (D1), disjointness of translated histories (D3), equality of projections (D9), or a carried item (D8) and the claims fail. **Credit: Derivation 3's consequence is scrupulously one-sided — "It does not follow that the survivor *is* unfaithful there — it may be faithful on all of \(C\) — and nothing here says the survivor will fail" — and Part XV states the refutation condition in the same shape. That is honest scoping.**

**Direction.** Derivation 5 runs backwards from the wanted solution set to components that resemble it, and the result is a fit that misses clauses (Finding 14). Derivation 6 runs from the conclusion ("no cycle") to one check. Derivations 1, 2, 3, 8, 9 run forward from definitions, which is why their errors are local and repairable.

**The answer hidden in the starting points.** Not hidden anywhere as a bare label, but it is close to the surface in two places. Derivation 2's definition puts "account-equivalence" itself in the premise, so the claim is nearly analytic (Finding 6). Derivation 6 puts Part XIV's parameter list in the premise and then asserts that everything follows the order (Finding 21).

**What the change list leaves out.** The document holds accounts to a standard it does not apply to its own derivations: Part V demands that "every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently", and Derivation 1's scope statement does not record the port-translation precondition; Derivation 3's does not record the disjointness condition; Derivation 5's does not record that \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\) are outside the construction; Derivation 6's does not record that Build's repertoire is excluded from the stratification; Derivation 8's does not record that states, executions, resources and capabilities are outside \(\phi\).

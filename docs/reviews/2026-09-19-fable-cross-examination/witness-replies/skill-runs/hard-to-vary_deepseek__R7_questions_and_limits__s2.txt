## Note before the run

The skill's word list says it was built from the source theory; the document under test is a revision of that same theory. So the mapping runs *from the document's terms to the skill's plain words*, not the other way, and where the two overlap I use the document's own words and do not treat the skill's frame as prior evidence about the document. Concretely: the skill's "a change to the world / a poke" = the document's *admitted edit* (Part II); "the change list" = the document's *contract* (Part III); "the question, over what range" = the document's `\(p=(D, C, b_0, \mathcal Q, O_p, \rho_p)\)`; "a part" = a component, port, edit, or clause of the document.

# 1. The question, frozen

**What.** The user's five-part attack on `20_semantics_revised`:
(a) is the merit condition of Part X trivial or too strong;
(b) does the question-organisation of Derivation 5 admit exactly the changes needed;
(c) is "add a pair to `\(K\)` with a meaning" an edit an organisation can have;
(d) is the stated limit on concept creation actually a limit, or does some reading of `\(\operatorname{Inst}\)` plus a new grain already cover it;
(e) which of Part XV's four open cases could be closed with what the document already has, and which cannot.

**Over what range.** The document's own statements of those parts in Part III (provenance clause), Part X (merit condition, stated limit), Part VII (treatments of impossibility, symmetry, artefact, mathematics), Derivation 5 (question-organisation), and Part XV (attack list). Nothing outside this revision.

**What is asked.** (a) does the merit condition do its job — separate a *found* question from a re-parameterisation — without becoming trivial or too strong; (b) is the question-organisation's edit set exactly what its stated job requires; (c) is that edit an edit at all under the document's Part II; (d) is the stated concept-creation limit a limit or an already-covered case; (e) which of the four listed cases the document has already closed.

**Kind.** Mixed. (a), (d), (e) are *does it achieve its purpose?* questions. (b) and (c) are *what counts as what under this rule?* questions — the document's own Part II rule for what an operation is (`\(A\)` closed under a partial associative composition with identity `\(1\)`). The tests therefore split, and I flag which kind each finding comes from.

Freeze note from the document's own Part VIII: a claim is indexed to its contract, and "A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change, and a claim at a new index is a new claim." So criticisms below are aimed at the claims at their own index; where a tightening would be a new claim, I say so.

**F1 — Merit condition, an idle-or-undefined clause.** "with `\(o\)` failing at the state before `\(e\)`" is either redundant with (P) or indexed differently from (P), and the document does not say which. (P) already requires `\(\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\)` — the obligation is failing at the *episode's* start. If "the state before `\(e\)`" is that `\(\xi\)`, the clause repeats (P) and can be removed with no loss. If it is not — because `\(e\)` is the moment of the originative act within the episode (per `\((\text{G})\)`'s `\(\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\)`) — then the merit condition uses a state-index that (P) never defines. *Remove test, clause by clause.*

**F2 — Merit condition, the trivial horn is available.** The condition only requires "there is an obligation `\(o\in O\)`". Part XIV lists "the obligation sets `\(O,P,O_p\)`" as *declared parameters* — "Neither derived nor primitive; supplied with each use, and every claim is relative to them". The document never restricts what may enter `\(O\)`. So a modeller may declare an `\(O\)` containing a general obligation (e.g., "come to know the answer to `\(p'\)`" for every `\(p'\)`), and then every coherently constructed question repairs some obligation. The user's first horn is therefore available on the document's own text. *Add-a-job test: the condition does not rule out a re-parameterisation that addresses a general obligation.*

**F3 — Merit condition, the too-strong horn is available.** If `\(O\)` is instead restricted, the condition requires that a *deployable account of `\(p'\)`* exists — "would repair". Deploy, in Part X, is defined through (R), which requires a "faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance". A genuine *finding* of the right question is precisely the case where such an account is typically not yet available. The document does not say whether the condition is checked at `\(e\)` (prospective) or after the episode (retrospective). Prospective → genuine findings rejected; retrospective → the verdict is carried by the later answer, not by the finding, and the "found" verdict is not held by the act it is meant to pick out. The second horn is available too.

**F4 — Best rival for the merit condition.** The tighter near-neighbour is Part XI's `\(\operatorname{ProducesVia}\)`: "the change of `\(o\)` lies on an active route that passes through a deployment of `\(c\)`". That version ties the found question's contribution to the episode's *actual* active route. The document's version is only the subjunctive "would repair `\(o\)` under (P)". No change the document names separates the two. So the document's version is the looser of the two candidates at this level.

**F5 — The merit condition does not rule out its named rival.** The rival to beat is a *re-parameterisation that repairs a general obligation*. The condition as written cannot rule it out: "a re-parameterisation of an old question that repairs no obligation is a constructed question and not a found one" defines the line at "repairs no obligation" and, on F2's `\(O\)`, that line is almost never crossed in the adverse direction. So the added job kills no rival version — the condition's job on the user's question is not done.

**F6 — The document knows the merit condition is the pivot, and names both horns.** Part XV (F): "A question found that the merit condition wrongly rejects, or a re-parameterisation it wrongly admits. Either refutes the merit condition of Part X as the line between finding and relabelling." This is honest disclosure — no hidden patch — but the part is still loose: the document states the condition without committing to which horn it will fail on.

**F7 — The question-organisation's reach is narrower than the question tuple.** Derivation 5 claims "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on `\(D\)`". The tuple is `\(p=(D, C, b_0, \mathcal Q, O_p, \rho_p)\)`. The ports cover the pairs (target edits), `\(\mathcal Q\)`, `\(S\)`, `\(K\)`; the boundary is "the target `\(D\)` and `\(b_0\)`, held fixed". Nothing represents `\(O_p\)`. So two questions differing only in their obligation set are *the same solution* of this organisation. The exactness claim is exact over `(C, \mathcal Q)` with `\((D,b_0)\)` fixed, and not exact over the tuple. The document does not say which of these it is claiming. *Remove test on the fields with no port.*

**F8 — Whole-tuple provenance, two readings.** Part III: "`\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\)` attaches to the whole tuple `\(p\)`, not to its contract alone". *Weak reading*: `\(\rho_p\)` is one provenance status for the question, not a separate status per field. On this reading it is fine and **held**. *Strong reading*: `\(\rho_p\)` is a provenance of `\(D, C, b_0, \mathcal Q, O_p, \rho_p\)` jointly — and this can only hold if the organisation represents changes to all of them. By F7 it does not. The claim cannot be tighter than the organisation it is attached through. The user's worry ("Part III attaches provenance to the whole question") is well-founded on the strong reading.

**F9 — Closure of the edit set is not verified.** Part II requires `\(A\)` to be "closed under a partial associative composition with identity `\(1\)`". Derivation 5 lists the edits but does not give a composition table (e.g., "alter `\(\mathcal Q\)`" followed by "add a pair to `\(K\)` with a meaning"). So the claim that this set is an admissible `\(A\)` is *asserted*; the axiom's job is unchecked. *Remove with no substitute collapses the axiom's job.*

**F10 — "add a pair to `\(K\)` with a meaning" — an edit under one reading, and only under one.** Part III gives `\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\)` with `\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\)` and `\(K\)` as "pairs the module does not admit", so `\(K\subseteq (A\times B)\setminus C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)`: every pair of `\(K\)` is already carried by a port `\(u_{(a,b)}\)`. Two readings follow.

- *Reading (i)* — "add a pair to `\(K\)`" means add the pair to the `\(K\)`-subset and give it a meaning, i.e., change the *value* of `\(u_K\)` (and of the pair port). Part II permits this: "An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port". On this reading the edit is **held** — it is an edit an organisation can have.
- *Reading (ii)* — "add a pair" means add a *new* pair, i.e., a new dimension of variation. This is the move the document says it cannot represent (F11). Under reading (ii) the edit is not an edit an organisation can have in this semantics.

The document's construction forces reading (i) — the ports run over the target's `\(A\times B\)` — but the phrasing of "add a pair to `\(K\)`" invites reading (ii). The user's question is therefore well-founded: the edit is admissible *as a value-change*, but the phrasing claims more than the construction gives. *Swap test: swap "add a pair to `\(K\)`" for "alter the value of `\(u_K\)`"; if nothing else changes, the phrasing was loose.*

**F11 — Is the concept-creation limit a limit?** The document: "What the construction cannot do is add a port to `\(D\)`: the invention of a new dimension of variation changes the target, and this document has no witness for that." Three of the document's own resources point the other way.

- Part II treats grain as an index with a licensed organisation behind it: "Claims can be rescued by moving to a grain only if the new grain is admissible, and the move is a new claim at a new index." If a different admissible grain licenses a decomposition of the same occurrence with the extra port, the "new dimension" is available at that grain — representable, as a new claim at a new index.
- Part IV makes `\(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\)` "a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain". A "new dimension" may already be instantiated by the same occurrence.
- Part III's question is `\(p=(D, C, b_0, \mathcal Q, O_p, \rho_p)\)` — the target is a field of the question. So a question with a *new* target `\(D'\)` is a question, and Derivation 5 builds an organisation for `\(D'\)`, not for `\(D\)`. The "invention" is then representable as the finding of a question with a new target, provided `\(D'\)` is instantiated.

Part XV (E) already hedges: "a case in which it must be inside to capture a genuine finding counts against attack (E)". So the fair statement is: **the limit is a limit on Derivation 5's particular construction, which "is built over an existing `\(A\times B\)`", not on the semantics** — unless the document commits to a question always being on a *fixed* target, which its own tuple definition (F7) denies. As written, the limit is loose.

**F12 — Part XV (i) impossibility: partly closed, partly scoped.** For the sub-case where "the introducing edit is admitted", the treatment uses "a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O)" and (O)'s own rule, "A deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports". This is closed by what the document has, given the caveat that this is *offered*, not derived, in Part VII ("offered as adequate for the first case and as scoped for the second"). For the sub-case where the introducing edit "is not admitted", the case passes through `\(K\)` "declared with its meaning", and the document says plainly what that establishes: "what it establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual". So it is scoped, not closed.

**F13 — Part XV (iv) mathematics: closed relative to a declared relation, and mis-listed with the others.** Part VII: "What the example does not do is fall under attack (B)'s quantifier over *physically* admitted contracts, because there is no physical module here; it falls under the declared one." The skew-symmetric worked example is a full case under a declared admission relation. So (iv) is *closed* as far as the semantics claims to close mathematics — relative to a declared relation, which is a declared parameter. It does not belong in the same list as (i)–(iii): it is a scope clarification of the necessity attack, not an argument that the semantics has overreached. Listing it under "Four cases are open" is loose in the same way as listing a resolved case as open.

**F14 — Part XV (ii) symmetry: the "not constrained" claim is asserted.** The document says the symmetry case is open because of "an entailment among the components of `\(E\)` that neither (F1) nor (F2) constrains". But the document's own (K) defines a signature for a *subnetwork* — "The signature of a subnetwork `\(U\)` on `\(C\)` is `\(\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}}\operatorname{Sol}_U(a,b))\}\)`" — and (F1) quantifies over anchors that are themselves subnetworks ("every active component `\(k\)` of `\(E\)` with anchor subnetwork `\(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\)`"). So a symmetry argument of the shape "component k has relation R because the subnetwork it is anchored to has it" can be checked under (F1) for the concrete cases the user's question names. The document does not show that (F1)+(F2) cannot be made to bite. Verdict: the *open* status is loose; some symmetry cases are arguably closable with what the document has, and the document does not say which.

**F15 — Part XV (iii) selection artefact: the ground given is single-target, the reasoning is missing.** The stated ground is that it "needs two targets … and a transport for each" — while Account has one transport with one target, "a transport `\(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\)`". The document does not show that the two-target case cannot be handled by, say, a product target or a pair of transports. So (iii)'s openness is *unknown* on the document's own grounds: the ground is a formal choice (single target), and the inference from "single target" to "not representable" is not in the text. *Hunt: the answer sits in the starting point "Account has one target" rather than in the case.*

**F16 — Which of the four can be closed with what the document has.**

- (i) sub-case with the introducing edit admitted: **closed** by (O)'s deleted-component rule.
- (i) sub-case with the edit not admitted: **scoped** — the ground is the stipulated meaning of a counterfactual edit.
- (iv) mathematics: **closed relative to a declared admission relation** — worked in Part VII.
- (ii) symmetry: **not closed, and its openness is asserted rather than argued**; arguably partly closable with subnetwork anchors.
- (iii) selection artefact: **not closed, and its openness is asserted rather than derived** — the ground given is single-target, and the reasoning from single-target to "not representable" is absent.

# 4. Whole-explanation checks

**F17 — Flip (merit condition).** Take a re-parameterisation that repairs no obligation, and a genuine finding that repairs an obligation. The same conjunct covers both. Under F2's `\(O\)` it passes both; under a restricted `\(O\)` it rejects both whenever the account is not yet deployable (F3). An explanation that covers both outcomes is held in place by neither. The only thing currently keeping the distinction visible is the document's own hedge in Part XV (F).

**F18 — Hunt the answer in the starting points (merit condition).** The condition's operative word is "deployable". Deploy (Part X) is defined through (R), which requires "a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance". So the merit condition's verdict on "found" is carried by whether a provenance witness exists for an account that may not yet exist. The answer — that the question was *found* — is partly sitting in the starting point "a deployable account exists". No change on the document's list removes the answer, which is what a Check-4 test of this skill would require.

**F19 — Check the patches.** The document conceals nothing. Part 0, "Where to attack this", Part XV, and the merit condition's own section all name the load-bearing parts and the tests against them — including (E) on the concept-creation limit and (F) on the merit condition. So this is honest disclosure, not hidden rescue. What is missing is not honesty but *tightening*: load-bearing parts are named rather than demonstrated, and the open cases are listed without a proof that they cannot be closed with the tools already on the page.

**F20 — Look inside.** The merit condition tells what *comes out* — a repair of some obligation — not how a finding works. Derivation 5 gives a working organisation for the finding, but only over `\((C,\mathcal Q)\)` with `\((D, b_0)\)` fixed — the very region where F7 says the tuple has unrepresented fields, and the very region the concept-creation limit would have to cover if the limit were real. So neither part shows the mechanism of the case the user is most worried about.

# 5. Where each part came from

- **The merit condition** — *asserted*. Part X states it, Part III restates it, Part XV names the horns that would refute it. No checking case is reported against a rival.
- **The question-organisation (Derivation 5)** — *built*, but its **exactness claim** and its **closure axiom** are *asserted*; neither is tested on the page.
- **The whole-tuple provenance attribution** — *asserted*; it follows neither from the tuple definition alone nor from the organisation (F8).
- **The concept-creation limit** — *asserted*; no derivation that no licensed grain or `\(\operatorname{Inst}\)` reading could cover it (F11).
- **Part XV's open cases** — *asserted* for (ii) and (iii) (no construction, no argument that none exists); *built* for (i) (via (O)'s deleted-component rule and `\(K\)`) and (iv) (via the odd-order skew-symmetric worked example).
- **Borrowed.** The merit condition borrows (P) from Part XI; the limit borrows the term "dimension of variation" from Part II's port structure; Derivation 5 borrows its (O)-shape from Part II. These borrowings are not defects in themselves — the document's own dependence order in Part XIV makes them explicit — and faults should be traced to the right host.

# 6. What would make it harder to vary

Two or three pokes, in the document's own vocabulary:

- **Merit condition.** Fix the reading. Say whether it is checked at `\(e\)` (prospective) or after the episode (retrospective), and say whether `\(O\)` means all declared obligations or only the epistemic `\(O_{\mathrm{ep}}\)` of Part XI. If the tighter F4 rival is meant, write it in with `\(\operatorname{ProducesVia}\)`. *The poke:* take (1) a genuine historical finding of a question whose deployable account did not exist at the time, and (2) a declared re-parameterisation that repairs a general obligation in a declared `\(O\)`. The document's text should give different verdicts on the two. If it cannot, the merit condition is loose in exactly the direction the user names.
- **Question-organisation.** Either add a port for `\(O_p\)` and say what a change of obligation is as an edit, or state explicitly that the organisation represents only `\((C,\mathcal Q)\)` with `\((D, b_0)\)` fixed as background — in which case F8's strong reading is retracted and Part III's "whole tuple" clause is rewritten. Give the composition table for the edit set (F9), or narrow the sentence so composition is not required.
- **The limit.** Either show that no admissible grain licenses a decomposition of the same occurrence with the extra port — in which case the limit is a theorem — or concede that the "invention" is representable as the finding of a question with a new target, with a nontrivial binding, under `\((\text{G})\)`. The middle ground ("the limit applies only to Derivation 5's construction, not to the semantics") is the honest, tighter statement, and the document is one sentence away from it.

# 7. What this does not show

This is a critique, not a truth-meter. It does not show that the merit condition is false, that the limit is false, or that Part XV's open cases *must* be closable. It shows which parts are held and by what, which are loose and what could replace them, and which are idle, borrowed, or unknown. A loose part may be true; a false part can be hard to vary. Nothing here bears on the truth of the constitutive conjecture, on (F1)/(F2)/(A), or on the results in Part XVI.

The tests were aimed at the parts the user named and their immediate neighbours. The physical module, `\(A_\Theta\)`, the observation edit, Part VIII's historical index, and (EK) are being tested, if at all, elsewhere.

# 8. One next step

Run the two-case poke in F2/F3's direction on the merit condition, in the document's own terms — each case a change to the world that the text says should matter:

1. **The finding whose answer came later.** A genuine historical finding of a question for which no deployable account existed at the time `\(e\)`. Ask the document's merit condition: is `\(p'\)` "found"? The answer being read prospectively *rejects* the finding (F3).
2. **The re-parameterisation that repairs a general obligation.** A declared `\(C'\)` differing from `\(C\)` by a single toggled pair, with an obligation in `\(O\)` that its eventual answer would help with. Ask: is `\(p'\)` "found"? The answer being read with a general `\(O\)` *admits* it (F2).

If the first is rejected and the second admitted, the merit condition is too strong and too weak at once, and the document must fix both the reading and the declaration of `\(O\)` before the condition can do the job it is stated to do.

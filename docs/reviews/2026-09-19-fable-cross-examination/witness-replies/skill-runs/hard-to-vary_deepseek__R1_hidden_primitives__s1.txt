# Hard-to-vary run on `20_semantics_revised`

**A note on vocabulary.** The skill's word list (`references/word-list.md`) says the skill was built from a source theory, and it maps its plain words onto that theory's terms. The document I am examining is a revision of that same theory, so words like *contract*, *admitted edit*, *transport*, *grain*, *provenance*, *fidelity* and *account* are the document's own vocabulary, not independent evidence. The five marks — held, loose, idle, borrowed, unknown — are the skill's; that is what I am applying.

---

## Step 1 — The question, frozen

**What, over what range, what is asked.** The document claims: "No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters" (Part XIV), with Derivation 6 as its witness. The frozen question is the one you gave me, in four parts, over the whole of Parts II–XIII:

1. a predicate used before or without definition;
2. a definition that quietly uses *represents*, *explains*, *is a cause*, *relevant*, *begs the question*, or a judgement word;
3. anywhere `Inst` or the admitted edit set `A_Θ` is asked to do more than Part XII says the module supplies;
4. the dependence order clause by clause, for any cycle, especially through (R), `Con`, `Build`, `Deploy`, `Attempt`.

**Kind of question:** mixed — "what can we tell from what we see?" (does the text contain a predicate with no upstream definition?) and "why can it not happen?" (is a cycle impossible?). Both are checkable by reading, which is the only "poke" available for a formal document.

## Step 5 — The ten tests

### Remove

**F1.** Remove `Inst` or `A_Θ` and every physical attribution, every contract and every world-kind claim collapses. Both are doing real work; the document says so and marks the cost: "the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle: that is what 'physics says what a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden." **Held** (declared).

**F2 (cycle).** Remove *"a binding already in the repertoire"* from `Build` and the cycle vanishes. It is there, and it closes a loop. `Con` — which (R) requires — reads "there is an episode (Part X) whose **construction witness** prepares `t`" (Part IV). That witness is `Build`, whose nontriviality clause reads: "it is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in **the repertoire** nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by `≡_ℓ`" (Part X). The repertoire is defined one section earlier by `Deploy`: "The repertoire `R_{β,ℓ}(s,ξ)` is the set of contents **deployable** in some nontrivial use respect." And `Deploy` requires (R): "`Deploy_{β,ℓ}(s,c,ξ;U)` holds when `s` at `ξ` holds a representation of `c` — **by (R)**, a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance". So (R) → `Con` → `Build` → repertoire → `Deploy` → (R). This directly contradicts the order's defence: "provenance depends on `Θ` and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on `Inst` and `≡_ℓ` **but not on (R)** — the stratification of Part IV … is what keeps this order well-founded." **Unknown→found: the cycle is there.** What would settle it: rewrite the nontriviality clause without the repertoire and re-check.

**F3.** The same clause is a type slip even if the cycle is repaired: the repertoire is "the set of **contents** deployable", while the clause speaks of "a **binding** already in the repertoire". A binding is "an assignment `λ(k)` of a component of the built organization to a subnetwork of its target". Contents and bindings are different things. **Loose.**

**F4 (cycle).** Ownership and capability define each other with no base case. Part X: "A subhistory is **owned** by `s` when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability `s` possesses in the sense of Part XII (`Can`)". Part XII: "`Can_{Ω,β}(ξ,T;χ)` requires **an owned retained realization** or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one under the same continuity and resource contract." `Ownership` → `Can` → `Ownership`. Part XIV lists "ownership" as an input to `Build` without noticing this. The retention fixed point (CT2) is a *different* fixpoint and does not close this one. **Unknown** — no base case is stated; what would settle it is a least-fixed-point clause or an unowned primitive realization.

**F5.** Part XII's other disjunct, "an owned, physically admitted, finite **construction** of one", is ambiguous between colloquial construction and `Build`. If it is `Build`, there is a third loop: `Can` → `Build` → ownership → `Can`, and `Build` → `(F1)`-or-`(A)` → (E) is the only reason it is not obviously fatal. **Loose.**

**F6 (possible cycle).** `Sel`'s blindness clause uses "representation": "A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no **itemised representation** of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation" (Part IV). Read as (R), this makes (R) → `Sel` → (R). Part IV also says of the two provenances: "a selection witness contains no **itemised representation** of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation" (repeated in Part X). **Loose** — the gloss ("a record pairing …") is not (R), but the document has not said which sense is in play, and this is exactly the word the frozen question asks about.

**F7.** Remove Γ and (S), (B) fall; but Γ is not on the declared-parameter list, though the "named background" — its complement — is (F13). **Loose.**

### Swap

**F8 (`Attempt`).** Part X: "`Attempt(s,c,p,h,e)` the relation that `c` is actually **used**, on an active route, to **address** `p`". *Address* is never defined, and *use* collides with (K2)'s "A response uses a reason", which is a different predicate in Part IX. If "actually used" is meant to be `Deploy`, `Attempt` inherits another (R)-link; if not, it is a bare judgement word carrying `Origin`. Both readings are findings. **Loose.**

**F9 (`relevant`).** Same sentence: "for which the **relevant** difficulty is that some obligation in `O_p` fails at the state before `e`". Swap *relevant* for *some* and `Origin` counts any difficulty at all. So the word is load-bearing and undefined. **Loose.**

**F10.** Swap the transport's own ingredients. `t=(π,τ,σ,λ)` is used everywhere, and λ carries anchors (Part V), and (R) is stated over `t`. But Part XIV's trichotomy does not place it: the primitives are `Θ` and `N`; the declared list contains contracts, queries, obligations, the edit family `V`, use tasks — no π, τ, σ, λ; "Derived" lists roles, kinds, respect, representation, provenance, account, anchoring, understanding, construction, newness, origin, repair, knowledge, capability, recursion, universality — no transport data. Add the job "every datum must be primitive, declared or derived" and the document does not survive unchanged. **Loose.**

**F11 (`explanatory`).** `Build` requires "an organization that some occurrence of `s` instantiates **for explanatory use** of `c`". *Explanatory* is the theory's central word and is nowhere defined; it recurs in "explanatory jobs `F`" (Part VI) and "the explanatory contents `𝔈_Θ`" (Part XIII). Swap it for "recorded" and `Origin` counts any recorded use. **Loose.**

**F12.** `Deploy` has two more: "**integrated into problem-directed activity**" and the repertoire's "**nontrivial use respect**", neither defined — the sharp contrast being that *nontrivial binding construction* is carefully defined in the same section. **Loose.**

**F13.** `Deploy` says `s` holds the representation "as a retained capability (Part XII)", which routes to the `Ownership`/`Can` loop (F4). **Loose.**

### Flip

**F14 (Derivation 6 is a promise, not a check).** "Each definition names its inputs at first use; the order of Part XIV is followed from each predicate to its base, and the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV." Had the text been cycle-free, this sentence would read identically. It names one candidate cycle and asserts there is no other; it does not run the check. **Loose** — the honest mark, given F2 must then be found by hand.

**F15.** Had the order been correct, would the document have said anything different about the patch? No: the whole of Part IV's careful clause — the target is "available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, **not** in the sense of the representation predicate below" — would be written the same. So the patch is real but partial (see *Check the patches*).

### Poke

**F16 (prose used as predicates).** Part X defines episodes in prose: "A complete critical episode contains a **recognized difficulty**, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a **content-sensitive response**." (EK) then uses `CreativeCriticalEpisode(s,Δ,h,e)` and `Result(Δ)` as if they were predicates. Poke: a change to what counts as "recognized" should matter to (EK); a change to the wording should not — but no definition lets us say which is which. **Loose.**

**F17.** `ProducedBy`: "every change of value of an obligation in `O` between them **lies on an active route from occurrences of Δ**" (Part XI). Δ is an episode, and the document never says an episode contains occurrences; and obligations are typed both ways — `¬o(ξ) ∧ o(ξ')` in (P) treats them as truth-valued, "change of value" treats them as valued. **Loose.**

### Reverse

**F18.** The dependence order runs from the conclusion back. The conclusion — "No predicate … is left undefined" — is in the header of Derivation 6; the "proof" then walks each predicate to its base. That is a reading-off, not a test. Combined with *Hunt* below, this is where the answer is hidden in the starting points.

### Hunt the answer in the starting points

**F19.** The completeness conclusion is partly in the starting points, because the declared-parameter list is itself the place where undefined things go. Γ ("an identified set of `Γ` of active commitments in `E`", Part V), the transport data (F10), "problem-directed activity" (F12), "operative deliberative rule" (F20), and "explanatory" (F11) are neither defined nor listed. **Loose.**

**F20.** Part IX's reason use: "a structural map from the **represented** objection into the response suborganization preserves **role bindings**, … sends content changes to the changes specified by the **operative deliberative rule**". *Role bindings* and *operative deliberative rule* are undefined anywhere in Parts II–XIII; *represented* is the flagged word. **Loose.**

**F21.** `NonCircular` defines independence by an unindexed transport: "A boundary condition `b` is **independent** of the answer when no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports." Between which organisations, on which contract, is not said; and its witness clause ends "the answer profile changes or **ceases to be determined in the claimed way**", with *sub-block* also undefined (blocks are defined only in Part VI). **Loose.**

**F22.** (R)'s active-route cousin: Part IX's "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a **represented** input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the **applicable** relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the **represented distinction** under the **declared contrasts**". If *represented* is (R), the route is an (R)-link into Anchoring's fourth condition and into (EK); if not, it is a second, unexplained sense of representation, in a document whose Part IV says the representation predicate is "defined in terms of" the layer facts. **Loose.**

**F24.** New job: "the dependence order must survive removing every phrase that names a set defined by `Deploy`." It survives (F2's clause is removable and F3's mismatch must then be repaired), which is a finding in the document's favour.

On the present text, I cannot decide — so F2 and F6 stay *unknown*, F8 and F11 stay *loose*. **Unknown.**

### Check the patches

**F26.** The construction witness has been patched once against exactly this attack: "available as a target, in the module's sense of `Inst`, not in the sense of the representation predicate below" (Part IV); Part XIV repeats "targets are *instantiated*, not *represented*, in a construction witness". The patch has a job (it breaks the one cycle Derivation 6 names) and is not idle. But it was applied to one clause: the same witness's nontriviality clause still reaches `Deploy` and therefore (R) (F2), and `Sel` still says "itemised representation" (F6). A patch against a cycle that leaves the cycle reachable by another clause narrows the claim quietly — and the narrowing was not said out loud. **Loose.**

### Look inside

**F27.** The document's own Derivation 9 is the standard to apply: "If `M₀,M₁` have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both." Applied to itself: the output here is a long list of definitions each naming inputs; the working is the actual reachability of those inputs. My findings are all of the second kind. **Held as a standard; the document fails its own test at F2, F4, F6.**

### Findings on `Inst` and `A_Θ` specifically (frozen question, part 3)

**F28.** Part II's admissibility condition asks the module to match components to edits: "every component of that organization has a signature that some **physically admitted edit realizes**". The module's edit supply is "the admitted edit set `A_Θ(o)` — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits, **stated as a set**". A set of alterations does not come indexed by components, so "realizes" is either a claim inside the decomposition oracle or an equivocation. Where `L_j(a,b)` is returned per component per edit, the signature is computable — but then "realizes" means "`a` is on the list", which makes the condition nearly vacuous. **Loose.**

**F29.** Anchoring's fourth condition needs counterfactual responses of occurrences that are *not* in the history: "each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and **not a backup that would have responded identically**". Part IX repeats it: "A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; **the difference between the two is a difference in `h`**". But `h` supplies actual connections; `Inst` supplies the decomposition of occurrences that exist. Deciding what a backup *would have* responded is more than "a decomposition, not a reading of a meter". **Loose** — and this is the clearest single overreach on `Inst`.

**F30.** `Sel` requires "a population `T` of **realized transports**" and "a variation operator `μ` on `T` that was physically applied at least once". The module returns `Inst`, `A_Θ`, `≺`, `Exec` and process data; it returns no population of transports and no operator on one. Part IV says this "is a claim about a physical history under `Θ`" — but a claim *about* the module is not the same as the module *supplying* the quantity. **Loose.**

**F31.** Part IX attributes to the module data missing from Part XII's inventory: "a physical interpretation supplying **process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated**". Part XII lists state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, `A_Θ(o)`, `I`, resources, grades, `Inst_ℓ`, `≺`, `Exec`. The actual wiring is not on that list, and it is what the active-route condition needs. **Loose.**

**F32.** Part III makes the module admit *pairs*: "`C_adm ⊆ A×B` is the set of pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization, the physically admitted edits `A_Θ`". `A_Θ(o)` is a set of alterations and `B` is organisation data. A small mismatch, but it is the shape of every adequacy and world-kind claim. **Loose.**

### Whole-explanation checks

- **Flip:** the order would read identically if it were cycle-free (F14), so it explains neither outcome.
- **Direction:** the order runs backwards from "no predicate is left undefined" (F18).
- **Answer hidden in the starting points:** the declared list absorbs the incompleteness (F19).
- **Change list:** the prose of Parts X–XI is excluded silently and used as predicates (F16).
- **Rivals:** the best rival — "undefined terms are intended declarations, the loops are bad phrasings" — is undecided by anything in the text; it needs the two rewrites of F25.
- **Where the parts came from:** above. Note the mixture: `Sel` is fitted and asserted of a primitive; `Inst` is asserted and openly priced; (R) is borrowed from a literature the document names.

### What would make it harder to vary

1. **Break the loop with a definition, not a clause.** Replace "a binding already in the repertoire" with something `Deploy` does not define — e.g. "a binding of a content instantiated by the system before `e`" — and replace `Sel`'s "itemised representation" with a module-level term ("a record over occurrences"). Only then does Derivation 6's claim become checkable, because only then is (R) not upstream of its own witnesses.
2. **Give `Ownership`/`Can` a base case** (an unowned retained realization, or a least fixpoint stated as one), and move Γ and the transport data into one of the three boxes.

### What this does not show

Hard to vary is not a truth-meter. Finding a leak does not show the semantics false, that explanation is not a question-relevant organisation of dependencies, or that no such definition can be given. Several findings are compatible with "bad phrasing": F2, F3, F6, F10, F37 and F38 could all be repaired without touching a claim. I read the text; I did not check the mathematics of (I2), (O1), (T2), (CT2) or Derivations 1–3, and I did not audit Part XII's physics or Part XIII's quantifiers.

Take the frozen question at its narrowest, as the skill's quick version allows: **"Does `Sel` depend on (R)?"** Two sentences decide it — `Sel`'s "no itemised representation of the defect" and `Build`'s "a binding already in the repertoire". Rewrite both into module vocabulary, then re-run the clause-by-clause order check in Part XIV; if the order then closes, F2 and F6 are loose phrasings and everything else in this report stands as written.

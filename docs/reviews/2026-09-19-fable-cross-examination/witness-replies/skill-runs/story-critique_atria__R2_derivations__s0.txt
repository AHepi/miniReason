## 1. Corrections first

Your six targets all exist on the page as you describe them, and five of the six are real faults. One correction to your framing: Derivation 1's injectivity hypothesis is not itself a defect. The derivation is valid with it; the fault is one step away, in Part V, which cites Derivation 1 while dropping the hypothesis. Two further corrections of degree, not of kind: Derivation 2's problem is not its proof but an under-specified definition plus an unused hypothesis; Derivation 3's proof is airtight, and the over-reach is in its Consequence, not in the lemma.

## 2. Scene-by-scene notes

### Group A — Derivations 1–3: fidelity and what it preserves

**1. Derivation 1 is valid, but it leans on two ingredients the definitions do not supply.** The proof says: "By the subnetwork signature of Part II, sig_C(λ(k))=…, the visible ports of the anchor being those λ translates to V_k." But Part II defines visible ports independently of any transport: "its **visible ports** are a declared subset V_U^vis of its ports, the rest being hidden." Identifying the anchor's visible ports with the λ-image of V_k is a coherence condition between λ's port translation and the subnetwork's declared V^vis, and it is assumed in the proof, not stated anywhere. Second, the conclusion is about a component of E and a subnetwork of D, while Part II's kind relation is defined for a pair in a shared setting: "Two components j,j' are of one forward kind on (ℓ,C) when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on C∖{(1,b):b∈B}." The remark's phrase "a faithful component is of one forward kind, on C, with its anchor subnetwork" therefore presupposes that Part II's definition extends across organizations via (τ,σ). Part V's condition 4 already uses that extended reading, so the groundwork is assumed rather than proved. *Job:* Derivation 1 is what makes the anchored grade's condition 4 more than a stipulation. It does that job once the two ingredients are granted. *Fix:* state both — anchors' visible ports are the λ-image of V_k, and forward-kind is defined for component/subnetwork pairs via the transport's translations.

**2. Injectivity is held; Part V and Derivation 10 forget it.** Derivation 1 hypothesizes "let (τ,σ) be injective on C," and the proof needs exactly that: "injectivity of (τ,σ) on C makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates." Remove it and two distinct pairs of C can collapse to one pair of τ[C] with differing third coordinates, so the signatures are no longer in bijection and the forward-kind definition has nothing to match. This is a real choice doing real work. But Part V cites the result without it: "By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of E has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract." (F1) alone does not entail that; (F1) plus injectivity does. Injectivity is not automatic — a transport whose E conflates two of D's edits has non-injective τ, and that is a live case for any coarse or abstraction-laden model. Derivation 10 repeats the omission: "If (F1) and (F2) hold for t_1 on the extended contract … then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks." Derivation 1's own "What this does and does not show" is scrupulous — "The anchored grade of Part V is where signature on C_adm is required; it is a condition, not a corollary" — which makes the Part V sentence the outlier, not the derivation. *Fix:* in Part V, write "By Derivation 1, (F1) together with injectivity of (τ,σ) on C entails…", or add injectivity to the context in which (F1) is asserted.

**3. Derivation 2: (F2) is a free hypothesis, and the definition omits the port data the proof needs.** The claim hypothesizes that both candidates "satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on C," but the proof uses only two of them: "(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles." (F2) appears nowhere, and the definition of account-equivalence involves no assembled solution set. Free hypotheses are not faults, but the claim reads as though all three are load-bearing. More substantively, the definition requires "the anchored relations proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b) coincide" under "a bijection between their active components." A bijection of components does not identify V_k with V_{k'}; the two relations live on different port sets and are not comparable without port maps. The proof's phrase "the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence" leans on precisely the port-translation data the definition suppresses. *Job:* the definition's real work is to state underdetermination, which the "What it does not say" paragraph does better in prose. *Fix:* make the bijection a component-plus-port bijection, and either drop (F2) from the hypothesis or give it a role.

**4. Derivation 3's proof is valid; its hypothesis is stronger than it looks, and its Consequence overreaches once.** The closure hypothesis runs: "for each (a,b)∈C∖H, each component j of E, and each relation L' … in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of T), T contains a pair agreeing with (E,t) everywhere except that L_j(τ(a),σ(b))=L'." Two things follow. First, quantifying over "each component j of E" presupposes the population shares E's component indexing; that is a substantive structural assumption about T, not something the definition of selection gives. Second, the "admissible class" is defined by realizability within T, so closure says: every relation realizable anywhere in T is realizable at this pair while agreeing elsewhere — a strong homogeneity condition that real populations rarely meet. The proof itself is airtight: the altered pair agrees with (E,t) on H, the survival condition requires only fidelity on H ("a survival condition requiring fidelity on H that **eliminated at least one member**"), so the altered pair survives and differs at (a,b). The over-reach is one sentence in the Consequence: "That is why the primitive layer is *not guaranteed*." Derivation 3 establishes non-determination under closure; the primitive layer's tracker population is never shown to satisfy closure — Derivation 10 stipulates several things about that population, but not closure. *Fix:* "the primitive layer is not guaranteed when its population satisfies the closure hypothesis," or "its fidelity is not established by its selection history alone."

**4b. Part XV's attack (D) is worded so that the proof blocks it.** "A non-fallible selected transport. A selection history H⊊C, in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off H, whose survivor is determined on C∖H. This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis." Under closure, the lemma exhibits a surviving member that differs at (a,b) whenever some admissible L' changes the solution set; so a "determined survivor" requires that no admissible alteration change any solution set, in which case the lemma's antecedent is never satisfied and nothing is refuted. The attack is therefore coherent only as "produce a counterexample to the proof," and there is none. This is a wording slip in Part XV, not a mathematical fault in Part XVI.

### Group B — Derivations 4–5: surprise, and question-finding

**5. Derivation 4 is correctly demoted, and its self-assessment is accurate.** "That a system with no transport is never surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract is never surprised, follow from the definition by unpacking. This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation, and nothing else in this document cites it as support." Both consequences do follow: no selected t means no violation is defined; H exhausting C makes C∖H empty. And the claim that nothing cites it is true on the page. The distinction it draws — "What would be a result — conditions under which a violation at some (a,b)∈C∖H actually occurs — depends on the world and on the population, and is not proved here" — is the right limit stated in the right place. Nothing to fix; protect it.

**6. Derivation 5's "exactly" is not earned. This is the answer to your question: no, its solutions are not exactly the coherently posed questions.** The construction concludes: "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on D." Part III's coherence conditions are: "It is **coherently posed** when Sol_D(1,b_0)≠∅, Q is defined on every pair in C, and S and K are disjoint from each other's complements as stated." Measured against the construction, one condition is unrepresented, one is prose, and a fourth constraint is imposed that Part III never asks for.

- *Non-vacuity is unrepresented.* The boundary is "the target D and b_0, held fixed." No component of D_p tests Sol_D(1,b_0)≠∅. If the target is vacuous, D_p still has solutions, and those solutions would represent incoherently posed questions. The condition is a property of the boundary, not a constraint that filters solutions.
- *The S/K condition is prose, not a relation.* "u_S and u_K are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III." "Consistent with … in the form of" is not an equation, so the solution set of D_p is not determined by the construction as written. The same applies to "Q is defined on every pair in C": the port u_Q has "domain the set of queries admissible on D," and "admissible on D" is not defined, so whether (ii) is enforced or trivially satisfied depends on a reading the page does not settle.
- *An extra constraint is imposed.* "If u_{(a_2a_1,b)} reads 'in' then so do its factors where the composition is defined" makes the in-set downward-closed under factorization. Part III places no closure requirement on a contract — (F2)'s composition law is a condition on τ, not on C. So if the in-set is C, then D_p's solutions correspond to factorization-closed contracts only, a proper subset of what Part III counts as coherently posed; if the in-set is not C, the construction does not say how it relates to C.

*Job:* this organization is what Part X's "constructed question" and Derivation 7's moving episode both rest on, so the fuzziness propagates. *Fix:* add a component whose relation is empty unless Sol_D(1,b_0)≠∅ (or restrict the claim to non-vacuous targets and say so); replace the S/K prose with an explicit componentwise equation enforcing C=(C_adm∩S)∪K; and either drop the factorization closure or add it to Part III's coherence conditions with a reason for it.

**7. Derivation 5's consequence and its limit are correctly drawn.** "What the construction cannot do is add a port to D: the invention of a new dimension of variation changes the target, and this document has no witness for that." This is planted consistently in three places — Part X ("the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing A×B"), Part XV attack (E), and here — and the limit is the same in all three. Nothing to fix.

### Group C — Derivations 6–7: the definition order, and indices

**8. Derivation 6's cycle is broken only under a reading of Build the page does not settle.** The proof says: "the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness." ### Group D — Derivations 8–10: recoding, outputs, scenario

**10. Derivation 8's hypothesis does not support its conclusion, and Part X cites it for more than it proves. This is the most load-bearing fault in Part XVI.** The hypothesis is: "Let φ be a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps λ), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module's admission (A_Θ, Inst, ≺), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and N." The proof then excludes the case that matters most: "because λ is among the transported data, a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted φ, since it does not preserve D's structure." "Preserves D's structure" is not in the hypothesis. The hypothesis preserves A_Θ, Inst, ≺, scopes, meanings and N — not the organizations' port-and-component structure. As stated, a bijection of D's ports carrying pole-height to shadow-length is permitted; the proof excludes it by a condition it never assumed, and the exclusion is the whole point of the pole/shadow sentence. The intended condition is the one Part X states, and attributes to this derivation: "a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise — the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents." Part X calls these "the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8," but Derivation 8 never states structure preservation; it is the missing hypothesis, not the conclusion. This matters, because ≡_ℓ is what (N) and Build's "not a composition of content-preserving transfers" turn on, and those feed (G) and (EK). Separately, the preservation list omits Exec, resources and accuracy grades, which Can requires and which Build, (G), (P) and (EK) therefore inherit. *Fix:* restate the hypothesis as an isomorphism of the whole typed structure — port bijections carrying component relations pointwise and admitted edits onto admitted edits, plus preservation of A_Θ, Inst, ≺, Exec, resources, scopes, meanings and N. Under that hypothesis the conclusion is immediate and the pole/shadow exclusion follows instead of being asserted.

## 3. How it fits together

Part XVI is where the document's argument and its mathematics meet, and the meeting points are the six derivations cited elsewhere: 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8. Four of the six (3, 5, 6, 7) are sound and honestly scoped, and their limits are stated in the same words at every place they are used — that is the document's structural strength and the thing to protect. Two of the six are cited for more than they prove, and in both cases the over-citation is at the load-bearing site: Part V invokes Derivation 1 as if (F1) alone preserved signatures, and Part X defines ≡_ℓ as "the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8" though Derivation 8 never hypothesizes structure preservation. So the plot and the theme do meet, but twice the theme's sentence is stronger than the plot's proof.

## 4. The faults that matter most, ranked

1. **Derivation 8's hypothesis is too weak for its conclusion, and Part X's ≡_ℓ inherits the gap.** ≡_ℓ feeds (N) and Build, which feed (G) and (EK), the document's central predicates. Fix it first, by strengthening the hypothesis to a full structure isomorphism.
2. **Derivation 5's "exactly" is unearned.** Non-vacuity is unrepresented, the S/K constraint is prose, and factorization closure over-restricts. Part X's "constructed question" and Derivation 7 both rest on this organization, so the class of constructed questions is fuzzier than the claim suggests.
3. **Part V cites Derivation 1 without its injectivity hypothesis.** One sentence, but it is the sentence that connects component fidelity to kind, and Derivation 10 repeats the omission.

Smaller, in order: Derivation 6's cycle-breaking depends on reading Build's "for explanatory use of c" as not requiring Deploy, which Part XIV's order supports and Part X's prose obscures (note 8); Derivation 2's definition omits the port maps its coincidence claim needs and carries an unused (F2) (note 3); Derivation 3's Consequence applies the closure result to the primitive layer without the closure hypothesis (note 4); Derivation 1 assumes two ingredients Part II does not supply (note 1).

- Whether Derivation 5's "consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III" was meant as an exact constraint that the author would write out on demand. If it was, the gap is presentational rather than structural — but the claim "exactly" is then still unproved as written.

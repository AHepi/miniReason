## 1. Corrections first

**Note 1. Your diagnosis, checked.** You say the classic attempts "each fail a named condition; a new one must fail none." On the page that is right for three of the four and wrong for the fourth. The lookup is expressly not excluded: *"A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account."* And the one-component candidate does not fail (E) at all: *"The one-component candidate whose single relation is Sol\_D satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3."* So the correction runs your way: the page has already conceded the shape of the counterexample you are asking for. One thing your framing needs to settle before any answer: **"plainly explains nothing" is not a predicate of this semantics.** Every other attack in the list names a condition, a witness, or a derivation. This one names a judgement. That governs everything below, and I say at each step which claims are held by the page and which are my judgement.

**Note 2. The type and its promise.** A formal semantics promises that it will tell you what would make it false and will not protect itself by notation. Part 0 keeps that promise unusually well — *"None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label."* The promise is kept in the attack list and broken in the definitions (Notes 9, 12, 17), which is why the document cannot settle its own first attack.

## 2. Scene-by-scene notes, by sequence

### Sequence 1 — Parts 0–III, the machinery

**Note 3. (A), the attack this critique is running — held, and the strongest page in the document.** *"(A) Sufficiency. Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing."* Its job is to fix the class's sufficiency claim to a single testable thing; it does that job, and it names the four attempts it already knows about. Protect it.

**Note 4. (K) makes kind-fidelity reflexive.** *"Two components j,j′ are of one forward kind on (ℓ,C) when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide."* Job: to make kinds behavioural. It does that. Consequence the document does not draw: with the **identity** bijection, a component is always of one forward kind with itself. So condition 4 of the anchored grade can never bite on a candidate that is the target under a port renaming. This is the seed of Note 9. (Low-confidence addendum: Derivation 8 refuses such a renaming — *"a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to … is not among the permitted φ"* — while (K) admits any footprint bijection that carries the signature. The two places answer "what does relabelling do?" differently and neither says why they may.)

**Note 5. The grain constraint is borrowed from a case it does not cover.** A grain is *"admissible for an occurrence o when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain"*, and Part XIV lists the grain as *"constrained by admissibility."* For a mathematical or modelled organization there is no `Inst`, so the constraint has no definition — and the document relies on such organizations (the odd-order example). The index is unconstrained exactly where it is used.

**Note 6. "Or the reverse," unpinned.** *"there is no admitted pair (a,b)∈C_adm∖C at which Ans\_p differs from its baseline value while Ans\_E(τ(a),σ(b)) does not, or the reverse."* Read literally, the reverse faults a candidate for being **more** sensitive than the target off C, which is not a fault. The protective-contract problem the condition exists for needs only the first half. **Unpinned; I do not pick a reading** (see §5).

**Note 7. A load-bearing prohibition stated in prose, not in a formula.** *"The target's answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component; moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named 'law' does not discharge this."* This sentence does work that the formal condition above it cannot: the formal condition asks only for *"a nonempty **proper** sub-block B⊊Γ"* whose removal moves the profile, and a one-component Γ has no such sub-block, which is why the doc has to say the rest in words. **Cause versus label:** the prohibition is a label in the paragraph; the quantifier in the formula is over the *active* set. See Note 14.

### Sequence 2 — Parts IV–V, the spine (transport, provenance, account, anchoring)

**Note 8. The anchored grade is four conditions doing three jobs, presented as one.** It is described as *"what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose."* In fact: condition 4 separates the decoy; conditions 1 and 3 separate the one-component repackaging (a trivial decomposition, not a decoy); condition 2 separates nothing on this page (Note 16). A reader who fixes one fault by the other's name will be misled. **Fix:** say which condition is doing which job.

**Note 9. The candidate you asked for — the identity copy — satisfies all six conjuncts of (E) and all four anchored conditions.**

*Construction.* Take any target D whose components share ports (a coupled mechanism) and whose answer profile depends on more than one of them. Let E be an organization with D's port set, domains, component index set, footprints, boundaries and component relations, and let t=(π,τ,σ,λ) be the identity in all four slots. Non-declared provenance: there is no construction witness, because Build requires *"a nontrivial binding construction"* and the copy's binding is *"obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡\_ℓ"*; by the precedence rule *"it is constructed if a construction witness exists; otherwise selected if a selection witness exists"*, so give it a selection witness (a population of perturbed copies, a variation operator applied, an encountered history with at least one failing member, an eliminating survival condition). It is *selected*, hence non-declared.

*Why each condition holds, in the document's own terms:*

| Condition | Why the copy satisfies it |
|---|---|
| (F1) | *"proj\_{V\_k}[Sol\_{λ(k)}(a,b)] = L\_k(τ(a),σ(b))"* with λ(k)=k, τ=1: both sides are D's own component relation |
| (F2) | π=identity and the component relations are D's: the assembled solution sets coincide |
| (A) | the query is held fixed and *"Ans\_E(τ(a),σ(b))=Ans\_p(a,b)"* holds by identity |
| Non-circular | Γ = E's components; deleting one drops its constraint, so the profile changes or *"ceases to be determined"* |
| Non-vacuity | `Sol_D(1,b_0)≠∅` is already required of the question |
| Adequacy | *"Ans\_p"* and *"Ans\_E"* agree on all of C_adm, so no admitted pair separates them and no scope can be protective |
| 1 Properness | anchors are D's own components; each is *"a proper subnetwork"* and they cover D |
| 2 Resolution | each separately-variable D-component *"lie[s] in distinct anchors"* — it is its own |
| 3 No global relation | a component's constraint L\_j is strictly weaker than the projection of `Sol_D` onto its footprint when components share ports, so no component's relation *"is … definable as Sol\_D(a,b) or a projection of it"* |
| 4 World-kind | the identity bijection makes each component of one forward kind with its anchor on all of C_adm |

Why nothing blocks it: **every condition above is an equality or invariance between E's and D's behaviour under the changes in C or C_adm, and equalities are reflexive.** Only two of the ten are not reflexive — properness (bites only if D has one component) and no-global-relation (bites only if a component's relation happens to be the projection of the whole solution set, i.e. if D's components do not share ports). For a coupled target neither bites.

What it does *not* show: whether it *"plainly explains nothing."* I can say what I judge — it adds no dependency the question's target description did not already carry and no contrast that was not already in C, so a reader who wants an account to add something gets nothing — but the page has no predicate for that, and the page already made the other judgement: *"in which case it is an account"* (Note 1). **Verdict: your attack succeeds against the stated aim and fails against the stated conditions, and only you can say which you meant.**

**Note 10. Which condition blocks each of the four attempts.**

| Attempt | What blocks it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| One-component repackaging of `Sol_D` | Conditions 1 and 3, and 3 alone suffices even with a proper anchor | Blocked, by name, as the page says |
| Two-component split of it | If the two relations are projections of `Sol_D`, condition 3 (*"or a projection of it"*). If they are the anchors' own projections, nothing — it has become the identity copy | No third option; it is either blocked or it is Note 9 |
| Lookup with a proper decomposition | Nothing. (F1) kills a table of *observed* answers; a table that *"genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change"* has, at each anchor, exactly the anchored projection, which is what F1 demands. The page says so itself | Admitted, and identical to Note 9 under another description |
| Decoy with the same world-kind | Condition 4 blocks it if it differs from its anchor on *some* admitted change — but then it is not "the same world-kind". If it differs on none, condition 4 is satisfied and its status rests on the unproven analysis-of-kinds conjecture: *"That kind-labels are eliminable in favour of signatures and histories is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture, and it is a conjecture."* | Blocked exactly when the word "decoy" is doing work; otherwise it is the mechanism, at that index |

**Note 11. Cost test — the costs you price and the cost you do not.** You price your primitives honestly: *"a decomposition oracle: that is what 'physics says a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden"*, and *"It does not claim that the two primitive packages … are few. It claims that they are all listed."* You do not price the anchored grade: properness and resolution each exclude classes of account (Note 16) and neither is costed anywhere. The price of a condition the writer never feels is a condition the writer will not miss when it starts doing harm.

**Note 12. Choice test on the grade's central choice — a true choice, protect it.** The alternatives were "same kind as a component" and "same kind as a subnetwork, on C_adm". *"It would not be evaluable by the changes in C where C contains no separating change … The right court of appeal is not C but C\_adm."* Two options, near-equal pull, taken with a reason. **Flip the choice:** had you anchored on C, the decoy would be admitted, as you say. The choice is not idle. One fault inside it, though: the reason given does not tell the two options apart — at C_adm the same-kind condition is evaluable too. The reason that does tell them apart is the subnetwork allowance, and it is stated somewhere else: *"the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component"* (Derivation 1). Move that sentence and the section becomes as strong as your own best case.

**Note 13. The provenance rule, run on the copy — the separation inverts.** Build needs a nontrivial binding; a copy's binding is a content-preserving recoding, so construction is closed to it. Selection is open to it, and its witness is easy to satisfy. So the requirement *"with a non-declared transport"* in (A) is met through the weaker category, and the more a candidate is a copy the more easily it counts as selected. That is not a contradiction of the layering story — *"Selection produces the raw material construction works on"* — but it does mean (A)'s provenance clause carries less weight than the attack expects. Three-dimensional character work, flaws and old wounds do not transfer here; there is no protagonist, and the nearest thing to one, the anchored grade, is run through the choice test in Note 12.

### Sequence 3 — Parts VI–VIII, the formal results

**Note 14. (F1), condition 3 and condition 4 are all quantified over *active* components, and the answer profile is not.** *"For every active component k of E"*; *"No active component's relation…"*; *"Each active component of E…"*. Part VI then defines the rest: *"the components outside Γ — the named background — fixed."* So an account is not fidelity-checked, kind-checked or repackaging-checked on its background. I tried to build the counterexample: put the load-bearing dependence in a background component, leave a faithful, properly anchored decoy active. It is blocked — by (F2), *"π[Sol\_D(a,b)]=Sol\_E(τ(a),σ(b))"*, plus the cover clause *"no component of D that some pair in C\_adm touches lies outside every anchor"* — because a background component that carries the answer alone makes the active removals harmless, so non-circularity fails. What the gap does leave is unchecked idle structure in the background. That is a second-rank fault, not a counterexample. So this attempt is blocked, and I can name the blockers.

**Note 15. Remove-in-pairs and swap-two-of-them: the furniture.** *"(D) Boundary"*, (T1) relational transport, (T2) approximate transport, (CT2) retention fixed point are defined and used nowhere after. Remove one and nothing stops getting done. Swap (D) with T1 in every Part and nothing changes — as far as this document goes they are one character: an unused relation. Merge or cut. **What works, by contrast:** the document *represents* the two-routes case and does so in the reader's terms — *"Redundant routes. Γ={a,b}, S={{a},{b},{a,b}}: each is contributory, neither indispensable."* That is the test done properly, and it is the one place the machinery pays for itself.

**Note 16. Resolution is planted in Part V and never collected.** *"Whenever two components of D can be separately varied by pairs in C\_adm with distinct effects on Ans\_p, they lie in distinct anchors."* No worked case, no mention in *"What (E) excludes"*, no derivation, and — see Note 19 — no entry in Part XV's defeat list even though your own (F) attack names it. Its only possible employment on this page is to rule out a faithful account that lumps two separately-variable components into one carrier: the effective theory, the equivalent circuit, the coarse-grained explanation. And that is a genuine mechanism: it can be faithful to its anchors, answer the question, and be no less about the world than the fine decomposition. So resolution either **excludes a genuine mechanism** or is **idle**. Both flips of my own note land on a fault (see §5b, Note 28).

### Sequence 4 — Parts IX–XI, use, understanding, progress

**Note 17. "Assessed elsewhere" points at a place that does not do the job.** *"A true mechanism guessed for bad reasons satisfies (E), and may be anchored; the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX)."* Part IX then gives *"Bearing(c,z,p\_δ) ⟺ Account(E\_c,p\_δ)"* and standing as Lic/Scope/Live over a **declared** record. So reasons are assessed by fidelity and by a declared licence set — nothing there evaluates the reasons. Given your own refusal of declared transports (*"A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does"*), a pointer to declared licences as the place where reasons are assessed needs one line saying why the two cases differ.

**Note 18. Pattern count: the document's central question, and who answers it.** Put plainly: *when is a candidate organisation about a target rather than only agreeing with it?* Answers on the page: fidelity on C (account at a contract); fidelity on **every** admitted change (condition 4); a history (selected or constructed, (R)); reasons (deferred, Part IX, Note 17); and, conjecturally, an unreduced label (*"the analysis-of-kinds conjecture"*). Four face each other; reasons is never confronted by the anchored grade — *"anchoring is not a reasons condition"* — and the label is left outside the ending, which is honest but leaves the theme's strongest answer unargued. The **second primitive package**, N, answers nothing: it appears in Part XI as *"a normative relation … declared as a substantive input"*, is carried in Part XIV, is preserved by φ in Derivation 8, and is invoked by no predicate in Parts II–XIII.

### Sequence 5 — Parts XII–XVI, base, collection, defeats, derivations

**Note 19. Your two lists of what would defeat the class do not match.** Part 0 carries (A)–(F). Part XV carries: a faithful anchored transport that does not explain; an explanation without a faithful transport; a discriminating kind; a non-fallible selected transport; construction reduced to selection; a question wrongly rejected or wrongly admitted; **a protective contract the adequacy condition admits**; **a mathematical error**. So **(F) — "the properness and resolution conditions on anchors" excluding a genuine mechanism or admitting a decoy — has no entry in Part XV at all**, and two Part XV entries have no entry in Part 0. Your attack, the one you are running, is the one the document forgot to write down.

**Note 20. Cause versus label: the ranking.** *"The load-bearing claims, in order of how much falls if they fail"* — the order is asserted, with no reason for the ordering, and it puts first the one attack whose success condition is an informal judgement (Note 1). A free detail presented as if it mattered.

**Note 21. Derivation 1's remark is the best argument you have, and it is in the wrong room.** *"It does not show that a kind-label can do no work, because the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component, because the contract may not be the admitted one, and because histories are not signatures."* That is a three-part reason why the anti-label claim is limited, it is honest, and Part V's defence of condition 4 would be stronger for containing it. Same practice, and equally good: Derivation 4 is marked *"as a remark and not as a derivation, and nothing else in this document cites it as support"*, and Part XII says its selection paragraph *"adds no second definition."* Protect both.

**Note 22. The one use of non-circular dependence is loose.** In the odd-order example, *"Non-circular dependence is witnessed by I\_3 under removal of skewness and by (0 1; −1 0) under removal of oddness, each removing a proper sub-block."* Each matrix is a witness that the *contrast* family contains an invertible member — a non-vacuity/adequacy witness — not itself a removal of a sub-block of the account. The step is sound; the phrasing invites the misreading, and it is the only place in the document where the condition is exercised at all.

## 3. How it fits together

**Note 23.** **Meeting points** (a formal move that is also the theme's move): (K) makes kinds behavioural and thereby makes the anti-label claim a claim about changes; condition 4 makes "about the actual mechanism" a claim about every admitted change; Derivation 3 makes fallibility a consequence of selection rather than a hope; grain-admissibility stops grain-shopping. **Not meeting points:** Part VI's support algebra, Part VII's identification and obstruction, Part XI's aesthetic clause, Part XII's (CT1)–(CT4). Those are formal events with no theme move. The world-rules mostly do make the theme happen — the theme being *a claim to explain is a claim about every change the module admits* — and the part that merely sits beside it is the capability layer. **Medium check:** this is a formal paper, so a definability clause is a device of the metalanguage; *"definable as Sol\_D(a,b) or a projection of it"* cannot be evaluated *by the changes in C*, and the grade's claim that it *"does not inspect a label"* is therefore true of (E)'s conjuncts and false of its own third condition. **Vanishing test:** resolution, (D), T1, T2, CT2 and N each matter once and are absent from the last third of the argument. Each needs a reason or a cut.

## 4. The faults that matter most

**Note 24. First: the anchored grade cannot tell the target's own decomposition from an account of it, and Part 0 asks you to decide.** Every condition in the grade is reflexive at E=D with the identity anchor map (Note 9), so the identity copy is anchored and explains nothing by the standard the rest of your document sets. The aim that should have caught it — *"that does not restate the answer"* — is enforced only by non-circular dependence, which the copy passes. Either that sentence comes out of Part 0, or Build's nontrivial-binding condition — you already have it, *"neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡\_ℓ"* — moves into the anchored grade. **Protect:** the C-versus-C\_adm choice (Note 12), Derivation 1's remark (Note 21), and the four-attempt list (Note 3); they are the parts a reviser must not touch.

**Note 25. Second: resolution either excludes a genuine mechanism or does nothing, and it is absent from the list of what defeats you.** The lumped, faithful, effective account is the class it excludes; the null class is what it has been tested against. Say which it is, and price it.

**Note 26. Third: the guards against repackaging live in prose while the formulas guard only the active set, and the two lists of defeats disagree.** *"moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named 'law' does not discharge this"* is doing exactly the work that NonCircular, (F1), conditions 3 and 4 each delegate to a quantifier over Γ (Notes 7, 14); and (F), the attack on the anchored grade, has no entry in Part XV (Note 19). The symptom and the cause are the same: the grade's own vulnerabilities were never written down.

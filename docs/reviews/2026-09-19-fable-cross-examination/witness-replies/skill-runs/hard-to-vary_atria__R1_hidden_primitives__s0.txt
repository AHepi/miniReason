# Hard-to-vary report: the two-primitives / no-smuggling claim (Part XIV, Derivation 6)

**A note on vocabulary.** The skill's word-list maps plain words to the source theory's terms. Since the document under examination is a revision of that same theory, I use the document's own names throughout — `Inst`, `A_Θ`, `Sel`/`Con`/`Dec`, `(R)`, `(E)`, `(G)`, `(P)`, `(EK)` — and treat the skill's translations only as a reading aid, not as evidence.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained:** the document's claim that the semantics rests on exactly two primitive packages, a stated list of declared parameters, and nothing else — i.e. that "Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module (with the components listed in Part XII), the normative relation where invoked, and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV, by the dependence order there," and that "No residual predicate is left undefined."

**Range:** Parts II–XIII (the claim is explicit that Part 0's prose and Part XVI's derivations are outside its scope; Part 0 says the gloss is informal and Part XVI is results).

**What is asked:** a *rule-status* question — does each defined predicate reduce to the listed bases? — combined with a *why is there none of it* question — why can no undefined predicate remain? The latter is tested by putting the missing piece in.

**Kind of question:** what counts as what under this rule, plus a blockage claim.

---

## 2. The explanation in parts, in the document's own words

- **P1 — two primitives.** "The semantics has two." Package 1 is Θ, supplying "substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set A_Θ, the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation Inst_ℓ, the process order ≺, and the execution families Exec." Package 2 is "the normative relation 𝒩, when a question invokes one."
- **P2 — the declared list.** "Neither derived nor primitive; supplied with each use, and every claim is relative to them: the organization interpretation (V,X,J,B,A,L) … the indices … the contract C … the declared admission relation for non-physical organizations; the query 𝒬; the obligation sets O,P,O_p; an appraiser's license set, contract and withdrawal log; the restriction operation's named background and the edit family 𝒱 … the use tasks U and enabling conditions χ."
- **P3 — everything else is derived.** "Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module … and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV, by the dependence order there."
- **P4 — the order is well-founded.** "the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness."
- **P5 — the standard.** Part 0: "It does not claim that the two primitive packages and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV are few. It claims that they are all listed."
- **P6 — the proof method.** Derivation 6: "Each definition names its inputs at first use; the order of Part XIV is followed from each predicate to its base."

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

### The dependence order, traced clause by clause

I traced the chain the document names as the danger zone:

`(R)` → `Inst`, `(F1)`, `(F2)`, `Sel`, `Con` → `Con` → "an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t" → `Build` → ownership → `Can` → `(CT1)` → `Exec` (Θ); and `Build` → `Inst`, `≡_ℓ`, and "required for the use … deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A)". `Deploy` → `(R)` and a retained capability. `(G)` = `Attempt ∧ New ∧ Build`, with `New` → repertoire → `Deploy`.

**Finding 4.1 — the cycle the document warns about is genuinely broken.** In `Build` the definiens uses `Inst` ("some occurrence of s instantiates"), `Can`, `≡_ℓ`, `(F1)`/`(A)` — never `(R)`, never `Deploy`. In `Con` the exclusion is explicit: "the organization t targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before e — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below." And `Sel` uses "fidelity on H", i.e. `(F1)`/`(F2)`, not `(R)`. So `(R) → Con → Build → (E) → (F1),(F2),(A) → (O),(Q),(K)` is a genuine descent. **P4 is held.**

**Finding 4.2 — but the recorded order contains a false cycle, and hides a real dependency.** Part XIV states: "(P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy, ProducedBy." Read literally, `(P)` depends on `(G)`. But Part XI's definition is:

> Repair_{O,P}(ξ,ξ';Δ) ⟺ ∃o∈O[¬o(ξ)∧o(ξ′)] ∧ ∀r∈P[r(ξ)⇒r(ξ′)] ∧ ProducedBy(Δ,ξ,ξ′;O).

No `(G)`. Meanwhile Part X's merit condition — a condition *on* `(G)` when the content is a question — does depend on `(P)`:

> the found question p′ counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation o∈O such that a deployable account of p′ would repair o under (P), with o failing at the state before e.

So the true relation is `(G)`-for-questions → merit → `(P)` and `Deploy`, and `(P)` → `ProducedBy` → active routes. That is acyclic, but it is the *reverse* of the recorded arrow, and it is unrecorded. If Part XIV's order were true, the merit condition would close a loop. **P5 is loose in a way that only a careful re-read catches.**

**Finding 4.3 — an apparent, not actual, mutual citation.** Part X defines `≡_ℓ` inline ("a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise") while citing "the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8." Derivation 8 (Part XVI) in turn proves equivariance of `(E)`, `(G)`, `(P)`, `(EK)`. The *definition* is in Part X; Derivation 8 is a later result about it. No cycle.

**Finding 4.4 — the order enumerates the named chain and omits about a dozen defined predicates.** Not in Part XIV's Derived list or order: admissible grain, coherently posed, faithful, expectation/violation/surprise, histories and active route, reason use, receipts, `(AR)`, `(CT1)`–`(CT4)`, `Can`, `CanAdv`, scrutinizability, barriers, `(T1)`–`(T2)`, historical index, `(I1)`–`(I3)`, `(O1)`, `(K3)`. I traced each of these I could find; **none creates an undefined reference or a cycle.** So the *substance* of P3 survives; the *record* is incomplete — which matters only because the record is the claim's only evidence.

### The hunt: predicates used before or without definition

**Finding 3.1 — the serious one: an unanalysed causal predicate inside `Sel`.**

> a survival condition requiring fidelity on H that **eliminated at least one member** of 𝒯 and t is a member that survived, with **the environment's filtering causally responsible for t's presence and not merely consistent with it**.

Part II insists "The semantics never asks whether a component 'is' a cause," and derives causal *assignments* from signatures. But `Sel` — one of the two provenances, on which `(R)`, `Deploy`, `(G)`, `(EK)` all downstream depend — uses *causal responsibility* as a condition, and nothing defines it. Part XII's gloss does not repair it: "a survival condition enacted by the environment. It is fallible and checkable as any physical claim is. This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one." A plausible repair sits unused in the module: Θ supplies "the process order ≺", so the clause could be rewritten as a ≺-closure condition. As written, the last causal predicate in the theory is smuggled into the definition of selection.

**Finding 3.2 — two undefined adjectives inside the same definition.** "a population 𝒯 of **realized** transports" and "a variation operator μ on 𝒯 that was **physically applied** at least once." Neither "realized" nor "physically applied" is defined; both are presumably recoverable as "both organizations instantiated by occurrences" and "occurring in an execution family," but the document does not say so.

**Finding 3.3 — an undefined conjunct inside `Deploy`, which feeds `(N)`, `(G)`, `(P)`, `(EK)`.**

> Deploy_{β,ℓ}(s,c,ξ;U) holds when s at ξ holds a representation of c — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance — **integrated into problem-directed activity** and supporting the declared use task U as a retained capability (Part XII).

"Supporting the declared use task U as a retained capability" is formal (`Can`/`(CT1)`). "Integrated into problem-directed activity" is not, and it is the one conjunct in `Deploy` with no defined content.

**Finding 3.4 — `Attempt` has two loose joints.**

> With Attempt(s,c,p,h,e) the relation that c is **actually used**, on an active route, **to address p** — where p is a coherently posed question that s instantiates before e, and for which **the relevant difficulty** is that some obligation in O_p fails at the state before e

"Actually used" is not pinned to `Deploy` or `(R)` (Part XIV asserts "(N), (G) depend on Deploy and Build" without the text making it so); "the relevant difficulty" is the judgement word "relevant" in filler position — the formal content is just ∃o∈O_p, ¬o(ξ_before). "Address p" is also unglossed.

**Finding 3.5 — `Build` carries an unglossed adjective that its own later clause rescues.** "prepares an organization that some occurrence of s instantiates **for explanatory use of c**". "Explanatory" is doing no formal work, because the next sentence pins "the use": "it is **required for the use** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for c on its contract." Recoverable, but the word "explanatory" sits inside the definition of the predicate the whole creativity story rests on.

**Finding 3.6 — two quantifier domains with unanalysed names.** From Part XIII: "𝔈_Θ the **explanatory** contents" and "𝔓^adv_Θ the coherently posed **advanceable** challenges." "Coherently posed" is defined (Part III); "explanatory" and "advanceable" are not, and the sets are independently specified data that appear on no declared-parameter list. If "explanatory" meant satisfying `(E)`, `(U1)` would be near-trivial; if it is a supplied classification, it should be listed as declared data.

**Finding 3.7 — `Con` inherits the prose ingredients of "episode."** `Con` needs "an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t," and Part X characterizes it as: "A complete critical episode contains a **recognized difficulty**, a target available before its criticism, a **conjectural objection**, and a **content-sensitive response**." None of the four is a defined predicate. The formal weight rests on the construction witness ("identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization"), which is fine; but the episode wrapper is unreduced prose. Note the acyclicity is preserved: `Con` needs only a *complete* critical episode, whereas "A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G)," so the dependency runs one way.

**Finding 3.8 — secondary prose (listed for completeness, each doing little or no formal work).** Part XI: "An epistemic obligation requires a **correct** account" — mitigated because `(EK)` separately conjoins `Account(c,p_c)`. Part II: a historical kind is "a predicate on the history … — selected, constructed, **descended from, copied from** — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance," but Part IV gives witnesses only for the three provenances; "descended from / copied from" have none. Part IV's blindness clause: "no **itemised representation** of the defect … lies on the **route** from the defect to the variation" — structural in intent, prose in wording. Part XII: "Possibility is the absence of a **law-imposed** limit." Part IX: "an evidence leaf is a reference to an event with an **interpreted claim**." Part XIII scrutinizability: "criticism can be directed at it, and the result can affect its operative use."

### Inst and A_Θ: are they over-asked?

**Finding 8.1 — the heaviest consumer is anchoring condition 4, and it needs *actual* history, not just admissibility.**

> Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor … Where the physical module supplies a history for D, each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of **actual occurrences** — and not a backup that would have responded identically.

Part IX asserts the supply: "a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the **connections actually instantiated**." Part XII's list supplies "the admitted processes", "the process order ≺", and "the execution families Exec" — from which actual runs can be read — but the word "actual" appears nowhere in Part XII. This is a stretch, not a break: covered by `Exec` if the actual run's family is given, and the document is candid elsewhere that Inst "is a decomposition, not a reading of a meter" and that "the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle."

**Finding 8.2 — `Sel` asks for two things Part XII does not name** (see 3.2): "realized" transports and "physically applied" variation. Both are within Θ's reach but are not listed supplies.

**Finding 8.3 — within remit, and well handled.** Inst is explicitly a relation, not a function ("one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain … Every definition that needs 'the organization o instantiates' quantifies over Inst_ℓ(o,·)"), so nothing breaks if Inst is many-valued. Grain admissibility ("the physical module licenses an organization at that grain … and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes") stays inside `Inst` + `A_Θ`. Derivation 5's question-organization is "a D_p in the sense of (O); Inst, Deploy, Build and New apply to occurrences that carry it" — Inst over organizations, no extension of its remit. `A_Θ` is "stated as a set, since every contract, every grain-admissibility claim and every world-kind claim quantifies over it" — and the world-kind condition quantifies over all of it, which is exactly the stated cost.

### Flip

**Finding 9.1 — the claim cannot detect the smuggle it denies.** Suppose the opposite had been the case: suppose `Sel`'s causal clause, or `Deploy`'s "integrated into problem-directed activity", had been there from the start. Would anything in the document have caught it? Derivation 6's method is: "Each definition names its inputs at first use; the order of Part XIV is followed from each predicate to its base." That is *input-tracing*. "Causally responsible" is not an input; it is an unlisted condition inside a definiens. So the proof establishes order-well-foundedness **given the list**, and is structurally blind to vocabulary. An explanation that would have been asserted unchanged under the opposite condition explains nothing about that condition. This is the flip failure, and it is the reason findings 3.1–3.6 are real findings rather than nitpicks.

### Direction

**Finding 9.2 — the list is written down and the body is claimed to match it, not the reverse.** Nothing in Parts II–XIII indexes itself to Part XIV's order; no predicate says "I am entry 7." So completeness is *asserted* (a summary after the fact), not *built* (each definition carrying its own index). Poke the list — add an item, remove `𝔈_Θ` — and no definition responds. By contrast, P4 *is* built: it names a specific possible cycle and the specific clause that breaks it, and can be checked by tracing.

### Hunt the answer in the starting points

**Finding 9.3 — the conclusion is not hidden in the starting points, and the document says so.** Derivation 6 closes: "Whether the defined predicates capture explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, and this derivation says nothing about it." That is the right separation. What *is* adjacent to the starting points is vocabulary, not structure: "explanatory" in `𝔈_Θ`, "explanatory use" in `Build`, "correct account" in the epistemic-obligation gloss. These are not the conclusion under another name — the formal work is done by `(E)`, `(F1)`, `(A)` — but they are unlisted words doing naming work at exactly the points the hunt covers.

### Rivals

**Finding 9.4 — the best rival, and the change that tells them apart.** Rival: *the semantics has two primitives, a declared list, and a handful of unreduced prose conditions inside five definitions, plus two unlisted declared sets.* The discriminating change is small and decisive: read the definiens of `Sel`, `Deploy`, `Attempt`, `Build`, and the `(U1)`/`(U2)` quantifiers. The document's claim predicts only defined predicates there; the rival predicts prose. On those five the rival wins; everywhere else I traced, the document wins. So the honest report is: **the order claim is held; the no-smuggling claim is loose at five named points.**

### Where the parts came from

- **P1 (two primitives): partly built.** Part XII states exactly what Θ must return, and Part XIV says "that is what 'physics says what a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden." The cost is exposed, which is what makes it criticisable. `𝒩` is conditional ("when a question invokes one") — honestly.
- **P2, P3 (the lists, "everything is defined"): asserted.** Summaries whose completeness is not independently checked anywhere in the document (finding 9.2).
- **P4 (stratification): built.** It names the one cycle that could arise and the clause that breaks it, and it survives tracing.
- **Patches, checked:** the good news. Part IV's "in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below" is a patch that does a job and says so. Part XIII's structural replacement for "begs the question" does a job and says so. Part IV's non-triviality clause ("no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to t, to H, or to the survival condition") blocks a trivial reading of selection. Part XII's "This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one." Part V's "(E) carries no provenance condition." All are patches with second jobs, not absorbing failures.
- **The patch that raises the bar:** Part 0's "It claims that they are all listed." This narrowing is what makes findings 3.6 and the missing "occurrence" count at all. By the document's own standard, unlisted is a failure of the claim as stated.

## 5. What would make it harder to vary

**One new job, and it is the job the user's hunt is already running:** add to Derivation 6 the requirement that *every condiens contains only predicates already defined, declared parameters, or Θ/𝒩 supplies*, and run it clause by clause. Input-tracing cannot do it; vocabulary-checking can. That single job kills findings 3.1–3.6 at once, and right now nothing in the document performs it.

**Five repairs, in order of how much falls:**

1. **`Sel`'s causal clause.** Replace "the environment's filtering causally responsible for t's presence and not merely consistent with it" with a Θ-supplied condition: the filtering occurrences ≺-precede t's occurrence and t's occurrence lies in the downstream closure. Define "realized transport" (both organizations instantiated by occurrences) and "physically applied" (occurring in an execution family). This removes the last unanalysed causal predicate from the theory — and it is the one that matters, because `(R) → Deploy → (G) → (EK)` all sit downstream of it.
2. **List `𝔈_Θ` and `𝔓^adv_Θ`** in Part XIV as declared parameters, and either define "explanatory" and "advanceable" or declare the sets as supplied classifications. `(U1)`/`(U2)` are the only places where a whole quantifier's domain is unnamed data.
3. **`Deploy`'s "integrated into problem-directed activity"** — either make it the already-formal second half (a `Can`-held use task on an active route) or declare it.
4. **Fix Part XIV's order:** record that `(P)` does not depend on `(G)` (its definition shows this), that the merit condition on `(G)`-for-questions depends on `(P)` and `Deploy`, and that the order covers the named chain plus `≡_ℓ`, ownership, binding, `ProducedBy`/`ProducesVia`, `Result`, `Lic/Scope/Live`, `Enable` and question-begging — leaving the remaining dozen defined predicates either enumerated or explicitly noted as unchecked.
5. **Name "occurrence"** in Part XII's supply list (or state that occurrences are the elements of executions), and move Part IX's "physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated" into that list so anchoring condition 4's actuality requirement is visibly supplied.

## 6. What this does not show

- **Not truth.** Tightening every bolt above leaves the constitutive conjecture exactly where it was: "whether the defined predicates capture explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, not a theorem." A formally closed theory can still fail to capture explanation, and the document's own attack (A) — "a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade … and that plainly explains nothing" — is a test the semantics cannot itself render, since "plainly explains nothing" is not a predicate here. That is a limit of the *conjecture*, not of Derivation 6, and the document says so.
- **Not a certificate of acyclicity.** "No cycle found by tracing" is weaker than "no cycle." The order is only partially enumerated, and my trace is a reading of a long document; a reference I missed could change finding 4.1 or 4.4.
- **Loose is not wrong.** Every prose condition I flagged could be true of the intended reading — "causally responsible" presumably *is* meant as a ≺-closure claim. The finding is that the document's own standard ("they are all listed"; "No residual predicate is left undefined") is not met by the text as it stands, not that the intended theory is incoherent.

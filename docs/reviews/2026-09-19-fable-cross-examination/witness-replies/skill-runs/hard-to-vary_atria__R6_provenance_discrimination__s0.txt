# Hard-to-vary report: Part IV's selection witness

## 0. Scope note

The skill's `references/word-list.md` maps its plain words ("fitted", "built", "asserted") onto terms of *Claude Fable Semantics*. The document under test is itself a revision of that theory, so those terms — selected/constructed/declared provenance, blindness, witnesses — are the document's own. I have not used the word list as evidence; every test below is run against the document's own clauses and its own examples.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being tested:** the definition of `Sel(t; 𝒯, μ, H, Σ_h)` in Part IV, together with the three-way precedence rule and its stated discriminators against `Con` and `Dec`.

**What is asked (five sub-questions, held still):** (1) trivial satisfiability; (2) whether "causally responsible" reintroduces a primitive; (3) whether the precedence rule lets a gerrymandered witness relabel a selected transport as constructed; (4) whether either witness type rewrites into the other; (5) whether Part IV's blindness clause is consistent with Part X's construction witness.

**Kind of question:** mostly *what counts as what under this rule* (Part II's "rules and definitions" domain — the clauses are tested by which cases change sides), with a strong *why can it not happen* component in sub-question 4 (obstruction: is there a route from a construction witness to a selection witness?).

**Range:** the formal clauses as written in Parts IV, X, XII and XV, plus the document's own worked case (Derivation 10's trackers). No claim is tested against the world; the "world" here is the physical module Θ as Part XII lists its supplies.

---

## 2. The explanation in parts, in the document's own words

The selection witness, as revised:

> "**Selected.** `Sel(t; 𝒯, μ, H, Σ_h)` holds when there are: a population `𝒯` of realized transports with `|𝒯| ≥ 2`; a variation operator `μ` on `𝒯` that was physically applied at least once in the selection history `Σ_h`; a finite history `H ⊆ C` of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of `𝒯` fails it); a survival condition requiring fidelity on `H` that **eliminated at least one member** of `𝒯`; and `t` is a member that survived, with the environment's filtering causally responsible for `t`'s presence and not merely consistent with it."

plus two further clauses:

> "Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of `Σ_h`: no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to `t`, to `H`, or to the survival condition."

> "A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation; a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted."

The construction witness:

> "**Constructed.** `Con(t; h, e)` holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares `t`, and in which the organization `t` targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before `e` — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below."

The precedence rule:

> "it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance."

The stated discriminator:

> "What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both."

And the patch record (Part XV):

> "A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded."

## 3. Part by part

### F1 — the cardinality clause is implied, and therefore idle

The elimination clause and the survivor clause already force two members. The definition says the survival condition required fidelity on `H` and "**eliminated at least one member** of `𝒯`", and that "`t` is a member that survived." An eliminated member is not a survivor, so `𝒯` contains the eliminated member and `t`, hence `|𝒯| ≥ 2`. The clause "a population `𝒯` of realized transports with `|𝒯| ≥ 2`" is removable without changing the extension of `Sel`. What is *not* removable is the word "**realized**", which is what ties the population to Θ.

### F2 — the variation clause does not require the variation to do anything

"physically applied at least once" is a clause about application, not productivity. A population of two pre-existing transports, one of which is pruned, plus a single application of `μ` that produced neither the survivor nor the eliminated member, satisfies the clause as written. Nothing in `Sel` ties `μ` to the origin of the diversity on which the survival condition acted. **Swap that would tighten it:** "a variation operator `μ` that produced at least one member of `𝒯` at which the survival condition acted." The document gives no reason to prefer its weaker version, and Part 0's own gloss — "produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes, with at least one candidate eliminated" — invites the stronger reading.

### F3 — the history clause is implied, or the definition is under-specified

If the survival condition's stated requirement is fidelity on `H`, then a member eliminated by that condition failed fidelity on `H`, so `H` is non-empty and contains a pair at which some member fails — exactly the parenthetical "(some member of `𝒯` fails it)". So the whole of the history clause is implied by the elimination clause. The only way it is *not* implied is if the survival condition may have requirements beyond fidelity on `H`, in which case elimination could occur with a trivial `H` — but the document never says the condition may have further requirements. Either the clause is idle, or it is doing work the definition does not authorise. Both readings leave it without independent content as stated.

### F4 — the elimination clause is the load-bearing anti-triviality part

This is the one clause the document itself identifies as essential: "Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation." It is held by the job of distinguishing selected from declared, and it is what Part XV's exclusion of "singleton population, empty history" actually rests on — since, as F1 and F3 show, the cardinality and history clauses do not independently contribute to that exclusion.

### F6 — "causally responsible" is an undeclared term

Part XII lists what Θ supplies: "substrate state spaces and attributes; the admitted processes and, for each occurrence, the admitted edit set `A_Θ(o)` …; the interpretation of some processes as controlled actions, meaning an intervention repertoire …; resources and accuracy grades; the instantiation relation `Inst_ℓ` …; the process-dependency order `≺` used by histories; and the execution families `Exec(π,z,i;χ)`." Part XIV states: "The semantics has two" primitive packages — Θ and the normative relation `𝒩`. The declared-parameters list in Part XIV does not include causation.

"Causally responsible" appears in the definition of `Sel`, in Grievance 9, and in Part XII's "The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under `Θ` … a survival condition enacted by the environment." So the claim is said to be *about a physical history under Θ*, using vocabulary Θ's supply list does not contain. The same goes for "the environment" and "filtering" — neither is supplied by Θ, declared as a parameter, or derived.

**The available repair, unstated in the document:** Part IX supplies "an **active route** — a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts" — and histories carry "the acyclic precedence `≺_h` supplied by the physical module as its admitted-process dependency order." Causal responsibility could be reconstructed as lying on an active route / in `≺`. The document does not make this move, and the phrase "and not merely consistent with it" suggests something stronger than precedence — a counterfactual-resilience reading that is nowhere defined. Until one of these is stated, the anti-stipulative force of Grievance 9's answer rests on a term the module does not supply. **This is the strongest candidate for a hidden third primitive**, which would contradict Part XIV's two-package claim.

### F7 — the events clause is the non-circularity condition on provenance

"no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to `t`, to `H`, or to the survival condition" does the same job for provenance that Part V's non-circular dependence does for accounts: it forbids the process from containing the answer it is supposed to produce. It is held by the discrimination job. But its **typing is unknown**: `H` is a set of edit–boundary pairs, not an organization, and a transport is a tuple `(π, τ, σ, λ)` between organizations. What is it for an *event* to be a faithful transport *to `H`*? Under a generous reading, a system that records its own encounter history has an event representing `H`, and would fail the clause — making any learner with episodic memory non-selecting by fiat. Under a stricter reading (the transport's *target content* must be the history-as-organization), ordinary memory may be harmless. The document does not say which. **Test that would settle it:** take Derivation 10's tracker population, give the system a record of its own encountered displacements, and check whether `Sel` still holds. If it does not, the clause over-reaches; if it does, the clause is doing less work than its wording suggests.

## 4. Whole-explanation checks

### Flip (F8)

Had `t` *not* been selected — had it been declared by a modeller, or merely present while another transport was pruned for reasons unrelated to fidelity — can `Sel` still hold? Yes. Given a physical history containing two realized transports that differed in fidelity on one encountered change and one was pruned, all eight clauses are satisfiable for the survivor, because the modeller chooses `𝒯`, `H`, `μ` and the survival condition. The flip is blocked *only* by "causally responsible," which is the undefined term (F6). So the definition does not by itself distinguish "selected for fidelity" from "present while another was pruned."

### Direction (F9)

The definition can be run backwards from the result: pick the survivor, then search for a population, history and condition that fit. Nothing requires the survival condition to have been **in force before** the encounters, nothing requires the population to be natural rather than gerrymandered, and nothing requires `μ` to have produced the diversity (F2). The direction is secured only by the causal-responsibility clause — again, the undefined term. This is the residual triviality: not satisfiable *ex nihilo* (realized transports and physical application are required), but satisfiable by construction over almost any cooperative history.

### Hunt the answer in the starting points (F10)

Grievance 9 asks "'Selected' is just as much a stipulation as 'is a cause'. Who says the selection happened?" and answers that it is "a claim about the physical module, fallible but checkable in principle." But the two notions doing the answering — causal responsibility and the environment's filtering — are closer to the conclusion ("selection really happened") than to anything in Θ's supply list. The answer to the grievance is, at present, the conclusion under a stronger-sounding name.

### What the change list leaves out (F11)

Changes the definition *does* respond to: population size, variation application, history contents, elimination, the causal structure of filtering, the presence of representations in the process. Changes it is **silent** on:
- **Antecedence** — was the survival condition specified before the encounters?
- **Productivity** — did `μ` generate the diversity on which selection acted?
- **Naturalness** — is `𝒯` a natural population, or any two realized transports the modeller chose?
- **Number of encounters** — one pair suffices; a single pruning event can make a transport "selected."
- **Origin of the survivor** — nothing ties `t`'s origin to `μ`.

None of these is stated as a limit, and none is excluded.

### Build the best rival (F12)

Rival: *a transport is selected iff it is the survivor of a physically realized filtering whose removal of a rival was causally responsible for its presence, and no event in the process faithfully represented the target, the history, or the condition.* This drops the cardinality, variation and history clauses (they are implied, per F1/F3, or idle, per F2). **The change that tells them apart:** a process in which no variation operator was ever applied but two pre-existing transports were differentially pruned. The document says not selected; the rival says selected. Since the document's own variation clause does not require productivity (F2), the difference between the two definitions is whether an *irrelevant* application of `μ` occurred — a change that makes no difference to anything else in the definition. At the level of what is actually ruled out, the two are close to the same account, and the document's extra clauses are close to decoration.

### Check the patches (F13)

The document shows its patch: Part XV's "A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded." The patch works against the *named* trivial reading. But two of the three patch clauses (cardinality, history non-triviality) are implied by the third (elimination), so the patch is partly redundant; and the patch introduces "causally responsible," which is itself undefined (F6), while leaving antecedence and productivity unaddressed (F11). The patch narrows the claim — "selected" no longer covers the trivial reading — but it does not say so loudly enough to count as a stated limit, and the remaining gerrymandered reading is not mentioned at all.

### Look inside (F14)

The definition lists ingredients of a selection process; it does not describe a mechanism by which filtering acts, what makes a population natural, or how causal responsibility is grounded. It is an extensional definition — what counts as selection — which is legitimate, but Grievance 9's promise of checkability requires the module to supply the checking resources, and for the central term it does not.

### Add a job (F15)

Add the job: *the survival condition was in force before the encounters in `H`.* The current definition fails it — no clause. Add the job: *the variation operator produced the eliminated member or the survivor.* The current definition fails that too. Both additions rule out the gerrymandered construction of F8/F9, and neither is present.

## 5. Answers to the five questions

### Q1 — Is it still trivially satisfiable by some construction?

**Not *ex nihilo*, but yes over any cooperative history — and the clauses added to block triviality do less work than they appear to.**

The purely formal trivial reading (singleton population, empty history) is genuinely excluded, by the elimination clause. But: (a) the cardinality and history-non-triviality clauses are implied by the elimination clause (F1, F3), so they add no protection; (b) the variation clause is satisfiable without productivity (F2); (c) nothing requires antecedence, naturalness of population, or tie between `μ` and the survivor (F11). So a modeller who wants a given `t` to count as selected needs only a physical history in which two realized transports differed in fidelity on one encountered change and one was pruned, with the pruning causally responsible — and "causally responsible" is the one term that would block the gerrymander, and it is undefined (F6).

### Q2 — Does "causally responsible" reintroduce a primitive?

**Yes, as things stand.** It is used in the definition of `Sel`, invoked in Grievance 9 and Part XII, and is: not among Θ's listed supplies, not one of the two primitive packages of Part XIV, not among Part XIV's declared parameters, and nowhere derived. Part XII says the selection witness "is a claim about a physical history under `Θ`" while using vocabulary `Θ` does not supply. Either it is a hidden third primitive (contradicting "The semantics has two"), or it is intended to be read off `≺` / Part IX's active routes — a repair that is available, natural, and not made. The phrase "and not merely consistent with it" suggests the stronger, counterfactual reading, which is not supplied by precedence at all.

### Q3 — Does the precedence rule let a gerrymandered witness relabel a selected transport as constructed?

**Not under the intended reading, but the blocker is carried by two undefined words rather than by a clause.**

`Con` requires "an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares `t`." Part X defines only "complete critical episode" ("a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response") and "creative critical episode"; bare "episode" is not defined, and "creative critical episode" would make `Con` circular (it requires an instance of (G), which requires `Build`). So "episode" must mean *complete critical episode*, and a blind selection history contains no conjectural objection — no criticism occurrence with an alleged defect and grounds — so `Con` fails. Similarly, "prepares" carries the origination requirement that "Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance" presupposes: if `t` originated by blind variation, no episode prepared it.

So the misclassification is blocked — but by the meaning of "episode" and "prepares", neither of which is defined in Part IV, and the pointer "(Part X)" is the only sign that the heavy lifting is elsewhere. The rule is **held, but loosely fastened**.

### Q4 — Can every construction witness be rewritten as a selection witness, or vice versa?

**Neither direction goes through as the document intends — but one of the two intended discriminators is not actually a conjunct, so a real class of witnesses rewrites.**

*Construction → selection:* Part XV's attack presupposes a construction witness is "an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation." Part IV's blindness clause forbids such a representation "on the route from the defect to the variation." So for a witness that *has* one, no re-description of the same physical history can be blind, and `Sel` fails. The discriminator works — **if** the representation is really there.

But it need not be. Part X's `Build` requires "an owned subhistory … contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers," with nontriviality defined structurally ("neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by `≡_ℓ`") and required-for-use defined by (F1)/(A). The witness-identification list is: "A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization." **No conjunct of `Con` or `Build` requires an itemised defect representation.** It is asserted in prose ("a construction witness contains both") and in Part XV's gloss, and enforced nowhere.

So: a construction witness with a target-before and a structurally nontrivial, required binding, but *no* itemised defect representation — which the formal clauses permit — satisfies both `Con` and `Sel` (`Sel` has no target-before clause and no defect clause), and rewrites as a selection witness without loss. Precedence still labels it constructed, but the distinctness claim ("Construction is not selection") fails for it, and Part XV's attack is live for exactly this class. **The fix is one line: make the itemised defect representation a conjunct of `Con` or of the construction witness, matching Part X's prose and Part XV's gloss.**

*Selection → construction:* blocked by "prepares" and "episode" (Q3). A transport that originated by blind variation was not prepared by any critical episode.

### Q5 — Is the blindness definition consistent with Part X's construction witness?

**Not contradictory, but stated at two different strengths, and the stronger version is not the one in the definition.**

Three mismatches:

1. **Scope.** Part IV: no itemised representation "lies on the route from the defect to the variation." Part X: "A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect" — unrelativised. Part IV permits an off-route representation; Part X forbids one. A process that keeps its defect records off the variation route is blind by Part IV and sighted by Part X. The definition has the weaker ban.
2. **The requirement on construction is asserted, not enforced.** Part X says "a construction witness contains both"; no conjunct of `Con`/`Build` requires it (Q4).
3. **The scalar-summary permission has no counterpart and no exercise.** "a scalar summary of fidelity … is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted" does a *stated* job (keep ordinary differential pruning blind) but is used by no example, derivation or scenario in the document — Derivation 10's trackers mention no summaries. It also leaves a boundary the document does not draw: a scalar summary *plus* a target instantiated before the variation is still blind, and nothing says how close to itemised a summary may get.

And one further strain (F17): Part X's "no target instantiated before the variation," read without qualification, is **false of the document's own paradigmatic selected transport.** Part IV: "`P` is held to `F` by a transport that is typically selected: the population is the variants of the system's tracking machinery, the history is the sensory changes encountered, and the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held." The field — the target of `t: F → P` — is instantiated throughout, before the trackers vary. The contrast only holds if "target instantiated before the variation" means *available within a critical episode*, which `Con`'s wording ("in the episode's history before `e`") supports but Part X's summary drops.

## 6. What would make it harder to vary

Two changes would tighten the definition most:

1. **Add an antecedence clause** — the survival condition must have been in force before the encounters in `H` (or at least not fixed afterwards). This kills the gerrymandered-witness construction of Q1 directly, and it is the one change that the current change list leaves entirely uncovered.
2. **Define causal responsibility from `≺` / Part IX's active routes**, or list it as a module supply. Every anti-stipulative promise (Grievance 9, Part XII's "checkable in principle") currently rests on this single undefined term; grounding it would also secure the flip and direction tests in one move.

Three smaller ones, in order of how much they would secure:

3. **Make the itemised defect representation a conjunct of `Con`** (or of the construction witness), so that Part X's contrast and Part XV's attack gloss are enforced rather than asserted. Without this, the two provenances are not formally guaranteed to differ.
4. **Tie `μ` to the diversity** — require the variation operator to have produced the eliminated member or the survivor, so the variation clause does the Darwinian job it appears to.
5. **Reconcile the two strengths of the blindness ban** (route vs anywhere) and state the episode-relative reading of "target before variation," so Part X's summary stops overstating Part IV.

After 1, 2 and 3, clauses F1 (`|𝒯| ≥ 2`) and F3 (history non-triviality) can be deleted outright as implied, and the definition becomes shorter and harder to vary at the same time.

---

## 7. What this does not show

- **Not truth.** Failing these tests does not show that selection so defined is the wrong concept, or that any transport is or is not selected. It shows which clauses hold the distinction in place and which do not.
- **The definition may be exactly right extensionally.** My finding is that the holding is done by fewer clauses than advertised (elimination, causal responsibility, events-non-triviality, blindness) and by terms not defined in the module — not that the extension is wrong.
- **The document anticipates much of this.** Part 0's "Where to attack this" (D) and Part XV's "Construction reduced to selection" already name the collapse worry and the trivial-reading loophole. My additions are that two of the three patch clauses are implied by the third, that the strongest discriminator is asserted but not conjunctive, and that the anti-stipulative term is undeclared.
- **The stratification is genuine.** `Con`'s target-before clause uses `Inst` rather than (R), and Derivation 6's claim that this keeps the dependence order well-founded holds. That part is held, not loose.

## 8. One next step

Take Derivation 10's tracker scenario and write out the selection witness for `t₀: F → P` clause by clause — naming the event that is causally responsible for the surviving tracker's presence, locating any scalar or itemised record the process keeps, and checking whether the survival condition was in force before the encounters in `H₀`. This is the document's own worked case, so it is the cheapest place to find out whether the antecedence and productivity gaps (F11) are satisfied by the intended example or merely untested by it. The same run would settle F7's typing question: if a tracker that records its own encounter history still counts as selecting, the events clause is narrower than it reads; if not, it over-reaches and needs a scope fix.

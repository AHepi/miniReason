A preliminary note on vocabulary. The skill's word list maps its plain words ("contract", "query", "provenance", "fitted/built/asserted") onto the terms of the source theory — which is the document under examination. So those terms are this document's own, and I use them as such. Nothing below treats the skill's terminology as independent support for any claim.

---

# 1. The question, frozen

Four attacks, held still for the whole run:

**(a) Merit condition.** Is Part X's condition — "the found question p′ counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation o∈O such that a deployable account of p′ would repair o under (P), with o failing at the state before e" — trivial (every constructed question satisfies it) or too strong (it rejects genuine findings)?

**(b) The question-organization's edit set.** Derivation 5 lists "Admitted edits: toggle a pair; alter 𝒬; alter S; add a pair to K with a meaning." Does that set admit exactly the changes needed? And is "add a pair to K with a meaning" an edit an organization can have at all?

**(c) The concept-creation limit.** Part X says the invention of a new dimension of variation "is not a question found in the sense above, because the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing A×B. It is a change to the target organization itself, and this document has no witness for it." Is that a real limit, or does Inst at a new grain already cover it?

**(d) The four open cases.** Part XV lists (i) elimination of a physically impossible rival, (ii) explanation by symmetry, (iii) explanation as selection artefact, (iv) mathematical explanation. Which could be closed with what the document already has?

Kind of question: mixed. (a) and (b) are *rule-status* questions (what counts as found; what counts as an edit). (c) and (d) are *purpose-achievement* questions (does the machinery do what it claims; are the limits honestly placed). Range: the whole document, with Part III, Part X, Derivation 5, Part XV as the load-bearing sites.

# 2. The explanation in parts

In the document's own words:

- **P1 — Question provenance.** "ρ_p∈{declared, selected, constructed} attaches to the whole tuple p, not to its contract alone, with the witness required by Part IV." (Part III)
- **P2 — The merit condition.** "the found question p′ counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation o∈O such that a deployable account of p′ would repair o under (P), with o failing at the state before e. This is the only merit condition in the semantics, and it is stated so that a random new contract does not count." (Part X)
- **P3 — The question-organization.** "A question p determines an organization D_p in the sense of (O), which can be the content c in (G)." Ports: u_{(a,b)} for each (a,b)∈A×B "with domain {in, out}"; u_𝒬 "with domain the set of queries admissible on D"; u_S "with domain the stated scopes and one u_K with domain the declared counterfactual sets with meanings." - **P5 — The limit.** "The invention of a new dimension of variation — a port the target organization did not have, a respect in which nothing was previously varied — is not a question found in the sense above, because the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing A×B." - **P6 — Grain admissibility.** "A grain ℓ is a declared index, but not a free one. (Part XV)

# 4. Findings

### Finding 1 — The merit condition does a job that (N) cannot do, and no swap does it better

(N) is: "New(s,c,h,e) ⟺ ¬∃d∈R_{<e}(s,h), d≡_ℓ c." And ≡_ℓ is: "there is a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise."

≡_ℓ is a relation on **organizations**. Two questions on the same target D share the organization D, and under Derivation 5 they share D_p as well — they differ only in *which solution* of D_p they are. Solutions are valuations, and ≡_ℓ has no grip on valuations. So (N), applied to question-contents, cannot tell a re-parameterisation from a new question. That is exactly why the merit condition exists: "a re-parameterisation of an old question that repairs no obligation is a constructed question and not a found one."

I tried the obvious swap — replace "repairs an obligation" with "is not a recoding of an old question." It fails, because recoding is ≡_ℓ and ≡_ℓ is the blind thing. So the merit condition is not idle: it is the only instrument in the document for this job.

### Finding 2 — But the same blindness creates a dilemma the document does not resolve

Either the content of a question **is** D_p, or it is something finer.

- **Reading A (content = D_p).** Then any previously deployed question on D puts D_p in the repertoire, so (N) fails for *every* later question on D — not only re-parameterisations but every genuine new question about the same target. Question-finding would require a new target, which is far stronger than the document intends, and the merit condition's stated rationale would be false (a random new contract would already fail (N), so the condition would not be what stops it).
- **Reading B (content is finer).** Then the merit condition does the job — but the finer notion of question-content identity is nowhere defined, and ≡_ℓ as written cannot supply it, since contracts are not part of an organization's data.

The document's own framing picks Reading B: the condition is "stated so that a random new contract does not count," which only needs stating if a random new contract *would* otherwise count. So the intended reading is the one for which the necessary notion is absent. **The merit condition is load-bearing and its ground is missing.** This is the single most important loose bolt in the assembly.

### Finding 3 — The triviality worry is not answered, only avoided

The condition requires an obligation that *actually fails*: "with o failing at the state before e." And (P) is "evaluated on the obligations' actual values at ξ and ξ′," so the modeller cannot just declare the obligation repaired. That is a real guard, and it stops the specific case the document names.

But nothing constrains O itself. Part XI says "'Claimed' and 'protected' are declared: they say which obligations the comparison is about." Adopt a maximalist O — a standing obligation to hold a deployable account of every question the system can pose — and for any p′ whose account is deployable, some failing obligation is repaired. The condition becomes vacuous. The document never considers this. Note also that the condition is counterfactual ("a deployable account of p′ **would** repair o"), so p′ need not even have been answered; that makes it easier still.

### Finding 4 — The too-strong worry is correctly acknowledged but unanswered

A question that *creates* obligations rather than repairing them — asked from curiosity or fruitfulness — is rejected. Part XV concedes the shape: "A question found that the merit condition wrongly rejects, or a re-parameterisation it wrongly admits. Either refutes the merit condition of Part X as the line between finding and relabelling." So this is an acknowledged unknown, and the test that would settle it is named. That is honest. It does not make the condition held.

### Finding 5 — The edit set admits what it must, and its main exclusion is forced

Poke the edit set: if it admitted a toggle on a port of D itself, solutions would include questions on a *different* target, and the job "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on D" fails. So the exclusion of target-changing edits is held in place by a checkable constraint, not stipulated. Conversely, toggling a pair, altering 𝒬, altering S are all needed to range over the coherent questions on D. As an edit set *for a fixed target*, it admits exactly what is needed.

### Finding 6 — But the edit set is bounded by declared domains, which is an unnamed second limit

All four ports of D_p draw on declared domains: "the set of queries admissible on D," "the stated scopes," "the declared counterfactual sets with meanings." So an episode can vary a question only *within* a pre-declared space of queries, scopes, and counterfactual meanings. It cannot invent a new *kind* of query, a new *kind* of scope, or a new *kind* of counterfactual meaning — only select from the declared ones. That is the same species of limit as the new-dimension limit, one level down, and it is not stated. By the document's own non-vacuity standard — "every admitted pair excluded from C is excluded by the stated scope S, not silently" — a silently bounded edit set is a gap of exactly the kind it says it does not have.

### Finding 7 — "Add a pair to K with a meaning" is an edit; "with a meaning" is not something an edit can supply

Part II: "An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port." Setting u_K to a larger counterfactual set is a legitimate edit of that kind, and D_p has a component whose footprint includes u_K (the closure condition that "u_S and u_K are consistent with the 'in' ports"). But the *meaning* belongs to the value, and the value comes from a declared domain. So the edit selects a declared counterfactual-set-with-meaning; it does not create one. The phrasing suggests creation — which is precisely what the machinery forbids and what P5 is about. The wording is loose; the substance is not. (Corollary: this reinforces P5 rather than undermining it.)

### Finding 8 — Baseline change is not an edit, and that is a small unnamed gap

"Boundary: the target D and b_0, held fixed." A question that reframes by changing the baseline is a different organization instance, not an edit of one. The document does not claim to represent it as an edit, so this is a scoping omission rather than an error — but it is another instance of the pattern in finding 6.

### Finding 9 — The new-dimension limit is real, but its source is bigger than stated

Inst plus a finer grain *does* describe a target with more ports: P6 licenses an organization at a finer grain, and D_p built over the finer A′×B′ would be a different, representable question-organization. So the limit is **not** a limit on the static representability of richer targets.

The limit is on the **event** of adopting a grain. P6: "A grain ℓ is a declared index." Part XIV lists the physical module as a primitive package and Part IV requires that in a construction witness "the organization t targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before e — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst." Targets are given, not built; grain and module are declared. So no provenance witness exists for changing either — which means *no* re-description of a target has a witness, not merely the new-dimension case. P5 states one instance of a general exclusion. The general exclusion — that the semantics has no account of how a target comes to be re-described, which is a fair share of scientific creativity — is not stated.

### Finding 10 — Case (i), physically impossible rival: partially closable with existing machinery

Part VII already handles the case where the introducing edit *is* admitted. For the non-admitted case the blocker is that K's meanings are declared parameters: Part XIV lists "the counterfactual set K with meanings" among the declared parameters, and Part VII says "what it establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual."

But the document has Build for modelled organizations with declared admission relations, and a transport from a *constructed* rival model could supply a meaning with a construction provenance rather than a declared one. Nothing forbids this and nothing attempts it. So the **provenance** gap is narrower than stated. The **worldly** gap is not: "the objectivity condition then ranges over C_adm only," so even a constructed rival model establishes only that no admitted change produces the effect. Correctly open as to the world; more closable than stated as to the record.

### Finding 11 — Case (ii), symmetry: genuinely open, and the diagnosis is right

(F1), (F2) and (A) are all pointwise: "For every active component k of E with anchor subnetwork λ(k)⊆D, and every (a,b)∈C"; "For every (a,b)∈C." Symmetry is a relation *across* pairs — invariance of a subnetwork's signature under a group action, entailing independence of an output from a port. No condition in (E) or in the anchored grade relates two distinct pairs of C. A symmetry explanation could be *described* (the signature is what it is, and invariance is a fact about it) but not *assessed*, because nothing in (E) is satisfied by a symmetry account that a non-symmetry account fails. Closing this needs a new cross-pair condition. The document's own statement — "whose force is an entailment among the components of E that neither (F1) nor (F2) constrains" — is the correct diagnosis and is itself checkable.

### Finding 12 — Case (iii), selection artefact: partially closable, and the document under-claims

Part XV: "(iii) explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact, which needs two targets and has no construction here," and Part VII: "which needs two targets (the data organization and the population organization) and a transport for each."

But (O) places no restriction on what an organization is, so a product D_data × D_pop is an organization; and (E)'s properness requires only that "the anchors of the active components refine a cover of D's components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of D that some pair in C_adm touches lies outside every anchor" — anchors in both factors satisfy that. Part IV's selection witness is defined exactly over a population, which is what D_pop is. The cross-factor transport that would do the explanatory work can live as a component of E, and non-circularity can witness it: removing it must change the answer profile.

So the materials appear available; the construction is simply not undertaken. What is genuinely missing is the worked construction, not the machinery. This is a finding in the document's favour on sufficiency and a gap in its self-assessment.

### Finding 13 — Case (iv), mathematics: substantially closed internally, open only externally

Part VII works the odd-order skew-symmetric example and reports that the Leibniz expansion "satisfies (F1) and (F2)... It satisfies (A)... Non-circular dependence is witnessed by I₃ under removal of skewness and by [the 2×2 matrix] under removal of oddness... Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation." That is a complete treatment under a declared admission relation.

Part XV lists the case as open only because attack (B) quantifies over *physically* admitted contracts: "mathematical explanation, which falls under a declared admission relation and not under the physical quantifier of this attack." That is a statement about the attack's quantifier, not about the absence of a treatment. Full closure would need a mathematical analogue of the physical module — an admission relation that is not declared — which would be a new primitive. This is the least open of the four.

# 5. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip.** Remove the merit condition: question-finding still counts as creative under (G), since (G) needs only Attempt ∧ New ∧ Build. The parity claim — "Finding the right question is a creative act on the same footing as answering one" — survives, but cheaply. That is consistent with the condition doing work. Flip P5 (suppose new dimensions *were* representable): nothing else in the document breaks. P5 is a stated exclusion doing no positive work, which is permitted — but it means the limit is load-bearing for nothing, and its only job is honesty about reach.

**Direction.** The merit condition is one-way ("only when"), so it does not run backwards from obligations to questions. Good. The repair relation (P) is likewise not claimed to generate questions. No reversal fault.

**Answer hidden in the starting points.** The merit criterion is not derived; it is declared: "This is the only merit condition in the semantics," and Part 0 says the document does not supply "beyond one condition under (P) — a merit function for questions." So the criterion is openly asserted rather than smuggled. That is good practice. It also means nothing holds the criterion in place except the one job of finding 1, and the criterion's *content* — that repairing a failing obligation is what makes a question worth finding — is a premise, not a conclusion.

**Change list.** The document's change list for question-finding covers contract variation on a fixed target. It leaves out, silently: grain change, module change, baseline change, and the creation of new query/scope/meaning domains. Only the new-dimension instance is named. By its own non-vacuity requirement, those are silent exclusions.

**Rivals.** The strongest rival to the merit condition is a question-content-identity criterion: two questions are one question iff their contracts select the same answer profile on the shared target. Under it, (N) would do the found/re-parameterisation work directly and the merit condition would be free to be a genuine merit criterion rather than a stopgap. The change that tells the rivals apart is a question that repairs no obligation yet is plainly not a recoding — curiosity-driven or obligation-creating finding. The document's condition rejects it; the rival admits it. The document concedes this case as a live refuter, so the rivals are genuinely separable and the document is on one side of a real line.

**Where the parts came from.** P2 is **asserted** (declared as the only merit condition, by the document's own account). P3 and P4 are **built** — Derivation 5 is an explicit construction checked against (O), and its central constraint is falsifiable. P5 is **asserted** with a **built** reason (D_p is built over an existing A×B). P6 is built, with two stated conditions. The four open cases are asserted as open, and my analysis finds the assessments uneven: (i) and (iii) are more closable than asserted, (iv) is more closed than asserted, (ii) is correctly open.

# 6. What would make it harder to vary

1. **State a notion of question-content identity.** Define when two questions are one question. Then re-run (N) and the merit condition on question-contents and see which is doing the found/re-parameterisation separation. This single step resolves the dilemma of finding 2, grounds the merit condition or makes it honestly idle, and decides finding 4.
2. **Constrain O, or show the constraint is unnecessary.** Either exclude maximalist obligation sets (e.g. require obligations to be recognized, as episodes already require "a recognized difficulty") or demonstrate that a maximalist O does not trivialise the condition. Until then, finding 3 stands.
3. **Name the declared-domain bound on the edit set** (finding 6), or say openly that question-finding within this semantics ranges only over pre-declared spaces of queries, scopes and meanings.

---

# 7. What this does not show

Nothing here shows the merit condition is false, that the question-organization is wrong, that the new-dimension limit is mistaken, or that any of the four open cases is misdescribed. Loose is not wrong. The strongest result of this run is a dilemma the document has not noticed, not a contradiction; and the two cases where it under-claims its own reach (selection artefacts, and partially the eliminated rival) are points in its favour.

# 8. One next step

Write down the missing definition: *when are two questions the same question?* Then check whether, under that definition, (N) blocks re-parameterisations (in which case the merit condition is idle for its stated job and question-finding is too strong) or admits them (in which case the merit condition is doing the work and needs the definition to stand on). Everything else in this report follows from which way that lands.

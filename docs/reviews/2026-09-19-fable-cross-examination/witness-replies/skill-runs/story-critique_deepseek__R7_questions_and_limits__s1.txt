# Applying story-critique to *Claude Fable Semantics*

The document is not a story, so I ran the five passes with substitutions: a Part or a derivation is a scene, a run of Parts chasing one claim is a sequence, the conditions and witnesses (Inst, Con, Sel, the merit condition) are the characters whose choices can be tested, and the "stated limit" sentences are promises of the type. Pass 3 transfers only through the choice, flip, inevitability and cost tests applied to the conditions themselves; the physical imagery tests (up/down, open/shut, slow/fast) have no counterpart and I dropped them. Passes 1, 2, 4, 5 transfer with the substitutions above.

## 1. Corrections first

Your framing is right on three of four points, and one point needs narrowing, in a way that strengthens your third question. Part III does attach provenance to the whole tuple: "$\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}$ attaches to the whole tuple $p$, not to its contract alone" (confirmed). Part X does add the merit condition, and Derivation 5 does build the question-organization (confirmed). But the document is not of one voice on concept creation. Part 0 says flatly: "What it does not represent is the invention of a new dimension of variation, and it says so." Part X narrows it: "is not a question found in the sense above, because the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing $A\times B$." Part XV (E) narrows it further: "is not representable as question-finding here." Only the opening makes the flat claim. That matters, because a construction that is *not* question-finding is exactly where Inst and a new grain would land.

## 2. Notes by sequence

### Sequence A — The question and its provenance (Part III; Part X; Part XIV)

**Note 1. The whole-tuple attachment is held; protect it.** "$\rho_p$ … attaches to the whole tuple $p$, not to its contract alone." Remove it: provenance on the contract alone means a change to $\mathcal Q$ or $O_p$ is not the provenance of a new question, and the merit condition's subject ("the found question $p'$") has no tuple to attach to. Held by the job "let a finding be a change anywhere in the question, not only in $C$."

**Note 2. Not trivial, but gameable through exactly the device the document elsewhere forbids.** The condition runs: "the found question $p'$ counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation $o\in O$ such that a deployable account of $p'$ would repair $o$ under (P), with $o$ failing at the state before $e$." It is not trivially satisfiable: the obligation must be failing at $\xi$, and "repair" is stronger than "address", so "every constructed question repairs something" is false. But $O$ is a *declared* parameter — Part XIV lists "the obligation sets $O,P,O_p$" among them — and the document's own guard against a declared record doing work after the fact is absent here. Compare (CA): "$J_p$ — specified by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed" — and Part XIII: "characterized by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed." The merit condition has no such clause. Better version, in the document's own idiom: "with $O$ fixed by a record before $e$." One clause.

**Note 3. The yardstick switches inside one breath.** Origin names "some obligation in $O_p$ fails at the state before $e$"; the merit condition quantifies over "an obligation $o\in O$". $O_p$ is the question's obligations, $O$ the episode's claimed obligations. Fix: name one set. (Lenses §6: hold your yardstick still.)

**Note 4. The condition has two possible homes, and they are the trivial and the too-strong reading.** The merit condition asks what a deployable account *would* repair; (EK) asks what was *actually* repaired ("$\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')$", "$\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')$"). Assessed at $e$ — the moment of finding — it demands an account of a question just found, and is too strong. Assessed at $\xi'$, "would" should be "did", and it duplicates (EK)'s repair conjunct. The page never says which. So my answer to your first question is: as written it is neither trivial nor too strong, but it is *unplaced*, and its two placements are exactly the two faults you named. This is also the "added detail undoes another's work" test: the hypothetical repair lets finding off the actual-repair cost that answering pays, which is the one place the document's fair-mirror claim ("Finding the right question is a creative act on the same footing as answering one") is not held by its own machinery.

**Note 5. Planted and never collected.** The merit condition is planted in Part III, cited in Part X, and named in Part XV as "the line between finding and relabelling", but no worked case of a found question, accepted or rejected, appears anywhere — while Part VII works the mathematical account in full. The rule is never shown small before it matters big.

**Note 6. Cause test: passes.** The turn is "a question counts as found"; the shown cause is that it repairs a failing obligation. Remove the cause and, by the document's own words ("stated so that a random new contract does not count"), a random new contract would count. So the cause is not a label. **Swap-two-characters test:** the merit condition and Part V's adequacy condition could not trade places — adequacy catches a narrowed scope, merit catches an unmotivated question. Two distinct characters; keep both.

### Sequence B — Derivation 5 and the stated limit

**Note 7. Derivation 5 is held by three jobs at once; protect the core.** It makes a question usable as content ("can be the content $c$ in (G)"), gives the merit condition a subject, and joins Part III's provenance to Part X's origin. Remove it and attack (E) has no object. This is the document's version of an inevitable scene; the faults below are in its edit set, not in its existence.

**Note 8. The edit set admits the needed changes only as *valuations*, and New cannot see valuations. This is the fault that matters most.** Derivation 5 builds the question-organization from ports: "one port $u_{(a,b)}$ for each $(a,b)\in A\times B$ of the target … one port $u_{\mathcal Q}$ with domain the set of queries admissible on $D$; one port $u_S$ with domain the stated scopes and one $u_K$ with domain the declared counterfactual sets with meanings." The scope, the query and the counterfactuals are therefore *values*, and each question is a solution: "A valuation is an element of $X_D=\prod_v X_v$." But newness compares contents by structure: "there is a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries." Two questions differing only in which pairs are in scope, or in $\mathcal Q$, have the same ports, domains, components, boundaries, edits and relations — the same organization up to valuation — so they are content-preserving recodings, so $\operatorname{New}$ fails. That directly contradicts Part III: "Two questions with the same $D$ and different $(C,\mathcal Q)$ are different questions." The consequence for your second question is sharp: **the question-organization's edit set admits exactly the changes that cannot make a question new.** The changes it admits are valuations; New compares organizations. (I flag in §5 that this turns on reading the $\mathcal Q,S,K$ entries as ports, which the page does say; if the author means them as components, the note falls.)

**Note 9. $O_p$ is not in the organization at all.** The question is "$p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)$", but Derivation 5 has ports only for $A\times B$, $\mathcal Q$, $S$ and $K$; $D$ and $b_0$ are the boundary, "held fixed", and $\rho_p$ "attaches to the whole question through this organization." Origin's difficulty clause and the merit condition both quantify over obligations. So the organization cannot see the coordinate that decides the thing it was built to decide. Add a port $u_O$ with domain the obligation sets, an edit "alter $u_O$", and a statement of whether a change of addressed obligation alone can make a question new.

**Note 10. "Add a pair to $K$ with a meaning" is an edit of the declared parameter $K$, not of the organization in $(O)$'s sense.** $K$ is "a declared set of counterfactual edits — pairs the module does not admit", so a $K$-pair is not in the target's $A\times B$ and holds no port $u_{(a,b)}$; it sits entirely in the value of $u_K$, whose domain is "declared counterfactual sets with meanings". So yes, it is an edit the organization can have — as a port-setting edit — but only because the domain bundles a *meaning* into the value, and $(O)$'s edits index $L_j(a,b)$, which no meaning can be. The work is done by the stipulation: "what such an account establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual" (Part VII). **Medium check:** this is a prose device in a formal slot — the author can say what the counterfactual means; nothing in the formalism carries it. Fix: either give a component relation for the $K$-pair's effect on the answer profile, or move the $K$-edit to the declared-parameter list where Part XIV already puts it.

**Note 11. The limit is stated four times and demonstrated never.** Part 0, Part X, Part XV (E), and Derivation 5's consequence all say the same sentence. A run of returns should grow; this one repeats. More seriously, the limit is not shown to be a limit. The document has, in Part XII, the distinction "the admitted edit set $A_\Theta(o)$" versus "an **intervention repertoire** $\mathcal I\subseteq A_\Theta$", and Part II's rule "A grain is **admissible** for an occurrence $o$ when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain … and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes." That yields three readings of "a new dimension of variation," and the page does not say which it means:
- an edit already in $A_\Theta$ but not previously varied: the fine grain is admissible, Inst licenses the target, and Con can target it ("the organization $t$ targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before $e$"). Covered by Inst plus an admissible grain; the residue is the grain-move, which Part II itself files as "a new claim at a new index";
- an edit in $A_\Theta$ not yet in the actor's repertoire $\mathcal I$: this is capability construction, which the document represents;
- an edit not in $A_\Theta$ at all: new physics, which is the physical module's burden, not the semantics' limit.

So the flat claim in Part 0 overstates; the narrower claim (that the *act of choosing the grain* is an index change, not an originative one) is defensible. Per the method, I do not pick between readings the page does not decide; I report that it does not decide.

## 3. How it fits together

The plot (definitions) and the theme (finding versus relabelling; representation as fidelity-with-history) meet in Derivation 5 and Part X — and that is exactly where the faults cluster. Where they fail to meet: the document's central line between finding and relabelling is drawn with a *declared* set $O$, in a document that elsewhere treats declared data as unable to carry weight ("These three are typed components of declared data, not judgements", Part IX). The one place the theme is adjudicated is the one place the document's own standard is suspended.

## 4. The two or three faults that matter most

1. **Derivation 5's edit set admits only changes that $\operatorname{New}$ cannot see** (Notes 8, 9). Scope, query and counterfactuals are valuations; $O_p$ is absent. So the change "what this question asks" never makes a question new, contradicting Part III, and the merit condition is unreachable by the machinery built to witness it. Fix: carry the question's commitments in $\equiv_\ell$, or define newness for questions over their tuple, and add the $O$-port.
2. **The merit condition's set is declared without the document's "fixed before $e$" guard, and its assessment point is unstated** (Notes 2, 3, 4). Fix: one clause in the document's own idiom, and say whether it is assessed at $e$ or $\xi'$.
3. **The stated limit is not shown to be a limit** (Note 11). Fix: name which of the three readings, or narrow the flat claim in Part 0.

Protect: Derivation 5's core, Part III's whole-tuple provenance, and the four-case list in Part XV — the last is the document's own honest map and the reason the critique has anything to aim at.

## 5. What I am unsure of

Whether $\mathcal Q$, $S$, $K$ are meant as ports or as structures (Note 8 turns on this, and the page says "port"); which of the three readings of "a new dimension of variation" is meant (Note 11); whether the merit condition is assessed at $e$ or $\xi'$. These may be deliberate underspecification rather than gaps — the page does not settle them, so I do not. I read a text, not a proof: no derivation in Part XVI was mechanised by me, and my findings are about typing and coverage, not about the mathematics of Derivations 1–3, 5 and 8.

## 6. One next step

Add a port $u_O$ with an "alter $u_O$" edit to Derivation 5, and state the merit condition's obligation set as fixed before $e$. That closes faults 1 and 2 at once and makes the merit condition witnessable — which is what the document's own attack (E) asks for.

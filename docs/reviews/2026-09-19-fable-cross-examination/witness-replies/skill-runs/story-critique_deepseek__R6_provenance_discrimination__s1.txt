**Framing.** I read the document as a formal text, not a story, so its type is the theory paper: a class definition with a constitutive conjecture and a self-stated defeat list. What that type promises its audience is that the definitions are well formed, the primitives are all listed, theorems and conjectures are marked, the class is not empty-by-accident or trivial-by-accident, and the declared limits are genuine limits rather than gaps. ## 1. Corrections first

**1. Blindness is not a conjunct of the selection witness.** Your summary says the witness "now requires ... blindness defined as absence of an itemised defect representation." On the page the witness is signed `Sel(t; T, μ, H, Σ_h)` and its clauses are population, applied variation operator, non-empty history with a non-trivial pair, an eliminating survival condition, survival, and causal responsibility. The blindness sentence is separately phrased — "A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation" — and is not among the things `Sel` "holds when there are". Your point gets stronger from the correction: the document says the two provenances are "distinguished by their histories", and one of the two distinguishing facts is not carried by either predicate.

**2. There are two non-triviality conditions, not one.** The witness needs "a finite history H ⊆ C … containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of T fails it)"; separately, "Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of Σ_h: no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to t, to H, or to the survival condition." The first is a condition on the history, the second on the events. Your "non-triviality over the events" merges them, and the difference decides note 8.

**3. The trivial readings are excluded on the page before you ask your first question.** Part XV: "A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded." So "trivially satisfiable" has to be aimed at a *non-trivial* construction, and I find one (note 17) plus one ambiguity (note 7) rather than the obvious ones.

**4. Precedence and `Con` read as you quote them.** But "now requires" implies a previous version I cannot see, so I cannot check what changed.

## 2. Pass 1 — scene by scene (each definition is a scene)

**5. Held: the two fidelity conditions are two routes with different jobs, not a redundancy.** "**Component fidelity**" prevents "an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition"; "And the assembled organization agrees" prevents "a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint." Remove either alone and a named failure mode gets through, because projections of an intersection do not determine the intersection. **Mark: both held; protect.**

**6. Held: the vacuity guard in non-circular dependence guards itself.** "The witness: there exists an edit τ(a) on E … under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way. Removing all of Γ changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing." A condition whose cheapest witness is excluded by its own next sentence is a scene doing its job. **Protect.**

**7. Fails: "causally responsible … and not merely consistent with it" is a contrastive notion the document's own vocabulary cannot supply.** What is on the page: "with the environment's filtering causally responsible for t's presence and not merely consistent with it." Its job: exclude a survival condition that was *applied* while the survivor's presence was settled by something else — the shadowed elimination your straw-man worry is about. Why it fails: on the non-contrastive reading ("responsible for t being there at all") any applied filter over a two-member population satisfies it, and the sentence then says nothing; on the contrastive reading ("responsible for t rather than t′") it needs a difference-making relation, and Part XII's list of what Θ supplies — "the admitted edit set A_Θ(o) … the **intervention repertoire** I ⊆ A_Θ … the process-dependency order ≺" — contains no such relation, while Part II insists "The semantics never asks whether a component 'is' a cause." So yes, as written it is a third primitive, or an ambiguity that decides a membership question. Better version, in the document's own idiom: *t's presence is due to the survival condition when some edit in I alters that condition and changes which member of T is present.* Extra work: it makes the clause checkable in A_Θ, and it kills the straw-man population that note 17 leaves standing.

**8. Fails, on typing: the events clause has two objects that are not organizations.** The sentence is "no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to t, to H, or to the survival condition." A transport is "a transport from an organization D to an organization E"; H is "a finite history H ⊆ C of edit–boundary pairs"; a survival condition is a condition. Two of the three targets cannot be the target of a transport. A condition that cannot be evaluated cannot be the load-bearing separation it is being asked to be. Fix in the document's own idiom: Derivation 5 already builds an organization out of a question-parameter tuple, so the same construction can be applied to H and to the survival condition, and the clause then reads as written. Extra work: the doc needs this anyway for "the process represents the test" (see notes 15, 18).

**9. Held: the blindness line is drawn exactly where it should be, and it is sharp.** "a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted"; a permitted record need not know *what* would have held. Swaps: a scalar summary survives; a list of *which* pairs failed, without the counterfactual, survives (it is not "a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held"); a pairing of the failed state with the right answer does not. So the line is "does the process know what would have been right", not "does the process know it failed." **Mark: held on the route-qualified reading — and the qualifier is the problem in note 15.**

**10. Fails: `Build`'s nontriviality is a claim about a description, and that is where your gerrymander lives.** On the page, a binding is nontrivial "when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ", and ≡_ℓ is "a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other." ≡_ℓ carries bindings to bindings, so a binding is "obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ" only if the carriers' *description* contains it. The witness chooses the grain at which the incoming carriers are described, and Part II constrains grains only from below: "a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed." Coarsen the incoming carriers so the selected binder is not a component there, and a binding that arrived by variation-and-survival is no longer "on the carriers" — and the construction witness that says so can be gerrymandered, exactly as you suspected. Fix: nontriviality also fails when an admitted process of Θ, applied to the incoming carriers at the same grain, realizes the binding. Extra work: it turns the condition into a claim about A_Θ rather than about a description, which is the only kind of claim the document says it accepts, and it makes a coarse redescription useless as evidence.

**11. Held: the price of the module is stated where the bill comes due.** "the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle: that is what 'physics says what a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden." **Protect** — but see note 27, because the one place it decides the theme is note 10.

**12. Mirror: the three-provenance pattern is a repeat, and the one that contrasts is the one to keep.** Declared/selected/constructed returns at transports (Part IV), at questions (Part III), and at epistemic standing (Part IX's licence set). Repetition is the weaker kind of mirror. The contrast is Part V: "(E) carries **no provenance condition** … because a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history. Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK), which each require Sel ∨ Con explicitly." One act (declaration), two verdicts, with a stated reason for the difference. **Protect the contrast; the repeats need no defence.**

**13. Returning device, and what it brings each time.** The "witness" device returns three times: the non-circular witness in (E), the construction witness in (X), the selection witness in (IV) — and each return brings a stronger claim: first that the answer does not sit in the input, then that the binder was made rather than received, then that the survivor was chosen by the world rather than by its author. A run of returns that grows is structure doing its own work; a reviser must not break the growth (which is what note 17 would do if left). Also, minor duplication that is **not** a fault: the baseline-solvability clause is a conjunct of both Part III's coherence and (E)'s non-vacuity; each has a second job (S/K disjointness; silent exclusions), so keep both, but say which is prior.

## 3. Pass 2 — sequences and the chain of questions

**14. Planted and never collected: "the discrimination requirement of Part IV" is a name with no referent.** It appears in Part 0's attack list — "construction reduces to selection under the discrimination requirement of Part IV" — and again in Part XV, where the collapse test is "rewritten as a selection witness satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV, without loss." No clause in Part IV bears that name. The two candidates are the non-trivial-pair clause and the blindness sentence, and they are neighbours in the same paragraph with different scopes (notes 2, 15). Consequence: the document's own defeat condition for the collapse cannot be run, so the collapse cannot be tested. This is the single place I would put a first fix.

**15. The chain of questions is swapped inside Part IV's own paragraph: blindness is route-qualified in one statement and unqualified in the other.** Part IV: "no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation." Part X, and again verbatim in Part IV's closing paragraph: "A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both." A process that keeps an itemised failure log in a subsystem that feeds the repertoire but not the mutation is blind under the first and not under the second. This is not an open question the page declines to settle; the page settles it twice, differently. Fix: keep the route qualifier but use the document's own route notion (Part IX: "a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result"), or drop the qualifier and check the witness's own inventory ("A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization"). Extra work: the second version makes the witness's list of parts do the check, at no new cost.

**16. Cause versus label at the document's biggest turn.** The turn is "Construction is not selection." Its stated cause is the itemised defect representation. Remove that cause and ask whether the turn still happens: yes — the two provenances survive as two differently parameterised predicates, the precedence rule, and (R), which accepts either: "`Rep_ℓ(o,c) ⟺ ∃D ∃t [Inst_ℓ(o,D) ∧ Faithful_{C_c}(t: c → D) ∧ (Sel(t) ∨ Con(t))]`." So the stated cause is a label, and the real cause is that the semantics needs creativity to have its own provenance (`Con` requires "an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t, and in which the organization t targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before e"). Note also that the sentence stating the cause appears twice, in two different parts, in identical words — which is how a commentary comes to look like a definition.

**17. The one trivial-satisfaction route that survives the document's own exclusions.** Clause: "a variation operator μ on T that was **physically applied** at least once in the selection history Σ_h." Its job: ensure the survivor's rivals are products of variation, so that the survivor's presence is explained by selection and not by whoever assembled the inventory. Why it fails: an application is required, not an effect. T is only "a population of realized transports", so a member need only exist. Swap test: take t and a rival t′ produced elsewhere and merely listed; apply μ once to a third variant that is discarded and never listed; let the survival condition eliminate t′. Every clause holds, and the swap — μ replaced by an irrelevant operator — changes nothing, so no job is holding the clause as written. This is the answer to your first question: yes, there is still a trivial satisfaction, it is not the one the document excluded, and the guarding job is the one your "causally responsible" question is about. Fix: μ's application must be responsible for the presence or the form of some eliminated member (or for the difference between t and some eliminated member). Extra work: it makes the population's difference an event rather than an inventory.

**18. Explaining scenes: the small case is shown before the big one, with one exception, and the document says which of its own scenes is not evidence.** The rules are shown small first — the two balances in Part VII ("the readings identify b_A − b_B and not x"), the I₃ and 2×2 witnesses for the skew result, the two trackers in Derivation 10 — and the last is labelled honestly: "This is a scenario, not a consistency witness." That is unusually good discipline and a reviser must not promote it. The exception is the rule that arrives big and unshown: causal responsibility, note 7.

## 4. Pass 3 — people and their choices

**19. The choice test on the precedence rule: a true choice, and its cost is never paid.** Options on the page: (a) precedence, constructed over selected over declared; (b) both provenances carried, with a creativity attribution required to name a witness not shadowed by a selection route; (c) selection over construction. (c) is visibly bad. (a) and (b) pull almost equally: (a) keeps one provenance per transport, which the layering story wants ("A physical system may hold selected transports between field and primitive layer and constructed transports at the simulation layer"), while (b) matches the document's own admission that "Selection may continue to operate beneath construction". So the choice is real. Flip it: under (c), a learner's reconstruction counts as selected, `Build`, `New` and `Origin` cannot fire, and (EK) has no instances — so the choice is not idle. Cost test: the price of (a) is that an originative act must be shown against a possible selection route, and nobody in the document pays it. Part X stages a **Selection** response — "H₀ is extended to include occlusions; μ re-tunes t₀ within its population" — and a **Construction** response to the same violation, but as two different transports, so no single transport ever carries both histories and the conflict is never seen on stage.

**20. The choice scene is where the fault is, by the inevitability test.** In the choice scene itself — the precedence sentence — two real options are not on the page. Read against Part X's two responses, that sentence concedes that a selection route can produce the very structure the construction witness claims to have built. **21. ## 5. Pass 4 — theme as a pattern

**22. The question, put plainly: when a system's structure fits something, what must be true of its history for the fit to be credited to the system as knowing? — and the pattern count passes.** Each character answers differently:

| Who | How they answer | Faces whom |
|---|---|---|
| Θ, the module | By licensing a decomposition | Nobody; it is the listed primitive |
| Declaration | By the modeller's assertion | (R): "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does" |
| Selection | By the world's elimination on a history | Construction, in Part X's scenario |
| Construction | By an owned episode with a defect representation and a prior instantiated target | Selection, same scene |
| The question | Constructed provenance plus the merit condition | The re-parameterisation it rejects |
| The criticism | Bearing earned through Account | The adverse signal that never earns it |
| The appraiser | A declared licence set | Nobody challenges it |
| The normative relation | Declared when invoked | Refused a derivation by name |

Two answers face no challenger — the module's and the appraiser's — and both are declared to be declared data ("These three are typed components of declared data, not judgements"), i.e. claims about who is using the theory, not about the world. So the theme is not short of an answer anywhere.

**23. The fair mirror test on selection against construction: here the document claims a difference its own rules do not enforce.** Lay the two acts side by side. Did both have options? Selection's options are its population; construction's are its conjectures. Did both know the cost? Construction does, by definition, if note 15 is fixed; selection does not, by design. Did both gain? Both produce the survivor. What did each do afterwards? Selection has no afterwards clause at all; construction has only the second half of a sentence. So when the document treats the two acts as different in kind, the difference is carried by one clause — blindness — which is (a) not a conjunct of `Sel` (note 1) and (b) stated at two different strengths (note 15). Fix the clause and the mirror is fair; leave it and the document is letting a selected binder be credited as built, which is the mirror failure in the opposite direction from the one the skill usually warns about.

**24. The unquestioned first case is questioned, which is worth saying plainly.** The pattern's first appearance is Part 0's opening: "An explanation is a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**, held to a target by a **transport** that is checked by **what changes when things are changed**." Attack (B) questions exactly that — "a genuine explanation whose organization cannot be captured by *any* transport satisfying component fidelity under *any* admitted contract" — and Part XV lists four cases still open. A theory that turns its opening sentence into an open question is rare and must be protected. What is *not* questioned is the single orientation of the transport, and it does not need to be: it is used twice, in the same direction, at "an explanation E is held to its target D by t: D → E" and at "a carrier's organization D … is held to the content c it represents by t: c → D".

**25. The ending and the one working open question.** Part XV settles: "None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label." Part X leaves one thing open — "The invention of a new dimension of variation … is a change to the target organization itself, and this document has no witness for it." That one is working, not a hole: what holds each side is on the page (Derivation 5's question-organization "is built over an existing A × B"), nothing settles whether such an episode exists, and any "fix" would be a different theory. Leave it alone. Everything else you might want to call a mystery here — the (B) cases (ii) and (iii) — is labelled as a limit and should be treated the same way.

## 6. Pass 5 — how it all fits

**26. Meeting points: four, so the plot is not a run of puzzles with the theme bolted on.** The reversed calculation in Part V ("it is an account at that contract, and not anchored as a production account"); the court-of-appeal paragraph, where a structural choice is justified thematically ("The right court of appeal is not C but C_adm: a kind-claim about the world is a claim about every change the module admits"); Part II's admissibility rule ("a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed"); and Part IX's Bearing ("An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears").

**27. The world rule does express the theme, and the fault is that rule's blind side.** Both the grain-admissibility rule and the adequacy condition enforce "no credit without a test". But admissibility constrains fineness only from below, and adequacy ranges over descriptions at the contract. Nothing in the semantics enforces "no construction without a route" — which is exactly notes 10 and 20. That is the one-line diagnosis: **the document polices descriptions against changes and never polices histories against routes, and the two provenance clauses that would do it are the two that are not conjuncts of anything.**

**28. Medium check and the vanishing test.** The document's own standard for a condition is "Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes. None inspects a label." All six conjuncts of (E) meet it. Two provenance clauses do not: "and not merely consistent with it" (contrastive), and "it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists" (existential over descriptions). Vanishing test: the three-layer paragraph (sensory field, primitive layer, simulation layer) matters in Part IV and vanishes from the formalism — it is absent from Part XIV's dependence order and used only by Derivation 10, which is "a scenario, not a consistency witness". Removing any one of the three costs nothing in the predicates, and removing all three costs nothing either, so this is not two routes to one job; it is texture with a single framing job (where selection actually happens). Texture is allowed once; do not cut it, and do not cite it as load-bearing. By contrast, Part VI's support machinery survives its own removal test because it is held twice — once by the infinitary case ("every unbounded index set determines x = 0; no minimal support and no singleton witness exists") and once by condition 4's active routes.

## Your five, answered

- **Trivially satisfiable?** Yes, in one respect that survives the document's own exclusion of the trivial readings: μ must be *applied*, not *responsible* (note 17).
- **Does 'causally responsible' reintroduce a primitive?** Yes as written, and it is load-bearing, because the document's defence of 'selected' rests on a one-clause answer where three clauses do the work (notes 7, 17).
- **Does precedence let a plainly selected transport count as constructed by gerrymander?** Yes, and the location is not the witness clauses, which each do work, but the grain at which the *incoming carriers* are described in `Build`'s nontriviality condition, plus the absence of any shadowing clause (notes 10, 19, 20).
- **Can each be rewritten as the other?** Both directions, for opposite reasons that are the same fault: construction rewrites to selection whenever the defect record sits off the route (note 15), and selection rewrites to construction whenever the carriers are described coarsely enough that the selected binder is not on them (note 10).
- **Is blindness consistent with Part X's construction witness?** No. Part X states the same condition at a different strength, drops the route qualifier, and treats it as a conjunct of neither `Sel` nor `Build` (notes 1, 15, 16).

## The faults that matter most, ranked

**First: the provenance separation is asserted and nowhere enforced** — notes 1, 14, 15, 16, 17 read as one fault. The document's own collapse test (D) names a requirement ("the discrimination requirement of Part IV") that has no referent; the witness signature carries no blindness argument; the two statements of blindness disagree; μ need not be responsible for anything; and the distinguishing sentence appears twice as commentary and no time as a conjunct. Nothing in (G), (EK) or (R) can catch this, because (R) accepts `Sel ∨ Con`.

**Second: causal responsibility is a contrastive notion in a document whose stated method is edit-signature** — note 7. Worth fixing before the first fault, because a one-line fix in A_Θ makes note 17's population an event and disposes of the straw-man case.

**Third: the precedence rule has no shadowing clause, and `Build`'s nontriviality is a claim about a description** — notes 10, 19, 20. It is invisible for representation and shows only in creativity attributions, which is precisely where the document's constitutive conjecture puts its weight.

**What a reviser or adapter must protect:** the (F1)/(F2) pairing; the written vacuity guard in non-circular dependence; the blindness line drawn at "knows what would have been right", with scalars permitted; the court-of-appeal paragraph and the admissibility rule; Part V's provenance-free (E) with its refusal to settle history before an account can be assessed; Derivation 3's honesty about the parametric population; and the stated open limit on new dimensions of variation. Do not turn any of these into concessions to a fault.

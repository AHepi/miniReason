# Pass 3 — People and choices

**55. Choice test on non-circular dependence.** 20 Part V: “there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\)… that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)”. The two real options are “proper sub-block” and “all of Γ”. The text says: “Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing.” So the choice is settled by the job “do not witness with a trivial removal”. Held.

**56. Flip the choice on contract adequacy.** Had contract adequacy not been added, a protective contract could pass (E). 20 Part V: “This is the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change”. Had it been defined only as “stated scope”, the job would fail. The choice is not idle.

**57. Cost test on K.** The cost of K is stated: “\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit — each with a stated meaning”. But the cost is not felt in the formal machinery: (F1) quantifies over “every \((a,b)\in C\)” and uses “\(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)”, while Part IV defines “\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\)”. For \(a\notin A_D\), \(\tau(a)\) is undefined. The price is in undefinedness, not in a worked example.

**58. Pattern count on provenance.** The central question is: “Does provenance matter to fidelity?” 20 answers differently in different places. Part IV: “What the semantics denies is that a component’s history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract.” Part V anchored grade condition 4: “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route**… and not a backup that would have responded identically.” That is a history condition inside an adequacy grade. The answers are not the same.

**59. The mirror is unequal: selection is denied “itemised representation” in one sentence and then given “faithful transport to” in the next.

**60. Swap two characters.** Can “selected” and “constructed” trade places? No. Part IV: “it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists”. The precedence and the witnesses differ. They are not one character.

# Pass 5 — How it all fits

**64. Meeting points.** The plot and theme meet in Part IV’s “Selected” and Part V’s “Two grades”. Part IV says: “a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect”; Part V condition 4 says: “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route**”. These are the places where provenance, fidelity and history are supposed to become one mechanism. They do not meet cleanly, because Part IV’s clause uses “faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition”, and Part V’s condition 4 uses “one forward kind… with its anchor”.

**65. World and theme.** The world rule “\(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\)” is meant to express the theme “scope-honesty is recorded”. But the rule breaks the typing of (K): “a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\)” against \(K\) as “pairs the module does not admit”. The rule does not express the theme; it sits beside it.

**66. Medium check.** This is a formal theory, not a film or prose, so the skill’s medium check does not transfer. One line and move on.

**67. Vanishing test.** \(A_\Theta(o)\) appears in Part XII: “the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\)”. \(A_\Theta\) appears in Part III: “the physically admitted edits \(A_\Theta\) (Part XII)”; Part V: “\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)”; Part XIV: “the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\)”. **68.** The document’s best move is the two-grade structure: “Account at a contract” and “anchored account”, stated in Part V as “Account-hood at \(C\)” and “anchored account-hood”.

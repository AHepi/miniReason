# 1. Corrections first

**1. Your paraphrase is right about the location and wrong in three words that carry your strongest attack.** You write that adequacy is "no admitted pair outside C at which the target's answer moves while the candidate's does not, or the reverse". The page says:

> "there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse."

"Moves" and "differs from its baseline value" are not the same test. Two profiles that both leave the baseline, to *different* values, pass as written while plainly telling candidate and target apart. That is where your too-weak attack lands, and the pages elsewhere make it stronger, not weaker: Part V's own gloss of the same condition — "An account whose contract excludes an admitted change that would tell it apart from the target fails adequacy" — states the disagreement test, not the movement test. Your attack is written in the gloss's vocabulary, so by the document's own words it lands.

**2. The revision is not a smuggled-in commitment; it is a promise already on the page, and the document has already named your attack as the one that would refute it.** Grievance 4: "An objectivity claim is the conjunction, and the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism." Part XV: "**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** … This shows the adequacy condition is too weak, and objectivity would again be contract-relative only." So the written position is not "the condition suffices" but "here is the case that would break it". Your job is to say whether it lands, and where. It does, in part.

*Type, medium, intent.* This is a foundational semantics with a named-refuter list, in mathematical prose, whose stated aim is to define classes and name what would defeat them ("It defines the classes. Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment"). I judge it against that aim, from the page only.

# 2. The five passes

## Pass 1 — Part by Part (scenes)

**3. Part III's contract form is held; protect it.** \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\). Its job is to keep counterfactual pairs out of the adequacy range. Swap test: the near neighbour a fair writer might choose, \(C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap(S\cup K)\), would let a pair that is both admitted and in \(K\) sit inside \(C\) and be shielded from adequacy. The written form blocks that. Held by the job "keep \(K\) out of the objectivity range". Do not touch it.

**4. Part III's joint clause on \(S\) and \(K\) is garbled and does no work.** Quote: "\(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated." Read literally that forces \(S\subseteq K\) and \(K\subseteq S\). Job: constrain the pair \((S,K)\) jointly. It fails; nothing else on the page constrains them jointly. Better version: "\(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint." Low stakes formally, but it is the clause your "collide with \(K\)" question runs into first.

**5. The reason slot is planted and never collected.** Quote: "\(S\) is a **stated scope**: the declared subset of admitted pairs the claim ranges over, with the reason for each exclusion recorded." Remove the clause "with the reason for each exclusion recorded": nothing stops getting done. Non-vacuity checks only that exclusions go through \(S\); adequacy checks movement, not reasons. This is the natural home of your fix — see note 11.

**6. Non-vacuity's "not silently" clause is idle — a label for what the form already guarantees.** Quote: "every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently." Given \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), a pair outside \(S\) is outside \(C\) by construction, so the clause cannot fail. Cause-versus-label: it names a guarantee of the form as if it were a check. Cut it, or move the job to adequacy, where legitimacy is actually tested.

**7. Part V's preamble is falsified by the revision.** Quote: "holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold, each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\)." The display right below lists six conjuncts, and the last of them — "there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)" — is not a condition under the changes in \(C\) at all. Better: "five named conditions; adequacy ranges over the module's admitted pairs outside \(C\)". Small, but it is the sentence a referee will quote back at you.

**8. Adequacy is an XOR-of-movement test, and it is too weak in one direction.** The condition (note 1) fails only when exactly one of the two profiles leaves the baseline. Counterexample the page does not exclude: at some \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) leaves the baseline for value \(1\) and \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) leaves it for value \(2\). Both differ from baseline; neither fails the clause; the pair tells candidate from target; a scope excluding exactly that pair passes. So Part XV's own attack ("excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target") is not refuted by the formula, only by the formula plus a case it omits. *Better version, one clause, doing one named job:* keep the condition, and add "and where both differ from baseline, \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)". That closes the both-move case without importing full answer-fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

**9. The other direction is too strong, and it contradicts Part 0's announced division of labour.** The same clause fails whenever the target's answer moves at an admitted pair outside \(S\) and the candidate's does not — that is, whenever the target has *any* admitted dependence outside the scope that the candidate does not share. Part 0 says: "The first grade is what can be assessed within a question. The second is … a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract." The revision puts a \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)-level condition into the first grade. So the same clause is too weak where exactly one value changes and too strong wherever the candidate is honestly partial. And its bite is set outside the condition: for a physical organization \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is \(A_\Theta\times B\) (Part XII), which is enormous; for a declared admission relation it is whatever was declared (Grievance 7), which can be tiny. Too strong and too weak are the two ends of one dial that the condition does not hold.

**10. The baseline is reconstructible but never stated.** Part III: the contract "contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)". Part V asks whether a profile "differs from its baseline value" without naming the pair. Reconstruction: the target's baseline is \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\); the candidate's is \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\), which coincides by \((A)\) at the baseline pair and \(\tau(1)=1\) (F2). Two loose ends: nothing says \((1,b_0)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\) (if it sat in \(K\) the "baseline" would be a counterfactual pair), and nothing says \(\sigma(b_0)\) is \(E\)'s declared baseline boundary. Better: name the baseline pair in the condition and place it in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\).

**11. \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is never defined, and adequacy needs it off \(C\).** Part III defines only the target's profile: "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\)." The candidate's profile appears only inside \((A)\): "For every \((a,b)\in C\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)." Adequacy evaluates \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) at \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) for \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) — outside the only range where anything fixes it. Better: define \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) once (the same \(\mathcal Q\) on \(E\)'s solutions and component structure), or state what an undefined \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) counts as. Part XIV's claim that "No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined" is strained by this.

**12. The undefined-\(\tau\) case is settled inside \(C\) and not outside.** Part VII: "\(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair." The document therefore already has a convention for undefined translation; it is simply not carried into Part V, where the relevant pairs lie outside \(C\). The two available resolutions differ in effect, so the hole is not cosmetic: treat undefined as "no answer, hence no movement" and the candidate fails (strong); treat it as unknown and the adequacy conjunct has no truth value at all (not well-defined). Better: state the Part VII convention again for adequacy, or require \(\tau\) and \(\sigma\) to be defined on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\).

**13. The "or the reverse" clause does no shown work.** Remove it in your head: no condition, example, or attack on the page exhibits a candidate that moves at an admitted pair outside \(C\) where the target does not. Either exhibit the case (a candidate with a spurious out-of-scope parameter) or note that the clause is stated for symmetry.

**14. Adequacy is the only conjunct of \((E)\) with no witness form.** Non-circular dependence names one ("The witness: there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\) …"); non-vacuity names one; (F1), (F2), (A) are pointwise on \(C\). Adequacy is a negative universal over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), with no certificate. Better: give it the witness an assessor would look for — either a separating pair, or a certificate that \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) is insensitive outside \(S\).

## Pass 2 — Sequences and the chain of questions

The chain itself holds. Part III closes on "what makes a question coherent?"; Part IV opens on what holds an organization to its target, and the link is \(C\). Part V closes on adequacy, and Part VI opens on \(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\), which grows directly out of \((E)\). No question is swapped for an unrelated one. Three consequences below.

**15. Part VI inherits the condition and never says so; the finite monotone theorem's hypothesis is now part of the inheritance.** \(\mathsf S\) is defined through \(\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\), so every restriction \(E|W\) must now be non-protective in the note-8 sense. The theorem is stated conditionally ("If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed…"), so nothing is broken; but the page also says "No upward closure and no minimal member are assumed", and the worked cases ("Redundant routes", "Interference") are given as if their support sets were determined by fidelity alone. Create-versus-inherit: adequacy is not monotone in \(W\) in general — adding a commitment can create a new out-of-scope movement. Better: state whether adequacy is preserved under the restriction operation, or list it among the theorem's hypotheses.

**16. \(K\) is a second place to hide a separating pair, and the only guard is the module.** Quote: "\(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit", and "the objectivity condition of Part V ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)". No collision by design — the ranges are disjoint — but a writer who declares an admitted pair as non-admitted puts it inside \(C\) and outside adequacy's reach. The guard is Part XII's module, which the document honestly owns ("how a lump of matter decomposes is the module's burden"). Owned limit; rank low. One phrasing worth fixing: "a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning" invites the misreading that \(K\)'s role is confined to non-vacuity, when (A) also ranges over \(K\). Separate predicates; keep the sentences separate.

**17. Part XI's sentence about narrowing assumes the opposite of what the revision does.** Quote: "A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK)." Derive: \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S\) (since \(K\) is disjoint from \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)). *Narrowing* \(S\) enlarges the adequacy range and makes adequacy harder, not easier; what narrowing buys relief from is (F1), (F2) and (A). So under the revision the sentence is either mis-worded ("to rescue account-hood") or false. Better: "A later change to the scope made to rescue the account is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK)." The same derive-and-check also answers your (EK) question: (EK) says "Account is evaluated on the contract of \(p_c\) fixed at \(e_c\)", but adequacy is not fixed at \(e_c\) — it appeals to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which is fixed by the module.

## Pass 3 — The choice in the condition

**18. The choice test on the conjunct itself.** The real options at that moment were two, and both are live. (i) *Movement-XOR off \(C\)*, as written: catches the case where one profile silently misses a dependence, misses the both-move-different-values case, and bites any honest partial candidate. (ii) *Plain disagreement off \(C\)*: catches both cases, but makes account-hood at a contract require full answer-fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which collapses Part 0's two grades into one. A third near neighbour exists and the page already has its slot — make an exclusion legitimate when \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) does not move at the excluded pair (note 5's reason slot; Part VII's eliminative case is built on exactly this shape, "the absence leaves both unchanged"). **Flip the choice:** had the page chosen (ii), I would fault it as swallowing the second grade and making narrow questions unanswerable — so the note is held by the page, not by taste. The fault is not the choice; it is that (i)'s two costs are paid nowhere.

**19. The inevitability test: the revision is caused, not arbitrary.** Grievance 4 says the second conjunct "is a condition in this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism", and Part XV lists the refuting case. Those scenes settle it. **Protect the conjunct**; cut it and Grievance 4's objectivity claim and Derivation 7's citation both lose their referent. The fix belongs to its form, not its existence.

**20. The cost test: the price of the revision is stated nowhere.** The price is four sentences elsewhere — Part 0's division of the grades, Derivation 7's index, Part VI's \(\mathsf S\), Part XI's narrowing clause. Compared with this document's own habit ("that is what 'physics says what a lump instantiates' costs, and it is listed rather than hidden"), the silence stands out. Show the price in Part V.

## Pass 4 — Theme as a pattern

**21. The pattern count.** Put the central question as one about how to act: *"How much of the world must an account answer to, to count as an account of a question?"* The answers on the page: Part III's contract states exclusions and calls it scoping; Grievance 4 requires the exclusions then be checked; Part V's adequacy checks movement; Part V's world-kind condition checks fidelity on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\); Part VII's mathematical organization makes everything relative to a declaration; Part VII's eliminative case confines objectivity to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); Derivation 7 says adequacy catches goalpost-moving; Part XV asks for a protective scope that passes. Different answers, good. **Who faces whom:** adequacy and world-kind — the two \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)-level answers — are adjacent paragraphs of Part V and are never put side by side, so the reader cannot tell which catches what; and Grievance 7's declaration-relativity is never brought back to Part V's flat statement. Two loose threads. Also test whether the two are one condition: they cannot trade places — one is answer-level, one is component-level, and a decoy with a component difference but no answer difference fails world-kind and passes adequacy.

**22. The unquestioned first case: Part 0's list of what an account at a contract is omits adequacy.** Quote: "An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated." That is the first thing a reader meets, and it is the un-revised account. Planted in Part 0, collected differently in Part V, never reconciled. Better: fold adequacy into the Part 0 sentence, or say Part V supersedes it.

**23. The fair mirror: Grievance 4's statement and Part V's.** Are the two acts equal? For a physical organization, yes — both are two-place on \((C,E)\), both XOR-of-movement, both over the excluded admitted changes. For a non-physical organization they are not: Grievance 4 says "some physically admitted change it excludes", and Grievance 7 concedes "'physically admitted' is not defined for a mathematical organization". Part V's \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) version is the general one. **Afterwards:** Grievance 4's condition has an afterwards (Part V collects it); Part V's condition's *consequences* have none (note 20). Since both share the hole in note 8, fixing one fixes both.

## Pass 5 — How it fits

**24. Meeting points.** There are exactly two places where a technical condition is also the document's answer to its own theme: adequacy, and world-kind. They are adjacent paragraphs never confronted (note 21). The world-rule *does* express the theme — "the contract is part of the claim, and only a stated, legitimate exclusion counts" is the theme — so this is a thin fit, not a bolted-on one.

**25. Derivation 7's "catches" claim is not established by the condition it names.** Quote: "Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change; the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded." Under note 8's counterexample, a scope narrowed to protect a candidate passes. "Whether or not it is recorded" is also weaker than it sounds: with \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), an unrecorded exclusion is impossible, because \(S\) *is* the record. Better: "the adequacy condition catches a change made to protect a candidate, wherever the excluded pair leaves one of the two profiles at its baseline."

**26. Derivation 7's and Part VIII's index is \(C\), but account-hood is now also indexed to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).** Derivation 7: "The assessment is indexed to \(C\); its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index (Part VIII)." Part VIII: "its truth value at its own index does not change". If a later theory changes the module — hence \(A_\Theta\), hence \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) — then "\(E\) is an account on \(C\)" can change truth value with \(C\) untouched. Grievance 4's "two indices" is the right place to say this, but it names the indices as \(C\) and the physically admitted changes, not a tuple. Better: index account-hood to \((C,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), or state that a change of module ends the assessment as a change of index does. This is your Derivation 7 and Grievance 4 question, and it is the same fault in both places.

**27. The constraint propagates into Part IX and is unremarked.** (K2): "\(\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\): every pair \(u\) ranges over lies in \(C_j\)." An appraiser's usable argument that adequacy holds must range over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), which lies outside a contract \(C_j\) chosen for the object question. So no such argument is *usable* unless the appraiser declares \(C_j\supseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\). Not a contradiction, but a new obligation the revision creates and the page does not state.

*Tests that do not transfer:* lucky meetings (no meetings, no luck); the physical mirror pairs of Pass 1 (up/down, slow/fast, open/shut — no such bodies here); "who benefits" (no agents).

# 4. How it fits together

The revision is caused, promised, and aimed at the right target: Grievance 4 asks for an objectivity conjunct in Part V, Part XV names the refuting case, and the contract's form already keeps \(K\) out of the objectivity range. What the page has not done is carry the consequence anywhere else. The same condition is stated twice in incompatible strengths (Part V's formula versus its own gloss in "What (E) excludes"), it puts a \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)-level burden into a grade the document says is assessable within the question, and it leaves four sentences elsewhere — Derivation 7, Part VIII, Part VI, Part XI — reading as if it were not there.

# 5. The faults that matter most

1. **The adequacy formula is too weak exactly where Part XV's attack lives and too strong everywhere else (notes 1, 8, 9).** Fix: one added clause plus a named baseline pair, and decide whose declaration sets \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).
2. **Three well-definedness holes, each a one-line fix, all of which must close before the conjunct can be evaluated at all (notes 10, 11, 12).** Baseline pair unnamed; \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) undefined off \(C\); the document's own undefined-\(\tau\) convention not carried over.
3. **The revision's consequences are unrecorded in five places (notes 15, 17, 22, 25, 26, 27).** Part 0's division of the grades, Derivation 7's catch-claim and index, Part VI's \(\mathsf S\), Part XI's narrowing clause, Part IX's appraiser scope. Do not cut the conjunct; these are the sentences it contradicts.

# 6. What I am unsure of

Whether the intended reading of the condition is movement-XOR or disagreement — Part V's formula and its gloss disagree, and I took the gloss as the intent. Whether the anchored grade already subsumes adequacy where the separating change separates components; I argue it plausibly does and cannot prove it from the page, which if true makes adequacy redundant at the top grade and division-breaking at the bottom. Whether the Part VI examples still satisfy \(\mathsf S\) with adequacy inside Account. I read the document; I ran nothing, and no construction was checked numerically.

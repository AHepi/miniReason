# Corrections first

Two of your eight pointers need adjusting before anything else, and both corrections sharpen rather than soften your list.

**(a) Derivation 2 is an island.** "Account-equivalent" and "underdetermination" occur nowhere else in the document. So there is no downstream claim to check for over-reach; the thing to check is whether the definition and the claim are well-formed at all.

**(b) Derivation 3 has no "admissible class" in its claim.** The words appear only inside the closure hypothesis, as a parenthetical: "each relation L′ ⊆ ∏_{v∈V_j}X_v in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of 𝒯)". So what is wrong there is a word doing a fourth job and a self-referential hypothesis — not a technical error in a named hypothesis.

One further fact, because it changes how I weight what follows: Part XV's list of what would be "A mathematical error" names "Derivations 1–3 and 8", and does not name 5, 6, 7 or 9 — even though Derivation 5 is cited by name in Part III, Part X (twice) and Derivation 7, and Derivation 6 by Part XIV. The mapping I use: each derivation and remark is a scene; the definitions, hypotheses and witnesses it cites are its characters; Part 0's claims and Part XV's limits are the promises of its type (a definitional framework that says what it does and does not define). # Pass 1 — the ten derivations, scene by scene

## Sequence I: what fidelity and selection do and do not preserve (Derivations 1–3)

**1. Derivation 1's real job, and what holds it.**
On the page: "(F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise". The job: to carry (F1), which is stated in the vocabulary of Part II's relations, into (K)'s vocabulary of signatures, so that Part V can say "By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of E has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract". Remove test: delete the derivation and Part V's sentence has nothing to stand on. So it earns its place — but as a translation, not as a result: its conclusion is (F1) restated in set notation.

**2. Derivation 1's injectivity hypothesis: stated where it is not used.**
On the page: "let (τ,σ) be injective on C", and the proof's last clause, "injectivity of (τ,σ) on C makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates". Remove test: drop injectivity and the stated conclusion still holds, because sig_C(λ(k)) and sig_{τ[C]}(k) are *sets* of triples, (F1) equates their third coordinates pointwise, and if (τ,σ) collapses two pairs then (F1) forces the two anchored relations at those pairs to be equal — the image is still k's whole signature. So the hypothesis is doing work only for the *bijective* reading ("k has exactly the anchor's signature", i.e. index sets recoverable one-for-one). The fault is not the extra hypothesis; it is that the derivation never says which reading it intends, and the three later citations drop the hypothesis: Part V's sentence above, Part XV's "it only shows fidelity preserves signatures", and Derivation 10's "then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things". Better version: state the conclusion as the image equality (which needs only (F1)), then add a second sentence: "if, in addition, (τ,σ) is injective on C, the correspondence is a bijection of index sets".

**3. Derivation 1's hypotheses are also insufficient in two smaller ways.**
On the page: "every active component k of E has, on τ[C], the signature that its anchor subnetwork λ(k) has on C". Two unstated requirements. First, τ[C] is used as an index set for a signature, and (K) is defined for "a contract C ⊆ A×B (Part III)"; (F1) alone does not make τ[C] a contract, and the identity clause that would help — τ(1) = 1 — is in (F2), which the claim does not assume. Second, the proof asserts "the visible ports of the anchor being those λ translates to V_k", which is a property of λ's port translation, not something (F1) gives. Neither is fatal; both should be in the hypothesis list.

**4. Derivation 1's summary sentence is looser than the definition it invokes.**
On the page: "a faithful component is of one forward kind, on C, with its anchor subnetwork". But Part II defines the relation only between two *components* — "there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on C \ {(1,b) : b ∈ B}" — on *one* contract, with the baseline pairs excluded on purpose. Derivation 1 compares a component with a subnetwork, on two different index sets (C and τ[C]), over all of C including the baseline. Its own next paragraph half-concedes one of these ("the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component"), which is honest; the other two are not mentioned. This is a held note because (K)'s exclusions carry a stated reason ("the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its state"), so the looseness is against a definition the document is otherwise careful with.

**5. Derivation 2's definition of account-equivalence: one clause is idle, the other is unproved.**
On the page: "the anchored relations proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide". Clause two is idle under (A): both candidates satisfy Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b)) = Ans_p(a,b) = Ans_{E′}(τ′(a),σ′(b)), so if "pulled back" means each candidate's own translation, the clause is automatic; if it means pulled back by E's translation for both, the claim's hypotheses do not supply τ = τ′ (they supply "the same anchor map up to that bijection", which is λ, not τ), and the proof's third step, "(A) gives the profiles", fails for E′. Either reading is a fault, and the derivation's own "What it does not say" paragraph does the work the definition was supposed to do.

**6. Derivation 2's bijection is between the wrong things, and nothing collects it.**
On the page: "there is a bijection between their active components carrying Γ onto Γ′", after which "the anchored relations proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b)" are compared with proj_{V_{k′}}Sol_{λ′(k′)}(a,b). Those are projections of one and the same target's solution sets onto the ports the two components call their own; without a *port-level* correspondence they are not comparable. Part II says "a bijection of their footprints"; Derivation 2 says "a bijection between their active components". Fix the wording or the comparison is undefined. Second point, in the "swap two characters" form: no other part cites account-equivalence, so as far as this document goes it is one definition doing no job a paragraph of prose does not already do.

**7. Derivation 3's proof rests on an unstated hypothesis about the survival condition — and Part XV's attack on the same derivation says the opposite.**
On the page, the proof's first sentence: "Survival on H constrains only the relations at pairs in H." But Part IV's selection witness requires only "a survival condition requiring fidelity on H that eliminated at least one member of 𝒯" — requiring, not exhausting. A survival condition that also constrained some pair outside H would break that first sentence. Now the mirror, Part XV: "A selection history H ⊊ C, in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off H, whose survivor is determined on C∖H. This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis." Under the derivation's proof as written, the refuting case is impossible: if survival is exactly H-fidelity, every altered pair survives on H and the survivor is not determined. Under the derivation's *stated* hypotheses, the refuting case is possible: nothing forbids a survival condition stricter than H. So exactly one of the two passages can be right, and the document does not say which. Better version: put "the survival condition constrains only pairs in H" into the claim, and then delete Part XV's bullet or rewrite it.

**8. Derivation 3's proof also needs the injectivity that Derivation 1 carries and does not use.**
On the page: "The altered pair agrees with (E,t) on H, hence survives." The alteration sets L_j at the E-pair (τ(a),σ(b)) for (a,b) ∈ C∖H. If (τ,σ) is not injective on C, that same E-pair can be the image of some (a′,b′) ∈ H, and then the altered pair fails (F1) — and (F2), whose right-hand side Sol_E(τ(a),σ(b)) depends on the same relation — at an H-pair, so it does not survive. The closure hypothesis itself presupposes this, since it indexes the alteration by a D-pair and writes its effect at an E-pair. The hypothesis is therefore in the wrong derivation: declared and unused in 1, used and undeclared in 3. This is the single cleanest fault in the sequence.

**9. Derivation 3's 'admissible class' is a fourth job for one word, defined by the thing it is supposed to constrain.**
On the page: "each relation L′ ⊆ ∏_{v∈V_j}X_v in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of 𝒯)". Elsewhere "admissible" means: a grain licensed by the physical module ("A grain is admissible for an occurrence o when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain"), the module's admitted pairs (C_adm), a query's definedness ("the set of queries admissible on D"), and an enabling condition the module permits (Part XIII). Here it means something purely population-relative, and the closure it defines is closure of 𝒯 under relations that members of 𝒯 realize. That is a self-referential hypothesis, but a well-formed one — the fault is naming and readability, not coherence. Rename it "the population's relation class" or similar.

**10. Derivation 3's conclusion, and its credit.**
On the page, the claim is conditional: "whenever some admissible L′ changes that solution set". This is weaker than the title "Selection guarantees nothing off its history": a change in one component's relation need not move the solution set at all, and then the derivation is silent. The "Consequence" paragraph says exactly this and better — "It does not follow that the survivor is unfaithful there — it may be faithful on all of C — and nothing here says the survivor will fail" — and Part XV's closing sentence, "A population not closed in that way ... does not refute anything", is the fair mirror. Protect both sentences; they are what keeps this derivation honest.

## Sequence II: what is represented, defined and indexed (Derivations 4–7)

**11. Derivation 4, as a remark: correct, and correctly disowned.**
On the page: "a system with no transport is never surprised, and ... a system whose history exhausts its contract is never surprised," and "nothing else in this document cites it as support". Checked against Part IV's definition, which is introduced by "Let t be selected on history H with contract C" and defines "surprise" as "a violation at (a,b) ∉ H": both facts follow, and the general fact is narrower than the remark's two examples — surprise needs a *selected* transport, so a constructed transport also has no surprise. Part IV cites the remark only to say the facts "are not results (Derivation 4 says so)", i.e. not as support. So Part IV and Derivation 4 are two routes to one job (telling the reader these are not theorems), and each has a second job: Part IV keeps the definition honest, Derivation 4 declares the isolation. Keep both.

**12. Derivation 5's "exactly" fails in both directions, against the coherence conditions it cites.**
On the page: "Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on D." Part III's coherence has three clauses: "Sol_D(1,b_0) ≠ ∅, 𝒬 is defined on every pair in C, and S and K are disjoint from each other's complements as stated." (i) Non-vacuity of D is a fact about the target, held fixed as boundary ("Boundary: the target D and b_0, held fixed"), and nothing in the construction imposes it; if Sol_D(1,b_0) = ∅ then by Part III there are no coherently posed questions on D, while D_p's solutions are unaffected — over-generation. (ii) 𝒬-defined-on-C depends on which pairs read "in", but "one port u_𝒬 with domain the set of queries admissible on D" fixes that domain as part of the organization's data, as (O) requires ("each with a nonempty value domain X_v"). Read strictly, the port admits only queries defined on all of A×B (excluding Part III-coherent questions whose 𝒬 is defined on C only); read loosely, it admits questions that fail coherence — either way, not "exactly". (iii) The construction adds a closure the coherence conditions do not contain: "if u_{(a_2a_1,b)} reads 'in' then so do its factors where the composition is defined". Part II closes A under composition, not C. That closure is an extra restriction on which questions D_p represents. Better version: add a nullary component that is satisfiable exactly when Sol_D(1,b_0) ≠ ∅, move the definedness condition into a relation between the pair ports and the u_𝒬 port, and drop the factor-closure condition unless a coherence clause is added to Part III to justify it.

**13. Derivation 5's u_S and u_K are two handles on one job, and their edits duplicate the pair toggle.**
On the page, the construction's admitted edits: "toggle a pair; alter 𝒬; alter S; add a pair to K with a meaning". By Part III's form, C = (C_adm ∩ S) ∪ K with S ⊆ C_adm, so given the "in" ports S is determined as C ∩ C_adm and K as C∖C_adm; the two extra edits change the contract by the same route as toggling a pair, and only the *reasons* and *meanings* ("with the reason for each exclusion recorded", "each with a stated meaning") are carried by the extra ports. The requirement that keeps them coherent — "u_S and u_K are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III" — is stated in words, not as a component relation. Better version: fold reason and meaning into the pair-port values and keep one route to the contract. This is the remove-in-pairs test applied: removing either u_S or u_K alone loses data (so neither is idle), but together they do one job with two handles and an unstated consistency component.

**14. Derivation 5 does not represent O_p or ρ_p — and the merit condition runs on O_p. (Fair-mirror failure.)**
On the page, the claim begins "A question p = (D, C, b_0, 𝒬, O_p, ρ_p) determines an organization D_p", but the construction's ports are the pairs, 𝒬, S, K — obligations and provenance are not among them, and no admitted edit touches an obligation. The consequence then says "Finding the right question is a creative act on the same footing as answering one, subject to the merit condition of Part X." The merit condition is "there is an obligation o ∈ O such that a deployable account of p′ would repair o under (P), with o failing at the state before e" — i.e. it turns entirely on the one component of the tuple D_p omits. So the mirror is unequal: the answer case has its obligations inside the account (O_p in Part III's tuple, Γ in (E)), the question case does not. What to protect, by contrast: making the ports one per pair of A×B is *held* by a job — Part X's stated limit, "the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing A×B", and Part XV's concession about the invention of a new dimension of variation. The document's hardest self-limit is a consequence of a construction detail, and that is good work.

**15. Derivation 6's Rep/Con cycle is broken, by the right clause — but two of its three sentences are promissory.**
On the page: "the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness." (ii) The consequence "No residual predicate is left undefined" is false as it stands: (U1) and (U2) quantify over "𝔈_Θ the explanatory contents" and "𝔓^adv_Θ the coherently posed advanceable challenges", neither defined nor listed in Part XIV's declared parameters (whose list ends "the use tasks U and enabling conditions χ"); and (EK) reaches "CreativeCriticalEpisode", whose constituents "a recognized difficulty" and "a content-sensitive response" are used, never defined, and not listed either. **16. Derivation 7 is sound, and inherits its one dependence.**

## Sequence III: recoding, outputs, and the illustrated scenario (Derivations 8–10)

**17. Derivation 8's restriction on bijections is the whole claim, and it is added in the proof rather than stated in the hypothesis.**
On the page, the hypothesis list: "a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps λ), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module's admission (A_Θ, Inst, ≺), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and 𝒩". Then the conclusion, "Transporting all data along φ preserves (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK)." # Pass 3 — the definitions as characters and their choices

**25. The choice test, the flip test and the inevitability test on hypotheses.** A hypothesis must be either forced (the conclusion dies without it) or a real choice (two readings of the claim, and the hypothesis selects one). Survey: Derivation 3's closure — forced; Derivation 8's restriction on φ — forced, and unstated (note 17); Derivation 1's injectivity — *neither*: the stated conclusion survives without it, and only the proof's phrasing wants it (note 2). So the note belongs to the citations, not the hypothesis, and I have merged it accordingly.

**26. Which two characters could trade places.** "Admissible grain" (Part II) and "admitted pairs" C_adm (Part III) cannot: one indexes what the module licenses as a decomposition, the other which edits the claim ranges over. "Stated scope S" and "counterfactual set K" cannot either — S ⊆ C_adm and the K pairs are exactly those the module does not admit. So no merge is available among the honest cases; the one case that *should* merge is Derivation 5's pair of ports u_S, u_K (note 13).

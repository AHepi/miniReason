## 5. Findings, with exact passages

### Finding 1 — Five conditions vs. six conjuncts
Part V says:

> “holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold”

Then it displays:

> “Account(E,p,t,Γ)⇔ (F1)∧(F2)∧(A)∧NonCircular∧NonVacuous∧Adequate. (E)”

That is six conjuncts. Part 0’s summary is also incomplete:

> “An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated.”

This omits the adequacy condition. **Mark: held.**

---

### Finding 2 — Two-place Account vs. Part VI’s `S_{E,p}`
Part V says:

> “The predicate is two-place on the organization and the question, with the transport and commitments existentially quantified: Account(E,p)⇔∃t,Γ Account(E,p,t,Γ)”

Part VI then defines:

> “Fix E. The restriction E|W … Define S_{E,p}={W⊆Γ: Account(E|W,p)}. (S)”

Because `Account(E|W,p)` existentially quantifies a fresh transport and a fresh set of commitments, the support set may not be about the original transport and original Γ. The finite monotone theorem later depends on this `S`. **Mark: held.**

---

### Finding 3 — `Account(c,p_c)` in (EK)
Part XI’s (EK) contains:

> “∧ Account(c,p_c) ∧ …”

The sentence immediately after says:

> “Here c is a content whose organization is the E of Account(E,p_c)”

`Account` is defined on organizations; `c` is a content. This is a notational inconsistency. **Mark: held.**

---

### Finding 4 — Forward kind applied to a component and a subnetwork
Part II defines forward kind only between components:

> “Two components j,j' are of one forward kind on (ℓ,C) when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on C\{(1,b):b∈B}.”

Part V’s anchored condition 4 says:

> “Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor”

But the anchor is a subnetwork `λ(k)`, not necessarily a component. Derivation 1’s prose repeats this:

> “a faithful component is of one forward kind, on C, with its anchor subnetwork.”

This is a category mistake: a component cannot be “of one forward kind” with a subnetwork under the definition as stated. **Mark: held.**

---

### Finding 5 — Baseline exclusion vs. Derivation 1 on all of C
Part II says:

> “The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states.”

Derivation 1 claims:

> “every active component k of E has, on τ[C], the signature that its anchor subnetwork λ(k) has on C”

Derivation 1 preserves signatures on all of C, including baseline pairs. That is stronger than needed for forward-kind identity. It does not contradict the exclusion, but it makes the exclusion irrelevant for transport-related components and sits awkwardly with the prose that says the component “is of one forward kind” with its anchor. **Mark: loose.**

---

### Finding 6 — Surprise on C and loss of Deploy
Part IV says:

> “surprise at (a,b)∈C is a failure of fidelity on C, so on that contract the system has, by (R), stopped representing the content, and by Part X stopped deploying it”

Part X defines Deploy as:

> “Deploy_{β,ℓ}(s,c,ξ;U) holds when s at ξ holds a representation of c — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance — integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task U as a retained capability”

The inference requires that the contract `C` in the surprise definition be the same as the contract `C_c` used in (R). That identity is not stated. **Mark: loose.**

---

### Finding 7 — Historical index vs. Derivation 7
Part VIII says:

> “A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change…”

Derivation 7 says:

> “The claim ‘E is an account on C’ and the claim ‘E is not an account on C'' are claims at different indices and may both be true.”

These are consistent. Part III already says an assessment is fixed at `(C,Q)` and any change ends it. No contradiction found. **Mark: held** (consistent).

---

### Finding 8 — Derivation 10 scenario vs. Part XIV membership
Derivation 10 says:

> “This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it.”

Part XIV defines classes by instances:

> “The creative-episode class: base interpretations with an instance of (G) connected to a critical episode.”

Derivation 10 says “If S1 was built … then (G) holds” but does not assert the conditions. No contradiction. **Mark: held** (consistent). It does inherit Finding 4 when it says “by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind”.

---

### Finding 9 — “Under (P)” vs. Part X’s merit condition
Part 0 says:

> “or — beyond one condition under (P) — a merit function for questions.”

Part X says:

> “This is the only merit condition in the semantics”

Part XI’s (P) is the Repair condition, not a merit condition for questions. The cross-reference is wrong. **Mark: held.**

---

### Finding 10 — Reversed calculation wording
Part VII says:

> “here τ sends the intervention on H to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning H, which the reversed organization has none of, so τ is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair”

The reversed calculation `H=L tan θ` does have a component assigning H. The intended point is that H is not an input port in the reversed calculation, so the intervention cannot be translated. The phrase “has none of” is misleading. **Mark: loose.**

---

### Finding 11 — Derivation 8’s unstated condition
Derivation 8 says:

> “Let φ be a family of bijections … that preserves the physical module's admission (A_Θ, Inst, ≺), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and N.”

Then it excludes recodings that change anchors:

> “a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted φ, since it does not preserve D's structure.”

Preserving `D`’s structure is not in the stated conditions on `φ`. The proof relies on it. **Mark: held.**

---

### Finding 12 — Forward-kind relation across organizations
Part II defines forward kind after “Fix an organization D”:

> “Two components j,j' are of one forward kind on (ℓ,C)…”

It does not say the two components must belong to the same organization. Part V condition 4 compares a component of `E` to its anchor in `D`, i.e. across organizations. The definition needs scoping or extension. **Mark: loose.**

---

### Finding 13 — One-component Sol_D candidate
Part V says:

> “The one-component candidate whose single relation is Sol_D satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3”

Condition 1 is properness; condition 3 is no-global-relation. This is consistent. **Mark: held.**

## 6. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip.** If the document had consistently said “six conditions”, used `Account(E,p_c)` in (EK), said “has the signature of its anchor subnetwork” instead of “is of one forward kind”, and referred to Part X instead of (P), Findings 1, 3, 4 and 9 would disappear. Those parts are not held in place by the theory’s core; they are editing slips.

**Direction.** The document is not running backwards from a result. The tensions are forward: definitions and their immediate uses.

**Answer hidden in the starting points.** The contradictions are not hidden; they are explicit in the text.

**Change list.** The changes that would remove findings:
- change “five” to “six” (Finding 1);
- change `Account(c,p_c)` to `Account(E,p_c)` (Finding 3);
- extend forward kind to subnetworks, or change the prose to “has the signature of its anchor subnetwork” (Finding 4);
- make `Account` four-place in Part VI, or fix `t` and `Γ` in the restriction (Finding 2);
- change “under (P)” to “under Part X” (Finding 9);
- add “preserves D’s structure” to Derivation 8’s conditions (Finding 11).

**Rivals.** The best rival is a revised document that makes these changes. What tells them apart is the exact text. No deeper theoretical change is required.

**Where the parts came from.** The document’s parts are mostly **built** (definitions worked out with the target in view) or **asserted** (declared parameters). The contradictions are not from provenance; they are from editing. Some terms are **borrowed** from the source theory, as the skill’s word list notes, but that does not affect these findings.

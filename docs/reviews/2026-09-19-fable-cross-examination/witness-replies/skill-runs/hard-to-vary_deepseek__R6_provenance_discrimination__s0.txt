## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained**, in the document's terms: the membership rule for `Sel(t;𝒯,μ,H,Σ_h)` and its relation to `Con(t;h,e)`, `Dec(t)` and the precedence rule in Part IV; over the range of the whole revised document; asking whether that rule is satisfiable by construction, whether one of its clauses re-imports a primitive, whether the precedence rule can reclassify, whether the two witnesses are inter-translatable, and whether the blindness clause agrees with Part X.

**Kind of question.** This is a *rule/definition* question, not a production or identification one. Per the skill's domain file for rules: *"Remove each clause and ask which cases change sides."* That is the frame I use. Naming the kind matters, because "is it trivially satisfiable" is a question about the extension of a defined class, not about a cause.

**Fair version check (the user's own summary).** The user's list is *nearly* the text. Verified clause by clause: population ≥ 2 ✓, applied μ ✓, non-empty history with a non-trivial pair ✓, eliminating survival condition ✓, causal responsibility ✓, event non-triviality ✓, blindness as absence of an itemised defect representation with scalar summaries allowed ✓. Two additions the user did not list, both in the text: the survivor clause (*"and \(t\) is a member that survived"*), and *"Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance."* One subtraction, and it is the crux: **the blindness clause is written after the clause list, and is not one of its members.**

## 5. The ten tests

### Remove — **F1** The non-trivial-pair clause is idle. *"at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it)"* is entailed by the neighbouring clause: if every member passes \(H\), no member is eliminated, so *"a survival condition … that eliminated at least one member"* already fails. Removing it changes no case, alone or in a group.

### Remove — **F2** The μ clause is loose. The text requires μ *"physically applied at least once"*, not applied-and-produced-a-variant, and Part XV excludes only *"singleton population, empty history"*. A null application satisfies the letter. **Swap** test: *"a variation operator that was physically applied"* for *"a variation operator that produced a member not present before"* — no case in the document tells them apart.

### Remove — **F3** The blindness clause is not idle: it is the only thing in the document that defines the term the separating sentence relies on (*"no itemised representation of a defect"*). But it is also not a listed clause, and the two later restatements of the witness drop it — see **F5**.

### Poke — **F7** *"causally responsible"* is not defined, is not in Part XIV's two primitive packages (*"The semantics has two"*: physical module, normative relation), and is not in the declared-parameter list. Derivation 6 nonetheless concludes *"No residual predicate is left undefined."* Either the term is a third primitive, contradicting the enumeration, or it is derived and the derivation is missing. It *can* be derived from materials the module already supplies — `A_Θ` (the admitted edits) and `≺` (the process-dependency order) — by the poke: intervene on the filtering event and see whether the survivor's presence moves. The document never says this.

### Reverse / Poke — **F8** Read as a poke, the clause breaks in the paradigm case. In the standard case the survivor was already present and what the environment did was remove its rivals; intervening on the filter leaves the survivor present, so the filter is not causally responsible for *"\(t\)'s presence"* under an intervention reading. Read as a counterfactual it is satisfied trivially — and Part III puts counterfactuals in the declared set \(K\), whose pairs *"enter non-vacuity only through its stated meaning"*. So the clause is either trivial or excludes the case the document's own Part IV calls typical: *"the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held."* "Environment" is likewise used undefined.

### Hunt — **F9** The answer sits in the starting point: the survival condition is *"requiring fidelity on \(H\)"*, and the conclusion wanted is that the survivor is faithful on \(H\). That is not automatically circular — the condition can be enacted independently — but the same definition separately forbids any event from being *"itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition"*, while Part XII says the condition is *"a survival condition enacted by the environment."* An enacted condition needs a carrier that maps states to survival; whether that carrier is a "faithful transport" in the full (F1)/(F2) sense is exactly what no case here settles.

### **F10** Same tension, sharper, in the scalar case. Blindness explicitly permits *"a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly)"*; event non-triviality forbids any event that is a faithful transport to the survival condition. When the environment's filter is *"the member with the best scalar survives"*, the scalar carrier is arguably both. Two clauses of the same definition can then be jointly unsatisfiable in the most standard physical arrangement. No case is offered either way.

### Flip — **F11** Partly answered, honestly. Had the other member survived, the same witness would call *that* one selected, and the document concedes the consequence: *"selection provides no guarantee of fidelity where it did not test … fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts."* So the flip is fine *for provenance*; it is fatal only for a reader who takes the witness to explain the survivor's fidelity. Say which claim is on the table.

### Add a job — **F12** The added job "provenance must be determinable without a record held by anyone" is met by Sel only through the undefined causal-responsibility clause, and is failed by Con, which requires *"an episode … whose construction witness prepares \(t\)."* Unlike Sel, Con is phrased as requiring a witness. That rules out output-only attribution (which is new work) and exposes an asymmetry between the two witnesses that nothing in the document discusses.

### Check the patches — **F5** Four non-coextensive statements of the same two provenances, each a patch over the last:

| Where | What selection is said to need |
|---|---|
| Part 0 | *"produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes, with at least one candidate eliminated"* — **no** applied μ, **no** blindness, **no** causal responsibility, **no** non-triviality |
| Part IV clause list (P2–P6) | all of the above except blindness and causal responsibility |
| Part IV continued (P7–P9) | adds causal responsibility, event non-triviality, blindness |
| Grievance 9 | *"a population with more than one member, a variation operator that was applied, a survival condition that eliminated at least one candidate, and a sequence of events causally responsible for the survivor's presence"* — **no** blindness, **no** non-triviality, **no** target clause |
| Part XII | *"population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility"* — same omissions, while claiming *"This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one."* |

Likewise for construction: Part 0 says *"produced by an episode of conjecture and criticism"*, and those two words appear in neither `Con` nor `Build`. The blindness narrowing is therefore real but unrecorded in the canonical list — a silent narrowing of the kind the skill's patch test is aimed at.

### Look inside — **F13** The tests do reach inside on one axis and not another. The witnesses differ on two changes (itemised record on the route; target instantiated before \(e\)), so *"same outputs, different insides"* is honoured for constructed/selected. But `Con` does not contain the itemisation clause at all, so the separation is carried by two prose sentences — *"What keeps the two apart…"* and *"Construction is not selection."* — and not by either witness.

### **F14** *"the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated … before \(e\)"* does a job (it blocks a lucky pre-adaptation from counting as construction) and is held. **Swap** it for "at any time" and that job is lost; the document names the job in grievance 6's vicinity, so the clause survives.

### **F4** Blindness is defect-relative (*"blind with respect to a defect"*); the separating sentence is absolute (*"no itemised representation of a defect"*). Nothing states which defect governs, at which grain or contract; an itemised record at a coarse grain need not be itemised at a fine one. **Unknown**; test: write the same episode at two grains and ask whether the blindness verdict moves.

## 6. Trivial satisfiability — the direct answer, with a construction

**F15** Under the letter, yes. Take **T\***: a system with two copies of one tracking mechanism, differing in a single component relation; world-state at which copy 1 fails fidelity for its anchor and copy 2 passes; μ := a copy-with-perturbation applied once; H := that single pair; survival condition := fidelity on H; copy 1 destroyed, copy 2 survives = \(t\). Every listed clause is satisfied: population 2 ✓, μ applied ✓, H non-empty with a non-trivial pair ✓, elimination of at least one member ✓, survivor ✓. Event non-triviality: the destruction is not a transport to \(t\), H or the condition ✓. Blindness: no itemised defect record anywhere ✓. Causal responsibility: satisfied only on the counterfactual reading (F8). Part XV excludes only *"the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history)"* — T\* is that reading's immediate neighbour, and it is admitted. So the revision replaced the excluded trivial case with a two-member, one-history case and named the exclusion in a way that conceals the neighbour.

**F16** Under the strongest reading — blindness and event non-triviality counted as clauses — T\* is still admitted, and the cost of admission is one duplicated mechanism plus one filter. Not vacuous, but very cheap; the clauses doing the heavy lifting are only P2 and P5, and per the skill's own caution, counting clauses holds nothing new in place. If the intended reading is the strong one, say so in the clause list and the price rises visibly; if the intended reading is the letter, then "selected" carries almost nothing beyond "had a rival and a filter".

## 7. Gerrymandering, in both directions

**F17 (constructed over selected).** Yes, and by three separate routes.
(i) The criteria for a valid construction binding are relational, not historical: *"neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)"*. A λ produced by variation and survival can meet both by novelty of combination alone.
(ii) The sentence *"Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not."* classifies by the kind of agent, not by the structure of the history — precisely the move the skill's "hunt" test flags as a label ("by nature / has a knack for"). It is also in direct tension with Part IV's own classification: *"A **selection response** extends \(H\) and lets \(\mu\) act."* For learners, the same process is named a selection response in Part IV and construction in Part X.
(iii) The only clause that could block the reclassification is the itemisation clause, and it is in neither `Con` nor `Build` (F13).
The genuine blocks are narrower than they look: ownership excludes only *"a description of a process the system could not run"*, and the "for explanatory use of \(c\)" condition does keep the F→P tracker out — so the gerrymander is reachable, not universal. The document's reassurance — *"the precedence rule says which provenance the resulting transport bears"* — is a ruling, not an argument.

**F18 (is the precedence rule doing any job?).** The priority order is **idle** for every predicate in Parts II–XIII. Every use is a disjunction: (R) is *"\(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t)\)"*; Deploy is *"by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance"*; (EK) is *"Deploy supplies the provenance condition (\(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\))"*. Changing which of the two wins changes no truth value anywhere. What makes construction originative is `Build`, not the precedence. Only the maintenance clause — *"Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance"* — does work, and it needs "origination" defined. So the site of the gerrymander is also a removable part: this is where to cut.

**F19 (selection over construction).** If blindness is a clause, the rewrite is blocked, and by that clause alone (a construction witness contains an itemised record by the separating sentence; a selection witness may not). If blindness is a gloss — which is what the clause list's grammar says — then nothing in `Sel` blocks a construction episode that also has a population, a μ and an elimination, and a learner's trial-and-error construction has exactly that. **The whole answer to "can every construction be rewritten as a selection" therefore turns on the grammatical status of one unnumbered sentence.** That is the central finding of this run.

**F20** Both directions remain open as the text stands, and they are not symmetrical: the selection→construction route is available under either reading of blindness; the construction→selection route is available only under the weaker reading. The document needs to choose.

## 8. Consistency with Part X, in detail

**F21** The separating sentence claims *"a construction witness contains both"* (an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation). `Con` contains only the second. `Build` contains neither — it requires ownership, a novel binding, requiredness-for-use, and *"not a composition of content-preserving transfers"*. The defect representation appears only in the *episode* definition (*"a recognized difficulty … a conjectural objection"*), and an episode-side condition is not a witness-side condition.

**F22** Part IX widens this: *"A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) fails. An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears."* So a construction episode may contain a criticism that fails Bearing, hence no itemised representation of a defect — and the transport still bears "constructed" by precedence while failing the sentence that is supposed to distinguish it.

**F23** The blindness definition may import (R). Its key term is *"an itemised representation"*, and (R) is the document's definition of representation. Part XIV asserts the opposite: *"(R) depends on (F1)–(F2), \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and physical provenance; provenance depends on \(\Theta\) and on Part X's witnesses … but not on (R) — the stratification of Part IV (targets are instantiated, not represented, in a construction witness) is what keeps this order well-founded."* That cycle-breaker addresses the *target* clause. The blindness clause concerns a *defect*, and is not covered. If "itemised representation" means (R)-representation, provenance depends on (R) and the well-foundedness claim is false; if it means a bare physical record, the word "representation" is being used in two senses in adjacent paragraphs. Either way, the document should say which.

**F24** A second loop the dependence order does not mention: `Build`'s novelty test quantifies over *"a binding already in the repertoire"*, and (N) quantifies over \(R_{<e}\), while the repertoire is *"the set of contents deployable"* and Deploy requires (R), which requires `Sel ∨ Con`, and `Con` requires an episode whose construction witness prepares \(t\). Derivation 6 claims *"the only cycle that could arise"* is the target one. The time-indexing \(R_{<e}(s,h)\) is the available defence — the recursion bottoms out at the first acquisition, where the repertoire is empty — but the document states the defence for a different cycle.

**F25** Two smaller typing gaps in the same region. (a) `Sel`'s argument list is *"\(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\)"* yet its history clause is *"\(H\subseteq C\)"*, while (R) applies `Sel` with no contract; either provenance is contract-relative, or the clause is mistyped — and grievance 9 claims the opposite ("a claim about the physical module"). Part IV's later paragraph does carry it — *"Let \(t\) be selected on history \(H\) with contract \(C\)"* — so the two statements must be reconciled. (b) Part IV's 𝒯 is *"a population of realized transports"*; Derivation 3's is *"a population \(\mathcal T\) of organization–transport pairs"*. Derivation 3's closure hypothesis needs the second; `Sel` names the first. (c) *"the discrimination requirement of Part IV"* is cited in Part III and twice in Part XV, and Part IV never uses the name.

## 10. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Put the blindness clause in the clause list**, indexed to a defect and a contract, and say whether "route" means the Part IX active route. This closes the construction→selection route and removes the grammar ambiguity that everything else hinges on (F19, F14).
2. **Cash causal responsibility as a poke** — an admitted edit that removes the filtering, plus the statement that the filtering's *content* (fidelity on H) is causally upstream of survival, not merely correlated with it. As written the clause either re-imports a primitive or is trivial (F7, F8).
3. **Decide the scalar case**: does a filter that enacts the survival condition by ranking scalars satisfy event non-triviality? One case settles a possible contradiction (F10).

---

## 11. What this does not show

Nothing here shows the theory false, or selection unreal, or that construction is really selection. A loose clause is not a wrong one; it is a clause not yet held in place. Passing these tests would not have made the revision true, and failing them does not make it false — it marks which bolts are loose and names the changes that would tighten them. The shared vocabulary between this skill and the document is not counted anywhere as evidence (section 0).

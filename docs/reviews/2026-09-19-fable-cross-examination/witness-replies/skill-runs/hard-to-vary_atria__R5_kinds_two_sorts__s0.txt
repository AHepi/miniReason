# Hard-to-vary run: *Claude Fable Semantics* (revised), the two-kind taxonomy and the transport/component asymmetry

**A note on vocabulary first.** This skill's own terms — parts, change list, contract, query, jobs, fitted/built/asserted — are mapped in `references/word-list.md` to this very document. So the skill's vocabulary is *drawn from* the theory under test. I have therefore used the document's terms only to name its parts, never as evidence that those parts are held in place. The document is the thing being tested; the skill is the wrench.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

Four sub-questions, held still for the whole run:

1. **(Q1)** Does excluding the baseline pairs from "separating changes" lose real distinctions — constants, initial conditions, things that differ only in state?
2. **(Q2)** Is a historical kind well-defined without provenance witnesses being available *for components*?
3. **(Q3)** Does the transport/component asymmetry (Grievance 12) hold up, or does the actuality-of-route condition in the anchored grade smuggle history into fidelity?
4. **(Q4)** Is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV) now stated so that it could be refuted?

Kind of question: mostly *"what counts as what under this rule"* (Part II's definitions are rules), with a strand of *"does it achieve its purpose"* (the asymmetry is a design claim) and a strand of meta-question (Q4 asks whether a conjecture is falsifiable). Range: the revised document — Part II, Grievances 1/2/11/12, Part V's anchored grade, Part IX's active route, Part XV attack (C), Derivation 1.

---

## 2. The jobs

Each put as a contrast.

- **J1.** Tell a cause from a correlation (Grievance 1) — why this and not that.
- **J2.** Tell a rule from a cause (Grievance 11).
- **J3.** Tell a measurement from a cause (Part II's three signature families).
- **J4.** Not collapse into operationalism: every kind-claim must carry its grain-and-contract index (Grievance 2).
- **J5.** Give the claim that two same-signature components "really" differ a home: finer contract (new question) or history (Part 0).
- **J6.** Keep historical kinds genuinely distinct from forward kinds and genuinely representable (Part II, Grievance 12).
- **J7.** Make the transport/component asymmetry principled rather than arbitrary (Grievance 12).
- **J8.** Keep history out of fidelity, if that is what is being claimed.
- **J9.** Separate a mechanism from a decoy, including a decoy that responds identically on every admitted change (Part V condition 4).
- **J10.** State the analysis-of-kinds conjecture so that a counterexample could count against it.

## 3. The explanation in parts, in the document's own words

- **P1 — the baseline exclusion.** "Two components \(j,j'\) are of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\) when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\). The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states."
- **P2 — forward kind as an indexed equivalence class.** "A forward kind is an equivalence class under this relation, and it is relative to the grain (which fixes \(J\)) and the contract (which fixes the changes)."
- **P3 — historical kind.** "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance."
- **P4 — the conjecture.** "The analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV) is that every discriminating use of a kind-label is one or the other."
- **P5 — the asymmetry (Grievance 12).** "A transport is an occurrence in a physical history… A component is a slot in an organization: what it does under changes is its forward kind… What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract. Provenance matters to *representation* because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change."
- **P6a — world-kind, clause 1.** "Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits."
- **P6b — world-kind, clause 2.** "Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically."
- **P7 — active route (Part IX).** "A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature, and it is what the anchored grade's fourth condition uses."
- **P8 — attack (C).** "A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work."
- **P9 — admissible grain.** "every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes: a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed."

## 4. The change list

Changes the claim says it covers: coarsen or refine the contract; change the grain; change \(A_\Theta\); change the boundary \(b_0\); alter the provenance of a transport; replace an anchor with a backup; add a discriminating kind-label; swap two components' footprints.

Changes it **leaves out**, and says so: the conjecture is unproved ("it is a conjecture"); nothing has been tested by experiment; a new dimension of variation has no witness. These exclusions are stated up front in Part 0, Part XV and Part X — the record is honest here.

One change is **silently absent**: attack (C) requires checking whether "no history distinguishes," but historical-kind predicates for component occurrences are not defined (Finding F12 below), so that half of the change list cannot be run as written.

## 5. The ten tests

### Remove

**F1. The baseline exclusion is doing a job — but a narrower one than claimed, and it is idle at fine contracts.** Remove it, and at a coarse contract two components differing only in state become different forward kinds. So it is not removable without loss *there*. But at a contract containing the displacement group, the exclusion is removable with no loss: for two things at positions 3 and 5, the very bijection that carries "position \(3+d\)" to "position \(5+d\)" for every displacement also carries the baseline pair, since the identity is the zero displacement. The document's motivating sentence — "two things at different positions are one kind in different states" — therefore does not by itself require the exclusion; the bijection already delivers it. The exclusion's real content is "the baseline relation never separates kinds," which is a stronger and less motivated claim than the one stated.

**F2. Removing historical kinds breaks a job.** Part 0's "the claim that they 'really' differ is either a claim about a finer contract… or a claim about their histories, which this semantics also represents, as a second kind of kind" would have nowhere to send the second disjunct. So P3 is not idle.

**F3. Removing P6a breaks the decoy job** — "a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4." Held.

**F4. Removing P6b breaks the backup job**, and active route is load-bearing well beyond it ("lands on an active route" in Part IX's reason use; "lies on an active route" in Part XI's ProducedBy; "actually used, on an active route" in Part X's Attempt). So P6b is a working part with several jobs, not a single-purpose patch.

**F5. Removing P8 removes nothing structural** — it is a limit-statement, not a working part. Its job is to mark the conjecture unproved, which it does.

### Swap

**F6. Swap "predicate on the history" for "a label on the component."** The document blocks this swap with "with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance." But Part IV's witnesses are witnesses *of transports*: "\(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\)", "\(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\)", "\(\operatorname{Dec}(t)\)". Nothing in Part IV is a witness of a component occurrence. The swap is blocked only by a cross-reference that lands one level up.

**F7. Swap "forward kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)" for "forward kind on \(C\)."** Blocked by the decoy case. Held, and held by a checkable change.

**F8. Swap the asymmetry: individuate components by history, transports by signature.** The document's defence (P5) is a *definition* of fidelity as change-response, from which the denial follows analytically. That is a drawn line, not a found fact. It is a legitimate line, but it is weaker than the prose suggests — see F16.

### Flip

**F9. The conjecture's flip is taken seriously.** Part XV names the refuting case (P8) and says "Derivation 1 does not protect the conjecture; it only shows fidelity preserves signatures." So yes, it is stated to be refutable. Two soft spots: "the distinction does explanatory work" is not defined anywhere, so a putative counterexample can be rejected as doing no explanatory work; and the conjecture's "some admissible grain and admitted contract" is an existential, so the refuter must show that *no* admissible grain and *no* admitted contract separates the pair — a universal burden. Refutable in principle, loose in its refutation condition.

**F10. The baseline exclusion's flip is taken seriously.** Include the baseline pairs and state-differences split into kinds at coarse contracts. The exclusion survives this flip — it is held by a job.

**F11. The asymmetry's flip is *not* taken seriously at the anchored grade — and the document's own summaries break on it.** A backup "would have responded identically," i.e. has the same signature on every change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), yet fails condition 4. So at the anchored grade, history *does* change the verdict. Part V nonetheless says: "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label." And in the same paragraph: "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change." A decoy that shares a world-kind on every physically admitted change passes clause 4a; only clause 4b can separate it. So the second sentence can only be true *because of* the clause the first sentence rules out. Likewise Part 0's summary — "whose components are of one forward kind with their anchors under every change the physical module admits" — covers conditions 1, 2 and 4a and omits condition 3 and the active-route clause entirely.

**F12. The precise scope of the failure.** Clause 4b is a condition on the *anchor*, a subnetwork of the target \(D\), not on a component of \(E\). Grievance 12's denial is about components — "a component's history makes any difference to fidelity" — and on that narrow reading it survives. What does not survive is the characterization of the anchored grade as fidelity. The honest rival, which fits condition 4 better than the document's own summary, is: *anchoring is fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) plus an actuality condition on the anchors.* That rival is not a rival theory; it is a correction of two summary sentences.

### Poke

**F13. P1 has both halves.** Change a component's baseline relation only → kind unchanged. Change its response to a non-identity edit → kind changed. Both are runnable.

**F14. P3's poke exists only conditionally.** Change the history that produced the occurrence → historical kind should change; change the baseline relation → it should not. But the first half requires occurrence-level provenance predicates, which are not defined, and may require Θ to supply a history, which Part V makes optional ("Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\)"). The poke is well-formed; its preconditions are not always met.

**F15. P6b's "would not matter" half is exactly what makes it non-fidelity.** Keep the signature on all of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and swap the actual route for a backup → verdict changes. A genuine fidelity condition cannot be defeated by a change that alters no response anywhere.

### Reverse

**F16. Forward kinds run forward.** They are built out of (O) and (K), and the document never claims otherwise: "These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data." No reversal problem; the direction is explicit and indexed.

**F17. Historical kinds point at the wrong level.** "A predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component" is stated as the component's own history; Part XIV's derivation order says "historical kinds, from provenance witnesses," and the only provenance witnesses defined are transport witnesses. So the component's historical kind is, on the document's own derivation, the provenance of a transport that carries the component's organization — a different thing from the history of the component occurrence.

### Hunt the answer in the starting points

**F18. The asymmetry is in the starting points.** "Fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change" is a stipulation that *makes* the denial true rather than reporting it. Combined with "(E) carries **no provenance condition**," the denial is secure at (E) by construction. That is a legitimate scoping move, but it means Grievance 12 answers "why doesn't history matter to fidelity?" with "because we defined fidelity without it." The anchored grade then re-admits history, and the summary sentences do not record the re-admission.

**F19. The baseline exclusion's justification is also a stipulation.** "The relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**" *defines* state; the following clause about things at different positions is an intuition pump, not an argument. Given that kinds are index-relative everywhere, this is admissible — but it is a terminological choice, and the alternative (include the baseline, let the bijection absorb state) is a coherent rival at fine contracts (F20).

### Add a job

**F20. Add the job the document itself needs: distinguish the actual route from a backup that would have responded identically.** Forward kinds cannot do it — the document concedes "the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature." Historical kinds as defined cannot do it either: a backup can share the carrying transport's provenance. The document uses a *third* notion, actuality of route, which is neither a forward kind nor a historical kind. This is not a counterexample to the analysis-of-kinds conjecture, since "active route" is a defined condition rather than an ordinary kind-label. But it does show that the forward/history dichotomy is not exhaustive of the distinctions the anchored grade draws, which is exactly the neighbourhood where Grievance 12's confidence sits.

**F21. Add a job that the taxonomy absorbs cleanly: distinguish two same-signature, same-history components sitting at different places in the organization.** Done by organization structure \((V,J,\text{footprints})\), not by kind. Survives unchanged.

### Build the best rival

**F22. Rival to P1: include the baseline pairs and rely on the footprint bijection.** At contracts rich enough to contain the displacement group, this rival gives the identical verdicts — so at that level the two are the same account, and the exclusion is idle there. At coarse contracts they differ, and the exclusion earns its keep. The document does not separate itself from the rival where they differ, because it never describes the coarse-contract case as the exclusion's real home.

**F23. Rival to the asymmetry's summary: "anchoring is fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) plus actuality."** This rival matches condition 4 exactly; the document's own summary does not. When the best rival to your summary is a more accurate paraphrase of your own condition, the summary is the loose part.

### Check the patches

**F24. The two-kind structure is a recorded, load-bearing patch.** It answers the grievance, it is stated openly ("a second kind of kind"), and it does a real job (J5). It also adds a disjunct to the conjecture, which makes the conjecture *easier* to satisfy — the classical patch signature. The document mitigates this honestly by calling it a conjecture in both Part 0 and Part XV, and by saying "Derivation 1 does not protect the conjecture." So: patch recorded, job real, weakening acknowledged.

**F25. Clause 4b is a patch with many jobs, but it is bundled into the wrong condition.** It repairs the case 4a cannot handle (identical signature, different actuality), and it earns its keep across Parts IX–XI. But it sits inside a condition named "World-kind" whose first sentence is about kind, and it is a *actuality* condition, not a kind condition. It is also omitted from both summary statements of the anchored grade (F11).

### Look inside

**F26. Forward kinds have no inside beyond the table.** A forward kind *is* an equivalence class of signatures; the document says the three families are "descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data." This is a strength (nothing loose is added) and a limit (kinds carry no information beyond (O), \(C\) and the grain — which the document does state).

**F27. Historical kinds have a placeholder inside.** "Selected, constructed, descended from, copied from" — the first two are defined for transports, not component occurrences; "descended from" and "copied from" appear nowhere else in the document and are never defined.

### Two further findings from reading the conditions closely

**F28. "Realizes" in the admissible-grain condition is undefined, and on one reading it bans constants.** "Every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes." A constant component's signature is one relation repeated at every pair. If "realizes" requires the component to *respond*, then constant components — parameters, fixed values, which organizations plainly contain — are inadmissible at every grain. If "realizes" means only "achievable," the condition does nothing. The document does not say which.

**F29. The conjecture is stated with an index in Part XV and without one in Part II.** Part II: "every discriminating use of a kind-label is one or the other." Part XV: "every discriminating use of a kind-label is a forward kind on some admissible grain and admitted contract, or a historical kind." Since kinds are index-relative everywhere, Part XV's is the careful version; Part II's is the loose one.

**F30. The attack on Q1's specific worries mostly does not land, and that is a finding in the document's favour.** Constants and initial conditions are *not* lost to the exclusion: an initial-condition component's value is present in its relation at every pair, not only at the baseline, so two initial conditions with different values differ at every non-baseline pair and are different forward kinds. What *is* lost is narrower and unremarked-by-the-document: a component whose baseline relation is the full relation (i.e. absent at baseline, since "A deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports") but which responds properly under every non-identity edit is declared one forward kind with an always-active component. That is a real over-exclusion, exotic but permitted, because nothing requires \(L_j\) to be a monoid action.

## 6. Where each part came from

- **P1 (baseline exclusion):** *asserted.* A design choice with a stipulated rationale ("the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its state"), not derived from anything.
- **P2 (forward kind):** *built.* Constructed from (K), which is constructed from (O) and \(C\); it commits to behaviour on unmet changes ("a coarser contract identifies more components") and can be caught out there.
- **P3 (historical kind):** *asserted* as to content, *borrowed* as to witness. The form is asserted; the witness is borrowed from Part IV's transport provenance, which is a different level.
- **P4 (conjecture):** *asserted*, and marked as such.
- **P5 (asymmetry):** *fitted* — fitted to the anticipated grievance — and *asserted* as a principle, held in place by the definition of (E).
- **P6a (world-kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)):** *built.* Says something definite about changes outside the contract.
- **P6b (active route):** *built*, with several independent jobs.
- **P8 (attack C):** *built.* Names its own refuting case.
- **P9 (admissible grain):** *built*, with one undefined term.

## 8. Whole-explanation checks

- **Flip:** the conjecture and the baseline exclusion both survive their flips (F9, F10). The asymmetry's summary does not survive its flip at the anchored grade (F11).
- **Direction:** forward kinds run forward and are index-relative (F16). Historical kinds conflate the component's history with the carrying transport's provenance (F17).
- **Answer hidden in the starting points:** the asymmetry is secured by the definition of fidelity (F18); the exclusion by the definition of state (F19). Both are stipulations, mitigated by the document's consistent index-relativity.
- **Change list:** one needed change is silently absent — "no history distinguishes" cannot be checked because component-level historical kinds are undefined (F14).
- **Rivals:** at fine contracts, include-the-baseline is the same account as exclude-the-baseline (F22). "Fidelity plus actuality" is a better account of condition 4 than the document's own summary (F23).
- **Patches:** the two-kind structure is an openly recorded patch that weakens the conjecture, acknowledged (F24). Clause 4b is a healthy patch in the wrong condition and missing from both summaries (F25).

## 9. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Define occurrence-level provenance, or state the inheritance.** Either add component-occurrence provenance predicates to the physical module, or say plainly that a component's historical kind is the provenance of the transport carrying its organization. This one fix tightens P3 (it becomes checkable), J5, and the "no history distinguishes" half of attack (C), which is currently unrunnable.
2. **Rewrite the two summary sentences of the anchored grade.** Part 0's summary should include condition 3 and the active-route clause; Part V's should read *fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), proper decomposition, and actual anchors* — not "component fidelity on the full admitted contract." As written, both summaries are falsified by the backup case that the very next sentence relies on.
3. **Define "does explanatory work" in attack (C)**, or the conjecture is refutable only in principle and the refuter's burden is open-ended.
4. **Define "realizes" in the admissible-grain condition**, or say whether constant components are admissible at any grain.
5. **State the exclusion's real job** — that it governs coarse contracts, and is idle at fine ones — and either accept or exclude the absent-at-baseline component.

---

## 10. What this does not show

Hard to vary is not truth. Every finding above is about which parts are held in place by named jobs and which are loose. In particular, the inconsistency in F11 is a *documentary* inconsistency between summary sentences and condition 4b; the underlying idea — anchoring is fidelity on the full admitted contract plus actuality of anchors — may be exactly right. Nothing here shows the two-kind taxonomy, the asymmetry, or the conjecture to be false. And nothing here has been tested against any real system: the document itself says "Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment."

---

## 11. One next step

Fix items 1 and 2 of §9 together — define the component-level historical-kind predicates, and rewrite the anchored-grade summaries to include the actuality clause — then re-run only the asymmetry test. Once "no history distinguishes" is runnable and the anchored grade is described as it is defined, Grievance 12's denial can be evaluated at the level where it actually bites, and the analysis-of-kinds conjecture's second disjunct will have real content rather than a cross-reference.

# Report: contract adequacy as a conjunct of Account

**Note on terms.** The vocabulary I use below — contract, admitted edit, baseline pair, provenance (selected / constructed / declared), the anchored grade — is the document's own (Parts II–V, Part XIV). The skill I am running maps some of these to plainer words ("change list", "fitted/built/asserted"); where that mapping does work rather than the document's own definitions, I say so. I have only the revised document; I cannot diff it against the earlier source file, so I check internal consistency and consequences, not "what changed".

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What:** the conjunction **Contract adequacy** in (E), Part V — the revision that makes "C is not protective" a condition of Account itself.

**Over what range:** organsations, questions \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\), and the module or declared admission relation that supplies \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

**What is asked:** is that conjunct well-defined; is it too strong; is it too weak (can a scope exclude exactly the separating change and still pass); and does it collide with \(K\), with Derivation 7's frozen assessment, or with Grievance 4's two-index objectivity. **Kind of question:** mixed — partly *what counts as what under this rule* (the definition), partly *does it achieve its purpose* (adequacy as a test).

Exact target text, Part V:

> **Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.

## 2. The jobs

1. Catch a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change — *and not* a scope honestly drawn.
2. Do it without letting a candidate's fate depend on anything other than the question, so the first grade stays "what can be assessed within a question" (Part 0).
3. Be checkable against the candidate's own machinery, not just its outputs.

## 3. The revision in its own parts

| part | document's words |

## 4. The change list

What the revision claims to cover: every admitted pair the scope excludes. What it leaves out, and where that is or is not stated: pairs in \(K\) ("a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning"), and, for organisations with no physical module, the admission relation itself, which is "a **declared admission relation** stated with the question" (Part III).

---

## 5. Findings from the ten tests

### Remove

**F1. As a conjunct it is not idle.** Removing "Adequate" from (E) leaves Part III's requirement that \(S\) has "the reason for each exclusion recorded" and Part V's non-vacuity ("every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently"), but no test of whether the exclusions are *legitimate*. So the conjunct has a job. It is **held** — by the job Part XV and Grievance 4 name for it.

**F2. Its placement contradicts Part 0's own summary of the first grade.** Part 0 defines the grade as: "An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated." Adequacy is not in that list, and Part 0 separately says "The first grade is what can be assessed within a question." A conjunct quantified over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is not assessable within the question. **Loose.**

### Swap

**F3. A near neighbour does the stated job better, and the document names it.** The neighbour is *disagreement*: some admitted pair outside \(C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\neq\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\). Compare Part XV's own attack clause: "A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that **would separate the candidate from the target**." "Separate" is disagreement; the formula tests only *which side moves from its own baseline*. The neighbour separates from the implemented condition on a case below (F5). **Loose:** what could replace it is available and named in the same document.

**F4. A second, opposite neighbour is also available.** No conjunct at all, leaving world-directedness to the anchored grade, which Part V says is where it lives: "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose." The document gives no reason to prefer the movement version over either neighbour.

### The baseline and the undefined translation — well-definedness

**F5. The baseline is determinate; the candidate's side is not.** The baseline pair is fixed: the contract "contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)" (Part III), and (A) forces \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(1),\sigma(b_0))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) there, with (F2) pinning \(\tau(1)=1\). So "which baseline value" has an answer, and the two sides start from the same place. Give credit: this half is clean.

**F6. \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is never defined, and \(\mathcal Q\) is defined only on \(D\) and only on \(C\).** (Q) is "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\)", with "\(\mathcal Q\) … a specified set-theoretic operation on \(D\), its solutions, and its component structure"; coherence requires only that "\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)". Adequacy evaluates the candidate at pairs outside \(C\).

**F7. The candidate's behaviour off \(C\) is outside the transport's stated domain.** Part IV: "\(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) **on the stated scope**"; "A transport is **faithful on \(C\)** when it satisfies (F1) and (F2) of Part V **on \(C\)**."

**F8. The document contradicts itself about \(\tau\)'s domain.** Part IV writes it total — "\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits" — while Part VII relies on it being undefined: "here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, **so \(\tau\) is undefined** and (F2) fails at that pair".

**F9. Two readings, each with a defect.** If "undefined" counts as "no answer" = "does not differ from baseline", the reversed calculation **fails** adequacy — contradicting Part VII: "The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract … **it is an account at that contract**", where the production edits are "admitted, and outside the identification contract". If undefined pairs are skipped, adequacy is vacuous exactly where the transport was never built. Either way the conjunct is **unknown** as written: no test in the document settles which reading holds.

### Too strong

**F10. It imports an off-contract, module-wide demand into the first grade.** The anchored grade is where Part V puts "fidelity … not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits" (condition 4, World-kind). The revision puts a weakened version of the same demand into grade 1. No, it does **not** make *every* restricted-contract account fail — a candidate whose answer-movement pattern matches the target's everywhere on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) passes. It fails accounts whose answer profile is coarse outside the scope, and it fails Part VII's identification account on the natural reading (F9).

**F11. It is monotone in the wrong direction.** Since \(K\) holds pairs "the module does not admit", \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S\). So every admitted pair you **add** to the scope makes adequacy easier; every pair you exclude makes it harder, honest or not. Part XI treats narrowing as suspect — "A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index" — but under the revision narrowing *widens* the adequacy range and so cannot "rescue adequacy"; it rescues only (F1)/(F2)/(A). The sentence and the direction of effect do not fit together.

**F12. For queries whose answer is an invariant the condition degenerates.** Part VII: "no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1)". If the target's answer never leaves baseline on the admitted set, the first clause can never fire and adequacy becomes the one-sided demand that the candidate must also never move off \(C\).

### Too weak — “can a scope exclude exactly the separating change and still pass?”

**F13. Yes, by construction — and this is the counterexample Part XV asks for.** The formula passes whenever both sides move, whatever they move to. Take a scope excluding one admitted pair \((a^*,b^*)\) at which the target moves; take a candidate identical to the target on \(C\), whose one component's relation is altered only at \((a^*,b^*)\) so that its answer there also differs from baseline, to a different value. At that pair both "differ from baseline", so no clause fires; (F1), (F2), (A), non-circularity and non-vacuity all range over \(C\) and hold. Part XV:

> **A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target. This shows the adequacy condition is too weak…

Together with F3 that is a flat internal contradiction: the formula cannot detect the case the gloss and the attack list describe.

**F14. It rules out none of the document's own non-explainers.** "The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract" — and it passes adequacy trivially, since its answer profile is the target's. By the rule that a job ruling out no rival holds nothing new, the added conjunct holds nothing new against the rivals the document names.

**F15. For every question without a physical module, adequacy is defeated by declaration.** "for a mathematical or modelled organization, a **declared admission relation** stated with the question" (Part III); "Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation" (Part VII); "every claim about it is relative to that declaration" (Grievance 7). The scope problem has not been closed; it has moved up one level, to whatever the modeller declares is admitted. The condition bites only where \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is supplied by the primitive package. That asymmetry — strong for physics, empty for declarations — is the honest summary of its strength.

### Poke, reverse, hunt, add a job, look inside

**F16. Poke answered.** *Would alter:* adding one admitted separating edit outside the scope flips the verdict. *Would not:* bijections preserving "the physical module's admission …, the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings" — Derivation 8: "Transporting all data along \(\phi\) preserves (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK)". So it is a working part, not a label.

**F17. Direction is not the trouble.** The condition is symmetric by its own words ("or the reverse") and derives nothing about cause and effect; it compares each side to its own baseline, and those coincide because \((1,b_0)\in C\) and (A) holds there. It is a correlation-shaped condition doing a correlation-shaped job; that is not a defect in itself.

**F18. Hunt: the answer sits in the starting points.** Whether the scope is protective is decided by \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which Part XIV lists either as the primitive package ("the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\)") or as "the declared admission relation for non-physical organizations". Grievance 4 concedes this is external: "whether or not anyone supplies the change." **Borrowed.**

**F19. Add a job, and see what it kills.** Add "do not let the first grade depend on the module" → failed (F2, F10). Add "be evaluable inside the question" → failed (F6–F9). Add "catch the change that separates candidate from target" → failed (F13). Three jobs, no rival version killed that the document had not already killed elsewhere.

**F20. Look inside: it does not reach the workings.** Adequacy is computed from \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\), an output profile; Derivation 9 says "**Output descriptions do not determine accounts** … no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both." Two candidates differing only inside get the same adequacy verdict. The inside work remains where the document put it, in the anchored grade's condition 4 — which is the other grade.

**F21. It rewrites the work, support and criticism machinery.** Account is not a leaf: "\(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\)", CriticalBlock and Boundary (Part VI); "\(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)\)" (Part IX, so whether a criticism *bears* now depends on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)); \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\) inside (EK) (Part XI). The document's own order confirms the propagation: "(E) depends on those and on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) for adequacy; anchoring depends on (E) … (S), (B), (D) depend on (E)."

**F22. It creates a new source of interference.** Part VI: "**Interference.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\}\}\): the full candidate fails although a subset succeeds." A candidate whose extra component makes its answer profile track the target off \(C\) cannot become *inadequate* by that, but a candidate whose extra component makes it respond off \(C\) where the query's answer does not can. So a candidate can now fail Account as *more responsive* than, and be rescued by, a strictly less faithful subset. That is a verdict the document's examples do not anticipate and does not argue for.

**F23. Anchoring is now incomplete as summarised.** Part V: "Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution." But anchoring is Account plus four conditions, and Account now contains a condition about answer *movement* off \(C\). Either condition 4's fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) entails the answer-movement agreement — then adequacy is redundant for anchored accounts and only costs the first grade — or it does not — then what excludes a candidate whose assembly diverges off \(C\) is adequacy, not the condition the document names. The document's own (F1)-holds/(F2)-fails case (the clock drawing missing the shaft) shows the two are not the same condition.

### Check the patches, and the index

**F24. Collision with Derivation 7 and Part VIII.** Derivation 7: "**The assessment is indexed to \(C\)**; its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index (Part VIII)." Part VIII: "A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change." But the same \(C\) can be presented by different \((C_{\mathrm{adm}},S)\) pairs, since \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\) does not determine \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is not an ingredient of \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) at all — it is "the set of pairs **the module in force** admits". So either the index is not \(C\) (and Derivation 7's proof, Part III's "An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\)" and Grievance 10's "an event with a frozen contract" are all incomplete), or the truth value of "\(E\) is an account on \(C\)" can change with a later physics, contradicting Part VIII. Derivation 7's closing sentence also overstates the conjunct's reach: "the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded" — by F13 it catches only some such changes.

**F25. Collision with Grievance 4's two-index treatment.** Grievance 4: "'Faithful on C' is a fact within C. 'C is not protective' is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes … An objectivity claim is the conjunction." Under the revision the second conjunct is no longer separable: it is inside the predicate that was the first conjunct. So the promised two indices are not visible in (E), which is written as one six-conjunct formula, and the first grade is no longer "a fact within C". Grievance 4's own wording ("would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's, or the reverse") is the movement form, so the two-index passage and Part V agree with each other and disagree with Part V's gloss and Part XV's attack (F3, F13).

**F26. Collision with \(K\).** No formal collision: \(K\subseteq C\) and \(K\) holds no admitted pairs, so \(K\)-pairs never enter \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\). Two interactions. First, adequacy is blind to the case the eliminative treatment rests on — Part VII: "the objectivity condition then ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) only, so what such an account establishes about the world is that no admitted change produces the effect, and what it establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual." Under the revision, Account-hood for such an account is decided by a conjunct blind to its central change. Second, for declared-admission organisations, \(K\) and \(S\) are both drafted by the modeller, so a question can be drawn so that the separating pair sits in \(K\) and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) is empty of it (F15). Also note Part III's clause "\(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated" is not decidable as written, which matters because my \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S\) step depends on \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\).

**F27. A provenance record with no slot for a condition.** The document's provenance machinery attaches to transports ("Three provenances", Part IV) and to questions ("\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\text{selected},\text{constructed}\}\)", Part III), and histories to components (historical kinds, Part II). There is no slot for a *conjunct of a predicate*. The revision adds a load-bearing, module-dependent conjunct that the document's own bookkeeping cannot record.

## 6. Where the parts came from

Using the document's own trichotomy (Part IV), not the skill's plain words, since these terms are the document's:

- **The movement test:** *declared*. No selection witness (the document says of itself: "Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment; the experiments that have been run test proxies"), and no construction witness of the kind Part X demands (an owned subhistory with a "nontrivial binding construction required for that use"). It is entered by the author.
- **The range \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\):** *borrowed*, from the physical module — "The **physical module** \(\Theta\): … the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\)", listed as one of "two primitive packages" — or from a declared admission relation. The conjunct's truth is held in place by something that is not under test here.
- **The baseline reference:** *held*, by Part III's \((1,b_0)\) and by (A).
- **The symmetry clause:** *loose*; no case in the document turns on it, and it is the half able to reject a candidate for responding where the query's answer does not.
- **The two glosses ("tell it apart", "separate"):** loose; they describe a different condition from the one written.

## 8. Whole-revision checks

- **Flip.** Had the conjunct not been promoted into (E), the document's predictions would differ in Part VI's support sets, Part IX's bearing, Part XI's (EK), and in Part VII's sentence that the reversed calculation "is an account at that contract". So the revision is live, not decorative.
- **Direction.** No backwards-working is introduced; the trouble is the range, not the arrow.
- **The answer hidden in the starting points.** The verdict "not protective" is fixed by an input the question either borrows from the primitive module or writes itself.
- **What the change list leaves out.** \(K\), and — for all non-physical organisations — the admission relation itself.
- **Best rival.** Either the disagreement neighbour (F3, F13) or no conjunct at all with world-directedness left to the anchored grade (F4, F23). Nothing in the document tells the two apart from what was chosen.
- **Patch check.** The revision does not narrow anything quietly. It does something worse for a weak condition: it keeps the condition's strength unchanged and multiplies its reach, so a test the document itself lists as refutable now decides Account, support sets, critical blocks, whether a criticism bears, and whether knowledge was created.

## 9. What would make it harder to vary

One cut and one rewording, and they are entangled — do one, or say which you mean:

1. **Replace the movement test with disagreement, or drop the conjunct and say so.** If disagreement, state whether undefined \(\tau\) pairs fail or are skipped, and where \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is defined (Part V never defines it). If dropped, add a sentence to Part 0 and Part V saying world-directedness is the anchored grade's business, and fix Derivation 7 and Grievance 4 accordingly.
2. **Index the claim honestly.** If adequacy stays, write the assessment's index as \((C, C_{\mathrm{adm}}, \mathcal Q, \text{module})\) in Part III, Grievance 10, Derivation 7 and Part VIII, and say what happens to "its truth value at its own index does not change" when the module gains an admitted edit.

## 10. What this does not show

Passing or failing here is not truth. I have not shown the revision is false about the world; I have shown that one condition is under-defined off the contract, too weak to do its stated job on the case the same document asks for, borrowed for its range, and placed where the document's own account of the grades and of the frozen index does not want it. I have not compared it with the earlier source file, and I have not checked the rest of the revision for other changes.

If it does, the condition is refuted by the document's own test and the fix is the choice in §9.1; if it does not, name the clause that catches it — that clause, not this one, is doing the work.

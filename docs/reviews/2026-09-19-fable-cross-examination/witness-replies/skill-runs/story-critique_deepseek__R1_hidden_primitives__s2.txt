## 1. Corrections first

And the page already concedes the packages are expensive: the physical module **"is more than 'physics' in the ordinary sense, because it must say how a lump decomposes."**

Held to *that* claim — every predicate defined from Θ, 𝒩, and the listed parameters — the document fails, and it fails in one recognisable way. ## 2. Scene-by-scene notes, by sequence

### Sequence A — the base schemata (Parts 0, II, III)

**1. The document declares what it is for, and the declaration is held.** *"A cause, a rule, a measurement, a thing — is not asked of a label. At a grain and a contract of admitted changes, a forward kind is how a component responds to the changes that contract admits."* The job of Part 0 is to fix the whole document's yardstick, and it does: every later "kind" claim carries a grain-and-contract index, and every later attack is stated as a falsifier rather than a topic. This is the strongest structural move in the document and a reviser should not touch it. It is held because swapping it for a label-first ontology would make Derivation 1 ("fidelity preserves signatures") say nothing.

**2. The first use of a technical term in the body arrives after a prose use — harmless, but note it.** Part 0 uses "faithful" colloquially (*"whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors"*) a page before Part IV defines it; Part IV then calls it a definition: *"A transport is **faithful on C** when it satisfies (F1) and (F2)."* Free detail. No leak.

**3. The organization schema is honest about what it takes from outside.** *"(O) is a schema over the supplied data… Those data are a **declared parameter** of every use of this semantics."* The job of (O) is to make the whole of Part V checkable; it succeeds, and the declared-parameter move is what keeps (F1)'s equation contentful rather than vacuous. Contrast this with note 8: the same strategy is not carried through everywhere.

**4. "Licenses" and "permitted by" are used as primitive gates and are not defined anywhere.** Twice, in load-bearing positions: Part II, *"the physical module must license the attribution"*; and the Commitments, *"Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physical module."* The job of these clauses is to be the boundary between modelling and physical claim. Neither "license" nor "permitted" is in the primitive list of Part XIV, and neither is reduced to Inst or A_Θ, even though the same Part IV talks as if Inst *were* that gate (*"When the organization is attributed to a physical occurrence, the physical module must license the attribution"*). Failure: two boundary conditions where the list admits one. Better version: identify "licensed" with "Inst holds", or list "licensing" as a third primitive. As written, this is a judgement word priced as a relation.

**5. The organization's own edit set and the module's edit set are never joined.** Part II defines *"A is a set of admitted edits, closed under a partial associative composition with identity 1"* — a parameter of D. Part III then writes *"C_adm ⊆ A × B is the set of pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization, the physically admitted edits A_Θ (Part XII)."* The job of this clause is to make contract adequacy (Part V) a condition on *physical* possibilities, which is the whole objectivity story. It fails: no clause states that for a physical attribution the organization's A is A_Θ(o). A can be any declared set; if it is, every "protective contract" finding is relative to the modeller's A while the document's prose says it is relative to the world's. Better version: one sentence in Part II making A = A_Θ(o)|_{V} an identity for physical attributions, not a hope.

**6. A_Θ is supplied in two incompatible forms.** Part XIV: *"the admitted edit set A_Θ"* — a set. Part XII: *"the **admitted edit set** A_Θ(o)"* — a function of an occurrence. Part IV then writes *"the admitted edits A_Θ restricted to the occurrence"* as if this were one operation. The job is to be the single quantifier base for contracts, world-kinds, and grain admissibility. If A_Θ is a set of alterations of *the occurrence's state*, then C_adm's first factor ranges over state alterations while the contract's own A ranges over organization edits — two typed things in one product. This is a genuine type gap, not a wording snag, and it is where the physical side of the theory is thinnest. Better version: define A_Θ(o) and then define the *induced* organization edits by the attribution, or drop the identity and say the relationship is licensed case by case.

**7. Inst is a relation in its statement and a function wherever it is used.** The statement is exemplary: *"It is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain (a mechanical one, a thermodynamic one, a computational one), and nothing here assumes uniqueness."* But (R) writes *"some organization o instantiates at ℓ is held to c"* — singular; Deploy says *"s at ξ holds a representation of c — by (R)"*; and the physical module is described as *"a decomposition oracle"* that returns *"a full organization."* A relation doing function-work. The job of "some organization" is to quantify over the several, and it *does* in (R)'s own quantifier (∃D); the leak is in the definite article when the semantics needs to pick *which* organization carries the transport for Deploy, Bearing, or (EK). This is the answer to the user's third hunt: Inst is being asked to supply, silently, what it was correctly declared not to supply — a canonical decomposition. Better version: carry D as an explicit argument of Rep, Deploy, and (EK), instead of relying on "an organization o instantiates".

### Sequence B — transports and provenance (Part IV, with X)

**8. The provenance witnesses carry an undefined representational notion, and this is the cycle.** Sel is defined by: *"no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to t, to H, or to the survival condition"* — and then the blindness clause is *"A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route."* Con is *"a construction witness contains both"* (a representation of a defect, a target instantiated before the variation). Now the sentence that is supposed to *use* this apparatus: *"Content identity at the primitive layer… does not use the representation predicate below, **which is defined in terms of it**."* Take that literally: (R) is defined in terms of content-identity-at-the-primitive-layer. If "itemised representation" is the technical predicate, then (R) — which requires Sel ∨ Con — is defined in terms of something defined in terms of (R). That is the cycle the user asked me to hunt, and it exists on the page.

Part XIV forbids it: *"provenance depends on Θ and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on Inst and ≡_ℓ **but not on (R)** — the stratification of Part IV (targets are *instantiated*, not *represented*, in a construction witness) is what keeps this order well-founded."* The offending item is exact and single: the "itemised representation of the defect" and the "record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held" in Sel, and the "representation of a defect" in the Con comparison. Recommendation, because the fault is serious: either (a) define that record without (R), as an organisation D' with an occurrence o and a transport satisfying (F1)–(F2) with Inst(o,D') — and say so, which keeps the stratification true — or (b) drop the clause and accept "Sel" as declared, which is honest but costs Grievance 9, which currently *passes* its self-test (*"Without the elimination clause every transport would be counted as selected; with it, 'selected' is a hypothesis, not a stipulation"* — held, by the elimination clause, and that clause carries no representational freight). Route (a) is the one the document's own grammar already allows.

**9. "Blind" is defined, italicised, and never used again.** *"A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when…"* The job the clause is meant to do — keep the selection story from being sighted — is instead done by the "itemised representation" phrase itself. One job, two routes, and the named route is idle: remove "blind" and every subsequent use still gets done. Keep it only if it is given its second job (e.g. as the standing condition on variation operators in Sel), or cut it.

**10. "(R) takes what it takes from outside is Inst_ℓ" — and nothing else.** *"What (R) takes from outside is Inst_ℓ."* That is honest and exactly right as a self-description, *given* note 8 is repaired. This sentence is the document at its best: it knows the debt and states it.

**11. What a physical system can represent is gated by "there is a provenance", whose absence-detection is untestable.** The precedence rule — *"it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*"* — makes "declared" the residue of unexamined history. The job is to keep "declared" honest as a modelling convenience. It half-works: the cost is stated (*"a claim about representation or creativity cannot rest on it"*), but the residue is defined by negation over events nobody has itemised, so in practice a working theory will label almost everything "declared", and the document does not say what standing an unexamined attribution has. Better version: require the ground to be marked — transported occurrence-level evidence for Sel, none for Decl — so a Decl attribution is visibly a place where a witness is *wanted*.

### Sequence C — the conditions (Part V)

**12. The five conditions are individually well-held, and one of them quietly introduces a second kind of "proper".** **"Non-circular dependence"** contains: *"there exists an edit τ(a)… that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block B ⊊ Γ while preserving the other boundary conditions"* — a proper sub-block is just a proper subset. Anchoring 1 then defines *"Every anchor λ(k) is a proper subnetwork of D"* and uses "properness" for two further things ("not the whole target", "refines a cover"). Three uses, one word, three relations: A ⊊ B; non-identical anchor; a cover-refinement whose definition ("refines a cover of D's components") appears nowhere. The job is to state the second grade in the same vocabulary as the first (*"all stated in the same vocabulary"*). Failure at "refines a cover", which is doing the work of "the components of E cover the dependencies of D" and is not stated as a condition on λ. Better version: say it as a condition on anchors' union covering touched components (as Part V's prose already does) and drop the covering-language.

**13. "Definable" in anchoring 3 is a semantic notion smuggled as a syntactic one.** *"no active component's relation is, on C, **definable** as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it."* The job is to exclude the one-component repackaging by name, which is important — the document is right that *"a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law."* Failure: "definable" relative to what class? Definitional logic is a parameter, and it is not listed. As it stands, the condition's extension depends on an unstated satisfaction class, so the exclusion of the repackaging is not yet a theorem. Better version: state it extensionally — "no active component's relation equals Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it at any (a,b) ∈ C" — which is what the surrounding sentences plainly mean.

**14. "The answer follows by evaluating E under its independent boundary conditions" is the one sentence in Part V's headline clause that is not a defined predicate.** The job: to be the negative half of the circularity prohibition, i.e. the thing the (I3)-style examples rely on. Failure: "follows", and "independent of the answer" is then defined only negatively — *"A boundary condition b is **independent** of the answer when no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports."* That definition is itself well-formed and defensible; the leaked word is "follows". The "p because p" case is caught (agreed), but a candidate that computes the answer from a boundary condition smuggled in at a *different* port with no faithful transport out is not obviously caught. Better version: replace "follows by evaluating" with the (F1)/(F2)-relative statement of what an evaluation is in this vocabulary, or list evaluation as a declared parameter.

**15. Non-vacuity, adequacy, and (A) are fully defined and among the best-built clauses in the document.** *"there is no admitted pair (a,b) ∈ C_adm \ C at which Ans_p differs from its baseline value while Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b)) does not, or the reverse"* — every term is defined; the two-way "or the reverse" closes the flip test by construction. This is what the rest of the document should look like. And the anchored grade has a genuinely elegant result built on it: the one-component candidate *"satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3"*. Held, by Properness and No-global-relation, and those are the correct holders.

### Sequence D — work and interference (Part VI)

**16. There is a circular dependence between "criticality" and Account(E|W,p), and I cannot tell from the page whether it is an incompatibility.** Part VI sets: *"Fix ℰ. The **restriction** E|W… Define S_{E,p} = {W ⊆ Γ : Account(E|W,p)}."* The job is to define which blocks are load-bearing. But Account(E|W,p) is (E), and (E)'s Non-circularity witness is a clause about Γ's proper sub-blocks: *"removes or replaces a nonempty proper sub-block B ⊊ Γ."* The restriction operation deletes components in Γ \ W; if it deletes *all of them* (W = ∅), Non-circularity's "removing all of Γ… witnesses nothing" fires; if it deletes any nonempty proper part, the witness fires and W ∉ S. So for a candidate that satisfies (E), every W ⊊ Γ fails S. Then S can contain only Γ. But Part VI's own examples require the opposite — *"Redundant routes. Γ = {a,b}, S = {{a},{b},{a,b}}: each is contributory"* and *"Interference. Γ = {a,b}, S = {{a}}: the full candidate fails"* — and {a} ∈ S is exactly what Non-circularity forbids. The two clauses are doing work in opposite directions. This is the most serious internal tension I found, because Part VI's whole apparatus (CriticalBlock, Boundary, the finite monotone theorem *"the critical singletons are exactly ⋃min S and the globally indispensable ones are exactly ⋂min S"*) presupposes a non-singleton S. Better version: in Part VI, define S over the *boundary apparatus* for a fixed Γ-restriction semantics, or state explicit that the (O)-deletion of Part VI's restriction is a different edit from the τ-witness of Non-circularity. If it is, say so in one sentence; the theorem becomes non-trivial again. This is the one place where I would not accept a mere rewording — the clauses need to be separated or the examples retired.

### Sequence E — the four places where the module is over-asked (Parts VII, X, XII, XIII)

**17. The mathematical route fixes the A/A_Θ gap by fiat, and the fiat is the tell.** *"But 'physically admitted' is not defined for a mathematical organization, so a mathematical question must **declare** its admission relation… as a physical claim is relative to the physical module."* The job is to make the odd-order example honest, and it succeeds on its own terms — the example later says *"the admission relation is declared… the admitted edits are alterations of the presentation"*, and the derivation 5 apparatus is what makes the construction checkable. But it also establishes, from the inside, that "what changes the module admits" is a *declared* object whenever it is not physical, and no test is supplied for whether a physical A_Θ is any less declared. This is the operationalism objection (Grievance 2) arriving through the document's own mathematics rather than from outside. Better version: for physical organisations, state a *procedure* — not a condition — by which an admitted edit is exhibited, so that "admitted" is a claim with a witness like every other heavy term in the document.

**18. "Explanatory use" carries Build, and is defined nowhere.** Build requires *"prepares an organization that some occurrence of s instantiates **for explanatory use of c**"*, and the "nontrivial binding construction required for that use" is *"required for the use when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for c on its contract."* The job is exactly the job of the account grade in Part V. Failure: no clause identifies "explanatory use of c" with Account-relative evaluation on c's contract, and Part XIV's list does not include it. So Build cannot be assessed except by appeal to (E) — which is available, and is precisely the sharpening the document should state. Better version: one sentence making "explanatory use of c" the use of c's organization as an E for its p, i.e. as evaluated in (F1), (F2), (A) on c's contract — which the paragraph *"Here c is a content whose organization is the E of Account(E,p_c), and Account is evaluated on the contract of p_c fixed at e_c"* in Part XI already says about (EK). Transplant that sentence into Build.

**19. "Nontrivial use respect" in Deploy is an undefined adjective repeated as a noun.** *"The repertoire R_{β,ℓ}(s,ξ) is the set of contents deployable in some **nontrivial use respect**."* "Deployable" is just Deploy; "nontrivial" is never defined, and the interesting cases (a content deployed only for trivial ends) cannot be distinguished. Better version: define it by the same measure used elsewhere, e.g. via (P)'s obligation change, or via ProducesVia.

**20. "Actually used, on an active route" in Attempt references a defined term, and the definition has one unbound word in it.** Active route is: *"a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction **under the declared contrasts**."* "Under the declared contrasts" is never declared anywhere, and "declared contrasts" appears in Part XIV's parameter list only as *"the restriction operation's named background and the edit family V of Part VI."* The job is to make active-route a checkable predicate, since Part XI's ProducedBy hangs on it. Failure at one word. Better version: "declared contrasts" := an element of V (Part VI), which is already a declared parameter.

**21. The execution families are the physical module's largest unexamined supply item.** The module supplies *"the execution families Exec(π,z,i;χ) — the set of executions of protocol π from state z on input i under enabling conditions χ"*, and RetReal quantifies: *"∀η ∈ Exec(π,z,i;χ), η completes with o ∈ T[i] and z' ∈ C."* The job is to make capability claims checkable in the base class. It fails in the same way as A_Θ: the *semantics of executions* — what an execution of a protocol *is* — is invoked and never characterised, so "completes", "deadlock is not vacuous success", and the "non-empty on legitimate inputs" requirement are all stated without a base. Of the two packages, this is where I would say the document asks Θ for *more* than "physics plus decomposition": it asks Θ for a protocol-execution semantics on top of a decomposition oracle. Part XIV lists Exec as one item; its weight is a Part's worth.

### Sequence F — the collected class (Part XIV, and the derivations)

**22. The dependence order is acyclic as written, with exactly one broken node.** I went clause by clause. (K) from (O), grain, contract — clean. (F1), (F2), (A) from (O), (Q), (K) — clean. Anchoring from (E), (K), C_adm, histories — clean. (S), (B), (D) from (E) — clean, and subject to note 16. (R) from (F1)–(F2), Inst, provenance — clean *provided* note 8 is repaired, because the only node in the claimed order that reaches outside the listed base is Sel's "itemised representation". **23. **24. ## 3. How it fits together

The document's plot and its theme do meet, in one place, repeatedly: every definition is followed by a *test* — a decoy that fails it, a contract that cannot separate two mechanisms, a selection history that guarantees nothing off its sample (*"Where the population is parametric and not closed under pointwise alteration, the survivor may be determined off its history… and this derivation is silent"* — the scoping here is exemplary). Derivations 3 and 9 are honest, small, and correctly deny more than they prove. Part XV's attack list is a falsifier list, not a menu, and the attacks are ranked by what falls (*"in order of how much falls if they fail"*) — that is the right shape for a theory being offered for refutation.

Where they do not meet: the provenance machinery. Selection and construction are the document's one genuinely new structural idea, and they are the two clauses that carry the smuggled representational notions. The formal side of the paper (Parts II–V) is nearly airtight; the historical side (the two witnesses, Build, Deploy) is carried mostly by prose a first-order reading cannot check. That is the seam.

## 4. The three faults that matter most, ranked

**1. The cycle (R) ↔ Sel through "itemised representation", and the stratification claim that denies it (notes 8, 22).** This is the fault the user's brief was built to find, and it is real. The fix is small and within the document's own grammar: define the defect-record as an organisation plus a transport satisfying (F1)–(F2) with Inst, and say so in Sel exactly as Con already says it.

**2. Part VI's support apparatus and Part V's non-circularity witness pull opposite ways, so whether Γ can have alternative supports is undecided (note 16).** This matters because CriticalBlock, Boundary, the finite monotone theorem, and Part XI's ProducedBy all stand on S. This one is not a rewording; the two operations (Part VI's (O)-deletion and Part V's τ-edit) must be told apart on the page.

**3. The completeness claim of Part XIV fails on the same item each time: undefined nouns inside chosen clauses (notes 4, 6, 14, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23).** The method of Part XIV is sound; the enumeration is short. Each missing item is one sentence.

## 5. What I am unsure of

I read the page only, as the brief said; where a term may be doing work I simply failed to see, I have said so in the note rather than asserting a fault — "refines a cover" (note 12) and "under the declared contrasts" (note 20) are candidates for that. Whether the Sel defect-record was *meant* to be the (R)-predicate, or was meant to be the Inst-and-fidelity structure it should be, the page does not settle, and I have not "fixed" it — the two routes are stated in note 8 and the choice is the writer's. And one judgement of type: this is a semantics document, so I have judged it against its own internal-completeness and non-circularity claims, not against explanatory success in the world, which the document correctly locates in the constitutive conjecture.

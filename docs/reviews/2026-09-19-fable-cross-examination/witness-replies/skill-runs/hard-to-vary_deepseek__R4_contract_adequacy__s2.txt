# Hard-to-vary run on `20_semantics_revised`

**A note on vocabulary, before anything else.** Every plain term I use below is a term *of this document*, not external evidence. The skill's "the change list" is this document's **contract** \(C\); the skill's "still explains" is this document's **Account (E)**; "the question" is \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\); "working parts" are **active commitments** \(\Gamma\). Where the skill and the document use the same word, the word came from the document. Nothing in the skill's own vocabulary is being used here as support for a conclusion about the document.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is being explained?** Why the revision puts *contract adequacy* inside the predicate Account, and whether that placement holds.

**The claim, in the document's words.** Part V:

> **Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.

and its placement:

> \[ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}. \tag{E} \]

**Range.** Physical and non-physical organizations alike, since \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is "the set of pairs the module in force admits" (Part III) and for a mathematical or modelled organization that set is "a **declared admission relation**" (Part III).

**Kind of question.** This is a *rule-status / question-fidelity* question ("does this condition do what it says on the cases it names?"), with a production question underneath it ("what produces the verdict?"). I test it as a definition — which is what the revision is.

**What would count as failing it, said up front.** Any case where the verdict of the condition comes apart from "the contract excludes a change that would tell the candidate apart from the target" (the document's own gloss, Part V: *"An account whose contract excludes an admitted change that would tell it apart from the target fails adequacy."*), in either direction; or where the condition has no verdict at all; or where it moves a claim's truth value without moving the claim's index.

## 2. The revision taken apart, and the change list

**Parts, in the document's own words.**

1. The **concept** "\(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\)".
2. The **quantifier**: "\((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)".
3. The **first clause**, target-side: "\(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value".
4. The **second clause**, candidate-side: "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not".
5. The **rescue clause**: "or the reverse".
6. The **placement**: adequacy as one of the conjuncts of (E).
7. The **rationale**, in Grievance 4 and Derivation 7.
8. The **stake**: Grievance 4, *"An objectivity claim is the conjunction, and the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism."*

**The change list — the changes to the world that would test it.** (For a definition, the "world" is the space of target/candidate/contract triples.)

| | Change | Should adequacy fire? |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Target flat off \(C\); candidate flat off \(C\) | No |
| C2 | Target moves off \(C\); candidate inert at that pair | Yes |
| C3 | Target moves; candidate moves to the *same* value | No (fidelity would hold) |
| C4 | Target moves; candidate moves to a **different** value | **Yes** |
| C5 | Target flat; candidate moves ("the reverse") | Yes |
| C6 | The separating pair is re-declared into \(K\) | — |
| C7 | The declared admission relation is narrowed so the pair leaves \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) | — |
| C8 | The baseline \(b_0\) is chosen so \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a^{*},b^{*})=\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) | — |
| C9 | \(E\) is replaced by an account-equivalent candidate (Derivation 2) that differs off \(C\) | No change |
| C10 | The module in force grows (more edits admitted); \(C\), \(S\), \(K\) fixed | No change |

The list is not empty and it contains cases that could have mattered. What it does not contain — deliberately, on my part — is any change to \(C\) itself: this is a test of the condition, so \(C\) is held still and the *rest* of the world moves.

## 3. The one fork that decides all three of the user's questions

**F1. Adequacy is evaluated exactly where the document says its translation is undefined, and the document never says what to do there.** Part IV types the transport total on edits:

> \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\) where \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) **on the stated scope**, \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits, \(\sigma\) translates boundaries

Note the asymmetry: \(\pi\) is explicitly scoped, \(\tau\) and \(\sigma\) are not. Then Part VII, on the document's own worked example of direction:

> here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, **so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair**

That pair — the production intervention on \(H\) — is *admitted* and *outside the identification contract*. It is precisely the kind of pair adequacy quantifies over ("\((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)"). So the condition's second clause contains a term the author elsewhere declares undefined at exactly the pairs that matter.

**F2. The fork.** Two conventions are available, and each one breaks a different half of the user's question:

- **Convention A — "undefined is a failure" (the convention Part VII uses for (F2): "\(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails").** Then at the production edit the candidate's answer is not the target's, the pair is a separating pair, adequacy fires, and the reversed calculation **is not an account at the identification contract** — contradicting the very next sentence of the same paragraph: *"The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\): it is an account at that contract."* Adequacy is then **too strong**: it kills the document's flagship two-stage example, and (worse, see F7) it kills every honestly scoped account whose candidate has no port for the excluded edit.
- **Convention B — "undefined is not a witness" (a clause is only a witness if it is true).** Then adequacy passes for the reversed calculation, and the document's example survives — but only by being blind at precisely the pair it exists to catch. Adequacy is then **too weak**: any candidate can immunise itself by keeping \(\tau\) partial off \(C\). And \(\tau\) is candidate data: it is part of the transport, which is part of the candidate triple \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\).

So the answer to "is it well-defined" is: not as written (F1), and the two ways of making it well-defined are the two failure modes the user asks about. The fork is not a quibble about wording; the *same fact* (partial \(\tau\)) is used by the document as the reason the reversed calculation fails production fidelity, and would be used by a candidate to escape contract adequacy.

**F3. The typing and the example contradict each other outright.** Part IV writes \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) — a function on the whole edit set, because that is what the arrow notation says. Part VII writes "\(\tau\) is undefined". Both cannot hold. This matters beyond bookkeeping: if \(\tau\) *is* total (Part IV's typing), then \(\tau(a_H)\) is some edit of the reversed organization — and the document's own gloss of what happens there is "*intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\)*", i.e. the candidate is inert at an admitted, out-of-scope pair. Under that total reading, adequacy fires by Convention A's logic and Part VII's claim that it "is an account at that contract" is false. So whichever way the inconsistency is repaired, something in the document has to give.

## 4. The ten tests, run

### T1 — Remove
**F4. Remove contract adequacy from (E). What job stops being done?** Traced through the document, the conjunct has three citation-jobs: the sentence in Grievance 4 that says the second conjunct "is a condition in this semantics (Part V)"; the sentence in Derivation 7, *"the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded"*; and Part XI's *"A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index."* None of the three is lost if non-protectiveness is stated as a **stand-alone condition on \(C\) against \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)** — which is exactly how Grievance 4 already words it: *"Two claims, two indices. 'Faithful on C' is a fact within C. 'C is not protective' is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes."* The third is a consequence of Part III's index rules, not of this conjunct. So the conjunct is **idle**: removable at the cost of editing a citation. Removing it in a pair with non-vacuity loses nothing either — non-vacuity is documentation ("every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently"), and adequacy is documentation-plus-substance-that-doesn't-arrive (F9–F11).
**F5. And keeping it breaks something the document states.** Part 0: *"The first grade is what can be assessed within a question. The second is what 'the explanation is about the actual mechanism' means, and it is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract."* Adequacy is a condition on the target's answer profile at pairs on a larger set (\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\)). So the revision puts a larger-contract condition inside the first grade. That is the document's own stated trap, in its own domains file's words: an added part that breaks what worked without it.

### T2 — Swap
**F6. Swap the baseline form for the direct form, and see what stops you.** Replace clause 3–5 with: *no admitted pair outside \(C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\neq\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)* (with a stated rule for undefined terms). Does the document survive? Everything survives *except* Part VII's claim that the reversed calculation "is an account at that contract" — because there, the candidate's answer and the target's simply are not the same, whether by inertness or by undefinedness. So the thing that holds the *baseline wording* in place is one example. That is a weak holder; the skill's design-domain marking applies — this is a **free choice presented as forced**. Grievance 4 presents the placement as forced ("the second conjunct **is** a condition in this semantics (Part V)"), and Part V presents the wording as forced, but the swap shows the wording is selected to keep one example alive, and the example is itself decided by an unstated convention (F1–F3).

### T3 — Flip the outcome
**F7. Had adequacy stayed outside (E), would Grievance 4 have read any worse?** No. The same paragraph could have said: *"the second conjunct is a separate condition on \(C\), stated in Part V alongside Account."* The objection it answers ("there is no objectivity") is fully answered by that sentence. So the flip is smooth: the same explanatory story covers both the revision and its absence. A part that covers both outcomes is not held in place by either. This is a **loose** part in the skill's sense, and the skill's warning applies verbatim: an explanation that covers the opposite equally well is held in place by nothing.

### T4 — Poke
**F8. The "should move it" half of the poke fails.** Name one change that *should* alter adequacy's verdict: add to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) a pair where the candidate and the target disagree. Name one that should not: relabel, or recode. The "should not" half passes — adequacy is label-blind, by construction. The "should move it" half fails for change **C4**: at a pair where the target's answer moves off baseline and the candidate's answer moves off baseline *to a different value*, neither clause is satisfied. Clause 1 requires "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does **[not]** [differ from its baseline value]" — false here. Clause 5, "the reverse", requires \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) to differ while \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) does not — also false here. So no witness, so adequacy passes, at a pair where a candidate and target visibly disagree.
**F9. What adequacy actually constrains off \(C\), stated plainly.** Clause 3 forbids the target being baseline-separating where the candidate is not; clause 5 forbids the mirror. Together they require exactly: **the set of admitted pairs at which the target's answer differs from its baseline equals the set at which the candidate's does.** The *values* off \(C\) are unconstrained. So adequacy is a condition on the **support pattern** of the target's answer profile, not on the candidate's agreement with it. A part with no "would-not" half is a label; here the missing half is the disagreement itself, and the consequence is F10.

### T5 — Reverse
**F10. Adequacy is a softened restatement of (A), read backwards off the target's own profile.** (A) is: \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\) for \((a,b)\in C\). Adequacy is: *(weakly)* the same comparison on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), collapsed to a baseline-relative shadow. So the "second index" of Grievance 4 is not a second kind of fact about the contract; it is more of the first fact, weakened. Asked as the skill asks it — is this running backwards from the result? — the answer is that adequacy is computed *from the target's answer profile at unassessed pairs*. It uses the explanandum's own values to police the question's scope. That is legitimate for "what can we tell from what we see" and not for "what produces it", which is the document's own distinction (Part VII's direction section). The document presents it as a condition on an account; it is a reading.
**F11. It is gameable through a declared parameter.** The baseline is a component of the question tuple — \(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\) — and clause 3 is stated relative to it. Choose \(b_0\) so that \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) happens to equal \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a^{*},b^{*})\) at the embarrassing pair (easy whenever the query's codomain is coarse, or when the target's answer repeats) and adequacy is defeated by a **free choice**, made by the same modeller whose candidate is being tested. Change **C8**.

### T6 — Hunt the answer in the starting points
**F12. "Non-protective" is not a second index; it is (A)-on-a-larger-set, weakened.** The name suggests a property of the contract. The formula is a relation between \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) and a baseline. Any contract is "protective" or not *relative to a candidate and a baseline*, i.e. it is a property of the pair \((C,\mathcal E)\), not of \(C\). Grievance 4 itself elides this: *"a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's."* Consequence: two accounts of the same question can differ in "objectivity". That is an odd thing for a claim presented under the heading of objectivity. And the direction of the ranking is wrong — see T8.

### T7 — Add a job
**F13. What else has to be so, if this conjunct is right?** Four things, none of them in the document:

1. The question tuple \(p\) must carry the admission relation, or the module in force must be part of the index. Part III's tuple does not list it; Part III's prose says only that for a modelled organization it is "stated with the question"; Part XIV lists it under *declared parameters*. So \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\) — the two-place predicate Part V declares (*"The predicate is two-place on the organization and the question"*) — is not a function of its two arguments.
2. A convention for undefined \(\tau\) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) (F1–F3).
3. A baseline for the candidate. Clause 4 says "does not [differ from its baseline value]", and no baseline for \(E\) is defined anywhere in the document. The natural reading, \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(1,\sigma(b_0))\), is pinned *in value* only by a sibling conjunct — (A) at \((1,b_0)\in C\), plus \(\tau(1)=1\) from (F2). So the condition cannot be evaluated one-part-at-a-time, as Part V's own preamble claims: *"holds exactly when the following five conditions hold, each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\)."* And that preamble is wrong for adequacy on its face: adequacy is a condition on the changes **not** in \(C\). (Non-vacuity, likewise.)
4. \(\mathcal Q\) must be defined off \(C\). Part III guarantees only *"\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)"* — as part of coherent posedness. Adequacy asks \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) at pairs outside \(C\). Change **C6-adjacent**; and for identification queries, whose answer is a fibre, the fibre can be empty off the contract ("The feature is identified at \(y\) exactly when \(Z_y\neq\varnothing\)"), so "the answer differs from baseline" may have nothing to be true of.

**Does the new job rule out any rival version?** It rules out nothing about candidates. It only repairs the revision's own bookkeeping. By the document's own standard (Part VI: *"More reach constrains variation"*), a job that kills no rival added nothing.
**F14. And the added conjunct did not update the document's own summaries.** Part 0's description of the first grade — *"An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated."* — has **no adequacy clause**. Part I's — *"Account at a contract is what a question can assess."* — has none. Part V's display lists **six** conjuncts where its own prose counts "the following **five** conditions" (F1 and F2 share one heading). So the revision is carried by one paragraph and the display, and contradicted or silently omitted by three others. That is the signature of a patch, not a part (T9).

### T8 — Build the best rival
**F15. Best rival: two candidates for one question, and the conjunct prefers the worse one.**

- Candidate \(A\): faithful on \(C\), inert at an admitted out-of-scope pair where the target is not (no port for that edit, or \(\tau\) partial there). This is the honest shape of a scoped claim. It **fails** adequacy (Convention A) or is immunised (Convention B, but then so is the reversed calculation).
- Candidate \(B\): agree with the target on \(C\); off \(C\), move whenever the target moves and not whenever the target does not — e.g. apply to the target's own answer profile a map fixing the baseline value. Then at every admitted pair outside \(C\), clause 3 and clause 5 are both satisfied, adequacy passes, and \(B\) is *arbitrarily wrong in value* off \(C\) while passing the condition that exists to catch exactly that: *"a change that would tell it apart from the target"* (Part V).

So the condition is satisfied by \(B\) and can fail \(A\). **What change tells the revision and the rival (non-protectiveness as a free-standing second claim) apart?** Only the Part VII example — and that example is the one the revision damages (F2). At this level, for every other case I can build, the revision and the rival are the *same claim at this level*: the revision has moved a second-index condition inside the first-index predicate without changing which cases it catches. Either say so, or ask the finer question — whether any admitted pair outside \(C\) is one at which the candidate and target **disagree**, not merely one at which only the target moves.

### T9 — Check the patches
**F16. The conjunct's history is visible and it was not carried through.** The document keeps its past objections in "Grievances, anticipated"; Grievance 4 is the one this conjunct absorbs ("there is no objectivity"). Part XV then *names the failure of the patch as an open defeat*: *"**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target."* So the author knows the rescue is partial. The patch also silently enlarges the first grade (F5) and is not reflected in Part 0, Part I, or the conjunct count (F14). The skill's warning covers this: a part added to absorb a failure, with the claim quietly widened, makes the explanation easier to vary. It does have two cited side-jobs (Derivation 7's catcher; the narrowing clause in Part XI) — so it is not *only* an epicycle — but one of those jobs is not discharged, see T10/F17.

### T10 — Look inside
**F17. The condition inspects outputs, and the document renounces output conditions in Part 0.** *"It does not say prediction is explanation. Fidelity in this semantics is over **component structure under change**, not over outputs."* Adequacy is a condition on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) and \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) — answer profiles — at pairs outside the contract. Whether the candidate has the right internal parts has nothing to do with it: the hybrid \(B\) of F15 has parts constructed to imitate a support pattern. So a candidate can pass by machinery that has nothing to do with the target, and a candidate with the right machinery can fail. That is the "same outputs, different insides" trap, inverted — the same *support pattern*, no relation between the workings.
**F18. Knock-on: the condition now sits inside the machinery of criticism and of support.** Part IX defines *"\(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)\)"* — so a criticism's bearing on its target now requires the critic's organization to pass contract adequacy on the defect-question's contract, a condition about the target's answer profile at pairs the defect-question does not admit. A well-taken criticism can fail Bearing for a scope-related reason unrelated to the alleged defect. Part VI defines \(\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}\), `CriticalBlock`, `Boundary`, and *Pres*, and its **finite monotone theorem** presupposes *"\(\mathsf S\) is upward closed"*. The restriction operation deletes components and *"each then imposes the full relation on its ports"* — it changes \(E\)'s off-\(C\) behaviour, hence adequacy, hence membership in \(\mathsf S\). Upward closure was already an assumption; adequacy makes it markedly harder to satisfy, and the illustrative examples in Part VI (redundant routes, interference) are unaffected only by fiat. Derivation 2's account-equivalence is defined on \(C\) alone, so two account-equivalent candidates can differ in adequacy.

## 5. Direct answers to the four questions

**Is it well-defined? No — on three counts, one of them fatal.** (i) It is evaluated where the document's own example says \(\tau\) is undefined, and while Part IV types \(\tau\) as total (F1, F3). (ii) The candidate has no baseline in the document; "its baseline value" is pinned only by another conjunct, (A) at \((1,b_0)\) (F13). (iii) The pair it quantifies over requires \(\mathcal Q\) to be defined off \(C\), which coherent posedness does not provide (F13). **Which baseline value:** the target's is \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\), which is determinate because Part III puts the baseline pair in \(C\) and coherent posedness makes \(\mathcal Q\) defined there; the candidate's is nothing in the document, and by (A) at the baseline pair it *equals* the target's — so the ambiguity is invisible in value and only disappears by leaning on a sibling conjunct. And since \(b_0\) is a declared parameter of the question, its choice can switch the verdict (F11). **What if the candidate's translation is undefined:** either the condition fires and Part VII's flagship example dies (Convention A), or it does not and any candidate can immunise itself with a partial \(\tau\) (Convention B). The document uses the first convention for (F2) and needs the second for adequacy (F2, F3).

**Is it too strong? Not in the way you ask, and yes in a way you did not.** Not every account on a restricted contract fails when the target has out-of-scope dependence: the candidate escapes whenever its own answer also leaves its baseline there, whatever value it lands on (F8). But it *is* too strong in that it imports a larger-contract condition into the grade whose characterization is "what can be assessed within a question" and whose second index Grievance 4 locates *outside* fidelity on \(C\) (F5, F6); and in Convention A it kills every scoped account whose candidate has no port for an excluded admitted edit (F2), which is the normal shape of a stated, reason-recorded scope.

**Too weak? Yes, five ways, and the first is a defect in the formulation rather than in anyone's scope.** C4 — both answers move off baseline to different values — passes (F8, F9). A partial \(\tau\) off \(C\) passes (F2). Moving the separating pair into \(K\) passes, since \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\) and adequacy "ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)" while a \(K\)-pair *"enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning"* (Part III) — and Part VII concedes that meaning is stipulated (F19). Narrowing a **declared** admission relation passes, because \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is then a declared parameter (F20). And choosing \(b_0\) passes (F11). So yes, to your third question: a scope (or a declaration, or a baseline) can exclude exactly the separating change and still pass — and Part XV says as much about the first of these.

**Collisions.** **(a) With \(K\):** none in the formula — \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S\), since \(K\) is disjoint from \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) — but that is itself the collision: the two escape hatches from a protective contract (state a narrower \(S\); declare the embarrassing change not admitted at all and file it in \(K\)) are policed by two conditions that do not talk to each other, and adequacy can see only the first (F19). **(b) With Derivation 7's frozen assessment:** direct. Part III: *"An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another."* \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), so \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) can grow while \(S\), \(K\), and hence \(C\), are untouched — and adequacy's quantifier grows with it. So **Account's truth value can flip while the frozen index \((C,\mathcal Q)\) is unmoved** (change C10). Part VIII's Historical index makes it worse: *"A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change."* With adequacy inside (E), a later revision of the physical module can change the truth value of "\(E\) is an account on \(C\)" at its own index. And Derivation 7's consequence rests the whole goalpost-catching job on this conjunct — *"the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded"* — which inherits every gap above. **(c) With Grievance 4's two indices:** the two-index treatment is flattened. Grievance 4 states the second conjunct "against the **physically** admitted changes"; Part V quantifies over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which Part III permits to be a *declared* relation, Part XIV lists as a *declared parameter*, and the document's own skew-symmetric example uses as a declaration. For declared questions, then, the second index is a stipulation — precisely what *"not a hope about future criticism"* denies (F20). And folding it into (E) means the first index is no longer a "fact within C" (F5), and objectivity is no longer assessable without a candidate (F12).

**F19 (the \(K\)-escape, stated as a case).** Take a physical question whose target responds to an admitted edit \(a^{*}\), and a candidate that does not. Instead of excluding \(a^{*}\) from \(S\) with a reason — which adequacy can catch when the candidate is inert in the baseline sense — the modeller declares \(a^{*}\) not admitted and places it in \(K\) with a meaning. Part III removes it from the objectivity quantifier by construction; non-vacuity is satisfied "through its stated meaning"; and Part VII's own treatment of eliminative explanation says the meaning is "stipulated". Change C6. Nothing in the semantics prices this move.
**F20 (the declared-relation escape).** Grievance 7 gives the licence — *"a mathematical question must **declare** its admission relation ... and every claim about it is relative to that declaration"* — and Part VII's example uses it: *"the admitted edits are alterations of the presentation — remove the skewness constraint; remove the oddness constraint; remove both"*, followed by *"Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation."* If those three are the whole declared relation and all three are in \(C\), then \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=\varnothing\) and adequacy is **vacuous** for that example. So where the document says answers are hardest to vary (blocks, invariants: *"For allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) ... no allowed path joins states of different invariant value"*) the target's answer is constant by construction, clause 3 can never be satisfied, and adequacy degenerates into clause 5 alone — "the candidate must not respond where the target does not", which is a condition on the candidate's spare machinery, not a condition on the scope. Neither the document's objectivity answer nor its hardest-to-vary claim is being served there.

**F21 (minor, but on the table).** Part V's preamble says the conjuncts are "each a condition on supplied relations **under the changes in \(C\)**". Adequacy and non-vacuity are both conditions on the changes **not** in \(C\). The revision changed what (E) is and the framing sentence was not updated.

## 7. Where each part came from

- **The concept "protective" — asserted, then fitted.** It appears first as an answer to an anticipated objection (Grievance 4) and is then given a formula. Its history is a list of objections, not a history of cases the condition was tested against; that is the fitted kind, and the fitted kind is held in place only on the cases it met. The document itself declares the case it has not met (Part XV's protective-contract defeat).
- **The baseline-asymmetry wording — asserted.** No test or worked example forces it over the direct-disagreement wording, and the only case that would distinguish them is the one it damages.
- **The undefinedness convention — unknown, and doubly so.** The document needs one convention for (F2) in Part VII and the other for adequacy in the same paragraph.
- **The placement inside (E) — built, but on the wrong target.** It was worked out with the objectivity problem in view; its exposure is that Part 0's first-grade characterization, Part I's, Part III's index rule, and Part VIII's historical index can each catch it out. It has not been criticised against those, only against Grievance 4.

## 8. What would make it harder to vary

1. **State the condition as disagreement, and pay the price for it.** *"No admitted pair outside \(C\) at which the candidate's answer and the target's answer differ, where an undefined \(\tau\) or an undefined \(\mathcal Q\) at an admitted pair counts as a difference."* This fixes C4, the \(\mathcal Q\)-domain problem, and the fork at once. The price: Part VII must stop calling the reversed calculation "an account at the identification contract". Decide which of the two sentences you want.
2. **Put the admission relation into the frozen index.** Write the assessment as an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), and say so in Part III, in Derivation 7, and in Grievance 10. Then change C10 moves the index and is recorded, Part VIII's historical index stops being contradicted, and Grievance 7's licence to declare an admission relation becomes explicit rather than silent.
3. **Give the candidate a baseline and require \(\sigma(b_0)\).** One sentence in the transport definition, and F13 disappears.
4. **Either take it out of (E) or correct (E)'s neighbourhood.** Grievance 4 already states the two-index form correctly; leaving non-protectiveness *there*, as a condition on \(C\) assessed alongside Account, restores Part 0's "assessed within a question", Part I's summary, and Part III's index rule. If it stays in (E), then Part 0, Part I, the count of conjuncts, the preamble of Part V, Part IX's Bearing, and Part VI's upward-closure assumption all have to be rewritten.

Of these, (1) and (2) are the ones that would tighten it most, because they are the two on which the three of your four questions turn.

## 9. What this does not show

Passing this test would not show the condition true, and failing it does not show objectivity is impossible. Nothing here shows that no formulation of non-protectiveness could work — item 8.1 is a formulation that would. Nothing here is a claim about whether the physical module should be primitive, or whether a declared admission relation is a reasonable device for mathematics. And I have not touched the load-bearing attacks the document lists for itself in Part XV; several of my findings sit inside one of them (the "protective contract the adequacy condition admits"), and I have said where they do (F8, F11, F19, F20) rather than claiming them as new. What is new is F1–F5, F12–F16 and F18: the fork, the baseline, the \(\mathcal Q\)-domain, the index collision, the fault in the placement, and the knock-on into Bearing and support.

# Referee report: attack (A) against the anchored grade of *Claude Fable Semantics*

Read from the page (document `20_semantics_revised`), Parts 0–XVI. I ran the story-critique passes in order. Where a test does not transfer to a formal semantics I say so in one line and move on. Every note is numbered, and every claim quotes the page.

---

## 1. Corrections first

**N1. The target is the document's own, not the user's.** "Attack (A)" is not an outside hypothesis. It is printed in the document's own "Where to attack this", in almost the words the user used:
> "**(A) Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing."

So the writer has already conceded the target. My job is not to find the vulnerability — it is to check whether the document's *defense* holds. That reframes everything below: I am testing four attempts the document half-anticipates, not four attempts I invented.

**N2. The "six conjuncts" are the user's count; the page counts five headings.** The page says "the following **five** conditions hold", then lists Component fidelity (which carries (F1) *and* (F2)), Question fidelity (A), Non-circular dependence, Non-vacuity, Contract adequacy — and then writes the display with six conjuncts: `(F1)∧(F2)∧(A)∧NonCircular∧NonVacuous∧Adequate`. Not a fault; just so the user knows "six conjuncts" is the (E) display and "five conditions" is the prose.

**N3. The document asserts twice that the one-component repackaging "satisfies (E)", and its own definition of Non-circular dependence contradicts that.** The page claims:
> "The one-component candidate that repackages Sol_D satisfies (E). It is an account at C and not anchored."

and again
> "The one-component candidate whose single relation is Sol_D satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3".

But Non-circular dependence is witnessed only by "an edit … that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block `B⊊Γ`", with the explicit rider "Removing all of Γ changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing." A candidate with one component has active set Γ of one member. The only sub-blocks are ∅ and Γ; neither is a nonempty *proper* sub-block. So by the letter, the repackaging has **no** witness and fails Non-circular — meaning it fails (E), not merely anchoring. This matters: the document leans on this candidate as its clean example of "passes (E), fails anchoring." Which it fails, and whether Γ is really a singleton, needs the writer's ruling. It does not rescue the repackaging (conditions 1 and 3 still kill it), but the bookkeeping is inconsistent.

**N4 (medium, one line).** The medium is formal prose mathematics, not film: there are no bodies, meals or faces, so the "cost felt in a body" test does not transfer; the analogous question — whether each condition is *evaluable from the supplied data* — does transfer and I run it at N31. There is also no luck in a semantics, so the "lucky meetings" test does not transfer.

## 2. Pass 1 — scene by scene

### Sequence I — the six conjuncts of (E). The remove test on each.

**N5.** Remove **(F1)** and the page names the job: "prevent an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition." Remove **(F2)**: "prevent a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint." Both are **held** — each blocks a distinct failure (local vs. global). Keep.

**N6.** Remove **(A)**: the account is no longer of *this* question — "an account of a different query is not an account of this one." Held.

**N7.** Remove **Non-circular**: the page names the exact casualty — "`p because p` fails non-circular dependence." Held. (See N3 for the crack in this conjunct's edge.)

**N8.** Remove **Non-vacuity / Contract adequacy**: the page names the casualty — "An account whose contract excludes an admitted change that would tell it apart from the target fails adequacy." Held.

**N9. A detail the attack's spec adds that (E) does not enforce.** The page states "(E) carries **no provenance condition**." The user's attack requires a *non-declared* transport (Sel or Con). So the attack demands **strictly more** than (E), and it also demands more than the anchored grade, which likewise adds only "four conditions on the anchor map." Worth flagging: the attack is testing a grade the document never claimed, or it is pre-empting the "but the transport is only declared" escape. Either way, a candidate that is anchored and faithful can be given a **constructed** provenance (the analyst builds it and binds it), so this extra clause bars nothing.

### Sequence II — the four anchored conditions. Each is a scene with a named job.

**N10. Condition 1 (Properness) is held by two jobs.** What is there:
> "Every anchor λ(k) is a proper subnetwork of D, and the anchors of the active components refine a cover of D's components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of D that some pair in C_adm touches lies outside every anchor."

Job one: *no anchor is the whole target* — this blocks the repackaging. Job two: *coverage* — every touched component must sit inside some anchor. Remove the condition and the repackaging gets in (the document says so at N3). Held, with a second job. Protect it.

**N11. Condition 3 (No global relation) is held, and it is *not* a second route to condition 1's job.** What is there:
> "No active component's relation is, on C, definable as Sol_D(a,b) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name."

Remove it: condition 1 is still satisfied if the anchor is a *proper* subnetwork whose deleted components happen to be redundant, because then the subnetwork's projection equals a projection of `Sol_D`. Condition 3 is the only thing that catches that case. Remove condition 1 instead: an anchor equal to `Sol_D` with a whole-target anchor is caught by both — but condition 1's coverage job is not done by condition 3. So the two share only part of a job; each has a second job that the other cannot do. **They are not one character; keep both.**

**N12. Condition 4 (World-kind) is held by two jobs, and the swap test proves it.** What is there:
> "Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits. Where the physical module supplies a history for D, each anchor is an active route (Part IX) … and not a backup that would have responded identically."

Swap its court from `C_adm` back to `C` — the near neighbour a fair writer might have chosen — and the document says the swap breaks the job: "on C alone the two are one forward kind." So *this* court is held by the decoy-blocking job. Remove condition 4 and "a decoy with the same signature on C but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4" is exactly what stops failing. Held. Protect it.

**N13. Condition 2 (Resolution) is held, but it is not doing the anti-decoy job.** What is there:
> "Whenever two components of D can be separately varied by pairs in C_adm with distinct effects on Ans_p, they lie in distinct anchors."

Its job is *resolution* — keep E at least as fine as the question's separating power. Removing it lets a coarsened but otherwise faithful E in; that E is a coarser account, not a non-explanation. So condition 2 carries none of the attack-(A) burden. The attack's weight rests on conditions 1, 3, 4 alone. (This matters for N22.)

**N14. The strongest move in the whole Part, and it survives the flip test.** The document considers the obvious rival and rejects it:
> "A condition of the form 'each component of E must be tied to a component of D *of the same kind*' would be evaluable — λ is transport data and Inst says what D's components are — but it would not be evaluable *by the changes in C* where C contains no separating change."

Two real options are on the page (tie-to-same-kind vs. tie-to-C_adm), they pull almost equally, and the choice is settled by the job "catch a decoy that shares the chosen contract." Flip it and decoys get in. This is a **genuine choice, held by a named job**. Tell the writer to protect it.

### Sequence III — the four named attempts. Blocking condition for each.

**N15. One-component repackaging.** Blocked by **condition 1** (anchor is the whole target) **and condition 3** (its relation is `Sol_D`). The page states both blocks itself. Verdict: blocked, on two independent routes. (See N3 — its "(E)-passing" status is separately doubtful.)

**N16. Two-component split — missing from the document's own enumeration.** The list of classic attempts is:
> "lookup tables, reversed calculations, conclusion-as-premise, and the one-component candidate whose single relation is the target's own solution set."

The split is not there. I ran it, concretely. Take the cleanest case: `D` has components `j1` (relation `R1` on ports `{u,v}`) and `j2` (relation `R2` on `{v,w}`), sharing port `v`. Build `E` with `k1` anchored to `{j1}`, `L_{k1}=R1`, and `k2` anchored to `{j2}`, `L_{k2}=R2`. Check: (F1) → `proj_{uv}[Sol_{j1}]=R1=L_{k1}` ✓; (F2) → the fibre product `Sol_D` equals `Sol_E` ✓; Non-circular witness → removing `k1` (a proper sub-block of `Γ={k1,k2}`) changes the profile ✓; condition 1 → proper, covering ✓; condition 2 → separable components in distinct anchors ✓; condition 3 → each part equals *its own* anchor's relation, not `Sol_D` or a projection of it ✓; condition 4 → if the presentation is edit-independent, equal on all `C_adm` ✓. **This candidate satisfies (E) and all four anchored conditions.** It is not a counterexample. It is `D`'s decomposition, faithfully mirrored — a genuine mechanism, not "explaining nothing." That is the whole result: the *only* way a split preserves the repackaging's content is for each part's relation to be a function of the **whole** solution set, i.e. a projection of `Sol_D`, which condition 3 forbids by name. The other branch — parts that are their own proper anchors' relations — is no longer a repackaging; it is a decomposition, and if it is faithful on `C_adm` it is a mechanism. **Blocking reason: condition 3 (repackaging branch), or "it becomes genuine" (decomposition branch).** There is no third branch: a deleted component "imposes the full relation on its ports," so a genuine-anchor split that is *not* faithful overshoots `Sol_D` and fails **(F2)**. The document should add this attempt to its list; it is the most obvious escape from a one-component example.

**N17. Lookup with a proper decomposition — the one attempt the document does not block.** Concretely: a component tabulating one answer per `(a,b)` cannot satisfy (F1), because (F1) is an equality `proj_{V_k}[Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b)]=L_k(τ(a),σ(b))` demanded *simultaneously* for every `(a,b)∈C`, and a component's relation is on **ports**, indexed by edits — it is a family, not a cell. So a per-edit table fails (F1)/(F2) as the document says: "A table of observed answers has no component whose relation is replaced by an intervention; it fails (F1)." But the document then concedes the rest in its own words:
> "A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account."

**"by nothing."** The document does not block the lookup-with-a-faithful-decomposition. It **reclassifies** it as "an account," which is the very word attack (A) denies. And "lookup" versus "mechanism" is a claim about *notation* — enumeration vs. formula — and the semantics is deliberately notation-blind: it is about relations and how they move under change. So this attempt's verdict is not "blocked"; it is **absorbed by definition**. This is the single strongest thing I can hand the user (see N23).

**N18. Decoy with the same world-kind — not an (A)-attack at all; it is an (C)-attack.** Run it in both readings.
- If the decoy shares world-kind on all of `C_adm`, then by the definition of kind it is not a decoy: "Two components j, j′ are **of one forward kind on (ℓ,C)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide…" Same world-kind = same kind. Calling it a decoy presupposes a distinction the semantics does not represent, and the document says so on purpose: the question "needs either a finer contract or a history, and if neither is available, that the distinction is not represented."
- If it differs on some admitted edit, condition 4 blocks it, by the document's own sentence.
- The "same kind, different **actual route**" version is blocked by condition 4's active-route clause — but only "Where the physical module supplies a history for D." With no history supplied, the distinction falls into the acknowledged gap.

So this attempt collapses into attack **(C)**, the analysis-of-kinds conjecture, not (A). Say that plainly to the writer: a decoy that is distinguishable only by history is not an (A) counterexample; it is the (C) attack wearing (A)'s coat.

## 3. Pass 2 — sequences and the chain of questions

**N19. The chain holds.** Part V opens on the six conjuncts (the widest question: "what can be assessed within a question?") and closes narrow on the anchored grade: "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose, and it is what 'the model is about the actual mechanism' means here." The opening and the closing click: the closing answers *why* a second grade exists. Good.

**N20. The chain breaks exactly at attack (A).** The question Part V leaves the reader holding is "does 'about the actual mechanism' actually separate explanation from non-explanation?" Attack (A)'s question — "a new one must fail none" — is the same question, and the document answers it with a promise, not a scene. **Planted and never collected**: the document plants the requirement of a candidate that fails nothing and never produces one; the closest it comes is to *redefine* such a candidate as an account (N17). That is a broken promise in the strict sense — not a hole, since it is flagged as open, but the reader is holding a question the document will not discharge.

**N21. Cause versus label — the decisive finding.** For the turn "the lookup-with-decomposition is excluded from the anchored grade," name the shown cause and remove it in the head. The shown cause of exclusion is *condition 3* ("the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name"). Remove that cause: does the turn still happen? No — the lookup, if it is a faithful decomposition, is admitted and called an account. So the exclusion of a *decomposition* lookup is not produced by any fidelity or properness condition; it is produced by the *wording* of "account." The cause is a label. This is the same move the skill's worked example flags, pointed at a theory instead of a script: the document's sufficiency claim for the anchored grade is **definitional**, not proved. It may be defensible — a decomposition faithful on every admitted change *is* plausibly a mechanism — but it is not what attack (A) asks for, and the document should say so in one sentence rather than let the reader discover it at N17.

## 4. Pass 3 — people and their choices. Do the four conditions exclude a genuine mechanism?

**N22. Yes, in at least two cases, plus one narrow third.**
- **Single-component targets.** Condition 1 requires *every* anchor to be a proper subnetwork and requires the anchors to cover every touched component. If `D` has exactly one component `j`, its only subnetworks are ∅ and `{j}`; ∅ covers nothing, so `j` "lies outside every anchor," and `{j}` is the whole target. **No anchored account exists for a one-component target.** This is a real exclusion of a genuine mechanism — a mechanism whose explanatory content is a single irreducible relation (say a conservation law presented as one constraint) — and the document nowhere acknowledges it. Ask the writer whether such targets arise in the intended applications; if they do, the anchored grade has a hole on the side of over-exclusion, the mirror image of attack (A).
- **Symmetry and whole-relation explanations.** The document already concedes one: "explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among the components of E that neither (F1) nor (F2) constrains, with no construction here." Condition 3's exclusion of a component "definable as `Sol_D` or a projection of it" is the same exclusion stated as a condition. So (A)'s conditions and (B)'s open case (ii) are the same wound seen from two sides.
- **A genuine redundant route whose relation is the whole's shadow.** The document admits redundant routes as legitimate — "each is contributory, neither indispensable" — yet condition 3 is stated per active component: if a contributor's relation happens to equal a projection of `Sol_D`, the *whole* anchored account fails. Narrow, but it is an over-exclusion on the document's own Part VI terms.

**N23. Does the flipped choice matter?** Flip the design choice at N14 (court = `C_adm`), and the decoy is admitted — so the choice is not idle; it is settled by a job. Flip condition 3 (allow projections of `Sol_D` as components), and the one-component repackaging walks straight in — so condition 3 is not idle either. The choices that carry the anti-decoy weight are held. The choice the document *does not* make — "state whether a faithful decomposition can fail to explain" — is the one the attack turns on, and it is left unmade.

## 5. Pass 4 — theme as a pattern

**N24. The central question, put plainly:** *what distinguishes an account of the actual mechanism from a recipe that merely reproduces its answer?* The document's own answer, and its own hedge, side by side:
> "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose" … "What remains after it — a label that discriminates where no admitted change and no history does — is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV, which this document does not claim to have proved."

**N25. Pattern count — who answers the question, and how.** Five answers, each by a different condition: (F1)/(F2) answer by *signature*; condition 1/3 answer by *properness and whole-relation*; condition 4 answers by *all admitted changes plus an active route*; Non-circular answers by *independent boundary*; Adequacy answers by *non-protective scope*. The answers are genuinely different — this is a good pattern count, not one answer five times.

**N26. Who faces whom.** Condition 1 and condition 3 face each other (N11) and both are needed. Condition 2 never faces the attack — it answers a *different* question (resolution). The one-component repackaging faces the anchored grade and loses; its two-component sibling never enters the ring. That is the loose thread: the document sets a known loser against itself and leaves the live challenger outside.

**N27. Fair mirror test.** The document treats the one-component repackaging as the representative of "passes (E), fails anchoring." Lay it beside the two-component split and ask whether the split really did what the one did: the one carries the whole solution set in one component; the split, to carry the same content, must have a part that is a function of the whole solution set — the same act, distributed — and condition 3 catches acts, not slates. So the mirror is fair *in effect* (the split is caught), but the document never held the two acts side by side, which is why N16 is a missing scene rather than a wrong claim.

Nearly everywhere else the anti-decoy work is done by conditions stated as plumbing (1, 3) whose thematic content — "the explanandum repackaged as a law" — is only in the gloss.

The rules of the world (which edits are admitted, supplied by the module `Θ`) are what make condition 4 evaluable, and the document says the price out loud — "how a lump of matter decomposes is the module's burden." So condition 4 is not merely placed beside the theme; it is the theme carried by the primitive.

**N30. Vanishing test.** "Active route" appears in condition 4 and again in Part IX's histories and Part XI's `ProducedBy`. Present in the first and last thirds. Does not vanish. Good.

**N31. That is not a fault; it is the honest locus of the load, and the memory of the load should be kept — a reader who thinks condition 4 "catches the decoy" is really trusting `Θ`.

## 7. The fault that matters most (ranked)

**First — the sufficiency claim of the anchored grade is definitional, not demonstrable, and the document hides this behind one sentence.** N17 quotes the sentence that gives it away ("… by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account"). The candidate attack (A) asks for, the document *admits* and renames "an account," rather than proving it cannot be a non-explanation. The fix is a single sentence either way: state that "responds faithfully to every admitted change via a proper decomposition" *is* the document's definition of "about the mechanism," so attack (A) is a challenge to the definition rather than to the conditions. Without that sentence, a careful reader will believe the grade has a theorem behind it that it does not.

**Second — the four anchored conditions exclude at least two genuine things.** A target with one component has no admissible anchor (N22). And condition 3's exclusion of a component equal to `Sol_D` or a projection of it is the same exclusion the document separately concedes under (B)(ii) symmetry — so the over-exclusion is real and the document already half-knows it. It should list the single-component case beside the symmetry case.

**Third — the two-component split is not in the document's own list of attempts.** N16. It is the first thing a hostile reader will try, it passes (E) and all four conditions when built faithfully, and only fails when it is still a repackaging (condition 3) — which is precisely the point the document should be making about its own conditions, and does not.

## 8. What I am unsure of

**N33.** Whether the one-component repackaging really satisfies (E) — the Non-circular tension (N3) is not resolvable from the page; I need the writer to say what Γ contains.

**N34.** What "plainly explains nothing" *is*, as a test. The semantics has no predicate for it. So the verdict on attack (A) is not decidable from the page in either direction: the document can neither produce the candidate nor prove it cannot exist, and a referee cannot rule, because the criterion the attack appeals to ("plainly") is not one of the document's conditions. This is the deepest uncertainty and it should be stated in the write-up, not papered over.

**N35.** Whether single-component targets arise in the intended applications. This is a question for the module `Θ`, which the document names as its primitive, and which I cannot see.

**N36.** Deliberate open question or hole, for the twin exclusions of N22: the symmetry exclusion is declared open and is therefore a *working* open question (nothing on the page settles it, and each side is held — condition 3 for the exclusion, Part XV (B)(ii) for the concession); the single-component exclusion is neither declared nor held, and is a **hole**. Do not "fix" the symmetry concession; do fix the silence about single-component targets.

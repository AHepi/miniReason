## 4. The tests, and what they found

### A. The merit condition

**F1. It is text, not a clause, and it demands no witness.** (G) is stated as "\(\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\)" — three conjuncts, none of them the merit condition. Every other conjunct demands a witness, down to "A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization". The merit condition demands none, and there is no place in any formula where it can fail. *Origin: asserted.*

**F2. It is ill-typed against (P).** (P) is "\(\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O)\)" — indexed to an actual episode Δ and an actual state pair. "a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P)" names neither Δ nor the end state at which the repair is claimed. *Swap* the actual Δ for the hypothetical episode in which the account of p′ is deployed: the document gives no rule for translating τ, σ, or ProducedBy to it. *Unknown*: what would settle it is a stated witness — which episode, which end state.

**F3. It runs backwards.** The condition is applied at the moment of *finding* (before \(e\)), but "deployable" is Deploy, defined at Part X as holding when \(s\) "holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance — integrated into problem-directed activity". That object comes into existence when the question is answered, after e. *Reverse*: step on the cause (find the question) — does the merit move? No rule is given. Step on the effect (deploy the account) — the merit was already fixed. This is the shape Part VII warns about: "The reversed calculation … is an account at that contract, and not anchored as a production account". A finding is a production act; the merit condition as written is an identification-style condition. *Loose.*

**F4. Trivial, on one reading.** (P)'s first conjunct needs one obligation to flip; O is declared — "'Claimed' and 'protected' are declared". A modeller can declare an O containing any obligation that fails before \(e\) and holds after. *Swap* p′ for a random new question: nothing in the condition tells them apart. The defence, "it is stated so that a random new contract does not count", is held by no part of the condition. *Loose.*

**F5. Too strong, on the other.** If O is fixed for the comparison, \(o\) must already be in O before the finding. A finding that identifies an obligation nobody had declared is excluded. So the condition swings: O extensible → trivial; O fixed → too strong. The document does not say which it is. *Loose.*

**F6. It is stated twice, differently.** Part III: "a re-parameterisation of an old question that repairs no obligation is a constructed question and not a found one" — a universal negative. Part X: "there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P)" — an existential positive. The two are not equivalent and are not reconciled. *Loose.*

**F7. The document already had a tighter condition and did not use it.** (EK) requires "\(c\in\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\land\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\land\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\)", where "\(\operatorname{ProducesVia}\) holds when, in addition, the change of \(o\) lies on an active route that passes through a deployment of \(c\)". Build the best rival: a merit condition requiring the question's account to be deployed *on the active route* to the repair. What tells the two apart is a question whose account is deployed off-route; the rival rejects it, the merit condition admits it. This was within reach and unused.

### B. Derivation 5

**F8. The "exactly" claim is not established, or is idle.** Part III's coherence needs three things: "\(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated." The construction's components cover the baseline reading and S/K consistency, not Q-definedness. *Remove* Q-definedness and run the closure conditions: a question with an undefined Q somewhere is still a solution, so the "exactly" is false as stated. The charitable reading — that "in the form of Part III" imports the whole coherence condition — makes the joint worse: the components then carry no content of their own, and the derivation becomes a restatement. Either way this joint is untestable rather than established.

**F9. "Add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning" is two things, and only one is an edit.** (i) Setting \(u_K\) is admitted by its domain. But Part II says "An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port" — and \(u_K\) is assigned by the S/K-consistency component, so a direct set removes the consistency condition rather than acting under it. The construction does not say which form is meant. (ii) The meaning is a declaration: K is "a declared set of counterfactual edits — pairs the module does not admit — each with a stated meaning", and "a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning". Nothing in the organisation's interpretation supplies or checks that a meaning is stated. **Answer to your sub-question: no — not as stated.** The second half is a supply of declared data conjoined to an edit. *Loose.*

**F10. "Toggle a pair" is also two things.** For a baseline-factor port the toggle acts under the closure. For a composition port \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) the toggle replaces the closure component, by the same Part II sentence. One edit is listed for both kinds of port. *Loose.*

**F11. A missing job: \(O_p\).** The question is "\(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\)" and O_p is "the set of obligations being addressed or protected". Derivation 5 builds ports for the pairs, for Q, for S, for K — none for \(O_p\). Yet the merit condition, the only merit condition in the semantics, is about O. So the question-organisation cannot be assessed for merit, and the claim "provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization" cannot cover the whole tuple. This is a hole, not a loose bolt.

**F12. \(b_0\) is a boundary, not a port.** "Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed." Consistent with \(b_0\in p\), but it means the questions on a fixed D are a disjoint family across baselines, not a connected space. Unstated.

**F13. Held parts, named.** One port per admitted pair is held by C's shape. "alter \(S\)" as a separate edit is held by the distinction between \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\) ("the objectivity condition of Part V ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)") and K ("enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning"). The baseline closure is held by C's containing the baseline. The composition closure is held by Part II's closure of A.

**F14. Ports versus fixed index.** Part III: "An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another." If S and K are *ports*, changing them is an edit inside the organisation's solution set; by Part III it is a change of index. Both readings are in the document. *Loose.*

**F15. Naming.** Derivation 5 says "A question \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) determines an organization \(D_p\)". But no port or closure condition depends on which question is at hand — the same organisation serves every question on D, and a given question is a solution. The correct claim is "a target determines a question-space organisation, and a question is a solution of it". As written, the assertion that "provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization" is loose, because ρ_p varies per solution. *Loose.*

### C. Is the stated limit a limit?

**F16. The stated reason does not hold.** The reason given is "the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing \(A\times B\)". But the same construction applies to *any* target D. If there is a target D′ carrying the new port, the same construction over D′'s A×B works. The document supplies D′ two ways: (a) Part IV says the module "must be able to return a full organization — ports, domains, components with footprints, boundaries, the admitted edits \(A_\Theta\) restricted to the occurrence", and Inst "is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain"; under that reading the new dimension is already in the module's organisation for the occurrence, and Derivation 5 already has the port. (b) If the pair is not in \(A_\Theta\), Derivation 5's own edit "add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning" covers it — subject to F9. So under either reading the case is covered: **some reading of Inst plus a new grain already covers it.** *Loose as a limit.*

**F17. If (a) fails, the limit is mis-stated.** If the target's A is narrower than \(A_\Theta\), the excluded case is not "the invention of a new dimension" but a mismatch between the target's decomposition and the module's edit set — which is not specific to new dimensions.

**F18. What actually generates the "limit" is borrowing, and it is broader than stated.** Part XIV lists "the organization interpretation \((V,X,J,B,L)\) of every organization not attributed through \(\operatorname{Inst}\)" as a declared parameter. Targets are instantiated or declared, never constructed. The document has no target provenance at all. Name the lender: the physical module (Part XII) plus the declared parameters (Part XIV). Everything said about "not represented" here follows from that, for every target, not just for new dimensions.

**F19. The concession has been narrowed three times, unreconciled.** Part 0: "What it does not represent is the invention of a new dimension of variation, and it says so." Part X: "It is a change to the target organization itself, and this document has no witness for it." Part XV (E): "not representable as question-finding here." Three widths for one concession; none matches F18's actual generator.

### D. The four open cases

**F20. Verdicts.**

*Case (i), impossibility — **closed for the admitted case**, scoped by choice for the rest.* Part VII gives the account and the engine is a derived result: "for allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) with \(zRz'\Rightarrow I(z)=I(z')\), no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1)". The document's own verdict is "offered as adequate for the first case and as scoped for the second". Nothing is missing here except a settled meaning for K, which is a declared parameter.

*Case (ii), symmetry — **loose, not held**.* The stated block is "an entailment among \(E\)'s components … that (F1), a per-component condition, and (F2), an assembly condition, do not constrain". But a symmetry is an edit of the target, an invariance is a signature fact under (K), and a forced independence is an identification fact: "The feature is identified at \(y\) exactly when \(Z_y\neq\varnothing\land|f[Z_y]|=1\)." Both fidelity conditions are quantified over the whole contract: (F1) "For every active component \(k\) of \(E\) … and every \((a,b)\in C\)", (F2) "\(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)". Put the symmetry edits in C and both bite. The document says they do not constrain the entailment, and gives no demonstration. *Swap* the block for "the entailment is a fact about D that the transport must reproduce" and nothing in the document blocks the swap.

*Case (iii), selection artefact — **loose, not held**.* The stated block is "needs two targets (the data organization and the population organization) and a transport for each." But "An organization is \(D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L)\)" admits any footprint, so a coupling component spanning both is available, and Part VIII already carries inter-organisation transport: "\(zRy\land z\xrightarrow{a}z'\Rightarrow\exists y'[y\xrightarrow{\tau(a)}y'\land z'Ry']\). (T1)". The document already treats an artefact-of-apparatus case: the two balances, "This is an account of a limitation." No construction is offered, but no reason is given why one cannot be built.

*Case (iv), mathematics — **already closed by the document's own lights**.* Part VII works it end to end: "The expansion is an account at that contract." The document says why it is listed anyway: "What the example does not do is fall under attack (B)'s quantifier over *physically* admitted contracts, because there is no physical module here; it falls under the declared one."

**F21. The quantifier in (B) is ambiguous, and that alone keeps (iv) open.** Part XV (B): "A genuine explanation whose organization no transport can preserve under any admitted contract." Part 0 (B): "whose organization cannot be captured by *any* transport satisfying component fidelity under *any* admitted contract." Neither says "physically admitted". Only the list under (B) says (iv) falls "not under the physical quantifier of this attack". Fix the quantifier and (iv) closes.

**F22. Add a job, on the four together.** If (ii) were closed over a contract containing the symmetry edits, the ruled-out rival would be "symmetry is an unrepresented pattern". If (iii) were closed by a composite target plus (T1), it would be "the semantics transports only between one target and one candidate". If (iv) were closed by fixing the quantifier, it would be "declared admission is second-class". Three of the four do work only against readings the document supplies elsewhere.

**F23. Where a real necessity counterexample would have to sit.** Every question has one target: "A question is \(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\)." Both Derivation 5 and the whole question machinery are built over one target's A×B. The case worth building is a pattern of explanation whose conclusion is *about a family of organizations* rather than the solution set of any one — not symmetry as such.

## 5. Whole-explanation checks

**Flip.** Had the document closed the four cases and made the merit condition precise, could we say from its own resources what that would look like? Yes: a port for \(O_p\) in Derivation 5; symmetry edits placed in C and (F1)/(F2) quantified over them; the two targets written as one organisation with a coupling component and a (T1) transport; the merit condition recast as ProducesVia. The concessions are choices, not forced.

**Direction.** The merit condition is a production claim ("would repair") run backwards from the obligation to the question, in the very shape Part VII separates from production. (F3.)

**Answer hidden in the starting points.** The merit of a found question is measured partly by O, which is declared. This is the shape the document warns of elsewhere: "moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named 'law' does not discharge this". (F4.)

**Change list.** Derivation 5's change list is its four edits, and it has not said what it leaves out; the three exclusions it states in prose (b_0 fixed, \(O_p\) absent, "add a port to D" excluded) are exactly the joints the tests found. The merit condition has no change list at all. (F8, F11, F12.)

**Rivals.** The best rival to the merit condition is assembled from (EK)'s existing clause — Result, Deploy, ProducesVia. The separating change is a question deployed off the active route to a repair.

**Patches.** The merit condition is presented complete ("This is the only merit condition in the semantics") with no account of what it replaced or absorbed. The limit's patch history is visible as F19.

**Look inside.** The question-organisation's outputs are "the coherently posed questions"; its workings are the closure conditions and the four edits. Matching the outputs does not test the workings; three of the four edits do not analyse as (O)-edits without further stipulation. (F9, F10.)

**Where each part came from** (document's terms: selected / constructed / declared). Merit condition: **declared**. Derivation 5's construction: **constructed**, but its exactness claim: **declared**. The limit: **declared**, and **borrowed** on the physical module and the declared parameters; name it. The four open cases: **declared** as concessions, with three of the four grounds not held by the definitions.

## 6. What would make it harder to vary

1. Put the merit condition into (G) as a clause with a named witness (which episode, which end state), and tie it to the active route as ProducesVia already does. It is the single change that closes F2, F3, F4 and F7.
2. Say whether O is fixed or extensible before the finding, and what happens when the finding identifies an obligation that was not in O. (F5.)
3. Give \(O_p\) a port or component in Derivation 5, or state that the question-organisation is not the whole of the question tuple. (F11, F15.)
4. Say that "add a pair to K with a meaning" is a value change at \(u_K\) *under* the S/K-consistency component, and either locate the meaning in the organisation or mark it as declared data. (F9.)
5. Move the "new dimension" limit to where it comes from — targets are instantiated or declared, never constructed — and either accept the larger concession or close the case with Inst plus an admissible new grain. (F16–F19.)
6. Fix the quantifier in (B), and drop or restate case (iv). (F21.)

## 7. What this does not show

Passing is not truth, and failing is not falsehood. Nothing above says the merit condition's intent is wrong, that the four concessions are mistakes, or that the constitutive conjecture fails. It says which parts are held by a job or a change and which are assertions, and what would settle each. The revision has kept the source theory's vocabulary nearly intact, so the skill's word list cannot be used as an independent test of the revision — every claim above quotes the revision itself.

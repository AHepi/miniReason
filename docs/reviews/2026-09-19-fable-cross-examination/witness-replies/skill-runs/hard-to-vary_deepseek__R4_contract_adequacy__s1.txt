# Hard-to-vary run on `20_semantics_revised`, Part V, “Contract adequacy”

**A note on the tool.** The skill I am running was built from an earlier version of this same theory (its word list says so: the source is “*Claude Fable Semantics — a structural class of explanatory creativity…*”, supplied as `10_Claude_Fable_Semantics_-_standalone_theory.md`). Its plain words — “the change list” = this document’s **contract**, “what is asked” = its **query**, “the kind of question” = its **respect**, “fitted / built / asserted” = its **selected / constructed / declared**, “freeze the question” = its **frozen assessment**, “moving the goalposts” = its “changing the index without recording the change” — are this document’s terms or their ancestors. So I do not use the skill’s own vocabulary as evidence about the revision; I use the document’s own words and quote it. One delta is worth recording because it is checkable: the skill’s word list maps Part V as five checks — component fidelity (F1), global fidelity (F2), question fidelity (A), non-circular dependence, non-vacuity — and says an Account “| Passes all five checks above. |”. It contains **no entry** for “C is not protective”. That tells me non-protectiveness is new in the revision; it does not tell me whether it is right. I use it only as a delta indicator.

---

## 1. The question, frozen

**What is under attack.** The revision’s addition of **Contract adequacy** as a conjunct of `Account` itself. In the document’s words (Part V):

> “**Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse. This is the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change; a stated exclusion is not thereby a legitimate one.”

and, in the same section,

> “\[\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}. \tag{E}\]”

**Range.** The clause itself, plus every place the document leans on it: Part 0 (the two grades); Part III (coherence and the scope \(S\)); Part V (the gloss and the anchored grade); Part VII (the worked examples); Part VIII (the historical index); Part XI (EK); Part XIV (the declared parameters and the dependence order); Part XV (the listed attacks); Grievance 4; Derivation 7.

**What is asked.** Four things, and I take them as given rather than re-litigating them: (i) is the clause well-defined — which baseline, and what happens where the candidate’s translation is undefined; (ii) is it too strong; (iii) is it too weak; (iv) does it collide with \(K\), with Derivation 7’s frozen assessment, and with Grievance 4’s two-index objectivity.

**Two kinds of question, so two sets of tests.** Partly “what counts as what under this rule?” (Part V’s by-domain counterpart: remove a clause, ask which cases change sides), partly “does it achieve its purpose?” (the purpose being to catch a scope drawn to protect a candidate). I am *not* asking whether protective scopes should be caught; that is the document’s aim and I take it as fixed.

**What it does not say, and what it therefore needs from elsewhere.** \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is used in (A) and in this clause but is never defined; only “\[\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b). \tag{Q}\]” is. \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is defined as “\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\) … the set of pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization, the physically admitted edits \(A_\Theta\) (Part XII)” — a set of *pairs* filled from a set of *edits*. The clause’s inputs, then, are: the contract, the module’s admission (or a declared admission relation), a translation \(\tau\) that the document elsewhere shows can be undefined, and two answer profiles — one of which has no definition.

## 3. The jobs, put as contrasts

- **J1.** Why catch a protective scope *inside* `Account`, rather than in a separate objectivity claim outside it? (The contrast is the arrangement Grievance 4 describes.)
- **J2.** Why compare each side with *its own baseline*, rather than compare the two sides with each other? (The contrast is Part V’s own gloss, “would tell it apart from the target”.)
- **J3.** Why range over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), rather than over \(C\) (where fidelity lives) or over all of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)?
- **J4.** Why does it say nothing about \(K\)’s pairs, and nothing about pairs at which a value is undefined? (The contrast is Part VII’s identification and constructive-elimination families.)
- **J5.** Why can a scope with “the reason for each exclusion recorded” still fail? (The contrast is Part III’s stated scope.)
- **J6.** Why should an account claim’s truth value be fixed by its contract? (The contrast is Part VIII.)
- **J7.** Why should assessing an account need data outside the question? (The contrast is Part 0.)

---

## 4. The change list of the clause

**It ranges over:** admitted edit–boundary pairs outside the contract, comparing the target’s answer with *its* baseline and the candidate’s translated answer with *its* baseline.

**It leaves out, and does not say it leaves out:** pairs that are not admitted (everything in \(K\)); pairs at which either value is undefined; pairs at which both sides move; the way the scope came to be drawn; the correctness of a declared admission relation. And, notably, it **never compares the candidate’s answer with the target’s answer anywhere outside \(C\)** — it compares each with its own baseline. That exclusion is the root of the too-weak finding below, and it is silent.

The document is severe about silence elsewhere in the same predicate — “every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently” (non-vacuity, Part V). The adequacy clause’s own exclusions are silent. **[N8]**

## 5. The ten tests

### Remove
Take the clause out of (E). Which job stops? The weak grade loses its only instrument against a protective scope, since non-vacuity only requires exclusions to be *stated*, and the anchored grade’s condition 4 is separate. So the removal costs something. **[N9a]** Remove in a pair: clause *and* non-vacuity together, and the contract need neither be stated nor unprotected — the grade becomes near-vacuous (bounded only by the non-circularity witness, which does need “an edit … translated from some \((a,b)\in C\)”). So the pair is needed.

But the removal has a second effect worth stating: it restores Part 0’s summary, Part III’s naming, and Grievance 4’s “second conjunct”. Anything whose removal repairs three other texts at the cost of one grade deserves the cost stated. **[N1, N12]**

### Swap
Swap P3/P4 for a direct comparison: *no admitted pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which the two answer profiles differ, where both are defined*. This catches everything the present clause catches, and additionally the both-move case (below). Nothing in the document stops the swap. Unless the document intends the weak grade to catch only **missing or extra sensitivity** and to leave **wrong sensitivity** to the anchored grade — an intent nowhere stated. **[N17]**

### Flip
Take a fixed case (contract, target, candidate, module). One reading of “its baseline value” returns *fails*; the other returns *passes*, with no change to the world. Same clause, both outcomes. Under the document’s own test for a rule — “it asks what its signature is” (Part II), and a change separates only when the signatures differ at it — a comparison whose baseline is not pinned separates nothing. **[N3]**

### Poke
- **P2** has both halves: widening \(A_\Theta\) should matter (and does); which glyph names the set should not. Fine.
- **P3** has both halves at the pair level (a moved target answer, an unmoved one). But its *baseline* has no poke: no change to the world tells the \((1,b_0)\) reading from the \((1,b)\) reading — only a re-reading of the text does. **[N3]**
- **P4** fails the poke: a change that *should* matter — the candidate’s \(\tau\) being undefined at an excluded admitted pair where the target moves, which is exactly a separation and therefore exactly a protective contract — changes no verdict, because the clause has no truth value there. On the other reading it changes the verdict to *fails*, and then contradicts Part V’s own worked example. **[N4, N5]**

### Reverse
The clause is structural; its stated job is historical: “a scope **drawn** to exclude the embarrassing change”, “a change **made** to protect a candidate”. The document is emphatic that these are different: “What the semantics denies is that a component’s history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract”, and “Provenance matters to *representation* because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change” (Grievance 12). A structural profile test cannot establish an act of drawing, and the two come apart both ways: an innocently drawn scope can be “protective” by the definition, and a scope drawn to protect can pass the definition (the both-move case). The document already has the right instrument for the historical claim and does not use it — \(\rho_p\), the provenance of the question, and the record: “An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); … the record must show the boundary” (Part III). Two instruments, and they can disagree. **[N14]**

### Hunt
The clause’s entire bite sits in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). For a physical organization that is the physical module — Part XIV’s first **primitive package**, of which the document says “the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle: that is what ‘physics says what a lump instantiates’ costs”. For a mathematical or modelled organization it is “the declared admission relation for non-physical organizations”, a **declared parameter**. The document concedes the consequence for its own worked case: “Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation” (Part VII, skew matrices). So the “objectivity” the second index is supposed to supply is, in one class, declared, and in the other, borrowed from a primitive. The answer is sitting in the starting point. Part XIV nonetheless lists “Account, from fidelity under change (E)” under **Derived**. **[N7]**

### Add a job
Job: *if adequacy is a conjunct of Account, then the account claim must still be assessable within the question, and its truth value must be fixed by its contract.* Does the clause survive? No — see Part 0 and Part VIII below. Which rival does the new job kill? The revision’s placement (adequacy inside (E)); it leaves standing the rival of §“Build the best rival”. The new job is a different kind of check, not a repeat. **[N19]**

### Build the best rival
**Rival.** Adequacy outside `Account`, as the second conjunct of an objectivity claim — exactly as Grievance 4 puts it: “An objectivity claim is the conjunction, and the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V)”.

**The change that tells them apart.** A candidate that is faithful on \(C\), non-circular, non-vacuous, and protective. Ask “is it an account at \(C\)?” The revision: no. The rival: yes, an account, but not objective. This is not a difference at the level of this question — it changes verdicts at named points, including (EK), whose clause reads “\(\operatorname{Account}\) is evaluated on the contract of \(p_c\) fixed at \(e_c\)”. Under the revision, whether a finding counted as knowledge depends on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) as well as on the contract; under the rival it does not. **[N20]**

**Second rival.** Keep adequacy only where it is already implied — the anchored grade, whose condition 4 says “fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits” — and leave the weak grade as Part 0 describes it. Unlike the first rival this one loses nothing at the anchored grade. **[N13]**

### Check the patches
The patch is the conjunct itself, and the document already records its own residual failure: Part XV’s entry “**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V’s adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target. This shows the adequacy condition is too weak…”. By the clause as written, that entry is not merely open, it is satisfiable — see N6 — and Part V’s gloss (“An account whose contract excludes an admitted change that would tell it apart from the target fails adequacy”) describes a condition **stronger** than the one stated. So the patch’s own record overstates what was added. **[N21]**

Does the added part have another job? At the anchored grade, largely the same job as condition 4. **[N13]** Did a claim get narrower quietly? Nothing narrowed; if anything the record widened. **[N15, N21]**

### Look inside
Take two histories with the same outputs: scope \(S\) drawn from the question’s own boundaries, versus scope \(S\) drawn after a failure and narrowed to protect. Same \(C\), same \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), same \(D\), same \(E\), same adequacy verdict. The clause cannot reach the difference, and by the document’s own doctrine (Grievance 12) it should not try. If the historical claim is wanted, it needs the record, not this condition. **[N14]**

## 6. The findings, numbered

**[N1]** Part 0’s summary of the first grade omits non-protectiveness and says the grade is assessed within the question: “An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated”; “The first grade is what can be assessed within a question.” Four requirements are listed; adequacy is not one of them, and the clause added in Part V quantifies over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) — outside the question’s contract. Part 0 and Part V disagree about what the first grade is.

**[N2]** “the following **five** conditions hold” versus six conjuncts in (E). The count turns on whether fidelity is one condition or two; it matters here only because the question is whether adequacy is a conjunct of `Account` “itself”. (E) says yes; the prose count says it is a fifth condition of a five-part list.

**[N3]** “its baseline value” is undetermined. The document uses *baseline* in two ways: “their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\). The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**” (Part II, the set \(\{1\}\times B\)), and “it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\)” (Part III, one pair). The clause names the target’s baseline and elides the candidate’s, and never says whether the comparison is with \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b_0)\) or \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(1,b)\). The two readings give different verdicts on the same case.

**[N4]** Undefined values. Four routes, each supported by the document: (i) \(\tau\) may be undefined — “\(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair” (Part VII); (ii) \(\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) may be empty and \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) then unavailable; (iii) \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) may be undefined off \(C\), because coherence requires only “\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)” (Part III) — the clause compares answers at pairs where the question is not required to be well-posed; (iv) \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is used in (A) and in this clause and is never defined. At such a pair the clause has no truth value, so the pair is silently dropped from the quantification.

**[N5]** The pivot — and the document cannot have it both ways. On the **loose** reading (undefined pairs are simply not instances of either “differs” or “does not differ”), adequacy is blind at precisely the pair Part VII constructs its reversed calculation around, and N6 follows. On the **strict** reading (undefined counts as “does not move”), adequacy fails any candidate whose off-scope translation is undefined where the target moves — which is the normal shape of a scope-restricted candidate that drops a port — so it contradicts Part V’s own verdict, “The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\): it is an account at that contract”, and it drags the weak grade close to the anchored one, undoing Part 0’s division of labour. Which reading is intended is not stated; the test that would settle it is named in §9.

**[N6] (too weak)** The clause never compares the candidate’s answer with the target’s outside \(C\); it compares each with its own baseline. So a scope that excludes a pair at which **both** answers move, but by different amounts, passes. Instance built from the document’s own example: target \(L=H\cot\theta\); candidate \(L=H\cot\theta\cdot k\), \(k\neq1\), fitted so the two agree at the baseline pair; scope \(S\) excludes interventions on \(\theta\). At an excluded \(\theta\)-intervention the target’s answer moves from its baseline and the candidate’s moves too, so adequacy holds — yet putting that pair back into \(C\) breaks (A). The scope “excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target”, which is Part XV’s attack, and by the clause as written that attack is satisfied, not open. Part V’s gloss (“would tell it apart from the target”) says this account fails; the formalism says it passes.

**[N7] (too weak, second door)** For mathematical and modelled organizations \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is a declared parameter, and the document says so in its own worked example: “Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation.” A narrow declaration makes the condition vacuous. The document’s own feared outcome for the too-weak attack — “objectivity would again be contract-relative only” — is then simply true for that class.

**[N8]** The clause’s own exclusions are silent, in a predicate that makes silence disqualifying for the contract: “every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently.”

**[N9] (too strong?)** In its broad form the charge fails: “whenever the target has any out-of-scope dependence” is not enough, because if the candidate carries the dependence — as a faithful organization of the same target would — both sides move and the clause is silent. The clause fires only when sensitivity is **missing or extra**, never when it is present but wrong (N6). It is over-strict only under the strict reading of N5, and then it is over-strict enough to fail the document’s own identification example. **[N9a]** By contrast, the remove test is passed: the clause is not idle at the weak grade.

**[N10] (collision with \(K\))** No formal inconsistency: \(K\) is declared as “pairs the module does not admit”, and the clause “ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)” only, as Part III says. But \(K\) is an unguarded second door: a pair placed in \(K\) is invisible to adequacy by construction, and needs only “a stated meaning”, while a pair excluded through \(S\) needs “the reason for each exclusion recorded”. And adequacy is idle for the whole eliminative family that \(K\) exists to serve — Part VII: “the objectivity condition then ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) only, so what such an account establishes about the world is that no admitted change produces the effect”.

**[N11] (collision with Derivation 7)** Derivation 7’s proof step reads “The assessment is indexed to \(C\); its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index (Part VIII)”; Part VIII: “A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change”. With adequacy inside `Account`, the truth value of “\(E\) is an account on \(C\)” turns on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), which the document treats as the module’s content — a “claim about the physical module, fallible but checkable in principle” (Grievance 9) — and as a declared parameter (Part XIV). Let \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) grow by discovery, with \(C\) and the record untouched: the same claim flips, at the same named index. Either the index must be written everywhere as a pair (contract, admission relation), which Derivation 7 and Part VIII do not do, or the conjunct must leave `Account`.

**[N12] (collision with Grievance 4)** Grievance 4’s answer to relativism is: “Two claims, two indices. ‘Faithful on C’ is a fact within C. ‘C is not protective’ is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes… An objectivity claim is the conjunction, and the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V).” Once the second conjunct is a conjunct of `Account`, the objectivity claim is `Account` ∧ (its own conjunct): idempotent. “Two claims, two indices” becomes one claim with a mixed index, and the second index stops being independent evidence of anything. Part III still calls it “the objectivity condition of Part V” and Grievance 4 still calls it “the second conjunct” — both are descriptions of the pre-revision arrangement. Either the naming is stale or two different conditions are meant; the document does not say which.

**[N13] (duplication)** The anchored grade’s condition 4 already requires, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), “fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits”. On the document’s own design — a query read off the solution set, with (F2) equating \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D]\) and \(\operatorname{Sol}_E\) — fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) makes the two answer profiles coincide on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and an anchored account cannot fail adequacy. So the conjunct’s only independent work is at the weak grade; the document nowhere says this, and nowhere says which of the two instruments is meant to do which job.

**[N14] (direction)** A structural condition carrying a historical verdict: “a scope **drawn to exclude**”, “a change **made** to protect a candidate”. See the reverse test.

**[N15] (no worked instance)** Nowhere in the document does adequacy change a verdict. The skew example makes it relative to the declared relation; the identification example must be rescued by the undefined-value convention; the two-balance case is an inference-circularity case; the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) passes it (“satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3”). A condition with no instance in the document is at least loose.

**[N16]** “A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK)” (Part XI). Under the revision’s own definition, narrowing \(S\) *enlarges* \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), so it can only hurt adequacy; what narrowing rescues is fidelity. The sentence names a rescue the new definition disables — a leftover, or a use of “adequacy” for “the account verdict”, which should be said.

**[N17]** The swap in §5 works at least as well as the clause for the clause’s stated job. What stops the swap is not stated anywhere.

**[N18]** \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is “the set of pairs the module in force admits”, filled for physical organizations from “the physically admitted edits \(A_\Theta\)” (an edit set). The clause quantifies over pairs; which boundaries pair with an admitted edit is not stated.

**[N19]** The added job of §5. The clause fails it; the rival survives it.

**[N20]** The rival of §5, with a discriminating case, so the two are not the same at this level.

**[N21]** The document’s own record of the patch understates the hole: Part V’s gloss and Part XV’s attack statement are both stronger than the formalism as written.

**[N22]** If the conjunct is new (see the tool-note), then every prior verdict “\(E\) is an account on \(C\)” at a restricted \(C\) is a new claim under the revision, and nothing in (E) or Part V marks that the predicate changed.

## 8. Whole-clause checks

**Flip.** Same case, two outcomes, on the baseline reading alone (**N3**); and on the undefined-value convention alone (**N5**). An explanation that covers both outcomes is held in place by neither.

**Direction.** The clause is structural, its claimed job historical (**N14**); the document’s own doctrine separates the two, and it has a better instrument for the historical claim (the record, \(\rho_p\)).

**Answer hidden in the starting points.** The second index rests on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) — primitive for physics, declared for mathematics — and the document concedes the mathematics case in so many words (**N7**). Part XIV lists `Account` as **Derived**.

**What the change list leaves out.** Undefined pairs; both-move pairs; all of \(K\); the drawing of the scope; the warrant for a declared admission relation. All silent (**N4, N6, N8, N10**).

**Rivals.** (i) Adequacy outside `Account`, as the objectivity claim’s second conjunct — discriminating case exists (**N20**); (ii) adequacy confined to the anchored grade, with condition 4 doing the work, and the weak grade left as Part 0 describes it (**N13, N20**).

**Where the parts came from.** The clause is a stipulation, not a transport, so the document’s selected/constructed/declared machinery does not formally apply to it; its nearest analogue is \(\rho_p\), the provenance of the question. I mark it **declared** for its content, and **borrowed** for its force, because its force is supplied by a relation it does not test — the module, or an author’s declaration. Its listing under **Derived** in Part XIV cannot make it derived from the question alone: a derived predicate whose truth turns on a declared parameter is still relative to that parameter.

**Check the patches.** See §5: known hole, and the hole is bigger than the record says.

**Look inside.** Same outputs, different drawings of the scope; adequacy cannot tell them apart, and by the document’s own doctrine should not try.

## 9. What would make it harder to vary

1. **Close the pivot, one way or the other.** State whether \(\tau\), \(\sigma\) and \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) must be defined wherever \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) is, and whether undefined counts as “does not move”. Define \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) (or say that the query is read on \(E\) as on \(D\)). This one decision fixes N4, N5 and most of N9.
2. **Compare the two sides with each other.** Replace the two baseline comparisons with one comparison between the profiles over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), keeping the baseline only as a way of saying what the change does. This kills N6 and makes Part V’s gloss and Part XV’s attack entry true statements.
3. **Say which instrument does which job.** Adequacy versus the anchored grade’s condition 4 (**N13**), and adequacy versus the record (**N14**).
4. **Write down the index.** If adequacy stays in (E), say that an account claim is indexed to (contract, admission relation) and update Part 0, Part III, Part VIII and Derivation 7 together (**N11, N12**).
5. **Supply the missing instance.** One candidate that adequacy actually fails, written out in the document’s own form (**N15**).

If the aim is to keep the weak grade assessable within the question, the cheaper repair is the rival: take adequacy out of (E) and leave it where Grievance 4 put it.

---

## 10. What this does not show

It does not show that protective scopes should not be caught, or that the revision’s two grades are wrong, or that the clause is unimplementable. It shows that this statement of it is undetermined at three named places (baseline, definedness, \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\)) and silent at three others (both-move pairs, \(K\), the declared admission relation); that the too-strong and too-weak charges are not both avoidable as written; and that the placement of the clause inside `Account` contradicts three of the document’s own texts. And, as the skill says and as the document says of itself — “whether the defined predicates *capture* explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, not a theorem” — being hard to vary is not being true.

**1. The merit condition is not well-defined: it uses a counterfactual repair predicate that the document only defines for actual episodes and actual states.**
Attacked passage (Part X): “the found question \(p'\) counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P), with \(o\) failing at the state before \(e\).”
Why it fails: (P) is defined in Part XI as
\[
\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O).

This is a predicate of an actual episode \(\Delta\) and actual states \(\xi,\xi'\). At the moment a question is found, there is no account, no end state, and no episode producing the repair. “Would repair” is not defined. Worse, “deployable account” invokes Deploy, which by Part X requires (R), hence a selected or constructed transport. So the merit condition presupposes a construction history for the account in order to certify the construction history of the question.
Severity: fatal for the claim that the merit condition can be evaluated at question-finding time.

**Direct answers to the four questions**

- **Is the merit condition trivial or too strong?** It is both. It is trivial because \(O_p\) is a declared parameter and can be gerrymandered to make any account of \(p'\) a repair. It is too strong because it requires a pre-existing failing obligation and a deployable account already in prospect, excluding genuine question-finding that creates or transforms obligations. It is also ill-defined because “would repair under (P)” is not defined for a future account.
- **Does the question-organization’s edit set admit exactly the changes needed?** No. It omits \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\), fixes \(b_0\), does not enforce coherence, and conflates edits to \(\mathcal Q,S,K\) with edits of a single question. “Add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning” is not a well-defined edit in the sense of Part II; it is at best a change to a port value whose semantics and effect on \(L_j\) are unspecified.
- **Is the stated limit on concept creation actually a limit?** Not as stated. If \(\operatorname{Inst}\) plus an admissible new grain supplies an organization with the new port, the document’s existing machinery can build a new \(D_p\) for that organization. The limit is only on dimensions not supplied by the physical module, which is a limit on \(\Theta\), not on the semantics’ concept creation.
- **Which of the four open cases could be closed with what the document already has?** (i) Impossibility is partially closable for the physically grounded case; (ii) symmetry is potentially closable by treating symmetry as an admitted edit; (iv) mathematics is already closable under a declared admission relation. (iii) selection artefact cannot be closed without adding a multi-target account; the current \(\operatorname{Account}\) is single-target.

## A. The merit condition in Part X

**1. The obligation set is ambiguous between \(O\) and \(O_p\), and this changes the condition from substantive to trivial.**
- **Claim attacked:** “the found question \(p'\) counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P), with \(o\) failing at the state before \(e\).”
- **Why it fails:** Part III defines a question \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) with its own obligation set \(O_p\). Part X’s attempted definition of origin for questions says “some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\).” The merit condition then switches to \(o\in O\), which in Part XI is the global claimed-obligation set used by \(\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}\). If \(O\) is global, then a new question can count as found because it would repair *any* claimed obligation anywhere, not because it addresses the obligations of *this* question. If it should be \(O_{p'}\), the text is inconsistent.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** State exactly which obligation set is used: \(O_{p'}\), \(O_p\), or a pre-existing fixed \(O\). Require the obligation to be one the question is addressed to, not any obligation in the global set.

**2. The merit condition is trivially satisfiable by declaring the obligation set to fit the question.**
- **Claim attacked:** “This is the only merit condition in the semantics, and it is stated so that a random new contract does not count.”
- **Why it fails:** \(O\), \(P\), and \(O_p\) are declared parameters (Part XIV: “the obligation sets \(O,P,O_p\)”). Nothing in the merit condition requires the obligation to be fixed before the question is constructed, independently recognized, or not itself introduced with the new question. If a modeller can declare \(O_{p'}\) to contain an obligation that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair, then every constructed question can satisfy the merit condition by carrying its own justifying obligation.
- **Seriousness:** serious, bordering on fatal for the claimed line between finding and re-parameterising.
- **Fix:** Require the obligation to be drawn from a set fixed before the episode, as Part XII does for \(J_p\) in \(\operatorname{CanAdv}\): “specified by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed.” Add an independence condition on \(O\).

**3. “Would repair \(o\) under (P)” is ill-typed: (P) requires an episode, states, and a produced change that the merit condition does not supply.**
- **Claim attacked:** “a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P)…”
- **Why it fails:** \(\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\) is defined only relative to an episode \(\Delta\), start and end states \(\xi,\xi'\), and \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O)\), which requires every change of obligation value to lie on an active route from occurrences of \(\Delta\). The merit condition mentions only \(p'\), \(o\), and \(e\). There is no \(\Delta\), no \(\xi,\xi'\), no produced change, and no active route. “Would repair” is therefore undefined.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Either define a separate counterfactual merit predicate with explicit states and possible account, or require the subsequent episode to satisfy (P) and make the merit condition prospective.

**4. The merit condition is too strong: it demands a deployable account of \(p'\) at the moment the question is found.**
- **Claim attacked:** “a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P)…”
- **Why it fails:** \(\operatorname{Deploy}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)\) is state-indexed: “\(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance.” A question can be found before any account of it exists. Requiring a deployable account of \(p'\) conflates the merit of the question with the merit of an answer, and may make genuine question-finding fail the condition simply because the answer has not yet been built.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Require that a deployable account of \(p'\) *would*, if built, repair \(o\), not that such an account is already deployable at the finding episode.

**5. The condition allows a constructed question to include the very obligation it repairs.**
- **Claim attacked:** “only when there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\)… with \(o\) failing at the state before \(e\).”
- **Why it fails:** If \(p'\) is constructed, its tuple includes \(O_{p'}\). Nothing prevents the construction from introducing a new obligation \(o\) into \(O_{p'}\) and then claiming that an account of \(p'\) repairs it. The condition says the obligation fails before \(e\), but if \(o\) is introduced with \(p'\) at \(e\), it did not exist before \(e\).
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Require \(o\) to be in the obligation set of the system at the pre-\(e\) state, not in the newly constructed question’s own \(O_{p'}\).

**6. The merit condition is not the “only merit condition” in the semantics, even for questions, because (P) itself is a merit condition for episodes.**
- **Claim attacked:** “This is the only merit condition in the semantics…”
- **Why it fails:** Part XI defines \(\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}\) as a condition on episodes, and \(\operatorname{CreateEK}\) requires it. That is a merit condition on epistemic repair, whether or not it is called one. The sentence overstates the isolation of the question merit condition.
- **Seriousness:** minor/cosmetic.
- **Fix:** Say “the only merit condition for found questions” rather than “in the semantics.”

- **Fix:** Allow removal and alteration of \(K\)-pairs and their meanings, with the same consistency conditions as \(S\).

**15. “Alter \(\mathcal Q\)” conflates the object-level query with a meta-level edit on the question-organization.**
- **Claim attacked:** “Admitted edits: … alter \(\mathcal Q\)…”
- **Why it fails:** \(\mathcal Q\) is part of the question tuple \(p\), an object-level operation on the target \(D\). The admitted edits of \(D_p\) are meta-level operations on the representation of \(p\). The notation does not distinguish \(A_D\) from \(A_{D_p}\). The same problem occurs for “alter \(S\)” and “add to \(K\).”
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Explicitly separate object-level \(A_D\times B_D\) from meta-level \(A_{D_p}\) and state which predicates quantify over which.

- **Claim attacked:** “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).”
- **Why it fails:** Part III defines coherently posed as requiring \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) disjoint from each other’s complements as stated. Derivation 5 has only a baseline port reading “in” and a vague “\(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the ‘in’ ports in the form of Part III.” It does not encode nonemptiness of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\), definedness of \(\mathcal Q\) on all of \(C\), or the exact disjointness conditions.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Add components enforcing each condition, or weaken the claim to “a subset of coherently posed questions whose remaining conditions must be checked separately.”

**17. The nontrivial binding condition in Part X is not satisfied for the question-organization as constructed.**
- **Claim attacked:** “A **binding** is an assignment \(\lambda(k)\) of a component of the built organization to a subnetwork of its target; a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling … nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\); it is **required for the use** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for \(c\).”
- **Why it fails:** Derivation 5 builds \(D_p\) from ports and closure conditions, but never says what the target of \(D_p\) is, what its components are anchored to, or which binding is nontrivial. If the target is \(D\), the components of \(D_p\) are closure conditions over the question tuple, not components about \(D\), so there is no obvious \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\). If the target is \(p\), then the circularity of Finding 9 applies. Therefore the construction may not satisfy \(\operatorname{Build}\).

**18. If \(K\) can introduce structure outside \(A\times B\), Derivation 5 has no port for it; if it cannot, Part III overclaims what \(K\) can do.**

If \(K\) is restricted to \(A\times B\), then it cannot represent a structure that requires a new dimension, contradicting Part III’s claim about \(K\).

**19. \(b_0\) is held fixed, but \(b_0\) is part of the question tuple, so the construction cannot represent questions with different baselines.**
- **Claim attacked:** “Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed.”
- **Why it fails:** A question is \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\). Fixing \(b_0\) as a boundary means the question-organization can only represent questions with that baseline. But baseline choice is part of question formulation. The claim that it represents coherently posed questions on \(D\) is therefore too broad.

## D. The four open cases in Part XV

**25. Case (i), impossibility: the admitted-rival subcase is already handled; the physically impossible-rival subcase cannot be closed without either accepting scoped counterfactuals as sufficient or adding a physical impossibility predicate.**

The case is not open for the class; it is open only if one demands that the admission relation be independently constrained. The example’s anchoring of background components “to themselves under the identity translation” is especially suspicious: it closes the case by stipulating that the background is already anchored.
- **Seriousness:** serious.

- **Seriousness:** minor/cosmetic.

## Findings

### Merit condition

**1. “Repair \(o\) under (P)” is ill-typed.**
Claim attacked: “the found question \(p'\) counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P), with \(o\) failing at the state before \(e\).”
Why it fails: (P) is not a predicate on one obligation \(o\) and a counterfactual account. It is \(\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\), requiring an episode \(\Delta\), start and end states, and \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O)\). There is no defined counterfactual repair predicate “would repair \(o\) under (P).”
Severity: serious.
Fix: define a separate counterfactual repair relation, or require an actual episode/deployment and make the merit condition depend on it.

**2. The merit condition is circular through \(O_p\).**
Claim attacked: Part III says “\(O_p\) is the set of obligations being addressed or protected,” and Part X says the merit condition requires “an obligation \(o\in O\)” that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair.
Why it fails: if \(O=O_{p'}\), then \(p'\) is “found” because it addresses an obligation that is defined as an obligation \(p'\) addresses. That is tautological. If \(O\) is some larger fixed set, the text never says so.
Severity: fatal to the claimed non-triviality of the merit condition.
Fix: require \(O\) to be fixed independently of \(p'\), and explicitly exclude obligations whose content is “answer \(p'\).”

**3. The merit condition is too weak: it filters usefulness, not finding-versus-reparameterisation.**
Claim attacked: “a re-parameterisation of an old question that repairs no obligation is a constructed question and not a found one.”
Why it fails: the condition is existential: any one obligation repaired by a deployable account of \(p'\) suffices. A trivial variant of an old question can inherit the old account, repair the old obligation, and therefore count as found. The condition does not require that \(p'\) itself be necessary for the repair, nor that no old question already repairs the same obligation. It therefore admits exactly the inherited-account reparameterisations it says it excludes.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require that the repair depend on \(p'\) on an active route, and that no pre-existing deployable account of an old question already repairs \(o\).

**4. The merit condition is too strong for question-finding that discovers a new obligation.**
Claim attacked: the found question “counts as found” only if there is an obligation \(o\in O\) failing before \(e\) and a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair it.
Why it fails: a genuine act of finding a question can consist in discovering that an obligation exists, or in making an inarticulate difficulty tractable, before any deployable account of \(p'\) exists. The condition requires an already-failing obligation and a counterfactual account, so it rejects question-finding prior to answer-construction.
Severity: serious.
Fix: separate question-merit from answer-merit. Let a found question count if it makes an obligation assessable, locates a failure, or is required for a later repair, not only if an account already repairs it.

**5. “Provenance attaches to the whole question” is type-confused.**
Claim attacked: “\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\) attaches to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone, with the witness required by Part IV.” Derivation 5 says provenance attaches “to the whole question through this organization.”
Why it fails: Part IV’s witnesses, \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\dots)\) and \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\), are defined for a transport \(t\), not for a question-organization. Derivation 5 supplies a question-organization \(D_p\), but no transport from a content to \(D_p\). So “provenance attaches through this organization” never says which transport is selected or constructed.
Severity: fatal to the formal definition of selected/constructed questions.
Fix: either define selection/construction directly for question-organizations, or supply the missing transport and state its target and fidelity condition.

**6. A single \(\rho_p\) erases internal provenance heterogeneity.**
Claim attacked: “\(\rho_p\) attaches to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone.”
Why it fails: \(D\), \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), \(S\), \(K\), \(\mathcal Q\), \(O_p\), and \(b_0\) can have different histories. A target may be physically given, a scope declared, a query constructed, and a counterfactual set merely stipulated. A single provenance label cannot represent that mixture.
Severity: serious.
Fix: make provenance componentwise with a stated precedence rule, as Part IV does for transports.

**7. The merit condition lacks a non-accidentality condition.**
Claim attacked: “This is the only merit condition in the semantics, and it is stated so that a random new contract does not count.”
Why it fails: a random new contract that accidentally repairs some obligation satisfies the existential condition. Nothing requires the question’s construction to be aimed at the obligation, nor that the obligation be relevant to the episode’s difficulty.
Severity: serious.
Fix: require the obligation to be in the episode’s recognized difficulty or connected by an active route to the construction of \(p'\).

### Question-organization of Derivation 5

**8. “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\)” is false.**
Claim attacked: “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).”
Why it fails: Part III defines a question as \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\). Derivation 5 supplies ports for \((a,b)\), \(\mathcal Q\), \(S\), and \(K\). It supplies no port for \(b_0\), \(O_p\), \(\rho_p\), or \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). Two questions differing only in \(O_p\), \(\rho_p\), or admitted relation \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) are not distinguished by the organization. It therefore cannot have exactly the coherently posed questions as its solutions.
Severity: fatal to the “exactly” claim.
Fix: add ports/components for \(b_0\), \(O_p\), \(\rho_p\), and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), or weaken the claim to “solutions are a proper subclass of coherently posed questions.”

**9. The organization imposes a closure condition not present in Part III.**
Claim attacked: “if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads ‘in’ then so do its factors where the composition is defined.”
Why it fails: Part III defines \(C\) as an arbitrary set of admitted edit-boundary pairs in the form \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\). It does not require that if a composite edit is in \(C\), its factors are in \(C\). The closure condition over-restricts the question-organization and breaks the “exactly” claim.
Severity: serious.
Fix: remove the closure condition, or add composition-closure to Part III’s definition of coherently posed questions and accept that not all Part III questions are solutions.

**10. “Add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning” is not a well-defined organization edit.**
Claim attacked: “Admitted edits: toggle a pair; alter \(\mathcal Q\); alter \(S\); add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning.”
Why it fails: Part II edits require \(L_j(a,b)\) relations. Part III says \(K\) is a set of “pairs the module does not admit.” If \(a\notin A\), then \(L_j(a,b)\) is undefined. “With a meaning” is natural-language annotation, not a relation in the organization data. So the edit cannot be evaluated within (O).
Severity: fatal to the treatment of \(K\) in Derivation 5 and Part III.
Fix: represent \(K\)-meanings as explicit declared relations/components, or restrict \(K\) to pairs that have a formal interpretation in \(L\).

**11. “Toggle a pair” conflates membership in \(C\) with the \(S/K\) partition.**
Claim attacked: “Admitted edits: toggle a pair; alter \(\mathcal Q\); alter \(S\); add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning.”
Why it fails: Part III distinguishes admitted pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\) from counterfactual pairs in \(K\). A pair can be in \(C\) because it is in \(S\) or because it is in \(K\). Toggling the \(u_{(a,b)}\) port does not by itself move a pair between \(S\) and \(K\), nor alter \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). The edit set therefore does not admit exactly the changes needed to change a question’s contract status.
Severity: serious.
Fix: add separate edits for moving a pair among \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), \(S\), and \(K\), and for altering the declared admission relation.

**12. Part III’s coherence condition is logically garbled.**
Claim attacked: “It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other’s complements as stated.”
Why it fails: in a common universe \(U\), “\(S\) is disjoint from the complement of \(K\)” means \(S\subseteq K\), and “\(K\) is disjoint from the complement of \(S\)” means \(K\subseteq S\). Together they imply \(S=K\). But \(S\) is admitted scoped pairs and \(K\) is non-admitted counterfactuals, so they should normally be disjoint, not equal.
Severity: fatal as written.
Fix: state \(S\cap K=\varnothing\), \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\), or whatever the intended condition is.

**13. Fidelity over \(K\) is undefined.**
Claim attacked: “Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\); the objectivity condition of Part V ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning.”
Why it fails: \(C\) includes \(K\), but \(K\) consists of “pairs the module does not admit.” (F1), (F2), and (A) require \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) and \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) for every \((a,b)\in C\). If \(a\) is not admitted, those relations are not supplied. A “stated meaning” is not a solution set.
Severity: fatal to the use of \(K\) in fidelity.
Fix: extend the organization interpretation to \(K\)-pairs, or exclude \(K\) from (F1), (F2), and (A) and give a separate semantics for counterfactual meaning.

**14. Derivation 5’s question-as-content move is a category error.**
Claim attacked: “The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a question. When it is a question, the originative act is the finding of a question…”
Why it fails: (G) and (EK) require \(\operatorname{Build}\) and \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\). If \(c\) is a question \(p'\), then \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\) assesses the question-organization as an explanatory account of \(p_c\). A question is not an answer or explanation. Derivation 5 conflates the content “question” with the organization that represents the question.
Severity: fatal to the claim that question-finding is represented by (G) without further machinery.
Fix: introduce a separate predicate for finding questions, or make the originative content the answer-organization that deploys the question, not the question itself.

### Limit on inventing a new dimension of variation

**15. The stated limit is overbroad: Inst plus a new admissible grain can cover some “new dimensions.”**
Claim attacked: “The invention of a new dimension of variation — a port the target organization did not have, a respect in which nothing was previously varied — is not a question found in the sense above, because the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing \(A\times B\).”
Why it fails: Part IV says “one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain.” Part II allows a grain if the physical module licenses an organization at that grain and every component has a signature some physically admitted edit realizes. If the “new dimension” is already licensed by \(\operatorname{Inst}\) at an admissible grain, then there is an organization \(D'\) with the new port and new admitted edits. Derivation 5 can be applied to \(D'\), and the question over \(D'\) is representable. The unrepresented case is not merely adding a port; it is changing the physical module or extending \(A_\Theta\)/\(\operatorname{Inst}\).
Severity: serious overclaim.

**16. The limit understates the real unrepresented case.**
Claim attacked: “It is a change to the target organization itself, and this document has no witness for it.”
Why it fails: Derivation 5 can be reapplied to a different target \(D'\) if \(D'\) is instantiated before the construction event. What the semantics cannot represent is the event of bringing \(D'\) into the module’s admissible decompositions, or adding a new physically admitted edit, because the physical module is primitive and supplies \(A_\Theta\) and \(\operatorname{Inst}\). The text does not say this precisely.
Severity: serious.
Fix: distinguish (a) finding a question over an already-instantiated finer organization, which is representable, from (b) inventing or extending the physical module’s admissible decompositions, which is not.

### The four open cases in Part XV

**17. Impossibility of a physically impossible rival cannot be closed with what the document already has.**

Why it fails: the treatment depends on \(K\)-pairs whose meanings are stipulated and whose relations are not in \(L\). **18. Symmetry could be closed in principle with existing resources, but the document’s reason for calling it open is false.**
Claim attacked: “explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among the components of \(E\) that neither (F1) nor (F2) constrains, with no construction here.”
Why it fails: if symmetry transformations are admitted edits and the invariance-to-independence entailment is encoded as a component relation in \(E\), then (F1) and (F2) can constrain the component, and (A) can constrain the answer profile if the entailment affects the answer. The open case is therefore a missing construction, not a necessity failure. If the entailment is supposed to float free of every component relation and every answer profile, then it is not part of the account being assessed.
Severity: serious misclassification.
Fix: give the construction, or show that the symmetry force cannot be represented as a component relation without circularity and cannot affect (A).

**19. Selection artefact could be partially closed with existing vocabulary if the single-target schema is relaxed.**
Claim attacked: “explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact, which needs two targets and has no construction here.”
Why it fails: the semantics already has populations, variation, survival, histories, organizations, and transports. A joint organization with a data subnetwork and a population subnetwork can in principle be instantiated if the physical module licenses the decomposition. The real obstacle is Part III’s single target \(D\) and the single transport \(t:D\to E\), not a missing primitive. If the selection history must be represented as a component relation, the existing (O), (F1), (F2), and (A) can constrain it.
Severity: serious.
Fix: either provide a product-target or multi-target account, or state exactly why selection histories cannot be represented as component relations under (O).

**20. Mathematical explanation cannot be closed as an objective necessity test; it is scoped away.**
Claim attacked: “mathematical explanation, which falls under a declared admission relation and not under the physical quantifier of this attack.”
Why it fails: for a mathematical organization, \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is declared. Part V’s adequacy condition ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), so a protective declaration can exclude exactly the edits that would separate the candidate from the target. The semantics then has no non-arbitrary condition on mathematical admission. Saying “every claim is relative to that declaration” is not a solution; it makes the necessity claim vacuous for mathematics.
Severity: fatal to the necessity claim if mathematical explanation is genuine and objective.
Fix: require mathematical admission relations to be fixed independently of the explanation, or explicitly restrict the necessity claim to physical contracts and stop listing mathematics as a treated case.

**21. Part XV mislists mathematics as an open case under attack (B).**
Claim attacked: “Four cases are open… (iv) mathematical explanation, which falls under a declared admission relation and not under the physical quantifier of this attack.”
Why it fails: attack (B) is explicitly about “any transport satisfying component fidelity under any admitted contract.” For mathematics, the admitted contract is declared, not physical. Listing it as an open case inflates the open cases while simultaneously excluding it from the attack’s quantifier.
Severity: minor to serious depending on how the open-case list is used.
Fix: move (iv) to the scope limitations, and state that the necessity claim is conditional on physical or independently declared admission.

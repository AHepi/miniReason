## The steelman: an anchoring condition beyond change-fidelity

**The condition (AC).** For an account 𝓔 = (E, p, t, Γ) with t = (π, τ, σ, λ), every active component k of E must be anchored by λ to a subnetwork of D that is *the same kind of thing as k at the grain ℓ at which the physical module fixes D* — where "same kind" is fixed by the world's structure at ℓ (via Org_ℓ), **not** by the contract C. (AC) constrains the λ-component of the transport, over and above (F1)–(F2) on C.

(AC) is well-posed *within the document's own primitives*. Part XIV says Org_ℓ — "the organization a physical occurrence instantiates at a grain" — is a primitive supplied by the physical module; Part 0 says "Physics says what organization a lump of matter instantiates." So D's component structure at ℓ is fixed independently of anyone's contract, and λ is given data of the transport. (AC) is therefore evaluable whenever the physics fixes the components of D, which is always, by "Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physics."

### Case 1 — correct at the contract, anchored to the wrong mechanism

Two subnetworks n₁, n₂ of D with identical signatures on C (no change in C separates them) but distinct at ℓ. A component k of E shares that signature; λ(k) = n₂, the bystander. (F1) holds, (F2) holds, (A) holds, non-circularity and non-vacuity hold. The account is, by (E), an account. Yet one anchoring latches onto the subnetwork that actually produces the phenomenon and the other onto a co-signature bystander. On the mechanist's view the first explains and the second does not. The semantics can only reply: "supply a separating change." The separating change *exists* — it is physically admitted, hence in A, excluded from C by a stated scope. But adopting it is declared a *new question at a new index*, not a verdict on this account. So the semantics has no verdict available at the index where the account is actually assessed.

### Case 2 — pole and shadow when the production contract is unavailable

Part VII concedes: "The reversed calculation H=L tan θ is not [faithful]… It is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed L." The concession understates the case. With θ fixed as a boundary condition, the reversed organization satisfies **all five** conjuncts of (E) on the identification contract:

- (F1) for its H-component anchored to the pole subnetwork: under the edit "observed L → l′", the constraint L = H cot θ forces Sol_{λ(k)} to project to {l′ tan θ}, and L_H(set-L = l′) = {l′ tan θ}. Equal.
- (F2): π[Sol_D(a,b)] = {H = l′ tan θ, L = l′} = Sol_{E′}(set-L, θ).
- (A): the identified feature is H, and E′ computes the same value.
- Non-circularity: replacing the H-component changes the answer.
- Non-vacuity: Sol_D(1,b₀) ≠ ∅.

The forward organization satisfies (E) on the *same* contract by the symmetric construction. So two organizations with opposite dependence direction are both accounts of the same question at the same grain.

Now make the production contract unavailable — the pole is a historical artifact, the illumination angle is not manipulable, only the shadow's length is observable and θ is fixed by circumstances. The identification contract is the only contract on which assessment is possible. On it, the reversed account is not merely unrefuted; by Derivation 2 it is pronounced *the same account* as the correct one. Yet it has the dependence backwards: shadows are explained by poles and suns, not poles by shadows. (AC) detects this — the pole-height component must anchor to the pole-height subnetwork, and in the reversed account it is anchored to the shadow subnetwork. Change-fidelity on the available contract does not.

### Case 3 — scientific realism about mechanisms

On the Craver-style view, a model explains only if its parts and activities correspond to the parts and activities of the mechanism that actually produces the phenomenon; "how-possibly" models (right counterfactuals within scope, wrong anchoring) do not explain. The document concedes the instance in Part V: "A true mechanism guessed for bad reasons satisfies (E); the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX)."

This relocation fails. The how-possibly/how-actually distinction is not a distinction in *reasons for adoption*: two models can be adopted for identical reasons, by the same critical episode, and still differ in whether their components are anchored to the actual mechanism. Part IX's apparatus — histories, active routes, Bearing (K1), standing (K2), receipts — is an account of *criticism and justification events*, not of the model–world correspondence relation of a single model. Nothing in (K1)–(K3) or the receipts machinery addresses whether λ(k) is the right subnetwork of D. So the framework has no place for the distinction that, on the mechanist's view, makes a model explanatory rather than merely accurate. (AC) is that place.

## What the document loses by dropping (AC)

1. **The how-possibly/how-actually distinction at a fixed contract.** Fidelity under change cannot separate a model anchored to the actual mechanism from one anchored to a co-signature surrogate, and the framework's attempt to relocate the distinction to Part IX is a category error.
2. **Direction of explanation when interventions are unavailable.** Direction becomes contract-relative ("Direction is derived from the admitted edits; the nouns 'pole' and 'shadow' fix nothing"), so in historical, cosmological, or otherwise non-interventional science the reversed account is not wrong but *identical* to the correct one at that grain.
3. **Component-level model–world correspondence as a semantic fact.** The framework retains a world-side fidelity fact for whole contents ("the transport from carrier to content is faithful while the content's transport to the world fails") but refuses its component-level analogue — an unargued asymmetry.
4. **Mechanism identification as an explanatory achievement.** Finding *which* mechanism it is, as distinct from getting its counterfactual profile right, has no semantic standing; it is pushed into contract provenance and criticism.
5. **Use of its own primitive.** Org_ℓ fixes D at a grain, but the semantics lets that grain constrain only physical realizability, never anchoring.

## Does "Why there is no anchoring condition" answer the steelman?

The section, in full:

> A separate condition — "each component of E must be tied to a component of D *of the same kind*" — would be either redundant or unevaluable. Where C contains a change separating two kinds, (F1) already fails for a component anchored to the wrong one. Where C contains no such change, the two are one kind on C by (K), and the condition would be asserting a distinction that C does not contain. There is no third case. Derivation 1 makes this exact.

**It does not answer the steelman as posed.** The dichotomy assumes the contested term: "kind" means *kind-on-C*, i.e., the equivalence class induced by (K). Against an opponent who accepts (K), the argument is sound. Against (AC), which indexes kind to the physics-fixed grain ℓ, there *is* a third case, and it is realized by the document's own definitions: **a change that is physically admitted (in A) but excluded from C by a stated scope.** Such a change separates the two kinds at grain ℓ while leaving their C-signatures identical. (F1) on C does not detect the mis-anchoring; the two are one kind *on C* but not at ℓ. The section's second horn therefore defeats only an anchoring condition *indexed to C* — a surrogate target — and misdescribes (AC) as "asserting a distinction that C does not contain": the distinction is contained in the physics, which the document itself makes a primitive.

The "unevaluable" horn is also too strong. λ is given data of the transport, and Org_ℓ is physics-supplied, so the claim "λ(k) is the pole subnetwork" is evaluable without any change in C. What is unevaluable is only its *kind-status under (K)* — which is exactly what (AC) denies is the relevant notion.

What the section does establish is the weaker, correct claim: **no contract-relative kind condition can be added to (F1) without redundancy.** The document's real answer to the mechanist lies elsewhere — Grievance 4 ("Objectivity lives in the physics and in the fidelity facts"), non-vacuity's stated-scope requirement, the historical index, and Derivation 2's "a claim that two such candidates 'really' differ is a claim that some admitted change separates them, and must supply it." That is a genuine *position*: explanatory adequacy is indexed, and wrong anchoring at a grain is a defect of the contract rather than of the account. But it is a position, not the refutation the section asserts with "There is no third case. Derivation 1 makes this exact."

## Findings

**1. "There is no third case" is false against grain-relative anchoring.**
- *Claim attacked:* "Where C contains no such change, the two are one kind on C by (K), and the condition would be asserting a distinction that C does not contain. There is no third case."
- *Why it fails:* A change can be physically admitted (in A) yet excluded from C by a stated scope. It separates the two kinds at grain ℓ but not on C. (F1) on C then does not detect a mis-anchoring that (AC) does. The dichotomy only covers kinds-on-C.
- *Seriousness:* Serious. This is the crux of the document's rejection of anchoring, and it assumes the definition at issue.
- *Fix:* Restrict the claim explicitly to kind-on-C, and either concede that a grain-relative anchoring condition is a genuine extra constraint, or show that (F1) on the contract generated by ℓ's admitted changes is *the* correct explanatory assessment contract — i.e., argue for the contract, not against the condition.

**2. "Unevaluable" contradicts the document's own primitive Org_ℓ.**
- *Claim attacked:* the second horn's "unevaluable."
- *Why it fails:* Part XIV lists Org_ℓ as supplied by the physical module; Part 0 says physics says what organization a lump instantiates; λ is part of the transport data. So anchoring claims are evaluable without any change in C. Only their *kind-status under (K)* is unevaluable.
- *Seriousness:* Serious. The section under-uses its own primitive and thereby misstates the opponent's position.
- *Fix:* Replace "unevaluable" with "not evaluable by the changes in C," and say why that is the right court of appeal.

**3. Internal tension with Grievance 2.**
- *Claim attacked:* "There is no third case" (Part V) vs. Part 0, Grievance 2: "At a finer level with more admitted changes they may separate."
- *Why it fails:* If finer levels with more admitted changes can separate components that a coarser level does not, then an anchoring condition indexed to the finer level is not redundant with (F1) on the coarser contract. Both statements cannot be the whole story.
- *Seriousness:* Serious (it is the same defect as Finding 1, but visible as an inconsistency rather than a dialectical gap).
- *Fix:* State the grain index on every kind claim in Part V, including in the anchoring argument.

**4. Derivation 1's corollary overstates the loss.**
- *Claim attacked:* "The word 'kind' is therefore eliminable from the definition of an account, and its elimination loses no case."
- *Why it fails:* The pole-and-shadow pair under the identification contract (Case 2 above) is a lost case: two organizations with opposite dependence direction, both satisfying all five conjuncts of (E) at the same grain, one of which is not an explanation on the mechanist's view. The corollary is true only for cases whose distinguishing change lies in C.
- *Seriousness:* Serious.
- *Fix:* Restrict to "loses no case whose distinction is realized by a change in C," and address the residual case explicitly.

**5. (K) is ill-typed for the use Derivations 1 and 2 make of it.**
- *Claim attacked:* Derivation 1's use of sig_C(λ(k)), and Derivation 2's "their components are pairwise of one kind on C."
- *Why it fails:* (K) defines signatures for components j ∈ J *of one organization* on *one contract*. λ(k) is a *subnetwork*, not a component, so L_{λ(k)} is undefined; Derivation 1 silently replaces it with proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}, a different definition. And comparing components of E and E′ requires a port translation between them and between τ[C] and τ′[C], none of which is supplied — "pairwise of one kind" across two organizations is undefined as stated. This matters concretely: the forward and reversed pole-shadow organizations have different τ[C], so Derivation 2 cannot be applied to them as written.
- *Seriousness:* Serious (formal).
- *Fix:* State a subnetwork-signature definition in Part II, and state the cross-organization comparison via a specified transport between E and E′.

**6. Derivation 8's equivariance either presupposes anchoring or licenses the pole/shadow swap.**
- *Claim attacked:* "Transporting all carriers, relations, transports, histories, and contracts along structure-preserving bijections preserves (E), (G), (P), (EK)."
- *Why it fails:* If λ is among the transported data, the result is trivial and says nothing about a swap that *changes* λ. If λ is not transported, the existence claims can be re-witnessed by a different λ′ — and then the bijection that maps the pole-height port to the shadow-length port preserves (E) while changing what the account is about. The proof ("Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data") does not distinguish these.
- *Seriousness:* Serious. This is the formal mechanism by which the reversed account is declared identical to the correct one.
- *Fix:* State whether λ is transported; if it is, show that the pole/shadow recoding is not among the permitted bijections because it is not structure-preserving with respect to D.

**7. Relocating how-possibly/how-actually to Part IX is a category error.**
- *Claim attacked:* "A true mechanism guessed for bad reasons satisfies (E); the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX)."
- *Why it fails:* The mechanist's distinction concerns the model–world correspondence of a single model, not the epistemic standing of its adoption. Two models adopted via identical critical episodes, with identical receipts, can differ in anchoring. Part IX's (K1)–(K3), histories, and receipts are all about criticism and justification events, and none of them inspects λ.
- *Seriousness:* Serious.
- *Fix:* Either add a correspondence condition on λ, or argue explicitly that how-possibly models explain, rather than deferring to a section that addresses a different question.

**8. Non-vacuity's "stated scope" does not catch protective scoping.**
- *Claim attacked:* "every physically admitted edit excluded from C is excluded by a stated scope, not silently," and Grievance 4's "it is caught by the requirement that the exclusion be stated, and then by any criticism that supplies the excluded change."
- *Why it fails:* A *stated* exclusion can still be protective; stating it does not make it legitimate. The offered remedy is critical, not semantic — so an account anchored to the wrong kind, adequate on a scope that excludes the separating change, counts as an account, and the objection is deferred to future criticism rather than entering Account.
- *Seriousness:* Minor-to-serious. It is the steelman's foothold, and the section under discussion does not address it.
- *Fix:* Either say why deferral is correct (adequacy really is index-relative, full stop), or add a condition linking the stated scope to the grain at which the mechanism's components are individuals.

**9. Part VII's direction derivation is imprecise about τ.**
- *Claim attacked:* "The reversed calculation H=L tan θ is not: intervening on H changes the target's L but not the calculation's H."
- *Why it fails:* In the reversed organization H is an output; no edit of E′ sets H, so τ(intervene-on-H) is undefined. The correct statement of failure is that no edit-translation exists on the production contract — a stronger and cleaner claim than the one given. Additionally, the reversed calculation's faithfulness under the identification contract requires θ fixed as a boundary condition (otherwise H is not identifiable from observed L at all, by (I2), since the fibre over an observed L contains states with different H); this prerequisite is not stated.
- *Seriousness:* Minor-to-serious. The direction result is load-bearing for the pole-shadow case.
- *Fix:* State the failure as τ-undefinedness, and state the θ-fixed requirement.

**10. Derivation 10's bullet-biting is stronger than the derivation supports.**
- *Claim attacked:* "A claim that 'component 1 is *really* thing 1' is a claim that some admitted change distinguishes them, and on this contract none does. That is not a defect of S₁; it is the correct report that identity, at this grain, is exhausted by trajectory."
- *Why it fails:* The swap edit *is* physically admitted and is in A; the two things are distinguished by a physically admitted change, just not within the prediction-relevant contract. The local conclusion ("on this contract none does") is fine; the general lesson ("identity, at this grain, is exhausted by trajectory") is a substantive metaphysical commitment presented as a corollary, and it is precisely what (AC) denies.
- *Seriousness:* Minor.
- *Fix:* State the local conclusion and mark the general claim as a commitment, not a derivation.

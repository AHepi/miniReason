## Part 2 — Findings

### F1 — Non-circular dependence is unsatisfiable for every single-commitment candidate, and 22 now asserts the opposite
**(1) Claim attacked.** Part V, non-circular dependence: "there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\), translated from some \((a,b)\in C\), that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)". And Part V, "What (E) excludes": "The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) for a target with several components satisfies (E): its single commitment is removable, so non-circular dependence has its witness."

**(2) Why it fails.** This is the second half of 21's finding 1.6, ruled Valid and serious, whose fix ("non-circularity's witness removes a nonempty sub-block, proper when |Γ| > 1") 21's §4 item 4 quietly dropped — and 22a marks the row "Done". When \(|\Gamma|=1\), the only nonempty sub-block is \(\Gamma\) itself, which is not proper. So no witness exists. The sentence "its single commitment is removable, so non-circular dependence has its witness" is refuted three lines away by "Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing." The contradiction is now *internal to Part V* (in 20 it was merely between Part V and an example). Any genuine single-mechanism account of a one-component target, and any single-commitment candidate, fails (E) outright.

**(3) Serious.** It makes (E) unsatisfiable for a class of candidates the document explicitly offers as paradigm cases, and it is the mechanism by which an ordered repair escaped the ledger.

**(4) Fix.** Replace "nonempty proper sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\)" with "nonempty sub-block \(B\subseteq\Gamma\), required proper when \(|\Gamma|>1\)". Delete the gloss "its single commitment is removable, so non-circular dependence has its witness" and state instead that for \(|\Gamma|=1\) the witness is the removal of that commitment itself.

### F4 — "Fidelity is well-defined there" on K is not earned
**(1) Claim attacked.** Part III, "Contracts": "\(K\) is a declared set of counterfactual edits — pairs with \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\) — each declared together with the component relations \(L_j(a,b)\) it imposes, which is what its 'stated meaning' is; so \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) is defined on \(K\) by (O) and **fidelity is well-defined there**."

**(2) Why it fails.** 21's §3 flagged that fidelity over K was undefined; item 14 supplied only the missing half. (F1), (F2) and (A) are evaluated "for every \((a,b)\in C\)", and \(C\supseteq K\). But (F1) evaluates \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\), and Part IV defines "\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits" — a function on \(A_D\). Whether \(K\)'s edits lie in \(A_D\) is undetermined by the text, and both readings break:
- If \(K\)'s edits are outside \(A_D\) — which Part VII's wording supports ("the rival's structure is not a possibility at all — the introducing edit is **not admitted** — the edit belongs to \(K\)") — then \(\tau(a)\) is undefined on \(K\), and (F1)/(F2)/(A) are not well-defined there. The claim is false.
- If \(K\subseteq (A\times B)\setminus C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), then \(\tau\) is defined, but "the introducing edit is not admitted" must then be read as "the pair is not admitted", which Part VII does not say.

Either way the declared relations make \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) defined on \(K\) while leaving the *transport side* of fidelity untouched.

**(3) Serious.** The eliminative-explanation treatment — attack (B)(iii), "scoped by design" — rests on fidelity being evaluable on \(K\).

**(4) Fix.** State explicitly whether \(K\subseteq A_D\times B\); if the counterfactual edits are not in \(A_D\), extend \(\tau\), \(\sigma\) and \(\mathcal Q\) to \(K\) as declared data (each counterfactual pair declares not only the relations it imposes on \(D\) but the edit it translates to on \(E\)), or restrict (F1)/(F2)/(A) to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S\) and say so.

### F5 — The objectivity condition is ill-typed: it conjoins a two-place predicate with a four-place one
**(1) Claim attacked.** Part V, "Objectivity: the second index": "An **objectivity claim** for \(\mathcal E\) is the conjunction of account-hood at \(C\) and a claim about \(C\) against the module in force: that \(C\) is **not protective** for \(\mathcal E\)." With "\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\iff\exists t,\Gamma\ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\)" and "protective" defined via "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\neq\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)".

**(2) Why it fails.** Account-hood at \(C\) is existential over \(t,\Gamma\); non-protection is a condition on a *specific* \(t\)'s \(\tau\) and \(\sigma\). The conjunction is therefore not a well-formed predicate of \(\mathcal E\) unless the transport is named. Under the natural existential reading, a candidate deployed through a protective transport counts as objective so long as *some* other faithful, non-protective transport exists — which is exactly the gerrymander the condition exists to catch. Under a universal reading, the condition is far stronger than the text advertises. The anchored grade was careful to become "a predicate on the same four data, with \(t\) and \(\Gamma\) named"; the objectivity condition, which replaces adequacy and carries Grievance 4 and the "protective contract" attack of Part XV, did not.

**(3) Serious.** The condition is the document's entire answer to "there is no objectivity", and its quantifier structure is ambiguous in the direction that defeats it.

**(4) Fix.** State the objectivity claim as a four-place predicate \(\operatorname{Objective}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\), or specify that non-protection must hold for every \(t,\Gamma\) witnessing \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\).

### F7 — The "selected representation is representation of less than was claimed" consequence does not follow from (R)
**(1) Claim attacked.** Part IV, "Expectation, surprise, violation": "since a selected transport is guaranteed faithful only on \(H\), what it represents by (R) is the content on the largest contract it is in fact faithful on, which may be smaller than the contract declared for the content: a selected representation is representation of less than was claimed."

**(2) Why it fails.** (R) is indexed to the content's contract: \(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\exists t[\ldots\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land\ldots]\). If \(t\) is faithful only on \(H\subsetneq C_c\), then (R) *fails* for \(c\); it does not silently succeed for a smaller content. The consequence is true only under a re-identification of the content as "the same organization with the largest faithful sub-contract", which (R) does not provide. Note also that "the largest contract it is in fact faithful on" is well-defined (the conjuncts of fidelity are pointwise in \((a,b)\)), so the repair is available — it is just not stated.

**(3) Minor** (charitable reading available) — but it is one of the two consequences 21 explicitly ordered kept, and as written it is an assertion, not a derivation.

**(4) Fix.** Add: "where \(t\) is faithful on \(H\subsetneq C_c\), (R) holds for the content \(c'\) obtained by restricting \(c\)'s contract to the largest sub-contract on which \(t\) is faithful, and fails for \(c\); that is the sense in which less is represented than was claimed."

### F14 — \(\mathcal Q_E\)'s pullback makes (A) fail for faithful non-injective transports
**(1) Claim attacked.** Part IV, "Transports": "\(\mathcal Q_E(E,\tau(a),\sigma(b)):=\mathcal Q\) evaluated on \(\pi^{-1}[\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))]\) and the components \(\lambda\) anchors", together with (F2) "\(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\)" and (A) "\(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)".

**(2) Why it fails.** (F2) gives \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D]=\operatorname{Sol}_E\), hence \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\subseteq\pi^{-1}[\operatorname{Sol}_E\)], with strict containment whenever \(\pi\) is non-injective on solutions. A natural \(\mathcal Q\) — "the set of values taken by port \(v\) across solutions" — evaluated on the superset returns \(\pi_v^{-1}[\text{answer}]\), not the answer. So (A) can fail for a transport that satisfies (F1) and (F2), unless \(\pi\) is injective on solution sets, which is nowhere required.

**(3) Minor** ((A) is a separate conjunct, so nothing is silently unsound — but the definition of \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) is the wrong one for the purpose).

**(4) Fix.** Define \(\mathcal Q_E\) on \(E\)'s own ports via the port translation that \(\lambda\) carries, or add "and \(\pi\) injective on \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\)" to the fidelity conjuncts where the pullback is used.

# Part XVI — Findings

## Derivation 1 — Kind preservation under fidelity

**1.1**  
**Claim attacked:** “By the subnetwork signature of Part II, \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\), the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\).”  
**Why it fails:** Part II defines a subnetwork signature using a *declared* visible-port set: “its **visible ports** are a declared subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) of its ports.” The transport definition in Part IV says only that \(\lambda\) assigns a subnetwork “with a port translation.” It never says the anchor’s declared visible ports are exactly the ports translated from \(V_k\). (F1) equates \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)]\) with \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\), but if \(\lambda(k)\) has extra visible ports, its subnetwork signature is a different set. The proof silently identifies \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\) with \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_{\lambda(k)}^{\mathrm{vis}}}\).  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Require the transport to specify \(V_{\lambda(k)}^{\mathrm{vis}}\) as the port-translated \(V_k\), or redefine the anchor signature explicitly by projection to \(V_k\).

**1.2**  
**Claim attacked:** “every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has … the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.”  
**Why it fails:** Part II defines forward kind only for *two components* of an organization: “Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind** on \((\ell,C)\) when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide…” A subnetwork is not a component, and the comparison here crosses two organizations \(D\) and \(E\). The derivation proves at most a set-of-triples equality; it does not establish the forward-kind relation as defined, because there is no defined bijection of footprints between a component of \(E\) and an anchor subnetwork of \(D\). The later sentence “a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork” is therefore not licensed by Part II.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Define forward kind for component–anchor pairs or for subnetworks, with an explicit port bijection and shared contract; otherwise weaken the consequence to signature equality only.

**1.3**  
**Claim attacked:** “up to the port translation.”  
**Why it fails:** The transport definition does not require the port translation to be injective or bijective onto \(V_k\). If it is not, the expression \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) is not well-defined as a projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}\) onto \(V_k\).  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** State that the port translation is a bijection between \(V_k\) and the visible ports of \(\lambda(k)\).

**1.4**  
**Claim attacked:** “let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\).”  
**Why it fails:** The injectivity hypothesis is sufficient for the proof’s bijection step, but it is stronger than the stated set-equality may require. If signatures are treated as sets of triples, (F1) already equates the third coordinates pointwise, and \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)\) equals the image of \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))\) even when \((\tau,\sigma)\) is not injective—provided the definition of \(\tau[C]\) is the image set. The derivation presents injectivity as load-bearing without showing it is necessary. Also, \(\tau[C]\) need not itself be a contract in the sense of Part III.  
**Severity:** Minor.  
**Fix:** Either prove the claim needs injectivity, or drop it and state the set-valued reading of signatures; note that \(\tau[C]\) is only a set of edit–boundary pairs, not necessarily a contract.

## Derivation 2 — Account-equivalence

**2.1**  
**Claim attacked:** “there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide.”  
**Why it fails:** A bijection between *components* does not supply a bijection between the port sets \(V_k\) of \(E\) and \(V_{k'}\) of \(E'\). The anchored relations are subsets of products over those port sets. To say they “coincide” one needs a compatible port translation. The definition never requires one.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Require a bijection of port sets compatible with the component bijection and with \(\lambda,\lambda'\), and define coincidence relative to that bijection.

**2.2**  
**Claim attacked:** “and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide.”  
**Why it fails:** The answer profiles \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) and \(\operatorname{Ans}_{E'}\) are defined on \(X_E\) and \(X_{E'}\). “Pulled back” along what map? The component bijection may not cover answer ports or the codomain \(Y_p\). The proof says “(A) gives the profiles,” but (A) only gives
\[
\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\operatorname{Ans}_{E'}(\tau'(a),\sigma'(b)).
\]
It does not relate \(\operatorname{Ans}_E\) to \(\operatorname{Ans}_{E'}\) directly unless one pulls both back to \(C\) via the translations. That must be stated.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Define the pulled-back profiles as functions on \(C\) via \((\tau,\sigma)\) and \((\tau',\sigma')\), or require the component bijection to extend to a bijection of state spaces and answer ports.

**2.3**  
**Claim attacked:** “have the same anchor map up to that bijection.”  
**Why it fails:** The proof says “the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence,” but if the anchor map is the same only up to a component bijection, the projections are onto different port sets. Without port compatibility (2.1), the anchored relations need not coincide.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Same as 2.1: require port compatibility between corresponding anchors.

**2.4**  
**Claim attacked:** “If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\)…”  
**Why it fails:** (F2) is not used anywhere in the proof. This is not a contradiction, but the hypothesis includes an unused condition.  
**Severity:** Minor.  
**Fix:** Remove (F2) from the hypothesis or explain its role.

## Derivation 3 — Selection guarantees nothing off its history

**3.1**  
**Claim attacked:** “Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\). The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives.”  
**Why it fails:** The selection definition in Part IV requires much more than fidelity on \(H\): population size \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\), a variation operator actually applied, a non-triviality condition, a survival condition that eliminated at least one member, and “the environment’s filtering causally responsible for \(t\)’s presence and not merely consistent with it.” The proof shows only that the altered pair satisfies fidelity on \(H\). It does not show it satisfies the full \(\operatorname{Sel}\) predicate, nor that the environment’s filtering was causally responsible for its presence. Therefore the conclusion about “selection data” not determining the survivor does not follow.  
**Severity:** Serious/fatal for the stated consequence.  
**Fix:** Either define a weaker “survives on \(H\)” relation and state the consequence for that, or add hypotheses that the altered pairs also satisfy all \(\operatorname{Sel}\) clauses—especially causal responsibility—or state that selection is exactly survival-on-\(H\) for this derivation.

**3.2**  
**Claim attacked:** “in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)).”  
**Why it fails:** “Admissible class” is not defined elsewhere in the document. The parenthetical defines it circularly via \(\mathcal T\). If it is meant to be the physically admissible relations, that is \(A_\Theta\) or the module’s relations, not “realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\).” The proof’s conclusion depends on what \(L'\) is admissible.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Define the admissible class independently—e.g., relations permitted by the physical module or by the organization schema—and state the closure hypothesis relative to that class.

**3.3**  
**Claim attacked:** “for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) … \(\mathcal T\) contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) everywhere except that \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L'\).”  
**Why it fails:** The derivation does not assume \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on \(C\). If \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) for \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) coincides with \((\tau(a'),\sigma(b'))\) for some \((a',b')\in H\), then altering \(L_j\) at that translated pair alters the relation at a pair in \(H\). The altered pair then does not agree with \((E,t)\) on \(H\) and may not survive. The proof’s claim “agrees on \(H\)” fails without injectivity or disjointness of the images.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Add the hypothesis that \((\tau,\sigma)\) is injective on \(C\), or that \((\tau,\sigma)[C\setminus H]\cap(\tau,\sigma)[H]=\varnothing\).

**3.4**  
**Claim attacked:** “Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\): selection provides no guarantee of fidelity where it did not test.”  
**Why it fails:** Even if there are multiple members satisfying fidelity on \(H\), the full selection data include causal responsibility and non-triviality. The proof does not show the alternatives satisfy those, so it does not show the selection data fail to determine the survivor. It shows only that “fidelity-on-\(H\) survival” does not determine off-\(H\) behavior.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Weaken the consequence to “survival-on-\(H\) does not determine…” or strengthen the closure hypothesis to include full \(\operatorname{Sel}\) satisfaction for the alternatives.

**3.5**  
**Claim attacked:** “It does not follow that the survivor is unfaithful there — it may be faithful on all of \(C\) — and nothing here says the survivor will fail.”  
**Why it fails:** This part is careful, but it sits uneasily with the title “Selection guarantees nothing off its history.” If the actual survivor \(t\) may be faithful on all of \(C\), then the selection history plus the fact that \(t\) survived may still determine \(t\)’s off-\(H\) behavior by its parametrisation. The derivation is silent on that case, as it admits. The title overstates what is proved.  
**Severity:** Minor.  
**Fix:** Retitle or state explicitly that the result concerns the insufficiency of the tested history alone, not the selected transport’s actual off-\(H\) behavior.

## Derivation 5 — Question-finding is representable

**5.1**  
**Claim attacked:** “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).”  
**Why it fails:** A coherently posed question is a tuple \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S,K\) disjoint. The constructed \(D_p\) has ports only for \((a,b)\in A\times B\), \(u_{\mathcal Q}\), \(u_S\), \(u_K\). It lacks ports or components for \(O_p\) (obligations) and \(\rho_p\) (provenance). Its solutions cannot therefore be the full questions.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Add ports/components for \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\), or restrict the claim to the contract/query/scope/counterfactual fragment and state that provenance is external.

**5.2**  
**Claim attacked:** “Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\).”  
**Why it fails:** The construction does not state any component relation enforcing \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\) or that \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\). Without such relations, solutions may violate coherence.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Specify the \(L_j\) conditions that enforce these clauses.

**5.3**  
**Claim attacked:** “if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads ‘in’ then so do its factors where the composition is defined.”  
**Why it fails:** Part III does not require a question’s contract \(C\) to be closed under factors of compositions. This extra closure condition makes the solution set a proper subset of coherently posed questions. So “exactly” is false.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Remove the closure condition or prove it follows from coherence—it does not.

**5.4**  
**Claim attacked:** “one port \(u_S\) with domain the stated scopes and one \(u_K\) with domain the declared counterfactual sets with meanings.”  
**Why it fails:** The contract is \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), which requires \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), the admission relation. \(D_p\) has no port for \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and no component that fixes it. So solutions cannot determine \(C\) for different admission relations; they depend on an unrepresented background.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Include \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) (or the module) as a boundary/port or state explicitly that it is fixed externally.

**5.5**  
**Claim attacked:** “It is a \(D_p\) in the sense of (O).”  
**Why it fails:** (O) requires full data \((V,(X_v),J,B,A,L)\) with \(L_j(a,b)\) for every component, edit, and boundary. The construction gives ports and informal “closure conditions” but does not specify the \(L_j\) relations. Thus it is not an organization in the sense of (O).  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Write out the \(L_j\) explicitly.

**5.6**  
**Claim attacked:** “provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization.”  
**Why it fails:** \(\rho_p\) is part of the question tuple and “attaches to the whole tuple \(p\)” (Part III). If \(D_p\) does not encode \(\rho_p\), then content identity via \(D_p\) cannot distinguish declared, selected, and constructed questions.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Include \(\rho_p\) as a historical kind or as a port/component, or revise Part III’s claim that provenance attaches to the whole tuple.

**5.7**  
**Claim attacked:** “A question \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) determines an organization \(D_p\) … which can be the content \(c\) in (G).”  
**Why it fails:** The question’s contract is \(C\), but \(D_p\)’s admitted edits are toggles/alterations of the question. The content \(c\) in (G) would be \(D_p\) with its own contract-relative commitments, not \(p\) with contract \(C\). The derivation conflates the question with the organization representing it.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Distinguish \(p\) from its representation \(D_p\) and specify the contract of \(D_p\).

## Derivation 8 — Equivariance under genuine recoding

**8.1**  
**Claim attacked:** “Let \(\phi\) be a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports …, histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module’s admission \((A_\Theta,\operatorname{Inst},\prec)\), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and \(\mathcal N\). Transporting all data along \(\phi\) preserves (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK).”  
**Why it fails:** The list of preserved data is incomplete. (E) depends on the edit composition and identity (F2: \(\tau(1)=1\), \(\tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\)), the query \(\mathcal Q\) (A), the grain \(\ell\) (anchoring), boundary conditions, state values ((P) uses \(o(\xi)\)), active routes and contrasts ((G)), result sets and episode boundaries ((EK)). \(\phi\) is not stated to preserve these. **Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Define \(\phi\) as an isomorphism of the entire structure including \(A\)’s composition, \(\mathcal Q\), \(\ell\), states, active-route predicates, \(\operatorname{Result}\), etc., and verify each condition.

**8.2**  
**Claim attacked:** “because \(\lambda\) is among the transported data, a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted \(\phi\), since it does not preserve \(D\)’s structure.”

The hypothesis does not require \(\phi\) to fix anchors pointwise.  
**Severity:** Serious.  
**Fix:** Explicitly require \(\phi\) to commute with \(\lambda\): \(\phi(\lambda(k))=\lambda(\phi(k))\), or state that anchor permutations are allowed when they are symmetries of \(D\).

**8.3**  
**Claim attacked:** “The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, lost event identities, or maps that alter admission.”  
**Why it fails:** \(\phi\) is not defined on boundaries or event identities in the hypothesis, so the claim that the result “does not apply” to them is either vacuous or indicates the hypothesis is incomplete.  
**Severity:** Minor.  
**Fix:** Include boundaries and histories in \(\phi\)’s domain or state the restriction precisely.

# Part XVI — Findings

## Derivation 1 — Kind preservation under fidelity

**Finding 1.**
**(1) Claim attacked:** “It shows that fidelity on a contract preserves signature on that contract: a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork.”
**(2) Why it fails:** The proof only establishes signature equality on \(\tau[C]\), not on \(C\). Forward kind is defined in Part II for signatures evaluated on the same contract \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\). Unless \(\tau[C]=C\), the component in \(E\) and the anchor in \(D\) are evaluated on different contracts. The claim in the derivation itself correctly says “on \(\tau[C]\)”; the follow-up remark drops the translation and says “on \(C\).”
**(3) Seriousness:** serious.
**(4) Fix:** State the comparison as a cross-organization signature correspondence under \(\tau\), or explicitly define forward kind across organizations via the transport translation. Do not say “on \(C\)” unless \(\tau[C]=C\).

**Finding 2.**
**(1) Claim attacked:** “the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\).”
**(2) Why it fails:** Part II defines the subnetwork signature using a declared visible subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\). Part IV only says \(\lambda\) assigns a subnetwork “with a port translation.” It does not state that the visible ports of the anchor are exactly the translated footprint \(V_k\). If the anchor has additional visible ports, (F1) only equates the projection onto \(V_k\), and the full subnetwork signature may not equal the component signature.
**(3) Seriousness:** serious.

**Finding 4.**

## Derivation 2 — Account-equivalence

**Finding 5.**
**(1) Claim attacked:** “Two candidates … are account-equivalent on \(C\) when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide…”

## Derivation 1 — Kind preservation under fidelity

**Finding 1.1. The hypothesis is insufficient for the paper's own conclusion.**

The construction supplies ports for \(A\times B\), \(\mathcal Q\), \(S\), \(K\); it never supplies a port or component for the obligations \(O_p\) nor for the provenance \(\rho_p\). The claimed attachment of provenance to the organization is therefore unsupported.
(3) Serious.
(4) Add ports for \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\), or restrict the claim to the \((D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q)\) core and say so.

**Finding 5.3. “Admissible queries on \(D\)” is an undefined term.**
(1) “one port \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) with domain the set of queries admissible on \(D\)”.
(2) The document defines admissibility for grains (Part II), not for queries. This is a new undefined parameter.
(3) Serious.
(4) Define “admissible query” or replace with “the class of queries \(\mathcal Q\) considered for \(D\)”.

**Finding 5.4. The consequence is a near-tautology.**
(1) “Hence an episode whose originative contribution is a new question satisfies (G) when the other conjuncts and the merit condition hold.”
(2) (G) is \(\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\). If “the other conjuncts” means Attempt, New, Build, then the sentence states “\(G\) holds when \(G\) holds.” The non-trivial content is supposed to be the validity of \(D_p\) as the content \(c\), and that is precisely what Findings 5.1–5.3 undercut.
(3) Serious.
(4) State the specific condition (G) requires that the construction supplies, and state the conditions it does not.

**Finding 5.5. “Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed” is not a boundary condition in the sense of (O).**
(1) “Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed.”
(2) In (O), a boundary \(b\) is an element of the set \(B\) of boundary conditions. \(D\) is not an element of \(B\). The construction imports a labelled constant into the place of a boundary parameter.
(3) Minor.
(4) State that \(b_0\in B_{D_p}\) and that \(D\) is held fixed via a declared admissible-edit restriction, not as a boundary element.

## Derivation 6 — Two primitive packages, and the rest declared or derived

**Finding 6.1. The Rep/Con cycle is not shown to be broken; it depends on an unexamined phrase in Build.**
(1) “the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness.”
(2) Part IV’s (R) requires \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\), and \(\operatorname{Con}\) requires “an episode … whose construction witness prepares \(t\)”. \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when “an owned subhistory … prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates *for explanatory use of c*…” The phrase “explanatory use of \(c\)” is not defined anywhere. The only candidate in the document for “use of \(c\)” is \(\operatorname{Deploy}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)\), and \(\operatorname{Deploy}\) **requires (R)** (“\(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R)”). If “explanatory use” instantiates \(\operatorname{Deploy}\), then
\[
(R)\Rightarrow \operatorname{Con}\Rightarrow \operatorname{Build}\Rightarrow \operatorname{Deploy}\Rightarrow (R)
\]
is a cycle. The defence only addresses the *target* of construction (instantiated vs. represented); it does not address the *use* the construction is for.
(3) Serious — load-bearing for the claim that no residual predicate is left undefined.
(4) Define “explanatory use of \(c\)” independently of \(\operatorname{Deploy}\), or restrict Build so its “use” clause is Deploy-free and say so.

**Finding 6.2. The proof is a survey, not a proof.**
(1) “Each definition names its inputs at first use; the order of Part XIV is followed from each predicate to its base.”
(2) This is an assertion about the manuscript, not a derivation. Even taking the manuscript as read, multiple predicates rely on terms not listed in Part XIV’s declared parameters or the two primitive packages: “explanatory use of \(c\)” (Build), “recognized difficulty,” “conjectural objection,” “content-sensitive response” (Episodes), “admissible queries on \(D\)” (Derivation 5), “full admitted contract” vs. “not only the ones the contract selected” (Part V), “the set of queries admissible on \(D\)” (Derivation 5). Part XIV’s claim “No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters” therefore rests on these terms being treated as either defined or primitive, which the derivation does not establish.
(3) Serious.
(4) Either define each named term locally, or list it among the primitives, and give the derivation a checkable proof obligation: for each undefined term encountered, cite its definition.

**Finding 6.3. “The only cycle that could arise” is a claim the derivation does not support.**
(1) “the only cycle that could arise …”
(2) The dependence order in Part XIV includes “\((R)\) depends on (F1)–(F2), Inst and physical provenance.” \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) refers to “realized transports”; is “realized transport” a transport in the sense of §“Transports”? If so, it is a structure that (R) itself is defined over, and the stratification claim that instances of \(\mathcal T\) are carriers rather than contents is not stated. Similarly, \(\operatorname{Con}\)’s “the organization \(t\) targets” is on the \(D\)-side; the use of “organization” there versus “content” elsewhere is not separated in the derivation.
(3) Minor.
(4) State explicitly that \(\operatorname{Sel}\) quantifies over carriers/transports as data, not over contents, and that \(\operatorname{Con}\)'s “organization” is an instance, not a content.

## Derivation 7 — The frozen assessment and the moving question are consistent

**Finding 7.1. The consequence attributes more to the adequacy condition than it does.**
(1) “Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change; the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded.”
(2) The adequacy condition reads “\(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse.” It is defined relative to the *stated* \(C\). An unrecorded index change is not a violation of adequacy; it is a violation of the required shape of \(C\), \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\) with reasons for exclusions — i.e., of NonVacuous, and of the standing requirement in Part III that “the record must show the boundary.” The adequacy condition catches a *protective* scope at the *stated* contract; it does not detect that the index moved or that the move was unrecorded. The disjunctive condition here — “whether or not it is recorded” — conflates two distinct failure modes.
(3) Serious.
(4) Separate the two: an unrecorded change violates the stated-form condition; a protective scope violates Adequate. Do not attribute both to Adequate.

## Derivation 8 — Equivariance under genuine recoding

**Finding 8.1. The family \(\phi\) is under-listed for the claim it is supposed to support.**
(1) “a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps \(\lambda\)), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module’s admission (\(A_\Theta\), \(\operatorname{Inst}\), \(\prec\)), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and \(\mathcal N\).”
(2) (E) and anchoring refer additionally to: the grain \(\ell\) (admissibility and world-kind), the boundary \(b_0\), the query \(\mathcal Q\) and its codomain \(Y_p\), the answer profile \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), and the port/value-domain structure of \(D\). None of these is in the listed domain of \(\phi\). Also (F2)’s \(\tau(1)=1\) and \(\tau(a_2 a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\) require \(\phi\) to preserve the monoid operation on \(A\), not just \(A_\Theta\) as a set. Without these, the “carried along” step does not apply.
(3) Serious.
(4) List everything \(\phi\) acts on and preserves, including grain, boundary \(b_0\), \(\mathcal Q\), profiles, port and value-domain structure, and the monoid structure of \(A\).

**Finding 8.2. The proof is essentially a tautology, and therefore does not establish what is announced.**
(1) “Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data, and each of its inputs is carried along.”
(2) If \(\phi\) preserves all inputs, preservation is immediate for any structure-only predicate. The content of the derivation is entirely in the restriction “\(\phi\) preserves \(A_\Theta,\operatorname{Inst},\prec,\) scopes, counterfactual meanings, \(\mathcal N\).” Once that restriction is stated, the equivariance claim is trivial. The derivation does not state a non-trivial consequence (e.g., that some natural but non-structure-preserving map is excluded — see 8.3 below).
(3) Serious — the derivation is a disguised definition.
(4) Either restate as a definition of the permitted recodings, or state a non-trivial consequence (e.g., a class of maps that are *not* permitted under the semantics).

**Finding 8.3. The pole-height/shadow-length exclusion is not argued from the stated preservation conditions.**
(1) “a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted \(\phi\), since it does not preserve \(D\)'s structure.”
(2) The derivation does not identify the feature of \(D\)'s structure that blocks the swap. If the two ports happen to be symmetric in the relevant structural respects (both appear in admitted edits, both with compatible \(X_v\), etc.), the swap would be a permitted \(\phi\); the proof gives no structural feature that excludes it. In \(H\cot\theta\), the asymmetry is that only \(H,\theta\) are input-edits and \(L\) is derived — but this must be made an argument, not asserted under “does not preserve \(D\)'s structure.”
(3) Minor.
(4) State the specific structural feature (input-status, distinguished domain, absence of a corresponding admitted edit) that blocks the recode.

**Finding 8.4. Part X cites Derivation 8 as stronger than it is.**
(1) Part X: “the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents” (defining \(\equiv_\ell\)).
(2) Derivation 8 does not *define* a class of “structure-preserving bijections”; it states an invariance result for a family \(\phi\) with a long list of preserved structures. The citation treats the derivation as a definitional source for a content-equivalence that does not in fact exist there. The list of things \(\phi\) preserves (physical module, \(\mathcal N\), scopes, counterfactual meanings) is far stronger than a recoding of contents alone.
(3) Serious.
(4) Give an explicit definition of \(\equiv_\ell\) over contents, and only then cite Derivation 8 to connect it to (E)/anchoring.

## Derivation 10 — A three-layer episode, as an illustrative scenario

**Finding 10.1. Derivation 1 is again cited as producing a forward-kind conclusion it does not produce.**
(1) “then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things”.
(2) As in Finding 1.1–1.5, Derivation 1 requires the injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) and does not by itself deliver the forward-kind statement (only signature coincidence). The local phrasing — “which would have to be checked by writing out \(P\), \(S_1\) and the relations, and is not checked here” — quietly covers the (F1)/(F2) check but leaves the injectivity check entirely unmentioned.
(3) Moderately serious as a pattern: Derivation 1 is repeatedly invoked as if it delivered forward-kind on its own.
(4) State injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) as a hypothesis of the scenario; or restrict the claim to signature coincidence on the contract.

**Finding 10.2. Under the swap, the scenario treats the forward-kind conclusion as if it settled “real identity”, and then denies that (correctly) without noting the deployment it just made.**
(1) “Under (K), with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap.”
(2) This is a direct unpacking of Part II’s definition, fine in itself, but the scenario is presented as illustrating the whole framework and in fact only exercises Part II. The exercise is heavier in appearance than in content.
(3) Cosmetic.
(4) State that the scenario exercises (K) and (F1)/(F2) only.

**Finding 10.3. The scenario is announced as “not a consistency witness,” yet is the only place where Derivation 1’s (F1)/(F2) check is genuinely needed and it is not performed.**
(1) “If (F1) and (F2) hold for \(t_1\) on the extended contract — which would have to be checked by writing out \(P\), \(S_1\) and the relations, and is not checked here”.

The illustration does not stand on its own in a way that would let a reader test Part V on a worked example.
(3) Minor.
(4) Either add a numerically/topologically trivial variant that is actually checked, or note explicitly that the scenario is an illustration only of the *form* of the definitions, not of their satisfaction.

### Cross-cutting note (not a finding in itself): the recurring pattern

Derivation 1 is used in Part V Condition 4, in Derivation 10, and in the “What this does and does not show” paragraph, with three different strengthenings each time — from “signature correspondence” to “forward kind on \(C\)” to “of one forward kind with its anchor subnetwork at \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)” — and without the injectivity hypothesis each time. This pattern is the strongest single criticism available against Part XVI: a derivation whose own proof is a single pointwise equation is repeatedly cited as if it secured a kind-theoretic conclusion it does not. Everything in Part V that centres on “world-kind” or “the anchored grade’s fourth condition” depends on this strengthening.

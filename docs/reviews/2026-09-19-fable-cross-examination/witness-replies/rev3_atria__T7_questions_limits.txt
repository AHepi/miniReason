# Referee findings

## §1 Do the remaining (G) filters do merit-work by another name?

**1. "New" collapses for question-contents, so question-finding is not representable as originative at all.**
(1) Claim attacked — Derivation 5, Consequence: *"Finding a question is representable as a creative act on the same footing as answering one, with no condition on whether it was worth finding."* Plus gap (0): *"for a particular question to be a content that New, Build and Deploy apply to, it must be an organization in its own right — \(D_p\) with its ports fixed at that valuation."*
(2) Why it fails — \(\equiv_\ell\) (Part X) is defined by *"a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries"*; Derivation 8 confirms arbitrary bijections on **domains** are permitted provided \(\operatorname{Sol}\) is carried to \(\operatorname{Sol}\). Take two questions on the same target, i.e. \(D_p@v\) and \(D_p@v'\). The identity on ports, the identity on components/boundaries, and any permutation of the domain of \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) (or of \(u_S\), \(u_K\)) sending \(v(u)\) to \(v'(u)\) maps the fixing component \(\{u=v(u)\}\) onto \(\{u=v'(u)\}\) and preserves the closure components, the admitted edits, and the singleton solution sets. So \(D_p@v\equiv_\ell D_p@v'\) for **any** two valuations. Hence by (N), once the repertoire contains one question on target \(D\), \(\operatorname{New}\) fails for every later question on \(D\). At most one question per target can ever be originative.
(3) Serious — this kills the question-finding result outright, and none of gaps (0)–(iv) notices; gap (0)'s stated repair is what triggers it.
(4) Fix — restrict \(\equiv_\ell\) on question-contents to bijections that fix the value domains pointwise (or fix the target's ports), and prove the restriction doesn't break Derivation 8; or define question-content identity by something finer than the schema-plus-valuation.

**2. The "no merit condition" concession is inconsistent with (N).**
(1) Claim attacked — Part 0, attack (E): *"the semantics has no condition separating a found question from a re-parameterisation, so as written a random new contract counts"*; Part X: *"a random new contract meeting those four counts"*; Part XV: *"The semantics has none (Part X)."*
(2) Why it fails — By (N) + \(\equiv_\ell\), a re-parameterisation that is a content-preserving recoding of a repertoire item **fails New**. So a condition separating a found question from (some) re-parameterisations already exists. Either the concession is false, or \(\equiv_\ell\) must be read more restrictively than written — the document never says which.
(3) Minor-to-serious (it is the hinge of the whole attack-(E) dialectic).
(4) Fix — state exactly which re-parameterisations (N) excludes and which it does not, and re-draw the concession accordingly.

**3. Closing gap (ii) the natural way reintroduces merit-work.**
(1) Claim attacked — gap (ii): *"\(b_0\), \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\) are not ports of \(D_p\) as constructed; adding \(b_0\) as a port … and \(O_p\) as a port … is straightforward and not done."*
(2) Why it fails — As constructed, two questions differing only in which obligations they address are the **same content**, so a genuinely valuable re-framing (new obligation set) is invisible to New and is excluded, while an arbitrary structural toggle is admitted. If you add \(O_p\) as a port, New becomes sensitive to obligation-change — which is precisely a merit condition. So the "empty place" is empty only because of the gap; the obvious repair fills it with merit-work by another name.
(3) Serious — the dilemma is structural, not a drafting oversight.
(4) Fix — either accept that (G) carries a declared-relevance condition and delete the "no merit function" claim, or exhibit a closure of gap (ii) that keeps \(O_p\) out of content identity without collapsing New (Finding 1).

**4. The "failing obligation" filter is a declared-relevance surrogate, and the four-filter list understates (G).**
(1) Claim attacked — Part X: *"that it is coherently posed, that some obligation of it fails at the state before \(e\) (Attempt), that it is new to the repertoire, and that it was built."*
(2) Why it fails — \(O_p\) is declared data (Part XIV), so the failing-obligation conjunct is satisfiable by declaring any failing obligation; it does no objective merit-work. And Attempt also requires prior instantiation of the question-organization and actual use on an active route, so the list of four is not "only this".
(3) Minor.
(4) Fix — write "a *declared* failing obligation", and list Attempt's further conjuncts.

## §2 Are the five gaps in Derivation 5 the right gaps?

**5. Gap (i) conflates a non-gap with three missing conditions.**
(1) Claim attacked — gap (i): *"Coherence (\(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\); \(\mathcal Q\) defined on \(C\)) is a global condition on \(D\) and is not a local component of \(D_p\)."*
(2) Why it fails — \(D\) and \(b_0\) are the **boundary** of \(D_p\), held fixed; so \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\) is constant across all solutions of \(D_p\) and cannot vary between candidate questions at all — it is not a missing local component, it is a schema-level constant. The genuinely varying coherence conditions are \(\mathcal Q\) defined on \(C\), \(S\subseteq C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and \(K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing\), and none of those is encoded either.
(3) Minor, but it misdirects the repair.
(4) Fix — split the gap: drop the \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) clause, and list the three varying conditions separately.

**6. A missing gap: the scope/counterfactual consistency condition is asserted, not written.**
(1) Claim attacked — Derivation 5, Construction: *"\(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III."*
(2) Why it fails — Part III requires \(C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\) exactly. That is a **three-way global consistency condition** among the "in" ports, \(u_S\), and \(u_K\); it is not a local component relation, and the phrase "in the form of Part III" is doing unexamined work. This belongs in the gap list.
(3) Minor-to-serious (it is the difference between "solutions of \(D_p\)" and "questions").
(4) Fix — add it as a gap, or write the components that enforce it.

**7. A missing gap: the collapse of Finding 1.** (1) Claim attacked — the gap list (0)–(iv). (2) Why it fails — the gaps omit that the proposed content for gap (0) makes New nearly always false (Finding 1). (3) Serious. (4) Fix — see Finding 1.

**8. Gap (iv) and Part X's limit are the same limit, double-counted.**
(1) Claim attacked — gap (iv): *"Whether Inst licenses an organization whose ports range over edits of another organization is a question for the physical module and is not argued"*; vs. Part X: *"The invention of a dimension of variation that the physical module does not license … is not a construction … because there is no organization for Inst to license."*
(2) Why it fails — Both are the same module-licensing restriction, presented once as a technical gap and once as a principled boundary. They differ (typing of a meta-organization vs. a new port-dimension) but the text does not separate them.
(3) Cosmetic.
(4) Fix — state that gap (iv) is about Inst typing for \(D_p\), and that the dimension-invention limit is a separate, stronger restriction.

**9. Gap (iii) has a typing problem.**
(1) Claim attacked — gap (iii): *"the domain of \(u_K\) must be the set of pairs-with-relations, and it is not written out."*
(2) Why it fails — "pairs-with-relations" ranges over arbitrary component relations, i.e. a proper class unless a admissible-class restriction (like Derivation 3's "relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)") is stated. Without it, \(X_{u_K}\) is not a set.
(3) Minor.
(4) Fix — declare the admissible class of counterfactual relations.

## §3 Is the concept-creation limit rightly placed on the physical module's fixity?

**10. The limit is placed on a primitive, so "not the semantics' vocabulary" is self-undermining.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part X: *"The limit is the module's fixity, not the semantics' vocabulary; it is stated, and Part XV lists it."*
(2) Why it fails — \(\Theta\) **is** one of the semantics' two primitive packages (Part XIV). Placing the limit on \(\Theta\)'s fixity is placing it inside the semantics, in a primitive. The honest sentence is "the limit is primitive, not derived".
(3) Minor, but it conceals that the limit is unassessable from inside the framework.
(4) Fix — say "primitive", and state that no internal predicate evaluates a module's richness.

**11. The limit is module-index-relative, not absolute.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part 0: *"What it does not represent is the invention of a dimension of variation the physical module does not license, and it says that too."*
(2) Why it fails — By Part VIII, *"a later module with a larger \(A_\Theta\) makes it a new claim at a new index."* So "not representable" means "not representable at this module index"; the Part 0 phrasing drops the index.
(3) Minor-to-serious.
(4) Fix — index the claim: "not representable relative to \(\Theta\)".

**12. "Build covers it" is unsupported, and grain-introduction is declaration, not construction.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part X: *"Where the module does license the dimension at some grain, its introduction is the construction of a new organization attribution and Build covers it."*
(2) Why it fails — Grain \(\ell\) is a **declared** index (Part II: *"A grain \(\ell\) is a declared index"*; Part XIV lists grain as a declared parameter). Introducing a new dimension at a new grain is therefore a declaration, and Part IV says declaration produces no provenance, so (R) and (G) exclude it. For Build to cover it, the text would need: what target the built transport runs from, what the nontrivial binding is, and how a declared index-change acquires a construction witness with an itemised defect record. None is given.
(3) Serious — the "covered" half of the limit is not covered.
(4) Fix — supply the construction witness for grain/dimension introduction, or move the case into the limit list.

**13. There is no accuracy condition on \(A_\Theta\) or \(\operatorname{Inst}\).**
(1) Claim attacked — Part XII: the module supplies *"the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta(o)\) — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits, stated as a set, since every contract, every grain-admissibility claim and every world-kind claim quantifies over it"*; Part V: *"'World' here means the physical module: a world-kind is a fact about \((\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), and the module is the semantics' only access to the world."*
(2) Why it fails — Everything world-facing (world-kind, non-protection, impossibility, the concept-creation limit) quantifies over \(A_\Theta\) and \(\operatorname{Inst}\), but nothing says what makes a given \(A_\Theta\) or \(\operatorname{Inst}\) correct. Part VIII's "new module, new index" is index-shifting, not checking. So objectivity's second index is defined against an unchecked stipulation.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — impose admissibility conditions on modules (closure of \(A_\Theta\) under composition; stability of \(\operatorname{Inst}\) under \(\equiv_\ell\); grain-admissibility relative to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\)), or state plainly that all world-claims are module-relative and no internal predicate assesses a module.

**14. For non-physical organizations the "module's fixity" is a stipulation, so the limit is vacuous there.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part X's limit, stated generally; Part VII: *"a mathematical question must *declare* its admission relation."*
(2) Why it fails — For mathematical/modelled organizations the module is declared, so the modeller can always declare a richer admission relation and the "fixity" imposes nothing. The limit is real only for physical modules.
(3) Minor.
(4) Fix — restrict the fixity claim to physical modules and say the non-physical case has no limit of this kind.

## §4 Is the artefact case rightly placed?

**15. Part 0/Part VII say attack (D); Part XV lists it under (B).**
(1) Claim attacked — Part 0 (B): *"explanation of an effect as a selection artefact is a missing *condition* rather than a missing transport and is listed under (D)"*; Part VII: *"it is listed under attack (D)"*; but Part XV places it as case (ii) immediately under the heading *"An explanation without a faithful transport"* (attack (B)).
(2) Why it fails — Direct textual inconsistency about which attack the case answers to.
(3) Cosmetic, but it signals the placement uncertainty in Finding 16.
(4) Fix — choose one and justify it.

**16. The proposed vehicle (historical kinds) cannot carry the artefact condition.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part XV: *"what is missing is not a second target but a provenance clause on the target's own components (that the effect's component was selected by the data-gathering, not by the population), which the historical kinds of Part II can carry."*
(2) Why it fails — Part II: *"A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component."* The target's components' realizing occurrences are physical occurrences of the target; data-gathering does not produce them. What data-gathering produces is the **attribution** — the \(\operatorname{Inst}\) claim that \(D\) has that component — and \(\operatorname{Inst}\) attributions are module oracle outputs with no provenance in this semantics (Part IV gives provenance only for transports/contents). So the missing condition concerns the provenance of an attribution, which the vocabulary lacks; historical kinds are the wrong vehicle.
(3) Serious — the "closable" verdict rests on a vehicle that does not exist.
(4) Fix — either add a provenance/correctness condition for \(\operatorname{Inst}\) attributions (new primitive or a module condition), or re-describe the case as a decomposition-error case and say which predicate is meant to detect it.

**17. The placement presupposes an infallibilist reading of \(\operatorname{Inst}\) the document elsewhere rejects.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII: *"what the semantics lacks is a *condition* on the provenance of the target's own components"*; vs. Part XII: the selection witness *"is fallible and checkable as any physical claim is."*
(2) Why it fails — If module claims are fallible, the artefact component's presence in \(D\) is a misattribution by \(\operatorname{Inst}\), and the missing thing is a condition on when to trust \(\operatorname{Inst}\), not a provenance clause on the target's components. Only an infallible \(\operatorname{Inst}\) makes the missing-condition reading right.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — say whether \(\operatorname{Inst}\) can misattribute, and place the case accordingly.

## §5 Is symmetry really beyond (F2)?

**18. For the extensional reading, (F1)+(F2) do carry invariance→independence; the "open" verdict mislocates the gap.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII: *"(F1) is per-component and does not constrain it; (F2), which constrains the assembled solution set, carries some inter-component entailments, and whether it carries this one depends on the edit set, which is the door and is not walked through here."*
(2) Why it fails — (F1) equates \(L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) with \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\), i.e. with the anchor's projected **solution set**; so any solution-set property of the anchor, including its invariance under a group, is carried to \(E\)'s component. (F2) equates \(\operatorname{Sol}_E\) with \(\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D]\), so any solution-set property of the target, including independence (projection onto \(\{v,w\}\) being a product, or equality of projections), is carried. With both antecedent and consequent extensional, the entailment is preserved because both ends are preserved. What is **not** carried is the intensional "because".
(3) Serious — the case is diagnosed as an extensional gap when it is an intensional one, so the repair is sought in the wrong place.
(4) Fix — restate the open case as intensional explanatory force, and note that it is the same gap as Derivation 9 and as the "three-line proof vs Leibniz expansion" contrast in Part VII.

**19. The blanket "open" conflicts with the document's own worked symmetry example.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII's "open" verdict, vs. the same Part's odd-order skew-symmetric example, which is a symmetry argument (\(\det A=\det A^{T}=\det(-A)=(-1)^n\det A\)) and is claimed to satisfy (F1), (F2), (A), non-circularity and non-vacuity.
(2) Why it fails — At least one explanation-by-symmetry is handled by the existing machinery, so "explanation by symmetry is open" is too blunt. The intended open case must be one where the symmetry resides in \(E\)'s presentation only — but then (F1) requires it to be anchored in \(D\), so it is in \(D\), so it is carried (Finding 18).
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — delimit the open case extensionally, or concede it is the intensional residue.

**20. The dependence is not on the edit set alone.**
(1) Claim attacked — *"whether it carries this one depends on the edit set, which is the door and is not walked through here."*
(2) Why it fails — Whether the entailment is carried also depends on the anchor map \(\lambda\) (whether antecedent and consequent are anchored in \(D\)) and on whether the consequent is a solution-set property. Only the edit set is mentioned.
(3) Minor.
(4) Fix — add the anchor-map and extensionality conditions to the statement of the open case.

## §6 Further findings

**21. Non-circular dependence: the displayed "witness" witnesses dependence, not non-circularity.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part V: *"The witness: there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\) … that removes or replaces a nonempty sub-block \(B\subseteq\Gamma\) … under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way."*
(2) Why it fails — "\(p\) because \(p\)" satisfies this witness (removing the premise changes the profile), yet the text says *"\(p\) because \(p\) fails non-circular dependence."* The excluding work is done by the separate clause *"no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports"* — a \(\Pi_1\) condition over transports with no witness and no checkability story, in contrast to the selection witness, which Part 0 insists is *"checkable in principle."*
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — relabel the displayed item as the dependence witness, and supply a witness or a checkability note for boundary-independence.

**22. Ownership requires retained realization of the protocol, so procedural novelty is not ownable.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part X, Ownership: *"every process occurrence in it is an execution \(\eta\in\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\) … of a protocol \(\pi\) for which that substrate holds \(\operatorname{RetReal}(\pi,T,C;\chi)\)."*
(2) Why it fails — \(\operatorname{RetReal}\) is a \(\forall z\in C\,\forall i\) condition, so one successful run does not establish it; an episode running a protocol the substrate does not retain is not owned, hence not Build, hence not (G). Genuine procedural novelty is therefore outside (G). Part XII applies retention to the *inquiry-enabling organization* instead (*"A first discovery is not a repeatable task; retention is applied to the inquiry-enabling organization"*) — an unexplained asymmetry.
(3) Serious, and absent from Part XV's limit list.
(4) Fix — apply the CanAdv treatment to Ownership, or justify why protocols must be retained where first discoveries need not be.

**23. Build requires a prior failure, so error-free construction is impossible.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part X, Build: *"contains an **itemised defect representation** — an occurrence instantiating a record that pairs a state at which some prior transport of \(s\) failed fidelity … — on an active route to the *acceptance* of the binding."*
(2) Why it fails — If no prior transport of \(s\) failed, no such record exists and no construction witness is possible. So every construction presupposes a prior failure of the same system; error-free creative acts are excluded. This is a substantive assumption not listed in Part XV.
(3) Minor-to-serious.
(4) Fix — add it to Part XV's limits, or weaken the record requirement to a disjunction.

**24. "External" means external-to-the-system, not external-to-the-parameters.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part I: *"Every success predicate in this document is external: it can hold of a system that does not know it holds."*
(2) Why it fails — (G) and (EK) are indexed to declared \(\beta\), \(\Omega\), \(\ell\) (Part XIV: \(\beta\) is *"the set of occurrences counted as the system"*, \(\Omega\) the continuity criterion). So "the system constructed \(c\)" is true relative to a declared system individuation; a change of \(\Omega\) changes the claim.
(3) Minor.
(4) Fix — write "external to the system's beliefs, relative to the declared indices".

**25. The task-based formulation of physics is an unexamined load-bearing assumption.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part XII: *"The physical module adopts a task-based formulation of physics for this purpose."*
(2) Why it fails — This one line grounds \(\operatorname{RetReal}\), \(\operatorname{Can}\), Ownership, Build, (G) and (EK). For continuous or dynamical substrates, "protocol execution", "input/output attribute", and "retained realization" are not natural categories and need construction; nothing argues that physical processes generally decompose into protocol executions with well-defined retention.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — either justify the task-based formulation, or mark (G)/(EK) as relative to it.

**26. Attacks (A) and (B) have no fixed module index.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part XV (B): *"A genuine explanation whose organization cannot be captured by *any* transport satisfying component fidelity under *any* admitted contract."*
(2) Why it fails — "Any admitted contract" is index-ill-posed: if the module is fixed, the attack is module-relative and can be escaped by index-shifting ("new module, new claim"); if any module is allowed, the defender can always enrich \(A_\Theta\)/\(\operatorname{Inst}\) and the attack fails trivially. The sufficiency attack (A) has the same shape. So the necessity/sufficiency dialectic has no fixed target.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — fix \(\Theta\), \(\ell\) and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) for each attack and say the attacks are module-relative, or define a notion of module-admissibility first (Finding 13).

**27. Mathematical explanations are exempted from (B) by construction, and the mathematical relativisation is stronger than the physical one.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII: *"What the example does not do is fall under attack (B)'s quantifier over *physically* admitted contracts, because there is no physical module here; it falls under the declared one"*; and *"every claim about it is relative to that declaration, as a physical claim is relative to the physical module."*
(2) Why it fails — First, since (B) quantifies only over physically admitted contracts, no mathematical case can bear on necessity, ever — the exemption is a scope-protection, not a result. Second, for mathematics \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is declared, so **both** indices are stipulated and there is no second index at all; the parallel with the physical module is false, since the physical module is claimed not to be stipulated. Third, the odd-skew example's admission relation is tailored so that exactly the contrasts witnessing non-circularity are admitted, and the framework offers no non-stipulated way to adjudicate a better admission relation.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix — extend (B) to declared admission relations, or state that mathematical explanation is outside the necessity claim; and say plainly that for mathematics the objectivity condition is declaration-relative with no corrective.

**28. Derivation 3's Consequence drops a necessary qualifier.**
(1) Claim attacked — Derivation 3, Consequence: *"Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\)."*
(2) Why it fails — The claim proved is conditional: *"whenever some admissible \(L'\) changes that solution set."* If \(E\)'s solution set at \((a,b)\) is the only admissible one, no distinguishing \(L'\) exists, the claim is vacuous, and the survivor **is** determined. The Consequence states the unconditional version.
(3) Minor.
(4) Fix — state the consequence with the existence condition on a distinguishing \(L'\).

**29. Typing slip in the selection witness's non-triviality clause.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part IV: *"no event of \(\Sigma_h\) instantiates an organization held to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition by a faithful transport."*
(2) Why it fails — \(t\) is a transport (a tuple, not an organization), \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition is a condition; "an organization held to" them is ill-typed.
(3) Cosmetic.
(4) Fix — write "an organization that encodes \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition".

**30. The eliminative case misstates properness.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII: *"the rival's supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork … and the anchor is proper because the deleted subnetwork is not the whole target."*
(2) Why it fails — Properness (Part V, cond. 1) is a cover-plus-injectivity condition on the components \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches, and forbids the whole target as an anchor only when \(D\) has more than one component. "Not the whole target" is neither necessary nor sufficient for it.
(3) Cosmetic.
(4) Fix — cite the actual properness conditions.

**31. The flagship mathematical example is asserted, not executed.**
(1) Claim attacked — Part VII: *"The full Leibniz expansion with skewness substituted satisfies (F1) and (F2): every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away."*
(2) Why it fails — The anchor map \(\lambda\) from the expansion's components to subnetworks of the constraint-presentation target is not written down; nor is it shown that the anchors cover the touched constraints as properness requires (the determinant is instead *"anchored to themselves under the identity translation"*). Since the framework's selling point is that such claims become checkable, an un-checked flagship example undercuts it.
(3) Minor-to-serious.
(4) Fix — write out \(D\), \(E\), \(\lambda\), and verify (F1), (F2) and properness; or label the example as illustrative, as Derivation 10 is.

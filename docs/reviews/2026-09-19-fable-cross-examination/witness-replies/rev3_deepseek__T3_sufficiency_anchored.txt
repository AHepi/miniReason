**Finding 1 — The requested counterexample to sufficiency exists, exploiting overlapping anchors and a redundant active component.**
(1) Claim attacked: Part XV says

(2) Why it fails: The anchored grade as written does not require the anchors to form a disjoint partition, does not require every component of \(E\) that affects \(\operatorname{Sol}_E\) to be active/anchored, and does not require active components to be non-redundant. Construct a target \(D\) with ports \(v_1,v_2,v_3\), components \(j_1,j_2,j_3\), baseline \(v_1=v_2=v_3=0\), and admitted edits: \(a_{12}\) replaces the pair \((j_1,j_2)\) with a joint relation \(v_1=v_2=1\); \(a_3\) replaces \(j_3\) with \(v_3=1\). **Finding 1 — Concrete counterexample to (A): the built target-mirror.**
The document itself supplies the candidate. Part X says: “So the target’s own faithful copy, built from error records, is an anchored, originated account that is \(\equiv_\ell\) to the target: New fails for it if the target is already in the repertoire, and holds if it is not — in which case building the target’s mirror from its errors is discovering it, and the semantics says so without embarrassment.”

Take a one-component black-box target \(D\):
- \(V=\{x,y\}\), \(X_x=X_y=\{0,1\}\);
- \(J=\{j\}\), \(V_j=\{x,y\}\);
- \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) contains set-\(x\) edits and observe-\(y\) edits;
- \(L_j(\text{set }x=r,b_0)=\{(r,f(r))\}\) for an arbitrary Boolean \(f\);
- question \(p\) asks “why is \(y=f(x)\)?”, with \(\mathcal Q\) reading \(y\);
- \(E=D\), \(t=\mathrm{id}\), \(\Gamma=\{j\}\).

This is an anchored account: properness is satisfied because “An anchor may be the whole target only when \(D\) has one component”; resolution is vacuous; world-kind is identity. It satisfies (E): (F1), (F2), (A) are identity, non-circular dependence holds by deleting \(j\), and non-vacuity holds. It satisfies Origin if \(s\) has no prior \(D\)-equivalent content, the learner builds the mirror from itemised failure records, uses them at acceptance, and deploys the result. It plainly explains nothing: it is the target’s own input–output relation restated, with no decomposition, no independent dependency, no answer to *why* \(f\) is \(f\).  
**Severity:** fatal to the sufficiency claim.  
**Fix:** add a non-copying/non-restatement condition to the anchored grade or Build; exclude \(E\cong D\) unless \(E\) supplies an independently constructed decomposition; or withdraw sufficiency.

**Finding 2 — The document contradicts itself on whether the target’s copy fails Origin.**

**Fix:** require decomposition even for one-component targets, or state that one-component targets cannot be explained by the anchored grade alone.

**Finding 4 — Properness excludes genuine decomposition of single-component targets.**

**Why it fails:** a genuine mechanism may explain a single observed phenomenon by multiple sub-mechanisms. The anchored grade forbids that unless the physical module already decomposes \(D\) into those subcomponents.  
**Severity:** serious exclusion of genuine mechanisms.  
**Fix:** allow \(E\)’s components to anchor to parts of \(D\) not listed as components of \(D\), or allow a refinement relation not limited to \(D\)’s given component inventory.

**Finding 5 — Properness excludes partial mechanisms.**
Properness requires a cover of “the components of \(D\) that some pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches,” and “no touched component of \(D\) lies outside every anchor.”  
**Why it fails:** a genuine partial explanation may leave some physically touchable components as background. Under this condition, those components must lie in some active anchor, so the account must cover the whole touched mechanism.  
**Severity:** serious.

**Finding 6 — Idealizations fail world-kind.**

**Severity:** serious exclusion of genuine idealizational explanations.

**Finding 7 — Coarse grains are underdefined and may be excluded.**

Exact coarse-grainings may survive, but statistical or approximate coarse-grained mechanisms are excluded or left formally undefined.  
**Severity:** serious.

**Severity:** serious.

**Finding 14 — Deploy can be satisfied by using the copy.**

**Finding 1 (fatal): A single-component target gives an anchored, originated account that plainly explains nothing.**

Attacked claim: Part V, “An anchor may be the whole target only when \(D\) has one component,” together with the non-circular dependence passage: “when it has one, removing it is the only witness there is, and the condition then does little—which is why the anchored grade, not this condition, is what excludes the one-component repackaging.”

Why it fails: Let \(D\) have exactly one component \(j\) with footprint \(\{x,y\}\) and relation \(L_j(a,b)=\{(x,y):y=f(x)\}\). Let the contract contain edits setting \(x\). Let \(E=D\) under the identity transport. Properness permits the whole target as anchor because \(D\) has one component. Resolution is vacuous. World-kind is tautological: \(E\)’s component is its own anchor. So \(E\) is anchored. If the target is not already in \(s\)’s repertoire, New holds; if \(s\) builds the single component from error records, Build can hold; if \(s\) deploys it to predict \(y\), Deploy holds. Thus Origin can hold. But \(E\) is literally the target’s one-component relation restated. It provides no decomposition, no independent dependencies, no mechanism. It “plainly explains nothing.” The anchored grade does not exclude it, because the only exclusionary clause for one-component repackaging is properness, and properness explicitly permits the whole target when \(D\) has one component.

Seriousness: fatal to the sufficiency attack (A) as stated.

Fix: either require \(D\) to have a proper decomposition for any anchored explanation, or add a condition that \(E\) is not identical to \(D\) and that the target’s component is decomposed into independently variable subcomponents. **Finding 2 (fatal): Part 0 and Part X contradict each other on whether the target’s own copy fails Origin.**

**Finding 3 (serious): Properness over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) excludes genuine partial mechanisms.**

Why it fails: Suppose \(D\) has components \(A\) and \(B\). \(A\) affects \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\); \(B\) is touched by some admitted edit in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) but is irrelevant to \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\). The condition makes physical admittance, not question relevance, determine the required decomposition.

Seriousness: serious; it excludes partial mechanisms that are legitimate relative to the question.

Fix: restrict the cover to components touched by \(C\) or to components that can affect \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\). Allow touched-but-irrelevant components to be named background or silent.

**Finding 4 (serious): Properness excludes a genuine single-component global explanation of a multi-component target.**

Attacked claim: “An anchor may be the whole target only when \(D\) has one component.”

**Finding 5 (serious): World-kind on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) excludes idealizations.**

Headline: Part XIV/Derivation 6's claim that everything is defined from two primitive packages plus declared parameters is false. At least four load-bearing notions are neither primitive, declared, nor derived — an unanalysed causal predicate inside the selection witness, an undefined "itemised representation/record" that carries the Sel/Con discrimination, an intentional "episode" notion at the base of Con, and several undeclared domains/predicates (actual connection structure, "achievement predicate", "advanceable", "explanatory contents"). There are also two live dependence-cycle routes the document's stated cycle-break does not cover, and one of (E)'s five conditions has a witness that is neither necessary nor sufficient for its own condition.

**Finding 1.** Quote: "with the environment's filtering *causally responsible* for $t$'s presence and not merely consistent with it" (Part IV, Selected); repeated in Part XII: "population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, *causal responsibility* — is a claim about a physical history under $\Theta$."
(1) Claim attacked: that selection is defined without a causal primitive, and (per Grievances 1/11 and Part II) that causation is derived from edit-signatures.
(2) Why it fails: "causally responsible" is an unanalysed causal predicate inside $\operatorname{Sel}$, which feeds (R), Deploy, (N), (G), (EK). Part XII's inventory of what $\Theta$ supplies (state spaces, admitted processes, $A_\Theta$, intervention repertoire, resources/grades, $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$, $\prec$, $\operatorname{Exec}$) contains no causal-responsibility relation, and $\prec$ is never said to be it. So causation is smuggled in at the base of representation rather than derived from signatures.
(3) Serious (fatal to Derivation 6's exhaustiveness; undermines the "cause = signature" selling point).
(4) Fix: define the clause structurally via $\prec$ — e.g. the filtering events $\prec$-precede $t$'s presence and every $\prec$-maximal alternative route to that presence is excluded — or add "causal responsibility" to Part XII as an explicit module-supplied relation.

**Finding 2.** Quote: "A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no *itemised representation* of the defect — *a record* pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation"; and "a selection witness contains no *itemised representation* of a defect … a construction witness contains both."
(1) Claim attacked: the Sel/Con discrimination requirement, hence the precedence rule, hence (R)'s provenance disjunct.
(2) Why it fails: "itemised representation"/"record" is never defined as a predicate anywhere, yet it is the sole separator of the two provenances. Worse, if "representation" is read as the technical (R) (Part IV), then $\operatorname{Sel}\to$ blind $\to (R)\to(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con})\to\operatorname{Sel}$ is a cycle; Derivation 6's cycle-break addresses only the Con branch and does not mention this one. The em-dash gloss does not discharge the issue, because "record" is equally undefined and is not among the declared parameters.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: define the notion in Inst-plus-fidelity terms — e.g. "an occurrence instantiating an organization whose ports include the failing state and the counterfactual holding state, with a component relating them" — and rename it so the word "representation" does not appear inside a provenance definition.

**Finding 3.** Quote: "holds when an owned subhistory of $h$, delimited at $e$, prepares an organization that some occurrence of $s$ instantiates *for explanatory use of* $c$, contains a nontrivial binding construction required for that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers."
(1) Claim attacked: the definition of $\operatorname{Build}$, and Part XIV's claim that "Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E)".
(2) Why it fails: "for explanatory use of $c$" is the load-bearing clause and is not cashed out. If it means $\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\cdot)$, then $\operatorname{Build}\to\operatorname{Deploy}\to(R)\to\operatorname{Con}\to\operatorname{Build}$ — a cycle Derivation 6 does not consider. If it means $(E)$ ($\operatorname{Account}$), the text should say so and does not. It also mis-types for question-construction: Derivation 5 makes the question-organization $D_p$ the content $c$ in (G), but an organization built "for explanatory use of a question" would be an answer, not the question.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: replace the clause with an explicit $\operatorname{Account}(E_c,c)$ (or, for question-contents, "$E_c$ is the question-organization of Derivation 5") and state that $\operatorname{Build}$ does not invoke $\operatorname{Deploy}$.

**Finding 4.** Quote: "the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness."
(1) Claim attacked: Derivation 6's well-foundedness argument.
(2) Why it fails: it is not the only cycle. Findings 2 and 3 exhibit two others (blindness→(R)→Sel; Build→Deploy→(R)→Con→Build) on the very branch the sentence claims is clean. The Con-stratification is sound; the claim of uniqueness is not.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: restate as "the cycles that could arise are broken as follows: …" and handle the blindness and Build-use clauses explicitly.

**Finding 5.** Quote (Part V): "By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of $E$ has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract"; quote (Derivation 1): "Let $t$ satisfy (F1) on $C$, and let $(\tau,\sigma)$ be *injective on* $C$."
(1) Claim attacked: the inference from (F1) to signature-preservation inside (E)'s own exposition.
(2) Why it fails: Derivation 1's conclusion needs injectivity of $(\tau,\sigma)$ on $C$; (E) never requires it, and (F2)'s homomorphism condition $\tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)$ positively permits non-injective $\tau$. So the cited derivation does not apply to every candidate satisfying (E).
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: add injectivity of $(\tau,\sigma)$ on $C$ to the transport requirements in (E), or restrict the sentence to "when $(\tau,\sigma)$ is injective on $C$".

**Finding 6.** Quote: "The witness: there exists an edit $\tau(a)$ on $E$, translated from some $(a,b)\in C$, that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block $B\subsetneq\Gamma$ while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way."
(1) Claim attacked: the operationalization of Non-circular dependence, one of the five conjuncts of (E).
(2) Why it fails: the witness tracks difference-making, not circularity, and it is neither necessary nor sufficient for the stated condition ("The target's answer does not appear … as a component"). *Not sufficient:* a circular candidate "$p$ because $p$ and $q$" has $\Gamma=\{c_p,c_q\}$; removing the proper sub-block $\{c_p\}$ changes the answer profile, so the witness is satisfied while the condition fails. *Not necessary:* Part VI's own redundant-routes case $\Gamma=\{a,b\}$, $\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}$ is non-circular, yet removing either singleton leaves an account with the same answer profile, so the witness fails. Additionally the witness is not well-typed unless $A_E$ contains component-deletion edits, which Part II does not guarantee.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: state the condition itself extensionally (no component of $E$ has, on $C$, a relation equal to $\operatorname{Ans}_p$ or to a projection of $\operatorname{Sol}_D$), and demote the removal edit to a heuristic that is explicitly noted to fail under redundancy.

**Finding 7.** Quote: "Con(t;h,e) holds when there is an *episode* (Part X) whose construction witness prepares $t$ …"; quote (Part X): "A complete critical episode contains a *recognized difficulty*, a target available before its criticism, a *conjectural objection*, and a *content-sensitive response*."
(1) Claim attacked: the definition of $\operatorname{Con}$, hence the constructed-provenance disjunct of (R), Deploy, (N), (G), (EK).
(2) Why it fails: "episode" is introduced only with intentional/epistemic vocabulary ("recognized", "conjectural", "content-sensitive") and is never given a structural definition. So the constructed branch of the provenance precedence rests on an undefined judgement-laden notion, contradicting Part XIV's "each definition names its inputs at first use".
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: either define episode structurally (a history segment containing a defect record, a target occurrence instantiated before the variation, and a response subhistory) or state that $\operatorname{Con}$ requires only the construction witness (owned subhistory + bindings) and that the critical-episode wrapper is not part of the predicate.

**Finding 8.** Quote (Derivation 5): "Its solutions are *exactly the coherently posed questions* on $D$"; and "provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization."
(1) Claim attacked: Derivation 5's support for question-finding being representable.
(2) Why it fails: a solution of $D_p$ fixes the in/out ports over $A\times B$, $\mathcal Q$, $S$ and $K$ only. A coherently posed question is the tuple $(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)$; $O_p$ and $\rho_p$ are not ports of $D_p$ ($b_0$ is merely held fixed as background), so solutions are strictly fewer than coherently posed questions. Also, $\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing$ is assumed as background, not enforced by any component. Finally, $\rho_p$ (Part III, a component of the tuple with a Part IV witness) is conflated with the provenance of an occurrence carrying $D_p$; Part III's "a selected question is the surviving member of a population of questions … with the witness required by Part IV" does not type-check, since $\operatorname{Sel}$/$\operatorname{Con}$ are transport predicates and a question is not a transport.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: add ports for $O_p$ and $\rho_p$ to $D_p$ (or downgrade the claim to "solutions are exactly the admissible $(C,\mathcal Q,S,K)$ parts"), and state one uniform account of question provenance.

**Finding 9.** Quote (Derivation 8): "Let $\phi$ be a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps $\lambda$), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module's admission ($A_\Theta$, Inst, $\prec$), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and $\mathcal N$"; and "a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted $\phi$, *since it does not preserve $D$'s structure*."
(1) Claim attacked: Derivation 8's equivariance claim for (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK).
(2) Why it fails: the hypothesis lists module-level and scope-level preservations but never requires $\phi$ to preserve each organization's data $(V,X,J,B,A,L)$; the pole/shadow exclusion invoked in the proof is exactly such a structure-preservation requirement and is therefore unlicensed. Separately, preservation of (G) needs $\phi$ to map repertoires onto repertoires and to preserve $\equiv_\ell$ (because (N) is a negative claim over $R_{<e}$), which the "each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims" argument does not establish.
(3) Serious.
(4) Fix: add "each $\phi$ restricts to an isomorphism of every organization's data $(V,X,J,B,A,L)$ and preserves $\equiv_\ell$ and the deployable repertoires" to the hypothesis, or restrict the claim to (E) and anchoring.

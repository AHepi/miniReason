Also, selection is still done on the evaluation half, so there is no independent test set for architecture selection; and the even/odd split by index may be confounded if index order correlates with configuration type.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: pre-register the split; use a random/balanced split; report train = test as exploratory; use nested validation if selection and reporting are to be separated.  
Referee verdict: I would accept held-out evaluation as a referee, but reject treating the post hoc version as confirmatory.

**Finding 18 — Change 18 (“The 0.95-of-ceiling adequacy threshold is dropped”).**  
Claim attacked: “It was a guess, and the diagnosis showed the ceiling is an in-sample bound: the tracker's gap to it is the change step itself and co-located starts, which no sensory predictor without a thing-count prior can see. Adequacy is now the ordering, slot-only > lookup-only, on held-out configurations. The fraction of ceiling is still reported (it was 0.88 at this point).”  
Why it fails: this drops a pre-registered failure threshold after seeing the tracker score 0.88, below 0.95. The stated diagnosis may explain why 0.95 is not reached; it does not justify replacing a quantitative adequacy threshold with a weaker ordering. The new ordering can pass even if both slot-only and lookup-only are poor. Moreover, the diagnosis is internally shaky: the ceiling is in-sample, so part of the gap may be ceiling overfitting, not “the change step itself” or co-location. The original Section 4 “Underpowered” outcome is met: no slot architecture reaches 0.95 × the ceiling, so the pre-registered result is failure or revision, not a dropped threshold.  
Severity: fatal.  
Fix: keep the original threshold and report the attempt as failed/underpowered, or re-freeze with a new adequacy criterion and rerun on fresh data.  
Referee verdict: I would not accept dropping the threshold as a pre-registered amendment; I would accept reporting 0.88 and the ordering as exploratory.

**Finding 19 — Change 19 (“Neutrality check restricted to the τ family”).**  
Claim attacked: “Under the amended check τ = 0, 2, 5, ∞ tied *exactly* on H_neutral and rebind sat 0.026 below; rebind's deficit is that it cannot keep velocity through a teleport, which is not the kind under test.”  
Why it fails: Section 2.6 says, “The kind under test is persistence — the τ gene and the `rebind` rule. A thing-tracker keeps a slot alive when nothing confirms it; a cell-tracker does not.” Change 19 excludes `rebind` after it sat 0.026 below, and declares its deficit “not the kind under test.” That contradicts Section 2.6 and changes the kind being tested. It is especially suspicious because Change 14 added the `j` gene for jump-keeps-velocity, and then Change 19 excludes `rebind` because it cannot keep velocity through a teleport. The grammar was expanded in a way that disadvantages `rebind`, and then `rebind` was excluded.  
Severity: fatal.  
Fix: include `rebind` and report the neutrality failure, or amend Section 2.6 and pre-register the narrower kind under test.  
Referee verdict: reject as a pre-registered pass.

**Finding 20 — Change 20 (“A thing-count prior was considered and not added”).**  
Claim attacked: “It would let a lone blob bind two slots and would close part of the co-location gap. It is a further piece of latent machinery and a candidate for a follow-up, not a change made after seeing results.”  
Why it fails: it is not a change, but it admits a known co-location gap in the grammar. If adequacy depends on that gap, the grammar is known to be inadequate for part of the contract, and Change 18 drops the threshold that would have flagged this.  
Severity: minor/serious limitation.  
Fix: report the size and effect of the co-location gap on M1/M2/M3; treat the thing-count prior as a separate experiment.  
Referee verdict: accept as disclosure, but it weakens the adequacy claim.

**Finding 21 — Change 21 (“Revision stops here”).**  
Claim attacked: “Three rounds before freezing. If the two checks do not pass under these criteria, the attempt is recorded as failed and reported, not revised again.”  
Why it fails: the stopping rule is retrospective. Three rounds of revision after seeing controls means the final criteria were selected after multiple looks. Saying “no more revision” does not cure the bias from the earlier rounds.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: number the rounds, report all amendments and control results, and run a fresh confirmation.  
Referee verdict: accept as transparency, not as validation.

## Cross-cutting findings

**Finding 22 — Multiple amendments were made after results pointed in a particular direction.**  
Claim attacked: the document’s status line says, “Nothing was run before that moment except the control check in section 8, step 2.”  
Why it fails: Changes 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, and 19 all cite control results, train/test scores, or observed deficits. Several explicitly favor the diagnosis: Change 16 cites “the first control run had already shown τ = 0 winning H_neutral and τ = ∞ winning H_full”; Change 17 cites lookup scoring 0.820 on train and 0.656 held out; Change 18 cites “it was 0.88 at this point”; Change 19 cites `rebind` sitting “0.026 below.” This is a garden of forking paths, not a pre-registered confirmation.  
Severity: fatal.  
Fix: separate discovery from confirmation; report every amendment and control score; rerun the frozen design on fresh histories/configurations.

**Finding 23 — `r = 5` on a 6-cell line is a confound for neutrality.**  
Claim attacked: the neutrality claim that “H_neutral” cannot distinguish a thing-tracker from a cell-tracker.  
Why it fails: Section 2.6 says `r ∈ {2, 5}` and a slot can re-bind “the nearest unconfirmed slot within distance *r*.” On a 6-cell line, maximum distance is 5, so `r = 5` lets any unconfirmed slot re-bind to any occupied cell. The τ = 0/2/5/∞ tie on H_neutral may therefore be an artifact of over-permissive re-binding, not genuine history neutrality.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: restrict `r` below the line diameter or report neutrality separately for `r = 2` and `r = 5`.  
Referee verdict: reject the neutrality conclusion until this is checked.

**Finding 24 — Section 4 still contains the 0.95 threshold that Change 18 says was dropped.**  
Claim attacked: Change 18: “The 0.95-of-ceiling adequacy threshold is dropped.”  
Why it fails: Section 4 still says, “No slot architecture reaches 0.95 × the sensory ceiling on H_full. Then the grammar in 2.6 cannot express an adequate predictor for this world.” The frozen document is internally inconsistent about the adequacy criterion.  
Severity: serious.  
Fix: reconcile Section 4 with Section 5 and Change 18; if the threshold is dropped, remove or mark the Section 4 outcome as superseded.

**Finding 25 — Section 5 excludes `rebind` while Section 2.6 includes it.**  
Claim attacked: Section 5: “`rebind` is reported beside them but is not in the check: it differs from τ = 0 in re-inferring velocity from scratch every step, which is a velocity-memory difference, not a persistence difference.”  
Why it fails: Section 2.6 says, “The kind under test is persistence — the τ gene and the `rebind` rule.” The document cannot both define `rebind` as part of the kind under test and exclude it from the neutrality check for that kind.  
Severity: fatal.  
Fix: reconcile the definition; if `rebind` is excluded, amend Section 2.6 and pre-register the narrower kind.

**Finding 26 — M3’s “if a match exists” clause can excuse ambiguous cases.**  
Claim attacked: Section 3 M3: “if a match exists, apply the **translated** change to the slot state … and check at every later step that each bound slot's (cell, velocity) equals its bound thing's (position, velocity).”  
Why it fails: two things can share a cell and have the same velocity, or cross. The “if a match exists” condition can let ambiguous configurations drop out of the denominator, inflating the thing-signature fraction. This interacts with the neutrality/adequacy checks because M3 is used to certify thing-kind.  
Severity: serious if M3 is used for the main conclusion.  
Fix: pre-specify matching and tie-breaking for co-located/crossing cases; report the fraction of dropped cases.

## Findings

**1. Fatal: the “target’s own copy” now may satisfy Origin, contradicting Part 0 and Part XV.**
24 Part 0: “The target’s own copy and the faithful modular table satisfy the anchored grade and fail Origin, and the semantics says so rather than hiding it.” 24 Part XV: “Candidates meeting the anchored grade and explaining nothing are already known — the target’s own copy, the faithful modular table — and the class does not claim they explain: they fail Origin.” But 24 Part X says: “So the target’s own faithful copy, built from error records, is an anchored, originated account that is \(\equiv_\ell\) to the target: New fails for it if the target is already in the repertoire, and holds if it is not — in which case building the target’s mirror from its errors is discovering it.” If the target is not already in the repertoire, New holds; if the copy is built and deployed, Origin can hold. That directly contradicts the claimed counterexample control. Fix: disambiguate “target’s own copy” (identity transport vs. built mirror) and either exclude the built mirror from Origin or revise Part 0/Part XV.

**2. Serious: mixed provenance is either impossible or unwired.**
24 Part IV: “If they are of both kinds, \(t\) has mixed provenance, which is declared as such and does not count as constructed for (G).” But 24 Part IV also says: “One transport cannot satisfy both for the same route.” If the construction witness has an itemised defect record on the same route to the occurrence, the selection witness’s blindness clause fails. So mixed provenance cannot arise under the unified-route rule. Further, (G) uses \(\operatorname{Build}\), not provenance, so “does not count as constructed for (G)” has no formal effect. Fix: either delete mixed as impossible, or unify the definitions so mixed is possible and wire the exclusion into \(\operatorname{Origin}\)/\(\operatorname{Build}\).

**3. Serious: Part VII and Part XV contradict each other on symmetry and (F2).**
24 Part VII: “Explanation by symmetry … is open (Part XV, attack (B)): (F1) is per-component and does not constrain it; (F2), which constrains the assembled solution set, carries some inter-component entailments, and whether it carries this one depends on the edit set, which is the door and is not walked through here.” 24 Part XV: “Explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among the components of \(E\) that neither (F1), a per-component condition, nor (F2), an assembly condition, constrains.” One says (F2) may carry the entailment; the other says it does not constrain it. Fix: choose one and align both passages.

**4. Serious: the artefact case is still under attack (B) in Part XV, despite 24a claiming it was moved.**
24 Part 0: “explanation of an effect as a selection artefact is a missing condition rather than a missing transport and is listed under (D).” 24 Part VII: “Explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact … is listed under attack (D).” But 24 Part XV, under the heading “An explanation without a faithful transport,” still says: “(ii) closable, not done — explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact: what is missing is not a second target but a provenance clause … the construction is not written.” 24a row 12 claims “Part XV’s artefact case moved”; it was not. Fix: move case (ii) out of the necessity attack and into (D)/(E).

**5. Serious: objectivity extends the transport “by \(\lambda\),” but \(\lambda\) is the anchor map, not the edit translation.**
24 Part V: “the transport being extended to such pairs by \(\lambda\) (the edit acts on an anchored component, so its translation is the corresponding edit on the anchored component of \(E\)).” But Part IV defines \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) as the edit translation; \(\lambda\) assigns components of \(E\) to subnetworks of \(D\). The objectivity condition is therefore ill-typed. Fix: say the transport is extended by \(\tau\), with \(\lambda\) fixing the anchor.

**6. Serious: Bearing treats \(\operatorname{Inst}\) as a function.**
24 Part IX: “whose target organization is (the organization \(z\) instantiates).” But Part IV explicitly says \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\) “is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain.” The definite singular is false. Fix: quantify over the relevant instantiation or let the criticism supply which instantiation its target is.

**7. Serious: “the largest contract” in (R) need not exist.**
24 Part IV: “what it represents without qualification is \(c\) on the largest contract on which some witnessing transport is faithful.” If one transport is faithful on \(C_1\) and another on \(C_2\), and \(C_1,C_2\) are incomparable, there need be no largest contract, and no single witnessing transport on the union. Fix: define qualification-free representation as a set of maximal contracts, or require a single transport and a closure condition.

**8. Serious: non-circular dependence still contradicts the one-component verdict.**
24 Part V says: “The target’s answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component; moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named ‘law’ does not discharge this.” Later it says: “The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) for a target with several components satisfies (E): with a single commitment the witness is its removal, which is allowed.” The one-component repackaging is exactly the target’s solution set appearing as a component. The text tries to escape by saying the singleton witness “does little,” but the prohibition remains in the condition. Fix: delete the prohibition or make the witness structural rather than merely removable.

**9. Minor: the transport tuple was not updated to include the fifth datum.**
24 Part IV still writes \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\), then says a transport serving a question “carries a fifth datum, the transported query \(\mathcal Q_E\).” The formal tuple and type are stale. Fix: write \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda,\mathcal Q_E)\) where applicable.

**10. Cosmetic: Derivation 6 says “Three places” but lists four.**
24 Derivation 6: “Three places could have produced a cycle and do not: (i) … (ii) … (iii) … (iv) Build’s ‘already carried’ …” That is four. Fix: change to “Four places.”

**11. Serious: Build’s exclusion clause has no clear subject.**
24 Part X: “contains an itemised defect representation … on an active route to the acceptance of the binding: the binding may be conjectured by any process, blind or not, and what makes the episode construction is that the record is used … and is neither a content-preserving transfer nor a composition of them.” The “is neither” has no grammatical subject: the record? the binding? the episode? the owned subhistory? Build is ill-defined. Fix: say explicitly that the owned subhistory is not a content-preserving transfer or composition of them.

**12. Serious: the survival condition is still an exact survivor-set equality, despite 23 §5 row 1.**
24 Part IV: “the witness records the checkable fact that, in this population and this history, the members that survived were exactly the members faithful on \(H\).” That is still a very strong equality. Real selection can eliminate a faithful member for unrelated reasons, or retain an unfaithful one. The repair changes the status from definition to recorded fact, but Sel remains unsatisfiable for noisy selection. Fix: state the survival condition as a law-like or probabilistic relation, and adjust Derivation 3’s hypothesis.

**13. Serious: \(H\) is determined relative to \(C_t\), which can be narrowed.**
24 Part IV: “the finite history \(H\) of all edit–boundary pairs within \(C_t\) … that occurred as events of \(\Sigma_h\), non-empty and not chosen.” If \(C_t\) is chosen, H is indirectly chosen. The objectivity condition is meant to catch protective contracts, but the selection witness itself can be eased by narrowing the content’s contract. Fix: fix \(H\) relative to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) or to an independently specified selection contract, not to the content claim under assessment.

**14. Minor: properness still does not explicitly permit overlapping anchors.**
24 Part V: “The anchors of the active components refine a cover … and the anchor map is injective on that cover: distinct active components have distinct anchors…”. 23 §5 row 9 said “Overlap is permitted; injectivity forbids identical anchors only.” 24 does not say this. Fix: add “anchors may overlap; injectivity forbids identical anchors only.”

**15. Minor: Attack (F)’s redirect to (C) is not obviously valid.**
24 Part 0: “If such a decoy nonetheless failed to explain where the mechanism succeeds, the difference between them would be one that no admitted change shows — and a discriminating difference no admitted change shows is exactly what attack (C) asks for.” But attack (C) is specifically about a “declared kind-label” doing discriminating work. A merely unexplained difference between decoy and mechanism need not be a kind-label. Fix: either broaden (C) or state the additional premise that the difference is carried by a kind-label.

**16. Serious: 24a’s ledger is false on the artefact move.**
24a row 12: “Part XV’s protective attack restated; the artefact case placed under (D) — Part XV; Part VII; Part 0 (B) — Done.” Part XV still places it under (B). Fix: correct the ledger or the document.

**17. Serious: the known \(t|W\) typing problem is admitted, not fixed.**
24a “Not done” says: “Part VI’s (S) still restricts \(t\) by \(W\); whether \(t|W\) is well-typed for the restricted candidate is not argued.” 24 Part VI still defines \(\mathsf S_{E,p}\) using \(\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p,t|W,W)\). If \(t|W\) is not shown to be a transport for \(E|W\), the support predicate is undefined. Fix: prove well-typedness or restrict \(\mathsf S\) to cases where it is defined.

**18. Minor: the aggregate exemption still permits leakage if magnitudes encode states.**
24 Part IV: “an aggregate summary — a function of the multiset of failure magnitudes alone, with no access to which state failed … is explicitly permitted.” A function of magnitudes can still encode which state failed if magnitudes are unique per state. Fix: require the magnitude statistic to be invariant under the relevant state permutations, not merely a function of magnitudes.

**19. Minor: the transported query is a free parameter, so (A) carries all the weight.**
24 Part IV: \(\mathcal Q_E\) is “declared with the transport,” and (A) requires only that \(\operatorname{Ans}_E=\operatorname{Ans}_p\) through the transport. This avoids the inverse-projection problem, but now \(\mathcal Q_E\) is not derived from \(\mathcal Q\); it can be chosen. Fix: say explicitly that (A) is the substantive constraint and that \(\mathcal Q_E\) is merely its carrier, or impose an inducedness condition.

**20. Minor: the claimed preservation of forward kinds by \(\equiv_\ell\) is unproved.**
24 Part X: “a content-preserving recoding preserves the forward kind of every part on every contract, and the converse fails.” This is a new structural claim, not proved. Fix: prove it or qualify it to contracts carried by the recoding.

**21. Minor: the restated protective-scope attack is nearly self-refuting.**
24 Part XV: “Under the structural definition of silence this can only happen when the candidate’s decomposition omits a component the change touches, which properness forbids for anchored accounts; a case where an anchored account is protected by silence would show the definition is too weak.” The attack asks for a case that the text says cannot occur for anchored accounts. Fix: restate the attack as testing the non-anchored case, or as showing that the structural definition makes the objectivity claim for non-anchored accounts too weak.

**22. Minor: “required” can be vacuous when there are no alternative bindings.**
24 Part X: “required for the transport when replacing it by any binding already carried by \(s\) or obtainable from the carriers fails (F1) or (F2).” If there are no such alternative bindings, the universal is vacuously true. The nontriviality clause partly blocks this, but not entirely. Fix: conjoin a non-vacuity condition on the replacement set.

**23. Minor: “understanding” is still awkwardly handled in Part XIV.**
24 Part XIV: “Deployment (which is what ‘understanding’ names here), construction, newness…” This says “understanding” is not a separate predicate but lists Deployment as derived; 23 §2.12 asked to delete “understanding” from the derived list or define it as Deploy. This is done only in prose, not by a clean list entry. Fix: add “understanding = Deploy” to the informal/derived mapping explicitly.

**24. Minor: the ledger overstates item 6/25 by claiming content provenance on \(C\) is fully fixed.**
24a row 6, 25: “(R) indexed to a contract; content provenance on C — Done.” The text does index (R), but the “largest contract” clause is not well-defined, as in finding 7. Fix: either repair the largest-contract clause or mark the item partial in 24a.

## A. Serious defects, including new ones introduced by 24

**Finding 1 (serious; new loophole, item 8/26).**  
**Claim attacked:** 24 replaces 22’s problematic \(\pi^{-1}\) query translation by making the transported query a declared transport datum.  
**Quote:** “When the transport serves a question, it carries a fifth datum, the **transported query** \(\mathcal Q_E\), an operation on \(E\) of the same type as \(\mathcal Q\), declared with the transport… \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\mathcal Q_E(E,\tau(a),\sigma(b))\); no inverse of \(\pi\) is taken, and (A) is the requirement that the transported query agree with \(\mathcal Q\) through the transport on \(C\).”  
**Why it fails:** \(\mathcal Q_E\) is declared by the transport-builder, not derived from \(\mathcal Q\). Since (A) merely checks \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\), a candidate can declare \(\mathcal Q_E\) to return the target’s answers by fiat, so question-fidelity becomes an unconstrained lookup unless “agree with \(\mathcal Q\) through the transport” is formalised independently.  
**Fix:** define \(\mathcal Q_E\) relationally from \(\mathcal Q\) and \(\pi\) (e.g. a quotient/pullback condition that is multivalued where \(\pi\) is non-injective), and require agreement with \(\mathcal Q\) as a condition on that definition, not as a free declaration.

**Finding 2 (serious; new cycle, item 5/16).**  
**Claim attacked:** 24 claims the Build/repertoire cycle is fixed and the defined predicates remain well-founded.  
**Quote:** Build “contains an **itemised defect representation** … on an active route to the *acceptance* of the binding: the binding may be conjectured by any process, blind or not, and what makes the episode construction is that the record is used, in the sense of Part IX, in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept…”

### Finding 1 — Build reintroduces a cycle through reason use; Derivation 6’s well-founded-order claim is false.

**(1) Claim attacked.** 24 Part X: “what makes the episode construction is that the record is used, in the sense of Part IX, in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept.” Part IX: “A response uses a reason when a structural map from the **represented** objection into the response suborganization…” Part IV defines representation by (R): \(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\exists t[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\). Con uses Build.

**(2) Why it fails.** Build → Reason use → “represented objection” → (R) → Con → Build. This is exactly the kind of cycle Derivation 6 claims does not exist. Derivation 6 says: “(iv) Build’s ‘already carried’ and ‘required’ clauses are stated by instantiation, not through the repertoire of Deploy, so Build does not pass through (R) either.” That sentence is now false: the defect-record clause of Build passes through reason use, which passes through representation. Part XIV’s dependence order also says only “Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E),” omitting reason use and (R).

**(3) Severity.** Fatal. It destroys the claimed well-founded stratification and the separation of construction from representation.

**(4) Fix.** Define the use of the defect record in Build without invoking (R): e.g., by an active route and instantiated structures, or exclude reason use from Build and add a separate non-representational predicate for “criticism that the binding survives.” Then update Derivation 6 and Part XIV’s dependence order.

### Finding 2 — Bearing is vacuous and ill-typed; Valid defect 2.10 survives.

**(1) Claim attacked.** 24 Part IX: “Let \(p_\delta\) be the question whose target organization is (the organization \(z\) instantiates) and whose query asks whether \(\delta\) obtains, and let \(E_c\) be the organization the criticism supplies, with the grounds \(g\) as its boundary conditions and the connection \(t_c\) as its transport. Then \(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta,t_c,\Gamma_c)\).” The text then says: “The named data do work: a criticism with the wrong target, or grounds that are not its boundary, does not bear.”

**(2) Why it fails.** \(p_\delta\) is *defined* from \(z\) and \(\delta\), and \(E_c\) is *defined* with \(g\) as its boundary conditions. So there is no independent condition that \(z\) is the target or that \(g\) are the grounds; any criticism is fitted to its own target and grounds by construction. “Wrong target” cannot be expressed. Also, “the organization \(z\) instantiates” uses the definite article where Part IV insists that \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\) is a relation and every definition must quantify over \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,\cdot)\). The connection \(t_c\) is merely called a “connection” and is then used as a transport in Account, with no typing.

**(3) Severity.** Serious. The exact Valid defect 2.10 survives under new wording, and a new type error is added.

**(4) Fix.** Require independently given \(p_\delta\) and \(E_c\), with explicit conditions \(\operatorname{target}(p_\delta)=z\) and \(\operatorname{boundary}(E_c)=g\), and type \(t_c\) as a transport from \(z\)’s organization to \(E_c\). Replace “the organization \(z\) instantiates” with an existential over \(\operatorname{Inst}\).

### Finding 3 — Derivation 3’s proof now equivocates between survival as a property and survival as an event in the run.

**(1) Claim attacked.** 24 Part IV: “the members that survived were exactly the members faithful on \(H\) (a fact about the run, not a definition of survival, and the hypothesis Derivation 3 uses).” Derivation 3: “The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives; it differs at \((a,b)\) by hypothesis.”

**(2) Why it fails.** Survival is now “a fact about the run,” i.e., an event in \(\Sigma_h\). The closure hypothesis says \(\mathcal T\) contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) except off \(H\). It does not say that this altered pair was generated by \(\mu\) in \(\Sigma_h\), subjected to the enacted survival condition, and actually survived. The proof uses “agrees on \(H\)” to infer “survives,” but the premise only records that, among the actual run’s members, survivors coincided with fidelity on \(H\). The altered pair may not be in the run at all. The derivation therefore proves at most a counterfactual claim about a different run, not a claim about the selected survivor’s fallibility off \(H\).

**(3) Severity.** Serious. It undermines Derivation 3, which is cited in Grievance 3 and Part XV’s non-fallible-selection attack.

**(4) Fix.** Either (a) define the survival condition as a condition on members (not merely a coincidence), while recording the run coincidence as evidence, or (b) state closure and the survival condition so that for every altered pair in \(\mathcal T\), the pair is generated and subjected to the same survival condition in \(\Sigma_h\). Otherwise the proof’s “hence survives” is invalid.

### Finding 4 — Mixed provenance is declared but not enforced; “does not count as constructed for (G)” has no formal effect.

**(1) Claim attacked.** 24 Part IV: “If they are of both kinds, \(t\) has **mixed** provenance, which is declared as such and does not count as constructed for \((G)\).”

**(3) Severity.** Serious. The promised distinction is not implemented.

### Finding 5 — Objectivity’s “transport being extended to such pairs by \(\lambda\)” is undefined; Valid defects 2.2/2.3 survive in a new form.

**(2) Why it fails.** \(\tau\) is introduced in Part IV as “\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits,” but its domain is never stated as all of \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). The contract \(C\) is \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\). For an admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), \(\tau(a)\) may be undefined. \(\lambda\) is an anchor map from components of \(E\) to subnetworks of \(D\); it is not an edit-translation map. “The corresponding edit on the anchored component of \(E\)” is asserted, not defined, and if the edit touches several anchors, no single corresponding edit is specified. ### Finding 7 — The aggregate-summary exemption still permits itemised computation on the route.

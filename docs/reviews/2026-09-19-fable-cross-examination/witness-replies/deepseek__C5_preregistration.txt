## The principled line

Pre-registration is not about whether the author thought about the world before or after running code. It is about the evidential relationship between the prediction and the data.

**Legitimate pre-registration:** the prediction, the measurement, and the decision rule are fixed and timestamped before the relevant data are observed; the prediction is risky and could fail; the success criteria are not altered after seeing the data.

**Post-hoc rationalisation:** the criterion, scope, or classification is changed after seeing data, especially when the change rescues the hypothesis from a specific observed failure. The key counterfactual test is: would the same change have been made if the data had come out the other way? If not, it is post-hoc.

Applied here:

- **Change 2:** The prediction “C will not build the crossing rule” is theoretically sound, but it is analytically entailed by C’s algorithm, and the document does not establish that it was written before the relevant phase-1 data. More importantly, it modifies S3 after S3 was fixed. At best it is a pre-freeze amendment, not a pre-registered outcome.
- **Change 4:** This is post-hoc rationalisation. The original check failed against the lookup at 0.819, and the check was split after seeing that score. Recording the numbers is transparency, not pre-registration. The principled fix is to report the original failure and run the revised check on fresh data or as an explicitly exploratory analysis.

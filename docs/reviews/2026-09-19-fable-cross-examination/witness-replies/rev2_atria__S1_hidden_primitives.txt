## A. Circularity and the well-foundedness claim (Part XIV order; Derivation 6)

### 1. `Can` quantifies over "construction" — either a cycle or an unlisted informal term

**Attacked claim.** Part XII, Capability:

> "$\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)$ holds when the substrate at $\xi$, within the boundary $\beta$ and under the continuity criterion $\Omega$, holds a retained realization of $T$ under $\chi$, **or holds retained realizations from which a physically admitted, finite construction produces one**, under the same continuity and resource contract."

together with Derivation 6(ii):

> "ownership is defined from retained realization, and $\operatorname{Can}$ does not mention ownership"

and the guard in Part XII:

> "Ownership (Part X) is defined from this predicate, not used in it."

**Why it fails.** The guard and Derivation 6(ii) block the edge $\operatorname{Can}\to\text{ownership}\to\operatorname{Can}$. They do not block the edge that is actually created by the second disjunct of `Can`. Build (Part X) requires an **owned** subhistory; ownership is defined from `Can`; and `Can` now requires "a physically admitted, finite **construction**". The only defined notion of construction in the document is Part X's `Build`/`Con`. So either:

- (a) "construction" = `Build`, giving the cycle $\operatorname{Can}\to\operatorname{Build}\to\text{ownership}\to\operatorname{Can}$ — which is exactly the cycle Derivation 6 is supposed to exclude, and which its three listed checks do not touch; or
- (b) "construction" is informal ("assembly"/"composition"), in which case a *defined* predicate of Part XII contains an informal term that Part XIV's list omits, and Derivation 6's claim that every defined predicate is built from "the informal terms listed in Part XIV" is false.

Note that this is the linchpin of the whole order: I traced every cycle candidate you named — $(R)\to\operatorname{Con}\to\operatorname{Build}\to\text{ownership}\to\operatorname{Can}$; $\operatorname{Deploy}\to (R)$ and $\operatorname{Deploy}\to\operatorname{Can}$; $\operatorname{Attempt}\to$ active route; operative condition $\to$ anchoring $\to (E)\to (O)$; $\rho_p\to (R)$ — and **every one of them closes only through this clause**. If (a) holds, there is exactly one cycle and it is fatal to Derivation 6; if (b) holds, there is no cycle but the completeness claim fails.

**Severity.** Serious (fatal under reading (a)).

**Fix.** Replace the second disjunct with an explicitly defined composition operation on retained realizations stated entirely inside Part XII, e.g. "$T$ is the composition of a finite sequence of tasks $T_1\circ\cdots\circ T_n$ for which retained realizations are held", with composition defined from `Exec` and `(CT2)`, and add that clause to the dependence order.

### 2. Derivation 6's universal quantifier is not covered by the order it cites

**Attacked claim.** Derivation 6:

> "**Claim.** Every *defined* predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module …, the normative relation where invoked, the declared parameters and the informal terms listed in Part XIV, **by the dependence order there**, and that order has no cycle."

**Why it fails.** The dependence order in Part XIV lists (O), (Q), (K), (F1), (F2), (A), (E), anchoring, the operative condition, objectivity, (S), (B), (D), (R), provenance, (K1), Deploy, Build, (N), (G), (P), (EK), (RC), (U1)–(U3). "Following the order of Part XIV from each defined predicate reaches the base" cannot be executed for predicates the order does not name.

**Fix.** Extend the Part XIV order to a total listing of defined predicates with their inputs, including the nine above, and re-run Derivation 6 against the extended list.

### 3. Historical kinds depend on $(R)$, which the dependence order does not record

**Attacked claim.** Part XIV:

> "Forward kinds, from signatures (K); **historical kinds, from provenance witnesses**."

and

> "(R) depends on (F1)–(F2), $\operatorname{Inst}$ and physical provenance; **provenance depends on $\Theta$ and on Part X's witnesses**, which depend on $\operatorname{Inst}$ and $\equiv_\ell$ but not on (R)."

**Why it fails.** Two distinct provenance notions are conflated. *Transport* provenance (Sel/Con/Dec) is as the order says. But Part II defines a historical kind via

> "the provenance of the transport that **witnesses (R)** for it; where several do, the earliest."

So historical kinds (and $\rho_p$, whose witness is likewise "the transport by which an occurrence represents the question-organization") depend on $(R)$, hence on Sel/Con, hence on Build. Not a cycle — but it is an unrecorded dependence, and it means the "well-founded order" as printed is not the order of the definitions.

**Severity.** Minor-to-moderate (no cycle; bookkeeping error that undermines auditability).

**Fix.** Split "provenance" into transport-provenance and content-occurrence-provenance in the order, and record historical kinds and $\rho_p$ under the latter.

### 4. The active route is built from three undefined ingredients

**Attacked passage.** Part IX:

> "An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining an occurrence that instantiates the input organization (Part IV, Inst) to **an operative result**, whose components satisfy **the applicable relations** and which has nonconstant dependence on **the instantiated distinction** under the declared contrasts."

**Why it fails.** "An operative result", "the applicable relations", and "the instantiated distinction" (definite article, never anteceded) are not defined anywhere and are not on the list. Active route is not a peripheral gloss: it is used by Build (hence $\operatorname{Con}$, hence $(R)$), by Sel's blindness clause, by Attempt (hence (G)), by reason use, by ProducesVia (hence (EK)), and by the operative condition of Part V. Derivation 6(iii) asserts "the active route is defined on instantiated inputs, not represented ones" — true of the *first* clause only; the rest of the definition is informal and unlisted.

**Severity.** Serious.

**Fix.** Define "operative result" (e.g. an occurrence whose organization satisfies the applicable component relations under the declared contrasts), "applicable relations" (the component relations of the process occurrences in $h$), and "the instantiated distinction" (the difference in the input organization's ports that the input occurrence realizes); add all three to the Part XIV list.

### 5. `(EK)` rests on four undefined, unlisted notions

**Attacked passage.** Part X:

> "A complete critical episode contains **a recognized difficulty**, a target available before its criticism, **a conjectural objection**, and **a content-sensitive response**. A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G) **connected to its inquiry**."

used in Part XI:

> "$\operatorname{CreateEK}(s,\Delta,h,e)\iff{}\operatorname{CreativeCriticalEpisode}(s,\Delta,h,e)\land\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\land\cdots$"

**Why it fails.** "Recognized" (a judgement word), "conjectural", "content-sensitive", and "connected to its inquiry" are neither defined nor listed. The knowledge-creation class condition therefore cannot be evaluated from the two primitive packages plus the listed informal terms. Part 0 says "Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment" — fine — but the stronger problem is that (EK) is not yet a predicate of the formalism.

**Severity.** Serious.

**Fix.** Either formalize the four (e.g. content-sensitive response = an instance of the Part IX reason-use map; recognized difficulty = an obligation in $O_p$ failing at $\xi$ with a Bearing-holding criticism) or add all four to the informal list and state that (EK) is conditional on them.

**Fix.** Define Attempt as $\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi;U_p)$ with the use task $U_p$ targeted at $p$, plus the active-route and failing-obligation clauses as already stated; or list "use for a question" as informal.

### 9. "Causal responsibility" is inserted into the selection witness

### 10. An unformalized modal constraint on a declared parameter

## C. Bugs in the account conditions (Part V)

### 12. The non-circularity witness cannot exist for a one-component candidate — contradicting Part V's own example

**Attacked passages.** Part V, non-circular dependence:

> "The witness: there exists an edit $\tau(a)$ on $E$, translated from some $(a,b)\in C$, that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block $B\subsetneq\Gamma$ while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way. **Removing all of $\Gamma$ changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing.**"

versus Part V, "What (E) excludes":

> "The one-component candidate that repackages $\operatorname{Sol}_D$ for a target with several components **satisfies (E): its single commitment is removable, so non-circular dependence has its witness.**"

**Why it fails.** If $\Gamma=\{k\}$, the only nonempty sub-block is $\Gamma$ itself, which the witness clause excludes as improper. So no witness exists, and the one-component candidate **fails** (E). The two passages are inconsistent. This is not a corner case the author can quietly repair: the one-component candidate is the flagship illustration of "(E) alone is too weak, which is why the anchored grade exists" — the document's central load-distribution argument. If it fails (E), then (E) is stronger than advertised and properness is doing less additional work than claimed.

**Severity.** Serious.

**Fix.** Replace "proper sub-block $B\subsetneq\Gamma$" with "nonempty sub-block $B\subseteq\Gamma$ whose removal leaves a nonempty remainder or leaves the profile undetermined", and restrict the "removing all of $\Gamma$ witnesses nothing" clause to $|\Gamma|\ge 2$; or state non-circular dependence primarily via the independence condition ("no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports") with the edit-witness as a sufficient test only.

### 13. Non-vacuity is near-tautological, and does not do what Part V says it does

**Attacked passages.** Part V:

> "**Non-vacuity.** $\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing$. The contract $C$ is a stated subset of the admitted pairs together with a declared counterfactual set, in the form of Part III, and **every admitted pair excluded from $C$ is excluded by the stated scope $S$, not silently**."

and

> "An account whose only substantive contrast is one that no admitted edit realizes and that is not declared in $K$ with a stated meaning **fails non-vacuity**."

**Why it fails.** Since $C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K$ with $K\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=\varnothing$, we have $C\cap C_{\mathrm{adm}}=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S$, hence $C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C=C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus S$ identically. The clause "every admitted pair excluded from $C$ is excluded by $S$" is a tautology given the normal form of $C$; non-vacuity therefore reduces to $\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing$. An account whose contract contains only the baseline is not excluded by non-vacuity as stated. (It *is* excluded by non-circular dependence — but only because that witness requires a translated edit, which in turn means (E) can fail for reasons of contract poverty rather than circularity; see finding 14.)

**Severity.** Moderate.

**Fix.** Add a genuine non-vacuity clause: $C$ contains at least one pair $(a,b)\neq(1,b_0)$, and every active component of $E$ is touched by some pair of $C$ (a candidate-side analogue of grain admissibility, which currently constrains only the target's organization).

### 14. The non-circularity witness is stronger than the condition it witnesses

**Attacked passages.** Part V:

> "**Non-circular dependence.** The answer follows by evaluating $E$ under its independent boundary conditions."

versus

> "The witness: there exists an edit $\tau(a)$ on $E$, **translated from some $(a,b)\in C$**, that removes or replaces a nonempty proper sub-block…"

**Why it fails.** The condition is a claim about how the answer is determined; the witness is an existence claim about a *translated admitted edit with a specific effect*. An account whose answer genuinely follows from its components under independent boundaries, but whose contract contains no edit that translates to a sub-block removal (e.g. a contract containing only observation edits), fails (E). The gap between condition and witness is unstated, and it makes (E) contract-dependent in a way the "five conditions, none of which looks outside $C$" framing conceals.

**Severity.** Moderate.

**Fix.** Either prove that the witness is equivalent to the independence condition under the stated form of $C$, or state explicitly that (E) is assessable only for contracts that contain a component-replacing edit, and mark contracts that do not as a limit case.

## D. Under-stated hypotheses and incomplete constructions

### 15. Derivation 8's hypothesis list omits data the predicates quantify over

**Attacked passage.** Derivation 8:

> "let $\phi$ be a family of bijections on carriers, organizations (…), transports (including their anchor maps $\lambda$), histories with their events and precedence, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, such that $\phi$ preserves the physical module's admission ($A_\Theta$, $\operatorname{Inst}$, $\prec$), carries $\operatorname{Sol}$ to $\operatorname{Sol}$ (…), preserves the stated scopes and the relations declared for counterfactual pairs, and preserves $\mathcal N$. **Transporting all data along $\phi$ preserves (E), anchoring, (R), (G), (P), (EK).**"

**Why it fails.** (R) is indexed by grain $\ell$; Deploy, (G), (N) and the repertoire are indexed by $\beta$ and $\Omega$ and by states $\xi$; (P) and (EK) quantify over $\xi,\xi'$ and over obligation *values* $o(\xi),o(\xi')$; Deploy and Enable quantify over use tasks $U$ and enabling conditions $\chi$. None of states, $\ell$, $\beta$, $\Omega$, $U$, $\chi$ appears in $\phi$'s domain or in the preservation hypothesis. The proof's remark that "each of its inputs … is carried along" therefore does not cover them.

**Severity.** Moderate.

**Fix.** Extend $\phi$ to states, indices, use tasks and enabling conditions, require preservation of $\Omega$ and $\beta$, and note that obligation values are carried because states are.

### 16. Derivation 5 needs "pointed organizations", which $\operatorname{Inst}$ does not supply

**Attacked passage.** Derivation 5:

> "a particular question is a particular solution, and an occurrence carries *that* question when its instantiated organization is $D_p$ **with its ports fixed at that solution**."

**Why it fails.** An organization is the tuple $(V,X,J,B,A,L)$; it contains no designated valuation. $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$ is a relation between occurrences and such tuples, so "$D_p$ with its ports fixed at $z$" is not in its domain unless pointed organizations are admitted. Gap list (i)–(iv) does not include this.

**Severity.** Minor-to-moderate.

**Fix.** Either allow pointed organizations in the signature of $\operatorname{Inst}$ and say so, or represent a question-occurrence as instantiating $D_p$ plus a separate component whose relation fixes the valuation; add the gap to the list.

### 17. The Bearing gloss uses "represents" inside the definition of criticism-hood

**Attacked passage.** Part IX:

> "$\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)$. A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) fails. **An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears.**"

**Why it fails.** (K1) itself is clean (it is (E)). But the sentence fixes when something *is* a criticism, and "represents" there is either technical — in which case criticism-hood depends on $(R)$, hence on Sel/Con, hence on Build, and since criticism is used by scrutinizability and therefore by (RC), this is an unlisted dependence running from (RC) down to (R) — or informal, in which case it is an unlisted informal term. (Not a cycle: I checked whether anything in the $(R)$-chain uses criticism — nothing does.)

### 18. Selection requires survival to be *exactly* fidelity — an idealization that makes "selected" near-uninstantiable

**Attacked passage.** Part IV:

> "a survival condition that **holds of a member exactly when it is faithful on $H$ (fidelity on $H$ is necessary and sufficient for survival)**, which eliminated at least one member…"

with Part IV:

> "$P$ is held to $F$ by a transport that is **typically selected**: … the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held."

and Grievance 9:

> "Whether it occurred is a claim about the physical module, fallible but **checkable in principle**."

**Why it fails.** The clause builds selection *for* fidelity into the definition. Any real selection regime — evolutionary, reinforcement-based, or social — has a survival condition not coextensive with predictive fidelity on the encountered history (reproduction, energy budget, acceptance by peers). Under the definition as written, such transports are not selected; so "typically selected" for the primitive layer is a claim about an ideal model, not about actual systems, and Grievance 9's "checkable in principle" is checkable-and-usually-false. This is the assumption that lets Derivation 3 go through ("survival on $H$ constrains only the relations at pairs in $H$"), so it is load-bearing, not incidental.

**Severity.** Moderate.

**Fix.** Weaken to "the survival condition is *entailed by* fidelity on $H$ on the members of $\mathcal T$ actually encountered" (or state the exactness as an explicit idealization), and re-derive Derivation 3 under the weaker clause.

### 19. Historical kinds are defined but used by nothing

**Attacked claim.** Part II:

> "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component … Two components of one forward kind on every admitted change may be of different historical kinds. **Both are kinds.**"

**Why it fails.** No defined predicate in Parts V–XIII uses historical kinds. They appear only in Grievance 12, in the analysis-of-kinds conjecture, and in Part XV's admission that the selection-artefact construction "is not written" but "the historical kinds of Part II can carry" it. **Severity.** Moderate (overclaim of coverage, not an error).

**Fix.** Either wire historical kinds into a defined predicate (the selection-artefact case is the natural one, and Part XV already says what is missing), or state plainly that the conjecture's second disjunct is unsupported by any current definition.

## E. Cosmetic / typing

### 21. Sel's non-triviality clause has a typing slip

**Attacked passage.** Part IV:

> "**Non-triviality:** no event of $\Sigma_h$ instantiates an organization held **to $t$, to $H$, or to the survival condition** by a faithful transport."

**Why it fails.** "Held to" is the transport relation from a *content* to an organization. $t$ is a transport (permitted as a content by Part X, so this one survives), but $H$ is a set of edit–boundary pairs and the survival condition is a predicate; neither is a content or an organization. The clause is not well-typed as written.

**Severity.** Cosmetic.

**Fix.** Say what the content is: e.g. "no event instantiates an organization held by a faithful transport to the content *that $t$ is the witnessing transport for*, to the history $H$ qua content, or to the survival condition qua content", or drop the latter two.

### 22. Properness is ambiguous about overlapping anchors

**Attacked passage.** Part V:

> "The anchors of the active components refine a cover of the components of $D$ … and **the anchor map is injective on that cover: distinct active components have distinct anchors**, and no touched component of $D$ lies outside every anchor."

**Why it fails.** Read strictly, "distinct anchors" forbids two active components from sharing an anchor subnetwork, and a cover that must be refined injectively is effectively a partition — which excludes legitimate decompositions in which two explanatory components share target substructure (the Leibniz-expansion example, where "every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization", is a plausible such case). Read loosely, overlapping subnetworks are permitted as long as they are not identical. The strength of a load-bearing condition (it is what excludes the one-component table) is undetermined by the text.

**Severity.** Minor.

**Fix.** State whether anchor subnetworks may overlap, and if they may, restate injectivity as "no two active components have the same anchor subnetwork".

- The hunt you asked for specifically: **no** defined predicate uses "explains" or "is a cause" as a primitive; "represents" appears in the Bearing gloss (17) and, legitimately, as the defined predicate $(R)$; the judgement words that do real work are "recognized" (5), "correct" (10), and "causal" (9).

## Part VII, "Explanations that remove structure" — the eliminative treatment

**1. The deleted-subnetwork anchor is ill-typed.**
- **Claim attacked:** "The rival's supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O)," together with "a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent."
- **Why it fails:** A transport is \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\) where "\(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation." A subnetwork of \(D\) has ports that are ports of \(D\). If \(D\) lacks the rival's ports outright, there is no subnetwork of \(D\) with those ports, hence no \(\lambda(k)\), no port translation, and (F1) cannot be stated for the rival-structure component. If instead \(D\) *has* those ports with deleted (full) components, then "the ports and components a rival account would need are absent" is false — they are present and vacuous. The construction has no well-defined reading.
- **Seriousness:** serious. It is the load-bearing sentence of the whole eliminative schema.
- **Fix:** State which of the two is the case. If the rival's ports are in \(D\) with full relations, say so and drop "absent"; if they are not in \(D\), define \(\lambda\) for components whose ports have no counterpart, e.g. by allowing partial anchors and proving (F1) is then vacuous rather than satisfied.

**2. The schema has no admissible \(\Gamma\), so non-circular dependence cannot be witnessed.**
- **Claim attacked:** The same passage, plus the non-circularity condition: "There exists \((a,b)\in C\) that removes or replaces a nonempty block of \(\Gamma\) ... under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way."
- **Why it fails:** On the natural reading of the schema, the active commitment is the deleted rival structure. But "a deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports," so removing or replacing it changes nothing; the answer profile is unchanged, and non-circular dependence fails. The odd-order example escapes only by taking \(\Gamma=\{\)skewness, oddness\(\}\) — constraints of the *target*, not the rival's structure — which is a different schema from the one stated in prose.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Specify \(\Gamma\) for the general eliminative case and exhibit the non-circularity witness explicitly; do not let the one worked example carry a schema its \(\Gamma\) does not instantiate.

**3. Derivation 2 collapses the eliminative account into the restatement.**
- **Claim attacked:** "This is the semantics' treatment of eliminative explanation; it is offered as adequate."
- **Why it fails:** Take \(E'=D\) with the identity transport. (F1) and (F2) hold trivially; (A) holds because \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b)\) by (Q); non-circularity is witnessed by removing a target constraint; non-vacuity by a baseline solution. So \(D\) itself satisfies Account for its own eliminative question. Derivation 2 then says \(E'\) and the eliminative account "are one account at grain \(C\)." The semantics therefore cannot distinguish "there is no \(X\)-effect because \(X\) is absent and \(X\) would produce it" from a genuine eliminative explanation. The document concedes the shallow half elsewhere ("depth is question-relative and (E) measures none of it") but still claims adequacy here.
- **Seriousness:** serious — this is what makes "adequate" unsupportable, and it is an internal contradiction, not an external objection.
- **Fix:** Either add a condition that separates derivational content from target structure (and show it does not reinstate a primitive "really explains"), or withdraw the adequacy claim and say plainly that at grain \(C\) the eliminative account and the restatement are identified.

**4. Eliminative explanations by impossibility are unrepresentable.**
- **Claim attacked:** "a contract containing the edits that would introduce them," together with Grievance 4: "The physical theory fixes which changes are possible at all. A contract is a declared subset of those."
- **Why it fails:** Where the rival's structure is physically *impossible* (perpetual motion, absolute simultaneity in the fixed theory), the introducing edit is not physically admitted, so no physically admitted contract can contain it; non-circularity and non-vacuity then have nothing to witness and the question cannot be posed. If you instead admit well-defined-but-impossible edits, Grievance 4's objectivity claim is void: "physically admitted" no longer means anything. The document must choose, and either choice loses a class of cases.
- **Seriousness:** fatal for eliminative explanations whose point is that the rival's structure is not a possibility at all; serious overall.
- **Fix:** Define "physically admitted" for model edits separately from physical possibility, state which class of eliminative explanations falls outside it, and withdraw adequacy for that class.

**5. Provenance of the contract is unchecked by Account.**
- **Claim attacked:** Part V's four conditions (none mentions \(\rho_p\)), versus Part XV attack (A)'s requirement "with a non-declared transport," and Part III's "A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\)."
- **Why it fails:** In an eliminative account the explanatory substance lives in the contrast set — which edits count as "introducing \(X\)." That contrast is part of the contract, which Account never inspects. So a candidate can satisfy all of (E) with a *declared* contract while the transport is non-declared, and the sufficiency defence is satisfied by the part that carries no explanatory weight. The non-declaration requirement is applied to the wrong component.
- **Seriousness:** serious (moved goalpost within the document's own defeat conditions).
- **Fix:** Add \(\rho_p\neq\text{declared}\) to the sufficiency test in Part XV, or explain why declared contracts are harmless here when they are not harmless for creativity claims.

## Non-causal explanation

**6. Symmetry explanation is absent and cannot be represented without circularity.**
- **Claim attacked:** Part VII as a whole (production, identification, obstruction, eliminative, constitutive rules — no symmetry case), against Part 0's Grievance 7: "An admitted change need not be a physical intervention... This is worked in Part VII."
- **Why it fails:** A symmetry explanation's force is the *entailment* from invariance to outcome-independence. One can anchor an invariance component of \(E\) to an orbit subnetwork of \(D\) and satisfy (F1) — but that records the invariance as a fact. The entailment itself is a relation *among* \(E\)'s components, and (F1) is per-component while (F2) is assembly; neither constrains it. Anchoring the entailment in \(D\) requires anchoring it to the very independence being explained. So the only faithful transports either omit the explanatory content (and by Derivation 2 are the restatement) or import the explanandum.
- **Seriousness:** serious — a live instance of attack (B).
- **Fix:** Add a symmetry case to Part VII, define how an entailment among \(E\)'s components is anchored without using the explanandum, and show (F1)–(A) non-vacuous on that anchor.

**7. The entailment gap is general; the odd-order example escapes only by accident.**
- **Claim attacked:** "The full Leibniz expansion with skewness substituted satisfies (F1) and (F2): every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away."
- **Why it fails:** The example works because determinant minors happen to be structurally mirrored in the target, so the inferential structure of the proof doubles as a componentwise correspondence. That is special to determinant-type arguments. For a proof whose explanatory step is a symmetry, a compactness, a counting, or a selection argument, the step is not mirrored by any subnetwork of \(D\), and nothing in (F1)/(F2)/(A) certifies that \(E\)'s inferential structure is doing work. (E) certifies component-matching, never derivation.
- **Seriousness:** serious — this is why the one worked mathematical case does not support the general claim.
- **Fix:** State the class of mathematical explanations for which the inferential structure is mirrored in the target, and mark the rest as open rather than claiming Grievance 7 discharged.

**8. Statistical / selection-artefact explanation is not treated, and the artefact gets reified.**
- **Claim attacked:** Part VII's absence, plus (K)'s classification: "a **measurement** has a signature invariant under interventions on the measured port and variable under edits to the measuring relation."
- **Why it fails:** To explain an effect *away* as a selection artefact, the effect must be absent in the population and present in the data. Under (F1) the artefact component of \(E\) needs an anchor in \(D\); if \(D\) is the data, the artefact is anchored as genuine target structure, which is what the explanation denies; if \(D\) is the population, the artefact's ports are absent and Finding 1's typing failure returns. The nearest existing machinery, (I2)'s identification condition, answers "what do the readings identify?" — an inference-limitation claim — not "why is the apparent effect unreal?"
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Add a two-target treatment (data organization and population organization) with a transport for each, and show how the artefact component is anchored in one and deleted in the other without the typing failure of Finding 1.

**9. Obstruction inherits the same gap.**
- **Claim attacked:** "For allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) with \(zRz'\Rightarrow I(z)=I(z')\), no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1)"
- **Why it fails:** (O1) is asserted in prose as a theorem the semantics *uses*. The invariant's anchor in \(D\), and the invariance-entails-unreachability step, are never organized into an \(E\) satisfying (F1). If \(I\) is a component of \(E\) anchored to a subnetwork of \(D\), the explanatory content is the preservation claim, which is a relation between \(R\) and \(I\), not a component.
- **Seriousness:** serious (the three worked non-eliminative cases all have the same shape).
- **Fix:** Construct the obstruction account explicitly with its \(\Gamma\), its \(\lambda\), and its non-circularity witness, rather than stating the theorem and asserting the account.

**10. Constitutive rules get an identification treatment, not an explanation.**
- **Claim attacked:** "A rule relation \(C_r\subseteq Z\times S\) answers 'which status under this rule?' by its fibre."
- **Why it fails:** The fibre answers *what* the status is. "Why is *this* the rule / why does this practice have this constitution" is a production question, and the semantics' own (K) classification makes the rule's content invariant under world-interventions and variable only under rule-edits — so there is nothing in the target for a transport to anchor the rule's content to, short of a history. That may be the right answer, but then Part VII should say the case is deferred to history, not list it among its treatments.
- **Seriousness:** minor.
- **Fix:** One line: constitutive explanation of content is handled at the history layer, not by (E).

## Explanations that change the question

**11. There is no Part VII treatment of question-changing explanation, and (A) forbids it.**
- **Claim attacked:** "For every \((a,b)\in C\), \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\). The query \(\mathcal Q\) is held fixed; an account of a different query is not an account of this one."
- **Why it fails:** A reframe ("the explanandum is not \(X\) but \(Y\)") is, by this condition, never an account of \(p\). It can only be an account of \(p'\). So the explanatory force of the reframe — that \(p\) was the wrong question about this target — is not assessed by (E) at all. The document's machinery (Derivations 5 and 7) addresses *representability* and *index consistency*, not adequacy.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Add a condition under which a contract-change is an improvement on a target, or state that the semantics assesses reframes only for novelty and construction, never for fit, and withdraw any adequacy claim for this class.

**12. (G) and (EK) credit novelty and provenance, not correctness of the reframe.**
- **Claim attacked:** Derivation 5's "It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O). Deploy, Build, and New apply," and \(\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c\).
- **Why it fails:** A reframe to a *wrong* question that is new and built satisfies (G) as fully as a reframe to the right one; a reframe to the right question reached by selection satisfies none of it. The only normative input is the declared obligation set \(O_p\) (Part III) and the primitive \(\mathcal N\) (Part XI), which the semantics "does not derive." So the correctness of the question found — which is the whole explanatory content of a question-changing explanation — is either declared or primitive.
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Say so explicitly in Part VII rather than leaving Derivation 5 to carry an adequacy claim it does not support.

## Scope mismatch between the attack and the defence

**13. "Physically admitted" is undefined for the flagship mathematical example.**
- **Claim attacked:** Part 0 attack (B): "under *any* physically admitted contract"; Part XV: "An explanation without a faithful transport... refutes necessity. Eliminative explanation (Part VII) is the exposed case."
- **Why it fails:** The odd-order contract is "remove skewness; remove oddness; remove both; field arithmetic and determinant–invertibility held fixed." Part XII's physical module supplies \(\operatorname{Org}_\ell\) and admission conditions for substrates, tasks, and constructors; nothing says which edits are physically admitted on a mathematical organization. Grievance 7 says an admitted change "need not be a physical intervention," but never defines the non-physical admission relation. So the strongest worked example does not fall under the quantifier of the attack it is offered to answer.
- **Seriousness:** serious (the example is analogical support, not a satisfaction of (B)).
- **Fix:** Define "admitted" for mathematical organizations — either via a representational/physical realization of the proof object, or by a separate admission relation — and then re-run the example inside that definition.

**14. Background components in the odd-order case have unstated (F1) status.**
- **Claim attacked:** "field arithmetic and determinant–invertibility held fixed."
- **Why it fails:** (F1) quantifies over "every active component \(k\) of \(E\)." The sign and permutation structure of the Leibniz expansion is neither in \(\Gamma\) nor shown to have anchors. Reading it as fixed background is reasonable, but the scope of "active" is left implicit, and the example's validity depends on it.
- **Seriousness:** minor / cosmetic.
- **Fix:** State that \(\Gamma\) is \(\{\)skewness, oddness\(\}\) and that the arithmetic is ambient, not a component of \(E\).

**15. The hedge does not cover the overclaim.**
- **Claim attacked:** "...it is offered as adequate and is listed under attack (B) as a place where it may not be."
- **Why it fails:** The concession that the treatment *may* be inadequate is not a repair. Under Findings 1–3 the schema is ill-typed, has no admissible \(\Gamma\), and is identified with the restatement by the document's own Derivation 2; under Finding 4 an entire subclass is unrepresentable. "May not be adequate" should read "is not adequate as stated."
- **Seriousness:** serious.
- **Fix:** Rewrite the passage with the repaired schema and the explicitly excluded subclasses, or downgrade the claim to a conjecture with the three defects above acknowledged.

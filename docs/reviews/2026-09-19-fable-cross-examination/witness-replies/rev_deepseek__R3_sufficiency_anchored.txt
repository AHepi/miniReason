## 2. The specific attempts and what blocks them

### Finding 1: One-component repackaging is blocked, but multi-component identity is not

The document says:

> “The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3.”

That is true for the one-component case. Properness blocks it because its single anchor is the whole target. No global relation blocks it because the component relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\). But the document does not consider the multi-component case where \(E\) is an isomorphic copy of \(D\) with anchors to \(D\)’s own components. That case passes properness, resolution, no-global-relation, and world-kind. It is a trivial restatement, yet it satisfies the anchored grade. The one-component exclusion does not generalize.

### Finding 2: Two-component split of the one-component repackaging

If \(D\) has only one component, then any proper subnetwork of \(D\) cannot include that component. Properness requires that no component of \(D\) touched by \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) lies outside every anchor. So any split into two components with proper anchors fails properness: the touched component cannot be covered. Thus the two-component split is blocked by properness only when \(D\) has one component. If \(D\) has two or more components, the “split” is just a decomposition of \(D\) into its own components, and it passes as in Finding 1. So the condition does not block the general two-component case.

### Finding 3: Lookup with a proper decomposition is not blocked

The document concedes:

> “A table that genuinely encodes an organization’s response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account.”

This is an admission that a faithfully decomposed lookup table satisfies the anchored grade. If we take a lookup table with multiple rows and make each row a component, with anchors to proper subnetworks, and design the rows so that no row relation equals a projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\), then condition 3 is satisfied. Properness is satisfied if there is more than one row. World-kind is satisfied if the rows respond to edits as their anchors do. So the anchored grade admits a faithfully decomposed lookup table. If lookup tables explain nothing, this is another counterexample to sufficiency. The document bites the bullet by calling it an account, but that is exactly the overclaim.

### Finding 4: A decoy with the same world-kind is not blocked

Condition 4 requires each active component of \(E\) to be of one forward kind with its anchor on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). If a decoy responds identically to every admitted change, it is of one forward kind with the anchor by definition. The only extra clause is the active-route condition:

> “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an active route (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.”

This excludes a backup. It does not exclude an active decoy that is behaviorally identical on every admitted change. Such a decoy may have a different history, but history enters only historical kinds, not the anchored grade. So condition 4 does not block a decoy with the same world-kind. It only blocks a decoy whose response differs on some admitted edit, which is already blocked by fidelity on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

## 3. Do the four anchored conditions exclude genuine mechanisms?

### Finding 5: Resolution excludes some genuine integrative mechanisms

Condition 2 says:

> “Whenever two components of \(D\) can be separately varied by pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with distinct effects on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), they lie in distinct anchors. The decomposition of \(E\) is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about.”

Suppose \(D\) has two components \(j_1,j_2\) that are separately variable with distinct effects on the answer, and a genuine mechanism \(E\) has one component \(k\) whose relation is a joint constraint coupling \(j_1\) and \(j_2\). For (F1) to hold, \(\lambda(k)\) must be a subnetwork of \(D\) whose projection equals that joint constraint. If the joint constraint is implicit only in the intersection of \(j_1\) and \(j_2\), then \(\lambda(k)=\{j_1,j_2\}\). But then \(j_1\) and \(j_2\) lie in the same anchor, violating condition 2. So condition 2 forces a finer decomposition than the mechanism. It can exclude a genuine joint constraint. The fix would be to allow an anchor to contain multiple independently variable \(D\)-components when the component’s relation is a non-projection joint function of them, or to apply resolution only to components of \(E\) that are not themselves the joint constraint.

### Finding 6: No global relation excludes genuine global constraints

Condition 3 says:

> “No active component’s relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target’s own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name.”

Some genuine explanations consist in a global conservation law, a symmetry principle, or a holistic constraint that is exactly a relation on all ports. The document already concedes that explanation by symmetry is not treated. Condition 3 makes it impossible to treat such cases at all, because any such global constraint is either \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) or a projection. So the anchored grade is too strong: it excludes a class of genuine mechanisms by name. The fix would be to distinguish a global constraint that is the mechanism from a mere repackaging by requiring independent support or by allowing global anchors when they are the only proper locus.

### Finding 7: Properness excludes holistic explanations

Condition 1 says:

> “Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\)… no anchor is the whole target.”

If a genuine mechanism is a global state or whole-system property, and no proper subnetwork carries it, then properness excludes it. For example, explaining local uniformity by global thermodynamic equilibrium. The document might reply that one can always decompose, but that is an assumption, not a consequence. Properness is too strong for holistic mechanisms.

### Finding 8: World-kind excludes redundant mechanisms

Condition 4’s active-route clause says:

> “A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature, and it is what the anchored grade’s fourth condition uses.”

In many genuine mechanisms, a standby or backup component is part of the mechanism’s reliability and disposition. The condition excludes it from anchoring because it is not on the active route. That can exclude a genuine mechanism whose function depends on redundancy. The fix would be to allow backups to be part of the anchor when their presence is part of the mechanism’s function, not merely an alternative route.

**2. The identity account \(E=D\) satisfies all six conjuncts of (E) and all four anchored conditions.**
(1) Claim attacked: the anchored grade is supposed to exclude trivial repackaging and admit only genuine mechanisms.
(2) Why it fails: let \(D\) be any target with at least two components, e.g. \(j_1\): \(y=x\), \(j_2\): \(z=y\). Let \(E=D\), with identity transport \(t:D\to D\), and \(\Gamma=\{k_1,k_2\}\) corresponding to \(j_1,j_2\). Anchors: \(\lambda(k_1)=\{j_1\}\), \(\lambda(k_2)=\{j_2\}\). Check: (F1) and (F2) hold by identity; (A) holds; NonCircular holds because deleting \(k_1\) changes the answer profile; NonVacuous and Adequate hold. Properness holds because each anchor is a proper subnetwork (D has two components). Resolution holds because \(j_1,j_2\) can be separately varied with distinct effects. No global relation holds because neither \(k_1\) nor \(k_2\) is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) or a projection of it. World-kind holds trivially. If \(D\) is physical and its components are on the active route, the active-route clause is satisfied. If a selection history is stipulated in which a swapped transport is eliminated and the identity survives, the transport is non-declared. This candidate is an account of \(D\) by \(D\)’s own components. It plainly explains nothing when \(D\) is a bare constraint set or lookup table.
(3) Seriousness: fatal to the sufficiency claim.
(4) Fix: add a condition that \(E\) is not isomorphic to \(D\) or that anchors cannot be the target’s own components, though this risks circularity.

**3. A decomposed ROM lookup table satisfies all conditions and plainly explains nothing.**
(1) Claim attacked: the anchored grade excludes lookup tables.
(2) Why it fails: concrete candidate. Let \(D\) be a ROM with ports \(A\in\{1,2\}\), \(Y\in\{a,b\}\). Components: \(j_1\): if \(A=1\) then \(Y=a\); \(j_2\): if \(A=2\) then \(Y=b\). Admitted edits: set \(A=1\), set \(A=2\). Question: why is \(Y=f(A)\)? Candidate \(E=D\), identity transport, anchors \(\lambda(k_1)=\{j_1\}\), \(\lambda(k_2)=\{j_2\}\). Properness: each anchor is proper. Resolution: \(j_1,j_2\) separately variable with distinct effects on the answer profile. No global relation: neither component is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\). World-kind: trivially satisfied. Actuality: if the contract includes both addresses, each component is an active route on some edit. NonCircular: deleting \(j_1\) leaves \(Y\) unconstrained for \(A=1\), so the answer changes. This candidate is a lookup table. It says \(Y=a\) when \(A=1\) because the cell for \(A=1\) says \(Y=a\). That is circular and explains nothing.
(3) Seriousness: fatal.
(4) Fix: clarify the active-route condition to require that every active component be on the active route for the specific \((a,b)\in C\) under assessment. This would block the ROM, but it may over-exclude genuine mechanisms with input-specific components.

**4. The active-route condition is ambiguous and therefore does not block the ROM lookup table.**
(1) Claim attacked: “Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.”
(2) Why it fails: the condition does not say whether “each anchor” must be an active route for every \((a,b)\in C\), for the history as a whole, or for the particular question. In the ROM candidate, \(j_2\) is not on the active route for \(A=1\), but it is on the active route for \(A=2\). Under the per-history reading, the ROM passes; under a per-edit reading, it fails. The text does not settle this.
(3) Seriousness: serious.
(4) Fix: state explicitly that for every \((a,b)\in C\), every anchor of an active component must lie on the active route for that pair, or state the weaker per-history reading and accept the consequence.

**5. A two-component split of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) is blocked only in its naive projection form.**
(1) Claim attacked: the anchored grade excludes two-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\).
(2) Why it fails: if the two components have relations that are projections of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\), condition 3 excludes them: “No active component's relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it.” But if each component is anchored to a proper subnetwork of \(D\), and its relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}\), then it is not a projection of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\). The candidate is simply \(E=D\) with a partition of \(D\)’s components. It satisfies all anchored conditions if \(D\) has a proper decomposition. This is the identity account again.
(3) Seriousness: serious.
(4) Fix: the condition needs to exclude not only projections of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) but also any account whose components are just a repartition of the target’s own components.

**6. A decoy with the same world-kind is not blocked if it is on the active route.**
(1) Claim attacked: “a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4.”
(2) Why it fails: if a decoy has the same world-kind on every physically admitted change, then by condition 4 it is of one forward kind with its anchor on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\). If it is not on the active route, the active-route clause excludes it. But if it is on the active route and shares world-kind, then under the semantics it is not a decoy; it is a genuine component. The only way to call it a decoy is to appeal to a finer contract or a history, which the semantics represents as a new question or a historical kind. The four anchored conditions cannot exclude an active component that shares world-kind on all admitted changes.
(3) Seriousness: serious.
(4) Fix: none within the current vocabulary; the attack must be framed with a finer contract or a history.

**7. The four anchored conditions exclude explanation by symmetry, a genuine mechanism.**
(1) Claim attacked: the anchored grade is adequate for mechanisms.
(2) Why it fails: the document itself concedes: “Two further families are **not treated** here and are listed under attack (B): explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among \(E\)’s components …; and explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact.” A symmetry explanation often uses a global invariant or an entailment among components that is not a proper decomposition into subnetworks each anchored to a proper part. Properness and no-global-relation exclude such a component.
(3) Seriousness: serious.
(4) Fix: either develop a separate condition for global invariants or explicitly restrict the anchored grade to non-symmetry explanations.

**8. The four anchored conditions exclude dormant capacities and dispositions.**
(1) Claim attacked: “each anchor is an **active route** … and not a backup that would have responded identically.”
(2) Why it fails: many genuine mechanisms are not currently active. A repair mechanism in a cell, a dormant immune response, or a safety system explains what would happen if activated, but has no active route in the current history. The anchored grade requires an active route, so it excludes these explanations.
(3) Seriousness: serious.
(4) Fix: allow anchors that are retained realizations or potential routes under an enabling condition, not only actually active routes.

**9. The four anchored conditions exclude selection-artefact explanations.**
(1) Claim attacked: the anchored grade handles explanations of apparent effects.
(2) Why it fails: the document concedes that explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact “needs two targets (the data organization and the population organization) and a transport for each. Neither has a construction in this document.” The anchored grade is defined for a single target \(D\). So it cannot represent this genuine explanatory form.
(3) Seriousness: serious.
(4) Fix: extend the anchored grade to multi-target explanations, or concede the limit explicitly under attack (F).

**10. Condition 3 may exclude legitimate effective components that are projections of the global solution set.**
(1) Claim attacked: “No active component's relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it.”
(2) Why it fails: in systems with hidden connections, the effective local relation of a subsystem can be exactly the projection of the global solution set onto its visible ports. Such a component can be a genuine explanatory component at a coarse grain. Condition 3 excludes it by name, even when it passes properness and world-kind.
(3) Seriousness: minor to serious.
(4) Fix: restrict the exclusion to components that are the whole target’s solution set or that fail properness, and allow projections when they are the correct effective relation of a proper subnetwork with hidden ports.

**11. The anchored grade has no provenance condition, so “non-declared transport” is not part of it.**
(1) Claim attacked: attack (A) asks for a candidate satisfying the anchored grade “with a non-declared transport.”
(2) Why it fails: the text states: “The stronger grade, **anchored account-hood**, adds four conditions on the anchor map.” None of the four conditions mentions \(\operatorname{Sel}\) or \(\operatorname{Con}\). (E) itself “carries **no provenance condition**.” So the anchored grade alone does not require a non-declared transport. The attack as stated cannot be assessed against the anchored grade without adding a condition that is not in Part V.
(3) Seriousness: minor to serious.
(4) Fix: add a provenance condition to the anchored grade if that is what is intended, or restate attack (A) as targeting the anchored grade plus (R) or (EK).

**12. The count of conditions is inconsistent.**
(1) Claim attacked: “An \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold” followed by (E) containing six conjuncts: (F1), (F2), (A), NonCircular, NonVacuous, Adequate.
(2) Why it fails: the text says five conditions, but Component fidelity contains two separate conjuncts and (E) has six. This is a bookkeeping error.
(3) Seriousness: cosmetic.
(4) Fix: say six conjuncts or group (F1) and (F2) as one condition consistently.

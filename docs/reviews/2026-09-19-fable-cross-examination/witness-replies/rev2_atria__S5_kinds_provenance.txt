## Findings

**1. The anchored grade's "world-kind" has no baseline to exclude, so condition 3 is not well-defined as indexed.**
Part II: "Two subnetworks $U,U'$ … are **of one forward kind on $(\ell,C)$** when there is a bijection of their visible ports … under which their signatures coincide on $C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}$. Only the question's baseline pair is excluded, on purpose." Part V condition 3: "Each active component of $E$ is of one forward kind, on $(\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})$, with its anchor subnetwork," glossed as "a world-kind is a fact about $(\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})$."
But $C_{\mathrm{adm}}=A_\Theta(o)\times B$ contains $(1,b)$ for *every* $b\in B$; it is not a question's contract and has no distinguished $b_0$. Part II's relation is defined only for question-contracts (it is introduced with "a contract $C\subseteq A\times B$ (Part III)," and Part III's contracts contain exactly one baseline). So condition 3 either drops the exclusion (changing the definition, and making every single-boundary signature difference a kind difference, since now every $(1,b)$ separates) or imports some question's $b_0$ (making the "world"-kind question-relative after all). *Serious.* Fix: add $b_0$ to the index of the anchored grade and state the world-kind as a fact about $(\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}},b_0)$, retracting the claim that it is a fact about $(\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})$ alone.

**2. The document's own gloss for the exclusion shows the exclusion is question-relative, i.e., that "state" vs "kind" is drawn by the question.**
"what a component imposes at the baseline is, as a gloss, its *state* … the identity edit at the baseline separates nothing, while the identity edit at a different boundary is a response to a change of boundary and does separate."
The second half is symmetric: relative to a question whose baseline is $b$, the pair $(1,b_0)$ is "a response to a change of boundary $b\to b_0$." The asymmetry is imposed by which boundary the question calls baseline, not by the module. So the anchored grade is fidelity on the full admitted contract *modulo the question's baseline* — not "component fidelity on the full admitted contract," as advertised. *Serious.* Fix: state the modulo explicitly, or justify the exclusion in module terms (e.g., the module supplies a rest state) rather than in question terms.

**3. The port bijection in the forward-kind relation is existentially quantified and not required to agree with $\lambda$, so condition 3 is weaker than its prose.**
Part II: "there is a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains, under which their signatures coincide." Part V condition 3 is meant to enforce "fidelity holds … on every pair the module admits."
Nothing ties the bijection to the anchor map. A component $k$ whose signature fails to match its anchor under $\lambda$'s port translation on some pair in $C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C$, but matches under a *permuted* bijection, satisfies condition 3 as written while failing the intended claim. Derivation 1 quietly repairs this by fixing the visible ports ("the visible ports of the anchor being those $\lambda$ translates to $V_k$"), but Part II makes visible ports "a declared subset," so Derivation 1 assumes a declaration Part II leaves free. *Serious.* Fix: define the subnetwork signature's visible ports and the kind-relation's bijection via $\lambda$'s port translation.

**4. A historical kind of a *component* is not defined: the definition does not type-check.**
Part II: "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component … with the witness that Part IV requires for the provenance of a content's occurrence, applied to the binding that realizes the component."
Part IV's witness is for *contents* — "(R)" is $\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)$, and "The **provenance of a content's occurrence** is the provenance of the transport that witnesses (R) for it." A component is an index $j\in J$, a slot ("A component is a slot in an organization," Grievance 12); a *binding* is transport data ("A **binding** is an assignment $\lambda(k)$ of a component of the built organization to a subnetwork of its target"). Neither is a content, so (R)'s witness relation does not apply. Also $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$ is explicitly a relation, not a function ("one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain"), so "the occurrence that realizes component $j$" is not unique. *Serious.* Fix: define, for a component $j$ of $D$ relative to a *named* occurrence $o$ with $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)$ and a *named* transport $t$ whose $\lambda$ covers $j$, a historical kind as the provenance of $t$; then state that it is indexed by $(o,t)$, not by the component.

**5. Historical kinds cannot carry the load Part XV assigns them, because they are undefined for *target* components and "a component was selected" is not well-formed.**
Part XV, attack (B)(ii): "what is missing is … a provenance clause on the target's own components (that the effect's component was selected by the data-gathering, not by the population), which the historical kinds of Part II can carry."
Target components lie in the *image* of $\lambda$, not its domain ("$\lambda$ assigns each component of $E$ a subnetwork of $D$"), so "the binding that realizes the component" is undefined for them; and Part IV's selection predicate is $\operatornamename{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)$ — a predicate on *transports*, with a population of organization–transport *pairs*. There is no predicate in the semantics for "a component was selected," so the proposed closure of attack (B)(ii) is not expressible as things stand. *Serious.* Fix: either define component-level provenance independently (a witness for the occurrence realizing the component, not for a transport) or withdraw the claim that Part II's historical kinds can carry the clause.

**6. "Descended from, copied from" are not among the defined provenances.**
Part II lists historical kinds as "selected, constructed, descended from, copied from." Part IV defines exactly three provenances (Sel, Con, Dec) and says "This is the only definition of selection." Two of the four historical-kind predicates are undefined anywhere. *Minor.* Fix: define descent and copying (e.g., as $\equiv_\ell$-preserving transfers with their own witnesses) or drop them.

**7. "The earliest" is not guaranteed to exist under a partial order, so the provenance of a content is a partial function.**
Part IV: "The **provenance of a content's occurrence** is the provenance of the transport that witnesses (R) for it; where several do, the earliest."
$\prec_h$ is "the acyclic precedence $\prec_h$ supplied by the physical module" — a partial order. Two witnessing transports whose carrier occurrences (or whose witness events) are incomparable in $\prec_h$ give no earliest element; an infinite strictly descending chain (histories are sets of occurrences with an acyclic order, not assumed well-founded) gives no earliest either. The text asserts a total selection ("where several do, the earliest") with no existence argument. The partiality propagates: $\rho_p$ is defined via this rule, and Part III makes "found a question" require $\rho_p=\text{constructed}$, so an undefined $\rho_p$ blocks that claim with no fallback. *Serious.* Fix: state the rule as partial, add a tie-break (e.g., a declared priority or the conjunction of all earliest witnesses), and say what happens to $\rho_p$ when no earliest exists.

**8. "Earliest witness in $\prec_h$" compares relata of different types, and the two earliness rules in Part IV are not the same rule.**
Rule 1: "the provenance of the **earliest** witness in $\prec_h$ whose events produced the occurrence that carries $t$." Rule 2: "the provenance of the transport that witnesses (R) for it; where several do, the earliest."
$\prec_h$ orders *occurrences*. A selection witness is a whole history $\Sigma_h$ (a set of occurrences, per Part IV's "selection history $\Sigma_h$" and Part XII's "a claim about a physical history"); a construction witness is an episode with an event $e$. Ordering a set-of-events against an event is not supported by $\prec_h$. Separately, Rule 1 orders by the events that produced the *carrier occurrence*, Rule 2 by the witnessing *transports*; a transport constructed on a carrier produced earlier by selection is earliest under Rule 1's key but not under Rule 2's. *Serious.* Fix: define the order on witness-events of a uniform type, and state which of the two readings is operative.

**9. The earliest rule plus the maintenance clause can label a transport "selected" that satisfies Con, and this drives a wedge between Part III's "found a question" and Part X's Origin.**
"Later histories are maintenance and do not change provenance; a witness assembled afterwards from events that did not produce the occurrence is not a witness for it." Combined with Rule 1, a transport built by construction on a carrier whose occurrence was produced by an earlier selection takes its provenance from the selection. Part IV concedes the label ("Construction may operate on selected material … the earliest-witness rule says which provenance the resulting transport bears"), but the consequences are not faced: Part III requires "$\rho_p=\text{constructed}$ with the witness" for a found question, while Part X's $\operatorname{Origin}=\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}$ requires only a construction witness. So a question can satisfy Origin and fail "found" (earliest witness is a selection) or satisfy $\rho_p=\text{constructed}$ and fail Build (no nontrivial binding construction, no defect record). The two criteria for the same act diverge in both directions. *Serious.* Fix: make Part III's condition for a found question exactly the Build/Origin condition, or make $\rho_p$ ignore witnesses that do not witness the transport's own production.

**10. Part VI and Part V contradict each other on whether the active-route condition belongs to the anchored grade.**
Part V: "This condition is about history, not signature; it is stated **outside** the anchored grade so that the asymmetry of Grievance 12 holds exactly: forward kinds and anchoring are history-free, and the operative condition is not." Part VI: "Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference, and **the anchored grade's active-route condition uses it**."
Both cannot hold. *Serious.* Fix: pick one; if the operative condition is inside the anchored grade, Grievance 12's "anchoring are history-free" must be withdrawn and the decoy argument of attack (F) restated; if outside, Part VI's sentence must be corrected.

**11. The operative condition is invoked by no defined predicate, so the asymmetry of Grievance 12 is preserved by inertia, and the backup/decoy distinction never enters adequacy.**
Part V defines "an anchored account is **operative** when each anchor is an active route … and not a backup that would have responded identically." But (E) has no provenance or history conjunct ("(E) carries **no provenance condition**"); the anchored grade is conditions 1–3 (Properness, Resolution, World-kind), all history-free; (S), (B), (D) are defined on (E); Origin's "active route" is the *candidate's* route in $s$, not the anchors' route in $D$; (P) and (EK) use active routes of $\Delta$. Nothing requires the operative condition. So the one historical adequacy condition does no work: the redundant-routes example ("$\Gamma=\{a,b\}$, $\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}$") and the backup case are represented but never excluded from any adequacy predicate — which is precisely the case that a decoy with an identical world-kind raises (attack (F)). *Serious.* Fix: wire the operative condition into the anchored grade (and accept the history leak, restating Grievance 12) or into (EK), or delete it and say that the backup distinction is represented but not adequacy-relevant.

**12. "Operative result" is undefined and not listed, violating the completeness claim; "a history for $D$" is underdetermined.**
Part IX: "an active route is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining an occurrence that instantiates the input organization … to an **operative result**." Part XIV claims the informal terms are "listed"; "operative result" appears nowhere in that list. Also Part V's operative condition is conditioned on "where the physical module supplies a history for $D$," but $D$ is an organization and $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$ is many-valued, so a history is supplied for an *occurrence* instantiating $D$, and which one is chosen is unstated. *Minor.* Fix: add "operative result" and the occurrence-selection to Part XIV's declared parameters.

**13. The analysis-of-kinds conjecture is insulated rather than refutable: the historical disjunct is near-trivially satisfiable and "no history distinguishes" is undefined.**
Part XV, attack (C): "A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in $C_{\mathrm{adm}}$ distinguishes **and no history distinguishes**, and the distinction does explanatory work."
Part II defines a historical kind as "a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence," so *any* history difference yields a distinguishing historical kind. The only possible counterexample is therefore two components with *identical* histories. But nothing in the semantics supplies an identity condition on histories or on occurrences: $\prec$ is a dependency order, $\operatorname{Inst}_\ell$ is many-valued, and $\Theta$ supplies no occurrence-identity relation. So "no history distinguishes" cannot be established or refuted within the vocabulary. Attack (C) is not well-posed as stated, and Part 0's presentation of the conjecture as exposed to attack overstates it. *Serious.* Fix: either give an explicit identity condition for histories (e.g., sameness of the module-supplied occurrence set and precedence) or restrict the conjecture to pairs whose histories are identical by construction, and say so.

**14. The forward disjunct is also hard to defeat, and the two disjuncts are coupled through the baseline exclusion.**
The conjecture's forward disjunct is "some admissible grain and admitted contract." Defeating it requires signature coincidence on *every* admissible grain — but admissible grains are not monotone: a coarser grain can merge two components into one whose signature is unlike either, so coincidence at a fine grain does not imply coincidence at a coarse one, and the universal ranges over all of them. Independently, the exclusion of $(1,b_0)$ (Finding 1) removes exactly the baseline pairs from the forward-distinguishing cases and thereby manufactures the work that the historical disjunct then mops up; the conjecture's plausibility is inherited from that stipulation ("on purpose"), not from any theorem. Derivation 1's caveat ("It does not show that a kind-label can do no work") is honest about (i) and (ii) but not about this coupling. *Serious* for falsifiability; *minor* to fix. Fix: state the conjecture with the baseline exclusion made explicit as an assumption, and note that its two disjuncts are not independent.

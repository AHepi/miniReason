The central claim in Part XIV and Derivation 6 — that every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the two primitive packages plus the declared parameters — is false. I attack it finding by finding.

**1. `Sel`/`Con` use “representation” and create a cycle with `(R)`.**  
- Quote: “no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation” and “a construction witness contains both.”  
- Claim attacked: provenance is defined without `(R)`, so `(R)` can depend on `Sel`/`Con`.  
- Why it fails: if “representation” means the formal `Rep` of `(R)`, then `Sel` and `Con` use `(R)`, while `(R)` requires `Sel∨Con`. That is a direct cycle. If “representation” is not the formal `Rep`, it is an undefined judgement word.  
- Severity: fatal.  
- Fix: replace “itemised representation of the defect” by a structural record defined without `Rep`, e.g. a pair of an edit and a state-transition in the physical process order, and use that record in `Sel` and `Con`.

**2. `Ownership` and `Can` are mutually defined.**  
- Quote: “every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability `s` possesses in the sense of Part XII (`Can`)”; Part XII: “`Can` … requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one.”  
- Claim attacked: the dependence order is well-founded.  
- Why it fails: `Ownership` depends on `Can`; `Can` depends on `owned`. This cycle is not addressed in Derivation 6.  
- Severity: fatal to the claimed dependence order.  
- Fix: define `Can` from `RetReal`/`Exec` without `owned`, or take owned capability as primitive/declared.

**3. `Ans_E` is used but never defined.**  
- Quote: “`Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b))=Ans_p(a,b)`.”  
- Claim attacked: `(A)` is a defined condition.  
- Why it fails: Part III defines `Ans_p(a,b)=Q(D,a,b)` only on the target `D`. No definition is given for `Ans_E` on the candidate organization `E`, nor for the query applied to `E`.  
- Severity: fatal to `(A)`, hence to `(E)`, `Build`, and `EK`.  
- Fix: define a transported query `Q_E` or define `Ans_E` explicitly from `E` and the question.

Findings.

**1. Fatal cycle through (R), Con, Build, Deploy.**
Claim attacked: “\(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))].\)”
Why it fails: (R) needs Con. Con says “there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\)”. The construction witness is the witness for Build, which says “a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\)”. The repertoire is “the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect”, and Deploy “holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R)”. So (R) → Con → construction witness/Build → repertoire → Deploy → (R).
Severity: fatal.
Fix: stratify. Define repertoire without Deploy, or define Con without Build/repertoire, or make Deploy’s representation condition independent of Con. The dependence-order claim that provenance witnesses do not use (R) must be rewritten.

**2. Fatal cycle between Ownership and Can.**
Claim attacked: “A subhistory is **owned** by \(s\) when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability \(s\) possesses in the sense of Part XII (\(\operatorname{Can}\))”.
Why it fails: Part XII says “\(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one”. Ownership depends on Can; Can depends on owned.
Severity: fatal.
Fix: make ownership primitive or define Can without “owned”; or stratify owned capabilities into levels.

**3. Derivation 6’s cycle-breaking claim is false.**
Claim attacked: “(R) is not used inside any provenance witness”.
Why it fails: Con uses a construction witness; construction witness is Build; Build uses repertoire; repertoire uses Deploy; Deploy uses (R). Also Sel uses “itemised representation of the defect” unless that phrase is non-formal, in which case it is smuggled judgement.
Severity: fatal for Derivation 6’s proof; serious for Part XIV’s “two primitive packages” claim.
Fix: restate the actual dependence order, or remove (R) from Deploy/Build/Con, or replace all provenance-witness uses of representation with a lower-level non-representational predicate.

**4. Sel uses causal/judgement vocabulary not in the primitive list.**
Claim attacked: Sel holds when “the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it”; “A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect … lies on the route from the defect to the variation”.
Why it fails: “causally responsible”, “not merely consistent”, “blind”, “defect”, and “itemised representation” are not defined from Θ, N, or declared parameters. If “representation” means (R), Sel enters the cycle in Finding 1. If it means ordinary representation, it is smuggled.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define causal responsibility through \(\prec\), Exec, or the physical module’s process order; define “defect representation” as an organization with (R) but stratify it, or remove the clause.

**5. Sel has a type error: faithful transport to non-organizations.**
Claim attacked: “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition.”
Why it fails: \(\operatorname{Faithful}_C\) is defined for a transport between organizations. \(t\) is already a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and the survival condition is a predicate. None is an organization.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define an organization representing the event/defect/history/survival condition, and quantify over transports into that organization.

**6. Con applied to (R) targets a content, not an organization, and requires it instantiated before construction.**
Claim attacked: Con “holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\)”.
Why it fails: (R) uses \(t:c\to D\), so \(t\) targets the content \(c\). But a content is “an organization together with its contract-relative commitments”, not an organization in the sense of Part II. Worse, requiring the content to be instantiated before the construction episode makes novel content representation look like copying an already instantiated target.
Severity: serious, possibly fatal for (R).
Fix: distinguish content from its organization; define Con for content-to-carrier transports so that the target instantiated is the target of the content, not the content itself; or drop the target-instantiated condition for representation transports.

   **Why it fails:** If “itemised representation” means the representation predicate, then `Sel` and `Con` depend on `(R)`, while `(R)` depends on `Sel ∨ Con`. If it does not mean `(R)`, it is an undefined predicate doing central work in distinguishing selection from construction.  
   **Seriousness:** fatal.  
   **Fix:** Define “itemised representation” structurally without `(R)`, or remove it from the provenance definitions.

14. **“faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition” is a type error.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part IV: “no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition.”  
   **Why it fails:** Faithful transports are between organizations. \(t\) is a transport, \(H\) is a set of edit–boundary pairs, and a survival condition is a condition—not organizations.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Restate as “no event in the process contains a faithful representation of \(t\), of \(H\), or of the survival condition,” after defining representation independently.

15. **`prepares` is undefined.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part IV: “\(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness **prepares** \(t\).” Part X: “an owned subhistory … **prepares** an organization…”  
   **Why it fails:** “Prepares” is not defined in terms of `Can`, `Exec`, \(\prec\), `Exec`, `Build`, or any declared parameter. It is a judgement word used in a central provenance predicate.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define preparation via controlled processes, ownership, and binding construction.

16. **`Attempt` is undefined and has a type error.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part X: “\(\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)\) the relation that \(c\) is actually used, on an active route, to address \(p\) — where \(p\) is a coherently posed question that \(s\) instantiates before \(e\)…”  
   **Why it fails:** “Actually used,” “to address,” and “relevant difficulty” are undefined. More formally, \(s\) instantiates organizations under `Inst`, not questions. Derivation 5 claims a question determines an organization \(D_p\), but `Attempt` uses \(p\) directly.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Replace \(p\) by \(D_p\) or define `Inst` for question-contents; formalize “use to address” via active routes and obligations.

17. **`active route` uses several undefined terms and an undeclared parameter.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part IX: “An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts.”  
   **Why it fails:** “Represented input,” “operative result,” “applicable relations,” and “declared contrasts” are undefined. “Declared contrasts” is not in the Part XIV declared-parameters list. If “represented” invokes `(R)`, then active routes depend on `(R)`, which the dependence order does not record.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define active routes from \(\prec\), `Inst`, and a declared contrast set; list contrasts as a declared parameter.

18. **`Deploy` uses undefined judgement terms.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part X: “\(\operatorname{Deploy}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)\) holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) … integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task \(U\) as a retained capability.” Also: “The repertoire … the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect.”  
   **Why it fails:** “Problem-directed activity,” “supporting,” and “nontrivial use respect” are undefined. They are not derived from `(R)` and `(CT1)` as the dependence order claims.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define deployment as a relation between a representation, a use task, and an owned retained realization; define “nontrivial use respect” via use tasks or obligations.

19. **`≡_ℓ` omits much of what a content contains.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part X: “Two contents \(c,d\) are **content-preserving recodings** … when there is a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise…”  
   **Why it fails:** Contents include contracts, queries, scopes, counterfactual sets with meanings, obligation sets, and question provenance. The definition does not mention contracts, \(C\), \(\mathcal Q\), \(S\), \(K\), \(O_p\), or \(\rho_p\). Newness and Deploy rely on \(\equiv_\ell\), so this omission can make recoding too easy.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Include all content data—contract, query, scopes, counterfactual meanings, obligations—in the bijection.

20. **`Build` uses “explanatory use,” which is undefined and may reintroduce a cycle.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part X: “\(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an owned subhistory of \(h\) … prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for **explanatory use** of \(c\)…”  
   **Why it fails:** “Explanatory use” is not defined. If it means `Account(c,p)`, then `Build` depends on `Account`; if it means `Deploy`, then `Build → Deploy → (R) → Con → Build`, compounding finding 1.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define “explanatory use” as a specific question \(p\) and an `Account` relation, and place it explicitly in the dependence order.

21. **Episodes are defined by judgement words.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part X: “A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response. A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G) connected to its inquiry.”  
   **Why it fails:** “Recognized,” “available,” “conjectural,” “content-sensitive,” and “connected” are not formalized. `CreativeCriticalEpisode` is used in `(EK)`.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Define episodes as tuples of occurrences, questions, criticisms, and response maps, using `Bearing`, `Account`, and active routes.

22. **`CanAdv` uses an undefined achievement predicate and continuing organization.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part XII: “\(\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi,p;\chi,J_p,C_I)\): for each starting configuration in the independently specified \(J_p\) … every maximal execution completes with a history satisfying the **achievement predicate** and a **continuing organization** in \(C_I\).”  
   **Why it fails:** “Achievement predicate” and “continuing organization” are not defined, and \(J_p\), \(C_I\) are not listed as declared parameters.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.

23. **`Can` uses an undefined “owned, physically admitted, finite construction” and equivocates on “construction.”**

   **Why it fails:** “Construction” here is not the provenance predicate `Con` or the epistemic `Build`, but it is never defined. It also depends on “owned,” which cycles with `Can` (finding 2).  
   **Seriousness:** serious.

24. **`Admit`, `Poss`, and the capability grades are undefined.**

25. **`Scrutinizable`, `RC`, `Barriers`, and `UU/UC` use undefined judgement terms.**

26. **`Enable`’s question-begging condition uses `Deploy` and mis-types \(\chi\).**

27. **Part XI’s aesthetic predicates use undefined sets.**

28. **`ProducedBy` uses active routes and “occurrences of \(\Delta\)” without definition.**

29. **`Bearing` ignores the criticism’s target, defect, and grounds.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part IX: “\(\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta)\).” The preceding sentence says a criticism has “target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection.”  
   **Why it fails:** `Bearing` does not require that \(E_c\) concerns \(z\) or \(\delta\), nor that \(g\) supports it. Any account of \(p_\delta\) satisfies `Bearing`, regardless of the criticism’s target or defect.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** Make the criticism a tuple and require the account to be of the alleged defect at the target, with grounds playing the specified role.

30. **`K3` is logically overclaimed.**  
   **Claim attacked:** Part IX: “For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\) and nothing narrower. (K3)”  
   **Why it fails:** If \(B\) and \(I\) are themselves established, then \(\neg O\) plus \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\) entails \(\neg T\), which is narrower. The claim “nothing narrower” depends on \(B\) and \(I\) not being established.  
   **Seriousness:** serious.  
   **Fix:** State the epistemic status of \(B\) and \(I\): if they are not established, only the conjunction is refuted; if they are, \(\neg T\) follows.

31. **Derivation 1 assumes injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) not required by F1/F2.**

32. **Part VII’s reversed-calculation example contains a false parenthetical.**

The failure of (F2) can still be argued—target’s \(L\) changes while reversed \(E\)’s \(L\) does not—but the stated reason is false.

34. **Derivation 5 omits \(O_p\), \(\rho_p\), and the baseline non-emptiness condition, and mis-types the boundary.**

35. **Derivation 7 overclaims that adequacy catches goalpost-moving.**

39. **Counterfactual meanings in \(K\) are not formalized.**

## A. Cycles in the dependence order

**Finding 1 — Derivation 6(iv) is false: Build → reason-use → (R) → Con → Build is a cycle.**

1. Claim attacked: Derivation 6, "(iv) Build's 'already carried' and 'required' clauses are stated by instantiation, not through the repertoire of Deploy, so Build does not pass through (R) either."
2. Why it fails: The two clauses Derivation 6 checks are not the ones that matter. Build's operative clause is: *"what makes the episode construction is that the record is used, in the sense of Part IX, in the criticism the binding survives before it is kept"*. Part IX's reason-use definition is: *"A response uses a reason when a structural map from the represented objection into the response subnetwork preserves role bindings…"* — "the represented objection" invokes the representation predicate, and Part IX adds: *"An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears."* Since (R) is *"\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]"*, and Con is *"an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares t — the Build predicate holds…"*, the edge set is Build → reason-use → (R) → Con → Build. Derivation 6(i)'s stratification ("targets are *instantiated*, not *represented*, in a construction witness") covers only Con's target clause; it is silent on the Build clause, which is where the cycle is.
3. Serious. It invalidates Derivation 6, one of the four claims under review, and the cycle sits on the construction/selection distinction the whole creativity thesis rests on.
4. Fix: re-stratify reason use on Inst — "the objection as instantiated by an occurrence" — and either list "represented objection" as informal or eliminate it; then re-prove Derivation 6 against *all* of Build's clauses, not two of them.

**Finding 2 — Part XII asserts the opposite of Derivation 6(ii); under Part XII's reading there is a second cycle.**

1. Claim attacked: Part XII, *"Ownership (Part X) is defined from this predicate, not used in it."*
2. Why it fails: Part X defines ownership from RetReal: *"every process occurrence in it is an execution \eta\in\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi) … for which that substrate holds \operatorname{RetReal}(\pi,T,C;\chi) … (Part XII, (CT1))"*. Can is not in that definition. Derivation 6(ii) says *"ownership is defined from retained realization, and Can does not mention ownership"* — consistent with Part X, contradicted by Part XII. Under Part XII's reading, Build → ownership → Can, and Can's second disjunct (Finding 3) gives Can → Build: a cycle.
3. Serious (internal contradiction at the exact premise Derivation 6(ii) depends on).
4. Fix: delete the Part XII sentence; state ownership = Exec + RetReal + β + Ω, and record that Can is a sibling, not a source, of ownership.

**Finding 3 — Can's second disjunct uses the technical word "construction", creating an unrecorded dependence (and cycle inheritance).**

1. Claim attacked: Part XII, *"or holds retained realizations from which a physically admitted, finite construction produces one, under the same continuity and resource contract."*
2. Why it fails: "construction" is a defined notion (Con/Build) everywhere else in the document. If technical here, Can → Build, and since Build → ownership → RetReal and Can → RetReal, Part XIV's order must list "Can depends on Build"; it does not. Worse, Build → reason-use → (R) → Con → Build means Can inherits the Finding-1 cycle. If non-technical, it is an unlisted informal use of a defined term.
3. Serious.
4. Fix: replace with non-technical wording ("a physically admitted finite sequence of process applications") and add the dependence to Part XIV, or make the appeal to Build explicit and repair Finding 1 first.

**Finding 4 — (G) omits the deployment condition Part 0 and Part XV advertise, and with it the provenance condition.**

1. Claim attacked: Part X, *"\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}. (G)"* versus Part 0, *"that satisfies Origin (Part X) — it is new to the repertoire, built, and deployed —"* and Part XV, *"satisfying Origin (new, built, deployed)"*.
2. Why it fails: (G) has no Deploy conjunct; Attempt's *"actually used"* is an informal term, not Deploy. Worse, Build requires only *"held to c by a transport faithful on c's contract"* — faithful, no provenance. So a candidate can satisfy (G) while its transport is *declared*. The (Sel ∨ Con) condition enters only through (EK)'s Deploy conjunct (*"Deploy supplies the provenance condition (\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}) through (R)"*). The sufficiency claim is therefore stated for a stronger predicate than the one defined: the target's own copy, if "actually used" informally, would satisfy (G) as written.
3. Serious (moved goalpost between the gloss and the formula, on the load-bearing attack (A)).
4. Fix: add Deploy(s,c,ξ;U) as a conjunct of (G) — noting this makes (G) → (R), so Finding 1 must be repaired first — or state explicitly that Attempt's "actually used" *is* Deploy and strike the informal term.

**Finding 9 — "active route" depends on an undefined, unlisted "operative result"; if that loops back to the operative condition, the operative condition ↔ active route is circular.**

1. Claim attacked: Part IX, *"An active route is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining an occurrence that instantiates the input organization … to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the instantiated distinction under the declared contrasts."*; Part V, *"an anchored account is operative when each anchor is an active route (Part IX)"*; Part XIV, *"the operative condition depends on anchoring and histories"*.
2. Why it fails: "operative result" is in none of the three inventories. If it is defined by the operative condition of Part V, the definition is circular; if it is informal, the informal list omits it (and "the instantiated distinction", "the declared contrasts", "the applicable relations"). Also, Part XIV's order omits the edge "the operative condition depends on Part IX's active route" — it lists only anchoring and histories.
3. Serious (the operative condition is the one openly historical condition and carries Grievance 12's asymmetry).
4. Fix: define "operative result" independently of the operative condition (e.g., an occurrence whose state changes subsequent activity), list it and the other three terms, and add the missing edge to Part XIV.

## B. The "two primitive packages / all listed" claims

**Finding 5 — The selection witness's survival condition (and population, variation operator, Σ_h) is in none of the three inventories: a smuggled third primitive.**

1. Claim attacked: Part IV, *"a survival condition enacted by the environment in \Sigma_h"*; Part 0, *"It does not claim that the two primitive packages and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV are few. It claims that they are all listed."*
2. Why it fails: Part XII's supply list for Θ covers state spaces/attributes, admitted processes, A_Θ(o), the intervention repertoire, resources/accuracy grades, Inst_ℓ, ≺, and Exec. It does not supply environmental enactments, so Part XII's own *"a survival condition enacted by the environment"* is not covered by Θ as specified. It is not a declared parameter, not an informal term. Same for the population: Part IV glosses it as *"the variants of the system's tracking machinery"*, a sameness relation Θ's list does not supply; and *"a variation operator μ on T, physically admitted"* needs a notion of admitted *operator on organization–transport pairs*, whereas Θ admits edits and processes, not operators on pairs.
3. Serious — this is the base of (R), Deploy, and hence the entire provenance story.
4. Fix: either extend Θ's supply list to include the environment's enacted survival conditions and the population-individuation relation, or add population/μ/survival-condition/Σ_h to the declared parameters as witness data supplied with each selection claim.

## C. Definitional well-formedness gaps

**Finding 7 — The non-circularity conjunct of (E) is not defined in the document's own vocabulary, and uses the judgement word "follows".**

1. Claim attacked: Part V, *"The answer follows by evaluating E under its independent boundary conditions. A boundary condition b is independent of the answer when no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports."*
2. Why it fails: A transport is *"t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E where \pi:X_D\to X_E …, \tau:A_D\to A_E translates edits, \sigma:B_D\to B_E translates boundaries, and \lambda assigns each component of E a subnetwork of D with a port translation."* None of π, τ, σ, λ is a port-to-port map from "answer ports" to "boundary ports", so "carries the answer ports into the boundary ports" has no reading in the supplied vocabulary. "The answer follows" is an unlisted judgement word. The formal content therefore rests on the witness clause, which the text itself concedes *"does little"* when Γ is a singleton.
3. Serious (this is the conjunct that excludes "p because p").
4. Fix: define independence via λ (e.g., no anchor subnetwork of a boundary-setting component overlaps the answer-determining subnetwork) or via π's factorisation; replace "follows" with the explicit condition; strengthen the single-component witness.

**Finding 8 — (R)'s "largest contract" may not exist.**

1. Claim attacked: Part IV, *"what it represents without qualification is c on the largest contract on which some witnessing transport is faithful."*
2. Why it fails: The sentence quantifies over *some* witnessing transport; different contracts can be witnessed by different transports with no single transport faithful on their union, so a maximum need not exist (only maximal elements). The adjacent sentence — *"the content on the largest contract it is in fact faithful on"* — silently switches to a fixed transport, which does have a maximum (unions of contracts of the form (C_adm∩S)∪K are of that form).
3. Minor (the repair is already half-written).
4. Fix: fix the transport first, then take the union; or replace "the largest" with "the set of maximal contracts".

**Finding 10 — The objectivity condition's justification for extending the transport is a non-sequitur.**

1. Claim attacked: Part V, *"the transport being extended to such pairs by \lambda (the edit acts on an anchored component, so its translation is the corresponding edit on the anchored component of E)."*
2. Why it fails: τ and σ are already total on A_D and B_D; nothing needs extending by λ — what is extended is the *evaluation*. And when an admitted pair touches several or overlapping anchors, "the corresponding edit" is not unique, so the justification does not select τ(a).
3. Minor.
4. Fix: say the evaluation is extended to C_adm∖C using the already-total τ, σ; add a clause determining τ(a) when multiple anchors are touched.

**Finding 14 — Bearing (K1) is contract-relative but p_δ is given no contract.**

1. Claim attacked: Part IX, *"Let p_\delta be the question whose target organization is (the organization z instantiates) and whose query asks whether \delta obtains"*, with *"\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta,t_c,\Gamma_c)."*
2. Why it fails: Account is indexed to a contract; p_δ's C is unspecified, so (K1) has no determinate truth value.
3. Minor.
4. Fix: declare p_δ's contract (e.g., the appraiser's C_j, or a contract declared with the criticism).

## Derivation 1 — Kind preservation under fidelity

**Finding 1.**
**Quote:** “Then every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.”
**Claim attacked:** D1 is supposed to show that a faithful component is of one forward kind with its anchor subnetwork.
**Why it fails:** Part II defines forward kind as signatures coinciding on the *same* contract \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\) under a bijection of visible ports carrying value domains onto value domains. D1 compares \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)\) with \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))\). These are indexed to different edit sets. D1 therefore shows a translated signature equality, not forward-kind identity as defined. It also includes the baseline pair, whereas Part II excludes \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\).
**Severity:** Serious.
**Fix:** State a cross-organization lemma explicitly: there is a port bijection and a contract isomorphism \((\tau,\sigma)\) such that for every \((a,b)\in C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\), the anchor relation and the component relation correspond. Do not call the result “of one forward kind” unless that definition is amended for cross-organization use.

**Finding 2.**
**Quote:** Part V: “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract.”
**Claim attacked:** Part V cites D1 to move from (F1) to signature preservation.
**Why it fails:** D1’s hypothesis is “let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\).” Part V’s component fidelity does not require or state injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\). The citation is therefore stronger than the derivation allows.
**Severity:** Serious.
**Fix:** Either add injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) to the definition of transport or to component fidelity, or weaken D1 to a statement about the image of the signature under a non-injective translation.

**Finding 3.**
**Quote:** D1, “What this does and does not show”: “It shows that fidelity on a contract preserves signature on that contract: a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork.”
**Claim attacked:** D1’s own gloss.
**Why it fails:** Same as Finding 1. D1 gives signature equality between \(E\) on \(\tau[C]\) and \(\lambda(k)\) on \(C\), not between two subnetworks on one contract \(C\). The phrase “on \(C\)” is unsupported.
**Severity:** Serious.
**Fix:** Say “signature equality up to the translation \((\tau,\sigma)\), not forward-kind identity as defined in Part II.”

**Finding 4.**
**Quote:** D1 proof: “the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\).”
**Claim attacked:** The projection \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)]\).
**Why it fails:** D1 never states that \(\lambda\) supplies a bijection from \(V_k\) onto the visible ports of \(\lambda(k)\) carrying value domains onto value domains. Without that, the projection is ill-typed or at least not guaranteed to compare the right coordinates. Part II’s forward kind explicitly requires such a bijection.
**Severity:** Serious.
**Fix:** Add hypotheses: \(\lambda\) supplies a port bijection \(\phi_k:V_k\to V_{\lambda(k)}^{\mathrm{vis}}\) carrying \(X_v\) onto \(X_{\phi_k(v)}\), and the projection in (F1) is along \(\phi_k\).

**Finding 5.**
**Quote:** Derivation 10: “then by Derivation 1, with \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on that contract (which it is, since the translation is a relabelling), the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things.”
**Claim attacked:** Use of D1 in the three-layer episode.
**Why it fails:** The same cross-contract and baseline problems apply. D1 does not establish forward-kind identity “on that contract”; it establishes signature equality between two contracts related by \((\tau,\sigma)\). The assertion that the translation is a relabelling also does not by itself prove injectivity of the pair \((\tau,\sigma)\) on the whole contract.
**Severity:** Serious.
**Fix:** Either prove a genuinely cross-organization forward-kind lemma with baseline excluded and port bijections, or state only the translated signature equality.

## Derivation 2 — Account-equivalence

**Finding 6.**
**Quote:** Definition: “there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide.”
**Claim attacked:** The definition of account-equivalence.
**Why it fails:** The anchored relations live on different port sets \(V_k\) and \(V_{k'}\). Without a bijection of those visible ports carrying value domains, “coincide” is ill-typed. Part II’s forward kind requires exactly such a port bijection; D2 omits it.
**Severity:** Serious.
**Fix:** Add a port bijection for each corresponding pair of active components, as in Part II.

**Finding 7.**
**Quote:** Proof: “(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence.”
**Claim attacked:** The proof step.
**Why it fails:** (F1) equates the D-side projection with the E-side component relation at the translated edit. It does not “give the anchored relations as the component relations.” The conclusion may be true if the anchor maps are the same up to bijection, because then the anchored relations are literally the same D-subnetwork relation, but the proof’s stated reason is wrong.
**Severity:** Minor to serious, depending on whether the proof is cited.
**Fix:** Correct the proof: shared anchor map gives coincidence of the D-side anchored relations; (A) gives the pulled-back answer profiles.

**Finding 8.**
**Quote:** “Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on \(C\) with different decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account, and no result here says they are.”
**Claim attacked:** The “What it does not say” remark.
**Why it fails:** The remark conflates “not identical” with “not account-equivalent.” As defined, two candidates with different decompositions could still be account-equivalent if a bijection and anchored-relation coincidence exist. The claim only says no result here says they are; it does not establish they are not.
**Severity:** Minor.
**Fix:** Say “not identical” or “not covered by this claim,” not “not one account.”

## Derivation 3 — Selection guarantees nothing off its history

**Finding 9.**
**Quote:** Closure hypothesis: “each relation \(L'\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)).”
**Claim attacked:** The type of \(L'\).
**Why it fails:** A relation realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\) may not be type-correct for component \(j\) at the relevant pair if it belongs to a different component with a different footprint or value domains. The hypothesis needs \(L'\) admissible for \(j\).
**Severity:** Minor.
**Fix:** Require \(L'\) to be realizable by some member at component \(j\) or in the admissible class for \(j\)’s footprint and domains.

**Finding 10.**
**Quote:** Consequence: “Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\).”
**Claim attacked:** The consequence.
**Why it fails:** The selection data include the actual survivor \(t\). The proof shows that survival on \(H\) alone admits alternative survivors differing off \(H\). It does not show that the full selection data, including the identity of the actual survivor, fail to determine it.
**Severity:** Minor to serious.
**Fix:** Say “survival on \(H\) alone does not determine the survivor off \(H\).”

**Finding 11.**
**Quote:** “That is why the primitive layer is not guaranteed; fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts.”
**Claim attacked:** The inference to the primitive layer.
**Why it fails:** D3 is conditional on a very strong closure hypothesis. If the actual population is not closed under pointwise alteration, D3 is silent. Part IV already says a selected transport is guaranteed faithful only on \(H\). Attributing the primitive layer’s lack of guarantee to D3 overstates what D3 proves.
**Severity:** Minor.
**Fix:** Attribute the guarantee point to the selection definition, or keep it explicitly under the closure hypothesis.

**Finding 12.**
**Quote:** Proof: “Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\).”
**Claim attacked:** The proof’s first step.
**Why it fails:** Survival on \(H\) constrains the whole organization’s fidelity on \(H\). The proof needs the closure hypothesis’s assertion that the altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) everywhere else, including any cross-pair constraints. Without that, altering one relation could affect fidelity on \(H\).
**Severity:** Minor.
**Fix:** Make clear that the closure hypothesis is doing exactly this work and that the result does not generalize to populations with cross-pair constraints.

**1. (D1/Part V) — Serious.**  
**Quote:** “By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract”.  
**Claim attacked:** Part V cites Derivation 1 as if it holds under (F1) alone.  
**Why it fails:** Derivation 1 explicitly assumes “let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\).” The definition of transport in Part IV imposes no injectivity on \((\tau,\sigma)\). Without injectivity, the map between signatures need not be a bijection on first two coordinates; distinct pairs in \(C\) can collapse to the same translated pair, so the signature-set comparison is not the one D1 proves.  
**Fix:** Add injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on the relevant contract to the anchored grade, or weaken the Part V citation to “there is a signature correspondence when \((\tau,\sigma)\) is injective.”

**2. (D1) — Minor.**  
**Quote:** “It shows that fidelity on a contract preserves signature on that contract: a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork.”

**Why it fails:** D1 shows equality of \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))\) with \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)\). Part II defines “one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)” for two subnetworks in one organization on the same contract. The cross-organization use in Part V is only mentioned, not fully defined. “On \(C\)” in this gloss is imprecise.  
**Fix:** Define the cross-organization kind relation explicitly, or state D1 as a signature-correspondence result, not a forward-kind identity on the same contract.

**6. (D2) — Minor.**

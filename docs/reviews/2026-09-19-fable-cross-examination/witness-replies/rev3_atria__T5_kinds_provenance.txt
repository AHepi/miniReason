**F1. Part II, "Kinds are edit-signatures, at a grain and a contract"**

> "Two subnetworks \(U,U'\) … are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains, under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\)."

> "what a component imposes at the baseline is, as a gloss, its *state*, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states"

(1) Claim attacked: excluding only the question's baseline pair loses nothing, because the baseline entry is "state."

(2) Why it fails: the gloss is apt for *subnetworks* — their baseline entry is \(\operatorname{proj}\operatorname{Sol}_U(1,b_0)\), a set of admissible valuations — but for a *single component* the baseline entry is \(L_j(1,b_0)\), which is a relation, i.e. the component's law, not a state. Two components with different laws \(f\neq g\) have identical signatures on \(C\setminus\{(1,b_0)\}\) whenever every edit in \(C\) that touches them is a replacement (after the edit, \(L_j(a,b)\) is the replacement's relation, independent of \(j\)). The exclusion then identifies them. The stated rationale — "the identity edit at the baseline separates nothing" — is also a non-sequitur: it argues that the *edit* \((1,b_0)\) cannot be a separating change, which is a reason not to count it as a witness of separation, not a reason to delete the baseline *relation* from the signature.

(3) Serious. It does not breach the anchored grade (see F2), but it makes the free-standing forward-kind relation of Part II strictly coarser than signature equality and silently identifies distinct laws.

(4) Fix: exclude the identity edit from being a *separating witness* while retaining baseline relations in the signature; or add a module requirement that admitted edits touching a component include relation-dependent edits (e.g. coefficient-scaling), and say that the exclusion presupposes this.

**F2. Part V, anchored grade condition 3, and the exclusion's actual role**

> "Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\) with the question's baseline pair excluded, with its anchor subnetwork"

> "a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 3"

(1) Claim attacked: the baseline exclusion is doing deliberate work in the anchored grade.

(2) Why it fails: it is inert there. By Part III, \((1,b_0)\in C\); (F1) ranges over all of \(C\), so \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(1,b_0)=L_k(1,b_0)\) is already required. Hence within the anchored grade the baseline relations of component and anchor are equal whether or not the kind relation excludes the pair, and a "decoy with a different baseline relation" fails (F1) at \((1,b_0)\) and never reaches condition 3. The exclusion is therefore vacuous where the document invokes it, and load-bearing only where it is not invoked — in Part II's free-standing relation, in Derivation 1's signature-preservation claim, in Derivation 10's swap case, and in the analysis-of-kinds conjecture.

(3) Serious (misplaced rationale; the "on purpose" justification defends a condition that has no effect where it is used).

(4) Fix: state that (F1) already covers the baseline in the anchored grade, and relocate the exclusion — or drop it — to the free-standing kind relation where it has effect, and justify it there.

**F3. Part II / Part V, the unstated \(b_0\) index**

> "A forward kind is an equivalence class under this relation, and it is relative to the grain (which fixes \(J\)) and the contract (which fixes the changes)."

> "a world-kind is a fact about \((\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\)"

(1) Claim attacked: the index of a forward kind is \((\ell,C)\), and a world-kind is a fact about \((\Theta,\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\).

(2) Why it fails: the exclusion is of the *question's* baseline pair, so two questions with the same \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and different \(b_0\) induce different kind-structures. The index is \((\Theta,\ell,C,b_0)\); \(b_0\) is a parameter of the relation but is never listed as one.

(3) Minor, but trivially checkable and trivially fixable.

(4) Fix: add \(b_0\) to every stated index, or define the excluded pair without reference to a question (e.g. "the identity edit at the boundary fixed by the assessment").

**F4. Part II, the unconstrained port bijection**

> "there is a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains, under which their signatures coincide"

(1) Claim attacked: forward-kind identity is a substantive, non-stipulative relation.

(2) Why it fails: the bijection is constrained only by domain isomorphism, so any two components whose ports have isomorphic domains and coincident signature *structure* are one forward kind regardless of what the ports are. Inside Part V this is harmless (\(\lambda\) supplies the bijection), but for the analysis-of-kinds conjecture — which is about kind-labels in the wild — the bijection is either arbitrary (making "one forward kind" nearly free) or supplied by transport data \(\lambda\), in which case the conjecture presupposes part of what it is supposed to eliminate.

(3) Serious for the conjecture; harmless for the internal use.

(4) Fix: require the bijection to be licensed by the module or by an admitted edit (as the swap edit does in Derivation 10), and restrict the conjecture's forward-kind disjunct accordingly.

**F5. Part IV, "earliest witness" under a partial order**

> "Among the witnesses whose events produced the occurrence that carries \(t\), take those that are \(\prec_h\)-minimal (an event of a witness precedes an event of another)."

(1) Claim attacked: the minimal/earliest witness is well-defined.

(2) Why it fails: \(\prec_h\) is a partial order on *events* (Part IX). The parenthetical lifts it to witnesses via "an event of one precedes an event of another," which yields a preorder, not a partial order, and it breaks in three ways: (i) non-antisymmetric interleaving — if \(e_1\prec_h e_3\prec_h e_2\) with \(e_1,e_2\in W_1\), \(e_3\in W_2\), both witnesses are non-minimal and *no* minimal witness exists, so provenance is undefined; (ii) incomparable minima are not unique, forcing "mixed"; (iii) an infinite descending chain of witness events has no minimal element. Nothing guarantees finiteness or totality, and the document elsewhere handles infinitary cases (Part VI) without noting this one.

(3) Serious.

(4) Fix: compare witnesses by a designated extremal event (e.g. the event that produced the carrying occurrence), or require the module to supply a total order on witness events, or designate the witness of the operative transport.

**F6. Part IV / Part III, several transports witnessing (R)**

> "The **provenance of a content's occurrence on \(C\)** is the provenance of the \(\prec_h\)-minimal transports witnessing (R) for it on \(C\)"

> "Its witness is the one Part IV gives for the provenance of any content's occurrence: the provenance of the transport by which an occurrence represents the question-organization of Derivation 5."

(1) Claim attacked: a content's provenance is the provenance of the transport that represents it.

(2) Why it fails: (R) is existential, and the document itself contemplates multiple witnesses — "whether the system still represents the content depends on whether another transport witnesses (R)" and "the largest contract on which *some* witnessing transport is faithful." Under the minimal-witness rule: an ancient selected transport that remains faithful on \(C\) stays minimal forever, so a content later re-represented by a constructed transport is still "selected"; and a content with one constructed and one incomparable-minimal selected witness is "mixed." Part III's definite description "the transport by which an occurrence represents" is therefore not well-defined. Concretely, \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) (required for "an episode found a question") can be assigned to a question whose representing transport was constructed while the content-provenance rule returns "selected."

(3) Serious.

(4) Fix: define the content's provenance via a designated witness — the operative transport, or the witness faithful on the largest/maximal contract — and replace Part III's definite description with the stated selection rule.

**F7. Part IV, mixed provenance and its consequences**

> "If they are of both kinds, \(t\) has **mixed** provenance, which is declared as such and does not count as constructed for (G)."

(1) Claim attacked: mixed provenance is a coherent third status with a stated cost.

(2) Why it fails: the real exclusion is not at (G) — which uses \(\operatorname{Build}\), not \(\operatorname{Con}\) — but at (R)/\(\operatorname{Deploy}\), which require \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\). A mixed content is therefore not deployable, cannot enter the repertoire \(R_{\beta,\ell}\), cannot satisfy \(\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\) in (EK), and (by F5's interleaving) can become mixed for purely structural reasons. Combined with F6, a genuinely constructed representation coexisting with an old selected one can disable (EK) entirely.

(3) Serious.

(4) Fix: say "does not count as selected or constructed for (R) and Deploy," and add a tie-break rule preferring the operative witness over incomparable minima.

**F8. Part II, historical kinds via "the binding that realizes the component"**

> "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for the provenance of a content's occurrence, applied to the binding that realizes the component."

(1) Claim attacked: historical kinds are defined for component occurrences generally, including selected, descended, and copied ones.

(2) Why it fails: "binding" is technical in Part X — "A **binding** is an assignment \(\lambda(k)\) of a component of the built organization to a subnetwork of its target" — and exists only inside construction witnesses. A component occurrence that was never constructed (a target component, or one carried by a selected transport) has no binding, so its historical kind is undefined, contradicting the list "selected, constructed, descended from, copied from." Conversely, "descended from" and "copied from" are defined in Part IV as *transport*-level relations ("descent is a chain of selections"; "copying is a content-preserving transfer"), not as predicates on component-occurrence histories obtained through a binding. The definition channel does not produce the kinds it lists.

(3) Serious — the historical-kind disjunct of the analysis-of-kinds conjecture is not properly defined.

(4) Fix: define historical kinds directly on the events that produced the occurrence under the module's \(\prec_h\), without routing through content-provenance and construction bindings; or state that "binding" here means any transport's anchor map \(\lambda\).

**F9. Grievance 12 and Part V, the operative condition is a dead letter**

> "the one thing history changes about an account is whether it is *operative* (Part V), which is stated as a separate, openly historical condition."

> "an anchored account is **operative** when each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically. This condition is about history, not signature; it is stated outside the anchored grade so that the asymmetry of Grievance 12 holds exactly"

(1) Claim attacked: the operative condition is the historical counterweight that answers the transport/component asymmetry objection.

(2) Why it fails: the predicate is never a conjunct of anything. It is not in (E), not in the anchored grade's three conditions, not in \(\operatorname{Origin}\) (\(\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\)), not in \(\operatorname{Deploy}\), not in (EK). Part XIV records only that "the operative condition depends on anchoring and histories"; nothing depends on it. Part VI uses the *concept* of redundant routes to motivate (S)/(B), not the predicate. Consequences: (i) an anchored account whose anchors are *all* backups is still anchored, still satisfies (E), and — if new, built and deployed — satisfies (G) and (EK), so the document's own attack (A) is answerable by a case its machinery generates; (ii) Grievance 12's defense therefore has no teeth — history changes nothing about any account at any grade. The active-route machinery *is* used elsewhere (Attempt, Build, ProducedBy/ProducesVia), so the omission is not forced.

(3) Serious.

(4) Fix: make operative-ness a conjunct of the anchored grade (or of (G)/(EK) for anchored candidates), or explicitly concede that backup-anchored accounts are indistinguishable from mechanism-anchored ones at every grade and withdraw Grievance 12's asymmetry defense.

**F10. Part V, the vacuous conditional on the module's history**

(2) Why it fails: \(\Theta\) always supplies histories and the precedence \(\prec_h\) (Part IX, Part XII: "the process-dependency order \(\prec\) used by histories"). The conditional is therefore always true, which makes the inertness of F9 a design choice rather than a limitation.

(3) Minor.

(4) Fix: delete the conditional, or state it as universal.

**F11. Part XV attack (C), the analysis-of-kinds conjecture is not refutable as stated**

> "A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work. This refutes the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture**"

(1) Claim attacked: attack (C) makes the conjecture refutable.

(2) Why it fails: neither "discriminating" nor "does explanatory work" is defined — not in Part II, not in Part XIV's list of informal terms. Any candidate can be dismissed as non-discriminating or non-explanatory. In the other direction, by F1 and F4 the counterexample conditions are *already* satisfiable trivially: two components with different baseline laws, identical responses to every non-baseline admitted change, and shared provenance, are one forward kind and one historical kind, and the difference can do explanatory work — unless the Part II gloss stipulates that a baseline law-difference is "state, not kind," in which case the conjecture is protected by stipulation. The conjecture is also near-tautological relative to the semantics's two resources: a difference that is neither a change-difference nor a history-difference is reported as "not represented" (Part II, Grievance 2) rather than as a counterexample. So it is unfalsifiable on one side and trivially threatened on the other.

(3) Serious — attack (C) is advertised in Part 0 and Part XV as a load-bearing refutation route.

(4) Fix: define "discriminating use" and "explanatory work" in the document's own vocabulary (e.g. a label whose removal changes Account-hood or the answer profile under some contract), restrict the forward-kind disjunct to module-licensed bijections (F4), and decide explicitly whether baseline law-differences are kinds or states.

**F12. Part 0 vs Part XV, two non-equivalent conjectures**

> Part 0: "the claim that they 'really' differ is always either a claim about a finer contract, which is a new question, or a claim about their histories … is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture"

> Part XV: "every discriminating use of a kind-label is a forward kind on some admissible grain and admitted contract, or a historical kind"

(1) Claim attacked: one conjecture is stated.

(2) Why it fails: the Part 0 version is a thesis about what a difference-claim is *about* (a finer contract = a new question, not itself a kind); the Part XV version is a thesis about what a label *is* (a forward kind at an index). A finer contract yielding a new question does not obviously make the original label-use a forward kind at the original index.

(3) Minor.

(4) Fix: state one version and derive the other, or mark them as two claims.

**F15. Grievance 12, category slip on transports**

> "A transport is an occurrence in a physical history: it came to hold by selection, construction, or declaration, and its provenance is a fact about events."

(1) Claim attacked: a transport is an occurrence in physical history.

(2) Why it fails: Part IV defines a transport as a tuple \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda)\); the physical item is the *occurrence that carries* \(t\), which is what the minimal-witness rule actually quantifies over ("the witnesses whose events produced the occurrence that carries \(t\)"). The Grievance's phrasing conflates the two, and the conflation matters because the whole asymmetry argument rests on transports being historical individuals while components are slots.

(3) Minor / cosmetic, but it weakens the very sentence doing the defensive work.

(4) Fix: rewrite as "a transport is carried by an occurrence in a physical history."

**F16. Part V, decoy sentence gives the wrong reason**

> "It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 3."

(1) Claim attacked: condition 3 is what excludes a decoy with a different response on an admitted edit.

(2) Why it fails: by F2, if the differing admitted pair is the baseline \((1,b_0)\in C\), the decoy fails (F1) before condition 3 is reached; if it is a pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\), condition 3 does apply — but the sentence does not distinguish these, and reads as though condition 3 alone does the work. Also, "shares its signature on \(C\)" and "has a different response to some admitted edit" are compatible only if that edit is outside \(C\), which should be said.

(3) Minor.

(4) Fix: split the two cases explicitly.

Direct answers to the five questions asked: excluding only \((1,b_0)\) loses the baseline *relation* from the free-standing kind relation (F1) while doing nothing inside the anchored grade, where (F1) already covers it (F2); content provenance is not well-defined under multiple (R)-witnesses (F6) and "earliest" is not well-defined under a partial order (F5); the operative condition does not let history back into fidelity — it lets history in nowhere, which is worse (F9); and the analysis-of-kinds conjecture is not currently refutable (F11), with two formulations in circulation (F12).

**Finding 5. Derivation 5 is internally inconsistent about \(b_0\).**

(2) "\(b_0\), \(O_p\) and \(\rho_p\) are not ports of \(D_p\) as constructed; adding \(b_0\) as a port with domain \(B\) … is straightforward and not done." (3) If \(b_0\) is a boundary, then two questions on the same \(D\) with different baselines are two different organizations, not two solutions of one \(D_p\); the construction then cannot supply "candidate questions on \(D\)" as its solutions, because each solution would have only one baseline. The gap is listed as an omission to be closed; it is a conflict with the written construction. (3) Serious. (4) Choose: if \(b_0\) is a port, rewrite the construction's boundary; if \(b_0\) is a boundary, drop the claim that the solutions are questions on \(D\).

**Finding 6. Gap (i) is not a gap; it is a reason the construction cannot be closed as the document assumes.**

(1) "the solutions of \(D_p\) include incoherent questions unless a component encoding it is supplied, and none is here." (2) A component whose relation encodes coherence must depend on \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\), i.e. on the whole solution set of the target. Encoding this as a component of \(D_p\) makes the question-organization a representation of the target's solution set, not just a formal object indexed to a name \(D\). Derivation 6 says the stratification of Part IV "keeps this order well-founded" precisely because provenance witnesses use instantiation, not (R). A coherence component that reads \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) is a step that pulls (R)-like material into the question-organization and would need to be re-examined against that claim. The gap is not closable in the same vocabulary. (3) Serious. (4) Either supply the component and re-prove the dependence order, or reclassify (i) as unclosable.

**Finding 7. The gaps omit the domain of \(u_{\mathcal Q}\).**

(1) "one port \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) with domain the set of queries admissible on \(D\)." (2) "The set of queries admissible on \(D\)" is not given by (O)/(Q). \(\mathcal Q\) is a "specified set-theoretic operation on \(D\)"; the collection of such operations is not, as written, a port domain the semantics has supplied. If the domain is the set of set-theoretic operations of a given type, say so; if it is looser, the construction has not met the document's own standard of supplied data. This gap is missing from the list of five. (3) Serious. (4) Name the set; specify whether "admissible" is an internal predicate of the semantics or a declared selection.

**Finding 8. Gap (iv) misdescribes the port it is about.**

(1) "Whether Inst licenses an organization whose ports range over edits of another organization is a question for the physical module and is not argued." (2) In the construction, the ports whose domains are not primitive are \(u_S\), \(u_K\), \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) — scopes, counterfactual sets with meanings, queries. The indexed ports \(u_{(a,b)}\) have domain \(\{\text{in},\text{out}\}\); they do not "range over edits" of another organization. The official statement of the gap therefore misidentifies the class of ports whose instantiation is in question, and is uncheckable as written. (3) Minor. (4) Restate as: "whether Inst licenses an organization whose ports range over sets of queries, sets of scopes, and counterfactual sets with meanings."

## On Q3 — Is the limit rightly placed on the module's fixity?

**Finding 9. The placement disowns a semantic gap onto the module.**

(1) "The limit is the module's fixity, not the semantics' vocabulary; it is stated." (2) If the module is fixed, a new dimension of variation is either already licensed (in which case nothing is invented, only recognised) or unrepresentable. "Inventing a new dimension of variation" — introducing a new port class, a new edit type, a new kind of target — is a recognised kind of creative achievement. The semantics supplies no episode whose content is the *revision* of the module; it only supplies the module and the revised module. So the limit is not the module's fixity; it is the absence of a vocabulary for the transition. Nothing in Part X or XV says what would count as a licensed revision, or how a history of revisions would be assessed. (3) Serious. (4) Either state that module-revision is representable (and how), or state the limit as a limit of the semantics.

**Finding 10. The limit is placed asymmetrically for physical and mathematical organisations.**

(1) Part XV (iv): "mathematical explanation falls under a declared admission relation; it is closed relative to that declaration." Part XIV lists "the declared admission relation for non-physical organizations" among the declared parameters with no constraint. (2) For physical organisations the limit is a substantive external bound ("the module's fixity"). For mathematical organisations the limit is a declaration with no constraint. If "invention of a new dimension of variation" is exactly the mathematical case — a new type, operation, or space — the semantics permits it whenever the mathematician declares an admission relation. The place where fixity is supposed to do the work becomes, for one whole class of organisations, empty. The asymmetry is asserted, not argued. (3) Serious. (4) State what constrains a declared admission relation, or admit that for mathematics the "limit" is not a limit.

**Finding 11. "The module licenses the dimension" is applied retrospectively, which is a moved goalpost.**

(1) "Where the module does license the dimension at some grain, its introduction is the construction of a new organization attribution and Build covers it." (2) Whether the module licenses the dimension is a claim about \(A_\Theta\) and \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\). At the moment of invention, the inventor typically does not know whether the dimension is licensed. Sorting an episode as construction (if later found licensed) or as unrepresentable (if not) uses the finished module to classify the episode that produced the pressure to revise it. The same episode is either represented or not depending on how the module is later read — which is exactly the goalpost-moving the semantics undertakes to avoid. (3) Serious. (4) State whether "licensed by the module" is a discovery or a stipulation, and, if a discovery, what decides it.

## On Q4 — Is the artefact case rightly placed under (D)?

**Finding 12. The artefact case matches none of (D)'s disjuncts.**

(1) Part XV: "what is missing is not a second target but a provenance clause on the target's own components (that the effect's component was selected by the data-gathering, not by the population), which the historical kinds of Part II can carry." Attack (D)'s disjuncts are: "a selection history can guarantee fidelity off the history …"; "construction reduces to selection …"; "the layers described in Part IV are not what explanation operates on." (2) A missing provenance clause on the *target's own components* is none of (i) selection over-guarantees, (ii) construction collapses to selection, or (iii) the layers are wrong. It is a claim that the four elements of the constitutive conjecture do not cover a class of explanations. Part 0 says such a case "counts against the conjecture." Filing it under (D) places a potential refutation of the conjecture inside an attack on a definition's coherence. (3) Serious. (4) Refiling: put it as a proposed fifth element of the constitutive conjecture, or exhibit an argument that it reduces to one of (D)'s three disjuncts.

**Finding 13. The artefact case need not be a "missing condition" at all.**

(1) "what the semantics lacks is a condition on the provenance of the target's own components (that the effect's component was selected by the gathering of the data and not by the population)." (2) If the target \(D\) is drawn to include the data-gathering subnetwork — as the semantics' own (F2) routinely allows D to do — then the artefact explanation is an ordinary transport whose anchor map lands on that subnetwork, and no new condition is required. If \(D\) is drawn without the data-gathering subnetwork, no clause on the target's component-provenance can distinguish artefact from real effect, because \(D\) contains no component for the distinction. So the artefact case is a question about how the target was drawn. The stated "condition versus transport" dichotomy is false. (3) Serious. (4) Exhibit \(D\) for the artefact case and show it under (F1)/(F2) before asserting a missing condition.

**Finding 14. "Missing condition" contradicts Part II.**

(1) Part II: "A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from." Part XV: "which the historical kinds of Part II can carry; the construction is not written." (2) If historical kinds can carry the provenance clause, the semantics does not "lack a condition"; it lacks a particular predicate. A predicate-to-be-written is not, without further argument, a missing element of the constitutive conjecture. The document's use of "missing" inflates the significance of the case and then relies on the inflation when it files the case under (D). (3) Serious. (4) Call it "a construction not yet written" and place it under (B)/(F)-style open items, not under (D).

**Finding 15. If the artefact case requires a genuinely new clause, it refutes the constitutive conjecture, and the document should say so.**

(1) "The constitutive conjecture is this: explanatory creativity is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports … checked by change-fidelity, (iii) the provenance of those transports and of the questions they serve, and (iv) their physical realization." (2) (iii) is about provenance of transports and of questions — not of the *target's own components*. A clause that has to be added to do the artefact work is therefore a fifth element. Part 0 states that a genuine explanatory achievement these four cannot represent "counts against the conjecture." The artefact case, if genuinely a missing condition, refutes the conjecture, and (D) is the wrong place to file it. (3) Serious. (4) Move it to a section that reports refutations of the conjecture, or deny (as Findings 13–14 recommend) that a new clause is needed.

## On Q5 — Is symmetry really beyond (F2)?

**Finding 16. The "open" verdict is a not-yet-checked verdict.**

(1) "(F1) is per-component and does not constrain it; (F2), which constrains the assembled solution set, carries some inter-component entailments, and whether it carries this one depends on the edit set, which is the door and is not walked through here." (2) Two standard encodings of an explanation by symmetry both place it inside (F1) or (F2). (a) If the symmetry is an admitted edit on \(D\) (a reflection of the apparatus, an interchange of two equivalent ports), then (F2) at the translated edit is exactly the equivariance to which the symmetry explanation appeals. (b) If the symmetry is a component of \(D\) (a constraint on \(D\)), the anchor subnetwork's solution set already carries it, and (F1)'s projection carries the entailment. The document needs a case where the symmetry does explanatory work but is neither an admitted edit nor a component; none is given. "The door is not walked through here" describes an unfinished check; it does not establish that the case is structurally beyond (F1)/(F2). Filing it as the leading case of (B) is over-filing. (3) Serious. (4) Exhibit the case, or downgrade "open" to "unfinished".

**Finding 17. "(F1) is per-component and does not constrain it" is a non-sequitur.**

(1) "whose force is an entailment among \(E\)'s components, from invariance to independence." (2) (F1) does not inspect how a component relation came to be held — as an axiom, by symmetry, by dimensional analysis, or by being lifted from a table. It compares the resulting relation to the anchor subnetwork. The document already treats derivation-style this way elsewhere: the reversed calculation's presentation as a reversed equation does not exempt it from (F2) (Part VII: "(F2) fails at that pair"). By the same standard, an entailment among components of \(E\) determines what those components' relations are; (F1) checks what they are. The inference from "entailment among \(E\)'s components" to "outside (F1)/(F2)" does not go through. (3) Serious. (4) Drop the inference, or supply a case whose *resulting* component relations cannot be matched to any anchor subnetwork on the admitted contract.

**Finding 18. If the symmetry is a property of the target's components, Finding 12–15 applies.**

(1) In the typical case (the field at the centre of a symmetric configuration), the symmetry is a property of the target's components, not of \(E\). (2) The same "condition on the target's own components" that underlies the artefact case is what is doing the work here, and the same question — whether the constitutive conjecture's (i)–(iv) cover it — arises. (3) Minor. (4) Consolidate with Findings 12–15.

## Additional findings on the same sentences

**Finding 19. "Scoped by design" for impossibility explanations is not a scope; it is a place where the module is invoked to do work the semantics then refuses it.**

(1) Part VII / Part XV (iii): "the edit belongs to \(K\), declared with its meaning ('a component enforcing conservation is removed') … what such an account establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual." (2) If the rival is physically impossible, the module's \(A_\Theta(o)\) should already exclude the edits realising it; impossibility should be derivable from \(A_\Theta(o)\) modulo the target's solution set, with no declaration required. Routing impossibility through a declared \(K\)-pair means impossibility is asserted by the modeller, not derived from the module. Calling this "scoped by design" labels a gap as a design choice. (3) Serious. (4) Supply a derivation of impossibility from \(A_\Theta(o)\), or state the limitation as a limitation of the semantics.

**Finding 20. "Relativises mathematics" removes mathematical cases from (B)'s test by construction.**

(1) "(iv) relative, not open — mathematical explanation falls under a declared admission relation; it is closed relative to that declaration and does not fall under this attack's quantifier over admitted contracts at all." (2) (B) is the necessity test. If mathematical cases are exempted from (B), they cannot furnish a counterexample to necessity; the exemption is an exclusion of a class of test cases, not a result about the class. The document's language slides from "does not bear on (B)" to "handled". (3) Minor. (4) State plainly that mathematical cases are outside (B), and therefore cannot confirm the constitutive conjecture either.

**Finding 21. The failing-obligation condition is temporally circular.**

(1) "\(p\) is a coherently posed question whose question-organization some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates before \(e\), and some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\)." (2) \(O_p\) is a component of \(p\), and \(p\) is the content being constructed at \(e\) (via Derivation 5). "Some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\)" then requires the obligation-set of \(p\) to antecede \(p\). If \(O_p\) is supplied by a standing obligation-set of \(s\) that \(p\) picks up, this must be said; as written, the timing is undefined. (3) Minor. (4) Distinguish the system's standing obligation-set from \(O_p\) as a component of the question, and state which one the failing-obligation condition quantifies over.

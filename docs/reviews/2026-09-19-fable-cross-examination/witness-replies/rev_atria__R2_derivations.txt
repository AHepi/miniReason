# Part XVI — Referee's findings

Findings numbered globally. Quotations are verbatim from the section under attack.

## Derivation 1

**1. Part V cites this derivation without its injectivity hypothesis.** Part V states: *"By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract."* But Derivation 1's own claim opens with the hypothesis *"let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\),"* which is not part of (F1), is not assumed anywhere in Part V, and is not implied by (F2)'s homomorphism conditions \(\tau(1)=1,\ \tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1)\) (a homomorphism need not be injective; Part VII even exhibits a transport where \(\tau\) is *undefined* on some pairs). So the entailment Part V relies on holds only under an unstated condition. **Serious.** Fix: either add injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) to the definition of a transport, or restate Part V's sentence as "By Derivation 1, under injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\), (F1) entails…". Note also that Part XV's safety net (*"A counterexample to … Derivations 1–3 and 8 under their stated assumptions"*) does not cover Part V's injectivity-free usage.

**2. "Of one forward kind" is applied outside its definition.** Part II defines the relation for *"two components \(j,j'\)"* of an organization, and calls a forward kind *"an equivalence class under this relation."* Derivation 1's remark concludes *"a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork,"* and Part V's load-bearing condition 4 says *"Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor."* The anchor is a *subnetwork of a different organization*; the component↔subnetwork comparison is not the relation Part II defined (though the subnetwork signature is defined, so the extension is available). **Minor.** Fix: state the cross-organization version explicitly (signature coincidence under the port bijection \(\lambda\) supplies) and drop the "equivalence class" framing there.

**3. The anchor's visible ports are silently declared.** The proof says: *"the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\)."* Part II makes \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) *"a declared subset"* — so this is an extra declaration, not a consequence, and (F1) as written (\(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}]\)) is only well-typed once the port translation is applied, since \(V_k\) are ports of \(E\) while \(\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}\) lives on ports of \(D\). **Minor.** Fix: make the port translation explicit inside (F1), or add the visible-port declaration to the transport data.

## Derivation 2

**4. The hypotheses are stronger than the definition requires, and (F2) is idle.** The claim assumes the two candidates *"have the same anchor map up to that bijection."* Identical anchor subnetworks have identical solution sets, so the definition's clause *"the anchored relations … coincide"* is then automatic and (F1) is not needed for it; and (A) makes *"the pulled-back answer profiles coincide"* automatic for both candidates (each equals \(\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)\)). (F2) is listed among the hypotheses and never used in the proof. The derivation is thus an unpacking of a definition, not a result. **Minor.** Fix: drop (F2); either drop the shared-anchor-map hypothesis and prove that (F1) + shared anchors forces component-relation coincidence, or present the claim as a remark.

**5. The definition omits the port bijection.** The definition requires *"a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\)"* under which *"the anchored relations … coincide."* Relations on different port sets cannot coincide without a port bijection, and none is part of the definition. **Minor.** Fix: include the port bijection (the one \(\lambda,\lambda'\) supply) in the definition.

## Derivation 3

**6. The proof needs a disjointness condition that is neither stated nor implied.** The proof says: *"The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives."* The closure hypothesis alters \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) for \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\). But if some \((a',b')\in H\) has \((\tau(a'),\sigma(b'))=(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) — possible exactly when \((\tau,\sigma)\) is non-injective on \(C\) — then the alteration changes the relation at a pair in \(H\), so the altered pair does *not* agree on \(H\) and its survival is unestablished (worse, under non-injectivity the closure hypothesis can be outright unsatisfiable, making the claim vacuous). Nothing in Part IV's selection witness or Part V's (F2) supplies injectivity. **Serious.** Fix: add to the hypotheses that \((\tau,\sigma)\) is injective on \(C\) (or at least \((\tau,\sigma)[C\setminus H]\cap(\tau,\sigma)[H]=\varnothing\)), and note that this is the same injectivity Derivation 1 needs and that the document nowhere guarantees it.

**7. "The admissible class" is not indexed.** The closure hypothesis quantifies over *"each relation \(L'\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\))."* Realizability is relative to a component *and* a translated edit–boundary pair. On the union reading, the hypothesis can demand members that do not exist (vacuity); on the indexed reading it is a substantially stronger mixing closure than the parenthetical suggests. **Minor,** with vacuity risk. Fix: define \(\operatorname{Adm}(j,\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\{L^{T}_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b)):T\in\mathcal T\}\) and state closure relative to it.

## Derivation 4

**8. Scope of "surprise" is narrower than the remark suggests.** The remark is correct as an unpacking, but Part IV defines expectation/violation/surprise only for *"Let \(t\) be selected on \(H\)"* — so *"a system with no transport is never surprised"* should read "no *selected* transport," and surprise is simply undefined for systems bearing only declared or constructed transports. **Cosmetic.** Fix: one qualifying clause.

## Derivation 5

**9. The solutions are *not* exactly the coherently posed questions.** The claim asserts: *"Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\)."* Part III's coherent-pose conditions are: \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\); \(\mathcal Q\) defined on every pair in \(C\); and \(S,K\) disjoint. Against the construction: (i) the component *"if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads 'in' then so do its factors where the composition is defined"* imposes composition-closure on the contract, which Part III never requires — over-constraining; (ii) no listed component enforces that \(\mathcal Q\)'s value at \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) is defined on every "in" pair — under-constraining; (iii) the \(S/K\) form is enforced only by the vague *"\(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III,"* and \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is neither a port nor in the boundary (*"the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed"*), so the constraint cannot even be evaluated. **Serious.** Fix: drop the composition-closure component (or add composition-closure to Part III and justify it); add a component testing \(\mathcal Q\)'s domain against the in-ports; put \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) into the boundary data.

**10. Questions are not individuated as contents, so (G)'s New conjunct is not established.** The construction yields one organization \(D_p\) whose solutions are *all* coherently posed questions on \(D\); the claim then says *"an episode whose originative contribution is a new question satisfies (G)."* But (G) requires \(\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\), and \(\equiv_\ell\) (Part X) is defined on *contents* via *"the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8."* On the only reading the construction supports, \(c=D_p\), which is determined by \(D\) alone — so any earlier question on the same target already puts a \(\equiv_\ell\)-equivalent content in the repertoire and New fails. The case the semantics most wants (finding a *new question on an old target*) is therefore not proved. **Serious.** Fix: define the found question's content as \((D_p,\text{designated solution})\) and extend \(\equiv_\ell\) to such pairs, or build a per-question organization with a singleton solution set.

**11. The claim is hedged into vacuity.** *"Hence an episode whose originative contribution is a new question satisfies (G) when the other conjuncts and the merit condition hold"* — since (G) *is* Attempt ∧ New ∧ Build, this reads "satisfies (G) when it satisfies (G)." Combined with finding 10, nothing is established. **Minor.** Fix: state which conjuncts the construction delivers and which remain open.

## Derivation 6

**12. Only one potential cycle is addressed; two live dependencies are unchecked.** The proof breaks only *"the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction."* Unchecked: (a) Part IX's active route requires *"a represented input,"* i.e. the (R) predicate, so (G) → Attempt → active route → (R) → provenance; (b) Part IV's \(\operatorname{Con}\) is defined via *"an episode (Part X),"* and Part X defines both the complete critical episode and the *creative* one, which *"contains an instance of (G)"* — so if Con's episode is the creative one, (G) → (R) → Con → (G) is a genuine cycle. Derivation 6 does not exclude that reading. (c) The assertion *"(R) is not used inside any provenance witness"* holds only if "representation" in Sel's blindness clause (*"no itemised representation of the defect"*) and "carriers" in the construction witness are informal; both terms are technical elsewhere. **Serious.** Fix: state that \(\operatorname{Con}\) requires only a *complete* (non-creative) critical episode; say whether "represented"/"representation" in Part IX and Part IV's blindness clause mean (R) or an informal record, and make the stratification explicit in each case.

## Derivation 7

**13. Derivation 5 is mis-cited.** The proof says: *"The episode is a history in which a question-organization with contract \(C'\) is built (Derivation 5)."* Derivation 5 constructs \(D_p\), whose own admitted edits are toggles and alterations — it does not construct "a question-organization *with contract \(C'\)"; the built content is the question \(p'=(D,C',\dots)\), which per finding 10 is not individuated by the construction. **Minor.** Fix: say the witness yields \(p'\) as a solution of \(D_p\), and cite Derivation 5 that way.

**14. Adequacy does not "catch" index changes.** *"the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate."* Adequacy is a condition on a fixed \(C\) relative to \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); it *evaluates* a contract, it does not detect that the index moved. A widened contract the candidate fails is not "caught," it merely fails; only a *narrowing* that excludes a separating admitted pair is flagged. **Minor.** Fix: reword to "adequacy is re-evaluated at the new index, and a narrowed scope excluding a separating admitted pair fails it."

## Derivation 8

**15. Solution-preservation is assumed, not proved — and Part X depends on it.** The proof: *"Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data, and each of its inputs is carried along."* But (F1) and (F2) are equalities involving \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) and \(\operatorname{Sol}_E\), and \(\operatorname{Sol}\) is *constructed* from the interpretation \(L\) by (O). That \(\varphi(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b))=\operatorname{Sol}_{\varphi(D)}(\varphi(a),\varphi(b))\) is an extra property of \(\varphi\) (essentially that \(\varphi\) is an isomorphism of the (O)-structure), not a consequence of being *"a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports, …."* Part X then cites this derivation as *the* definition of \(\equiv_\ell\), which (N) and Build both use. **Serious.** Fix: state the solution-preservation property as the defining condition of the permitted \(\varphi\), and establish closure of \(\equiv_\ell\) under composition.

**16. The restriction on bijections is not licensed by the stated hypotheses.** The hypothesis lists what \(\varphi\) preserves: *"the physical module's admission (\(A_\Theta\), Inst, \(\prec\)), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and \(\mathcal N\)."* The proof then excludes the pole-height→shadow-length recoding *"since it does not preserve \(D\)'s structure."* But "preserving \(D\)'s structure" is not among the stated preservation conditions; a length-port swap can preserve \(A_\Theta\), Inst, \(\prec\), scopes, meanings and \(\mathcal N\) in a symmetric module. What it violates is preservation of the component relations, which appears only under the unexplained *"relations."* So the exclusion is not entailed by the hypotheses as written. **Serious.** Fix: add to the hypothesis that \(\varphi\) is an isomorphism of each organization — ports, domains, components, boundaries, admitted edits, component relations pointwise — i.e. make the \(\equiv_\ell\)-style condition the *definition* of the permitted \(\varphi\), not an afterthought in the proof.

**17. Provenance and causal responsibility are not shown to be preserved.** (G), (P), (EK) all route through Deploy → (R) → \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\). \(\operatorname{Sel}\) requires that *"the environment's filtering [be] causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it."* The hypothesis preserves \(\prec\) but not causal responsibility as such; nothing shows a \(\varphi\) preserving \(\prec\) preserves \(\operatorname{Sel}\), hence (R), hence Deploy, hence the predicates claimed equivariant. **Serious.** Fix: either define causal responsibility from \(\prec\) and say so, or add preservation of the selection/construction witnesses to the hypothesis.

## Derivation 9

**18. Valid but conditional on an unexhibited antecedent.** *"If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both."* The proof (*"Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs"*) is correct, but the load-bearing antecedent — two systems sharing an I/O projection while differing on Account — is never instantiated (Part V's counterexamples are within-organization, not two-system). **Minor.** Fix: exhibit such a pair (two wiring diagrams, same I/O table, different active routes), or re-label as a remark.

## Derivation 10

**19. The surprise requires the occlusion pair to be in the contract.** *"That is a violation off \(H_0\): surprise by definition."* Part IV defines surprise as a violation at \((a,b)\in C\) with \((a,b)\notin H\). The scenario specifies \(H_0\) (*"containing displacements and velocity changes but no occlusions"*) but never states that the occlusion edit–boundary pair is in \(S_0\)'s contract \(C\); if it is not, then per Part IV *"a change outside \(C\) altogether can neither violate nor surprise,"* and the scenario's central event is unrepresented. **Minor.** Fix: state that \(C\) contains the occlusion pair while \(H_0\) does not.

**20. The citation of Derivation 1 inherits its unverified injectivity.** *"then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things."* Derivation 1 needs \((\tau,\sigma)\) injective on \(C\); \(t_1\) is not written out, so this is unverified — and the scenario's hedge (*"(F1) and (F2) … is not checked here"*) does not cover the extra injectivity requirement. **Minor.** Fix: extend the hedge to include injectivity.

**21. "Any contract containing the swap" is too weak.** *"the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap."* Signature coincidence under the swap-induced footprint bijection requires the *rest* of the contract to be swap-symmetric (a contract containing "displace thing 1" but not "displace thing 2" breaks it, since the two components' relations then differ at an admitted pair). **Minor.** Fix: restrict the claim to swap-symmetric contracts.

---

**Summary of the damage.** Derivations 4 and 9 are sound as labelled; Derivation 10 is honestly hedged apart from findings 19–21. The failures that matter are: Part V's injectivity-free use of Derivation 1 (finding 1); Derivation 3's unproved survival step under non-injective \((\tau,\sigma)\) (finding 6); Derivation 5's false "exactly" and its failure to individuate questions as contents, which jointly leave question-finding not established as a creative act (findings 9–10); Derivation 6's incomplete stratification argument (finding 12); and Derivation 8's circular-feeling proof, which assumes solution-preservation, mis-specifies its own restriction on bijections, and ignores provenance — while being cited by Part X as the definition of \(\equiv_\ell\) (findings 15–17). Derivation 2 is valid but near-vacuous.

**Short answers to the five questions.** (i) Structural silence is well-defined *relative to* λ, Γ, ℓ and C_adm, but "touches" is only glossed, never defined with a baseline, and the resulting notion is only immune to gaming under the anchored grade. (ii) Properness does guarantee non-silence on pairs that change a component relation of D — but not on pairs that change the answer through the boundary or the query alone. (iii) No: the extension "by λ" is either redundant (τ is already total on A_D) or a new unstated constraint on τ, and under the first reading it is manipulable, under the second it trivialises non-protection. (iv) It is too weak in at least four distinct ways (silence at the first grade, τ off C, Q's declared domain, undefined candidate answers) and, given anchoring, not too strong. (v) It does not collide fatally with (K), Derivation 7 or Part VIII, but it does collide with Grievance 4's own gloss of protection, and its Part VIII citation is unsupported.

---

**1. The objectivity condition is imposed on first-grade Account, but its anti-silence defence only works for anchored accounts.**
Claim attacked: "An **objectivity claim** for \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) is the conjunction of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) and a claim about \(C\) against the module in force: that \(C\) is **not protective** for \(\mathcal E\)", together with Part XV's defence: "Under the structural definition of silence this can only happen when the candidate's decomposition omits a component the change touches, which properness forbids for anchored accounts".
Why it fails: properness is a conjunct of the *anchored* grade, not of (E) and not of the objectivity claim. So a candidate satisfying (E) alone can declare an anchor map that omits a C_adm-touched component of D from every active anchor. (F1) is only required on C; if the omitted component is touched only by pairs outside C, it behaves as a fixed background relation there, (F2) still holds on C, and the candidate is *silent* on exactly the pairs that would expose it. Its objectivity claim then comes out true. Part XV's defence explicitly hedges "for anchored accounts" and therefore does not cover the condition as stated.
Severity: serious — this defeats the condition's stated purpose (the reply to Grievance 4) for every non-anchored candidate.
Fix: make the objectivity claim **Anchored(E,p,t,Γ) ∧ non-protection**, or add properness of λ on the C_adm-touched components as an explicit conjunct of the claim.

**2. "It is not under the candidate's control: it is read off λ" is false at the first grade.**
Claim attacked: "Silence — a pair touching no anchor — is a stated limit of scope, not protection, and it is not under the candidate's control: it is read off \(\lambda\)."
Why it fails: λ is transport data, declared with the transport, and unconstrained off C by (E). Far from being outside the candidate's control, silence is *directly* controllable by choosing a coarser or shifted λ. Only the anchored grade's properness, resolution and world-kind jointly remove that freedom. The sentence is true for anchored accounts and false for the accounts the condition actually ranges over.
Severity: serious (it is the load-bearing assurance of the whole clause).
Fix: restrict the claim to anchored accounts, or state the degree of control explicitly and accept the consequence.

**3. The extension of the transport to excluded pairs "by λ" is not well-defined, and is manipulable in the direction that matters.**
Claim attacked: "the transport being extended to such pairs by \(\lambda\) (the edit acts on an anchored component, so its translation is the corresponding edit on the anchored component of \(E\))".
Why it fails: Part IV defines "\(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits" — τ is already total on A_D, so no extension is needed and λ does no work. But then nothing requires τ(a), for a ∈ C_adm\C, to have anything to do with λ; a candidate can declare τ on the excluded pair to be an edit that forces E's answer onto the target's (e.g. a port-setting edit, which A_E contains by Part II's definition of "input"), and non-protection is bought by declaration. Conversely, if the parenthetical is read as a *constraint* ("τ(a) is the edit making E's anchored component respond as the anchor does"), non-protection becomes near-trivially automatic and the test is empty. The clause is ill-defined between two readings with opposite consequences. Additionally, "the corresponding edit on the anchored component" is singular where a touches components lying in several active anchors.
Severity: serious.
Fix: state an explicit λ-consistency condition on transports for objectivity claims — τ(a) must act on exactly the components of E anchored to the components of D that a touches, compositionally — and prove it is checkable on C_adm; or define protection via the anchor's own projected answer rather than via τ.

**4. "Touches" is only glossed, and the missing baseline lets boundary-driven answer changes escape.**
Claim attacked: "every admitted pair that **touches** the anchor — changes the relation of some component in it", and "by properness an anchored account is silent on no admitted pair that touches a component the question's contract reaches".
Why it fails: "changes the relation" is never given a baseline (relative to (1,b)? to (1,b₀)?). The choice is not idle. Take the identification question of Part VII, whose query reads the fibre at a boundary value y; the pair (1,b) with b ≠ b₀ changes Ans_p while changing no component relation L_j. Under the natural reading it touches no anchor, so it is silence, so a candidate wrong about the fibre at an excluded boundary is not protective — even though it is wrong about an admitted change on a component it covers. Properness, which covers only components touched by C_adm pairs, says nothing about this. So the answer to "does properness make an anchored account silent on nothing it should answer for" is: yes for component-relation changes, no for boundary/query-mediated answer changes.
Severity: serious.
Fix: define touches_U(a,b) := ∃j∈U, L_j(a,b) ≠ L_j(1,b) with the baseline stated, and add "or changes Ans_p through the boundary"; equivalently, define protection by answer-divergence directly and drop the component-level intermediary.

**5. Non-protection can be vacuous because Q's domain is only required to cover C.**
Claim attacked: the protective test's guard "on which the target's answer is defined", read with Part III's coherence condition: "\(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\)".
Why it fails: nothing requires Q (or the transported query Q_E) to be defined on C_adm\C. A question whose Q has domain exactly C makes "the target's answer is defined" fail everywhere in C_adm\C, so non-protection holds vacuously — and this hole applies to *anchored* accounts too, since world-kind is signature-level and never looks at Ans_p off C. The same applies on the candidate side if Q_E's domain is only τ[C].
Severity: serious (an escape route, admittedly unnatural).
Fix: for any objectivity claim, require dom(Q) ⊇ {touched pairs of C_adm} and dom(Q_E) ⊇ their translations; or index the claim's strength by the pair-set on which both answers are defined.

**6. An undefined candidate answer on a touched excluded pair is not protection.**
Claim attacked: "and on which the target's answer is defined and the candidate's transported answer differs from it".
Why it fails: the condition requires a *defined differing* answer. If Sol_E(τ(a),σ(b)) = ∅, or Q_E is undefined there, the candidate breaks exactly where the scope hid it, yet the test does not fire. "Differs" must cover undefined-where-the-target-is-defined.
Severity: minor.
Fix: treat undefined candidate answer on a non-silent excluded pair as protection (or as a separate failure of the claim).

**7. The test is answer-level only, which is in tension with the document's own rationale for (F1).**
Claim attacked: "Being wrong about a change the scope hides, on a component the candidate covers, is protection", against "(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition."
Why it fails: for a first-grade candidate, a component relation can be wrong on a covered excluded pair while the answer coincides; that is not protection. For an anchored candidate, world-kind already excludes component-level wrongness on C_adm, so the objectivity condition's only remaining work is assembly-level ((F2)/Q_E) failure — a fact the text never states, and which makes the clause's "on a component the candidate covers" phrasing misleading: what is covered is the answer, not the component's relation.
Severity: minor.
Fix: either add (F1)-failure on touched excluded pairs to protection, or say plainly that objectivity is answer-level and that component-level off-C wrongness is excluded only by the anchored grade.

**8. World-kind's "fidelity holds … on every admitted pair that touches the anchor" is ambiguous between (F1) and (F1)+(F2).**
Claim attacked: "fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every admitted pair that **touches** the anchor".
Why it fails: Part IV defines "faithful on C for Γ" as (F1) for those components *and* (F2) on C. If world-kind extends the whole fidelity relation, the anchored grade requires assembly fidelity on C_adm-touched pairs — far stronger than the surrounding text ("Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract") suggests, and it makes the objectivity condition's answer-test largely redundant. If it extends only (F1)/signature, "fidelity" is being used loosely and the anchored grade permits assembly decoys off C. The choice changes what the objectivity condition is even for.
Severity: minor-to-serious (drafting ambiguity with substantive consequences).
Fix: state that world-kind extends only (K)/(F1) to C_adm, and say what the objectivity condition then adds.

**9. Grievance 4's gloss of protection diverges from Part V's definition.**
Claim attacked, from Part 0: "a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's, or the reverse."
Why it fails: that is a *change-of-answer* test, (Δtarget ∧ ¬Δcandidate) ∨ (¬Δtarget ∧ Δcandidate); Part V uses a *value* test, Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b)) ≠ Ans_p(a,b). A pair on which both answers change but to different values is protective under Part V and not protective under Grievance 4. The two formulations must be reconciled.
Severity: minor (cosmetic inconsistency, but checkable against the text).
Fix: rewrite Grievance 4 to the value test.

**10. "Its objectivity claim is correspondingly smaller" is unformalised, and Part I/Part 0 mislocate the relata.**
Claim attacked: "its scope is a stated limit, and its objectivity claim is correspondingly smaller"; Part I: "Objectivity is account-hood plus non-protection of the contract against the module in force"; Part 0: "'C is not protective' is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes".
Why it fails: formally, the objectivity claim is a conjunction; silence makes the second conjunct *hold*, not shrink. There is no notion of a smaller claim anywhere. And non-protection is not a fact about C against the module — it depends on λ, τ, Γ and Q_E, i.e. on candidate and transport data. Relatedly, protection is a property of the *quadruple*, but Grievance 4 and Part XV ("a scope whose exclusion … protects the candidate") cite it as a property of the candidate; since Account(E,p) existentially quantifies t and Γ, and different witnesses give different verdicts, "protective for the candidate" is not well-defined at the two-place predicate.
Severity: minor.
Fix: state protection as quadruple-relative throughout; either formalise claim-strength (index the claim by the non-silent pair-set) or drop "correspondingly smaller".

**11. The Part VIII citation is unsupported: the objectivity claim carries a module index and an occurrence index that Part VIII never states.**
Claim attacked: "The claim is indexed to the module in force: a later module with a larger \(A_\Theta\) makes it a new claim at a new index (Part VIII)", against Part VIII: "A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract…".
Why it fails: Part VIII's Historical index is stated for *contract* indices only; it says nothing about module indices. And Part III defines C_adm as \(A_\Theta(o)\times B\) — occurrence-relative — so the index of the objectivity claim is really (C, Θ, o), not (C, Θ).
Severity: minor.
Fix: extend Part VIII to state that claims are indexed by (contract, module, occurrence), and cite that rather than the contract-index paragraph.

**12. No fatal collision with (K) or Derivation 7 — but the contact points are loose.**
Claim attacked: Derivation 7's consequence, "the objectivity condition of Part V answers it only where the candidate is evaluable and wrong on the excluded change."
Why it fails: "evaluable" must mean "non-silent", which is not Derivation 7's term; and Derivation 7's consistency proof runs through Part VIII's contract-index fixity, which (finding 11) does not cover the objectivity claim's second index. There is no contradiction — the claim can keep its truth value at (C,Θ,o) while a later module creates a new claim — but the derivation does not establish it. Against (K) there is no collision: world-kind's use of signature on C_adm minus the baseline is consistent with Part II's exclusion of (1,b₀) (which is in C anyway, hence not in C_adm\C); the only effect is that the objectivity condition is nearly redundant with world-kind at component level (finding 7).
Severity: minor.
Fix: add one clause to Derivation 7 noting the objectivity claim's second index and that its fixity follows from the same principle, once Part VIII is extended as in finding 11.

**One further observation, not a defect in the condition itself:** the objectivity claim is only as objective as the module. "\(\mathsf{Admit}\)" and \(A_\Theta(o)\) are primitive (Part XII), so Grievance 4's demand is met with objectivity *relative to the module in force*, not simpliciter. The text half-concedes this ("the module is the semantics' only access to the world"); it should say so where the objectivity condition is stated, not only in the anchored grade.

**Minimal repair that closes findings 1–5.** Define: an objectivity claim for \(\mathcal E\) is **Anchored**(E,p,t,Γ) ∧ non-protection, where non-protection reads: for every (a,b) ∈ C_adm\C, if Ans_p(a,b) is defined then Ans_E(τ(a),σ(b)) is defined and equal to it — with τ required to be λ-consistent on C_adm, dom(Q) required to cover the touched pairs of C_adm, and "touched" defined with a stated baseline plus the boundary clause of finding 4. That version is not gameable by λ or τ, is not vacuous, and makes properness do the work the text already attributes to it.

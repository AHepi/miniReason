## Part XVI, Derivation 1

**Finding 1. (F1) is ill-typed, and the proof smuggles in an unstated visible-ports hypothesis.**
Attacked: (Part V) "`proj_{V_k}[Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b)] = L_k(τ(a),σ(b))`" together with (Derivation 1) "the visible ports of the anchor being those λ translates to `V_k`".
Why it fails: `Sol_{λ(k)}` is a set of valuations on ports of *D*, while `V_k` is a set of ports of *E*; the projection is not defined without λ's port translation. Part II says a subnetwork's visible ports are "a declared subset `V_U^vis` of its ports", and Part IV says λ comes "with a port translation" — nothing requires `V_{λ(k)}^vis` to be the preimage of `V_k`. If the anchor has extra visible ports, its signature carries strictly more information than the component's and Derivation 1's conclusion fails.
Severity: serious.
Fix: add to the transport definition (Part IV) the clause that λ's port translation is a bijection `V_{λ(k)}^vis ≅ V_k` for each active *k*, and rewrite (F1) as projection along that translation.

**Finding 2. Part V cites Derivation 1 as stronger than it is: the injectivity hypothesis is dropped.**
Attacked: (Part V) "By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of *E* has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract".
Why it fails: Derivation 1's hypothesis is "(F1) **and** `(τ,σ)` injective on `C`". (F2) gives only `τ(1)=1` and `τ(a₂a₁)=τ(a₂)τ(a₁)` — a homomorphism, not a monomorphism. Without injectivity the two signature sets need not be in bijection: if `(a₁,b)≠(a₂,b)` in `C` with `τ(a₁)=τ(a₂)`, then `sig_C(λ(k))` has two triples and `sig_{τ[C]}(k)` one (F1 does force the third coordinates to agree on such a fibre, but the sets still differ), so the component is *not* of one forward kind with its anchor.
Severity: serious.
Fix: restate the Part V sentence as "By Derivation 1, (F1) **plus injectivity of `(τ,σ)` on `C`** entails…", or prove the weaker quotient-statement actually available without injectivity.

**Finding 3. Derivation 10 asserts the injectivity Derivation 1 needs, on a bad reason.**
Attacked: "by Derivation 1, with `(τ,σ)` injective on that contract (which it is, since the translation is a relabelling)".
Why it fails: a relabelling is a bijection on ports/components; injectivity of the *pair*-map `(a,b) ↦ (τ(a),σ(b))` on the contract additionally requires σ to be injective on the boundaries occurring in `C`, which the scenario never fixes. The parenthetical is a non sequitur, not a check.
Severity: minor (the scenario is explicitly unchecked, but the justification is invalid as written).
Fix: either stipulate σ injective on the extended contract's boundaries or mark injectivity as an unverified assumption, as (F1)/(F2) are.

## Part XVI, Derivation 2

**Finding 4. "Account-equivalent" is not well-defined as stated: the bijection is only on components.**
Attacked: "there is a bijection between their active components carrying `Γ` onto `Γ'` under which, for every `(a,b)∈C`, the anchored relations `proj_{V_k}Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b)` coincide".
Why it fails: the anchored relation of *E* lives on ports of *E*, that of *E'* on ports of *E'*; "coincide" needs a port-level bijection and an anchor identification, neither of which the definition supplies. The claim then imports one — "have the same anchor map up to that bijection" — which is not a condition of the defined relation.
Severity: serious.
Fix: state the bijection as a pair (component bijection, port bijection carrying anchor visible ports onto anchor visible ports and `λ` onto `λ'`), and require coincidence of the transported relations.

**Finding 6. "The pulled-back answer profiles coincide" is ambiguous, and the proof's step is valid under only one reading.**

## Part XVI, Derivation 3

**Finding 7. The derivation is valid, but Part XV's specified refuter is impossible as stated.**
Attacked: (Part XV) "A selection history `H⊊C`, in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off `H`, whose survivor is determined on `C∖H`. This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis."
Why it fails: Derivation 3's claim is existential and explicitly compatible with a faithful survivor ("It does not follow that the survivor *is* unfaithful there"). Under the closure hypothesis, either no admissible `L'` changes the solution set at any pair — then the claim's antecedent is false and it is vacuous, not refuted — or some does, and then closure supplies ≥2 surviving members differing at that pair, so the survival data do not determine the survivor. If "determined" means determined by the actual physical history, that is trivially true of every selection and refutes nothing.
Severity: serious (a reader hunting this counterexample is hunting for one that cannot exist).
Fix: restate the attack as exhibiting a closure-satisfying population whose *actual* survivor is faithful on all of `C` — which refutes nothing — or drop the line and keep only the honest reading: Derivation 3 establishes non-entailment, never actual unfaithfulness.

(The closure hypothesis itself is strong — a population containing, for every admissible relation, a variant of the survivor — but it is stated, so it is not a defect.)

## Part XVI, Derivation 5

**Finding 8. The "in"-ports' role is ambiguous, and the factor-closure component is unjustified — this gap is not in the list.**
Attacked: "the baseline port reads 'in'; if `u_{(a₂a₁,b)}` reads 'in' then so do its factors where the composition is defined", together with the claim "Its solutions are candidate questions on `D`; a particular question is a particular solution".
Why it fails: Part III imposes no factor-closure condition on contracts (`C=(C_adm∩S)∪K`). If the "in" set encodes `C` (or `C_adm∩S`), the closure component excludes legitimate, non-factor-closed contracts, so "solutions include the question" is false for those questions. Severity: serious.
Fix: say exactly what the "in" predicate ranges over; if it is `C_adm∩S`, either prove `S` may be assumed factor-closed or replace the closure component with a component that tests membership in a declared scope without imposing algebraic closure.

## Part XVI, Derivation 10

**Finding 17. "(G) holds" is asserted from `Build ∧ New`; (G) is `Attempt ∧ New ∧ Build`.**

**Finding 18. The swap paragraph misapplies (K): it needs a reading Part II does not state.**
Attacked: "Under (K), with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap".
Why it fails: Part II's forward-kind condition is "there is a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains, under which their signatures coincide on `C∖{(1,b₀)}`" — the bijection acts on ports only; the `(a,b)` coordinates are fixed. So coincidence requires `β(L₁(a,b)) = L₂(a,b)` at the *same* pair. At `(displace thing 1, b)`: `β(L₁(displace₁,b))` is component 2's relation under *displacing thing 2*, i.e. `L₂(displace₂,b)`, which differs from `L₂(displace₁,b)` (component 2 is untouched by displacing thing 1). Hence on the scenario's own contract — which contains displacements and velocity changes — the two components are *not* of one forward kind under the strict reading; the claim works only if the bijection is also allowed to act on the edit/boundary coordinate (`β(displace₁)=displace₂`), which Part II never says. The paragraph's conclusion ("identity, at this grain and contract, is exhausted by trajectory") therefore does not follow as written: under the strict reading trajectory *does* distinguish the components; under the liberal reading it does not, but the liberal reading is unstated.
Severity: serious.
Fix: amend Part II to state explicitly whether the port bijection induces (and is required to induce) a bijection on the edit–boundary coordinates; if it is, verify that this does not collapse any distinction Part II relies on (cause vs. correlation, measurement vs. rule); if it is not, restrict the swap claim to contracts whose acting edits are swap-invariant and withdraw "on any contract containing the swap".

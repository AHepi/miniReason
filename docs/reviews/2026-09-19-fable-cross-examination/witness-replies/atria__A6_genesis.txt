# (D) The three-way provenance distinction

**D1. *Selected* is trivially satisfiable, so every transport is selected and the distinction collapses.**
*Claim attacked:* "There is a population \(\mathcal T\) of candidate transports, a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\), a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, and a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\). The transport \(t\) is a member of \(\mathcal T\) that survived."
*Why it fails:* Nothing requires \(H\neq\varnothing\), nothing requires \(\mathcal T\) to have more than one member, nothing requires \(\mu\) to have operated, and nothing requires the survival condition to have *eliminated* anything. Take \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\), \(\mu=\mathrm{id}\), \(H=\varnothing\): the survival condition ("fidelity on \(\varnothing\)") is vacuous, \(t\) survives, and the negative clause is vacuously true. So \(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\) holds for **every** transport. Even the natural patch fails: requiring \(H\) nonempty still permits \(H=\{(1,b_0)\}\), which Part III guarantees is in \(C\), and fidelity at baseline is already demanded by (R) of any candidate for representation. Hence no non-vacuity is achievable without a *discrimination* requirement. Consequences: (i) \((\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))\) collapses to \(\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t)\), so (R) becomes mere fidelity; (ii) Part IV's "A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does" is now false, since a declared faithful transport is also selected; (iii) Derivation 3 becomes vacuous ("for every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\)" with \(H=\varnothing\) says every selected transport is unconstrained everywhere, including ones selected on rich histories); (iv) the Part I commitment "Two provenances, not one" is unfulfilled.
*Severity:* Fatal.
*Fix:* Require (a) \(H\) nonempty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial; (b) \(|\mathcal T|\ge2\) with at least one member *failing* the survival condition (selection must be discriminating); (c) \(\mu\) to have been physically applied; (d) the environment's filtering to be causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence, not merely consistent with it.

**D2. The answer to "can every construction witness be rewritten as a selection history?" is yes, and no relabelling is needed.**
*Claim attacked:* Part XV's defeat condition — "A demonstration that every construction witness can be rewritten as a selection history without loss. This collapses the two provenances and removes creativity from the semantics."
*Why it fails:* \(\operatorname{Sel}\) is existentially quantified over \((\mathcal T,\mu,H)\) with no requirement that the witness be the *actual* causal provenance of \(t\). Given \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\), choose \(\mathcal T=\{t\}\), \(H=\{(1,b_0)\}\) or any set of deployment encounters at which \(t\) was faithful, survival = fidelity on \(H\); \(t\) survives; and the negative clause holds because deployment encounters typically do not represent \(t\). So \(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\land\operatorname{Con}(t)\). The feared defeat is realized by the document's own definitions.
*Severity:* Fatal.
*Fix:* Make the selection witness *unique and causal*: require that the variation-and-survival process be the cause of \(t\)'s presence in the system, and require the witness to be non-trivial as in D1; or define provenance as a functional of the full physical history of \(t\)'s acquisition rather than by an existence claim.

**D3. The negative clause is circular through (R).**
*Claim attacked:* "No member of the history represents \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition."
*Why it fails:* The only defined sense of "represents" in the document is (R): "\(\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists t[\operatorname{Faithful}_C(t:\operatorname{Org}_\ell(o)\to c)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]\)". So \(\operatorname{Sel}\) is defined by negating \(\operatorname{Rep}\), and \(\operatorname{Rep}\) is defined by affirming \(\operatorname{Sel}\). This is a definitional cycle, not a well-founded derivation, and it contradicts Part XIV's dependence order ("(R) depends on (F1)–(F2) and physical provenance"). The alternative reading — that "represents" here is pre-theoretic — reintroduces representation as a primitive, contradicting Part 0 ("Correspondence between a representation and what it represents is not a primitive of this semantics") and Derivation 6's two-primitive claim. Additionally the clause is impredicative: \(H\) and the survival condition are themselves constituents of the witness whose negation mentions them.
*Severity:* Serious (fatal in combination with D1; reparable).
*Fix:* Replace "represents" with a non-semantic structural condition, e.g. "no occurrence in the history instantiates an organization admitting a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition" (fidelity carries no provenance dependence), plus a causal-independence condition on \(\mu\) (see D5).

**D4. The negative clause is ill-typed as written.**
*Claim attacked:* "a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered … No member of the history represents \(t\)…"
*Why it fails:* \(H\subseteq C\subseteq A\times B\), so a member of the history is an *edit–boundary pair*, not an occurrence. Pairs do not represent anything, so the clause is vacuous on the literal reading — which collapses Sel from Con independently of D1. On the intended reading the members must be *encounter events*, but the document never defines \(H\) as a set of occurrences; it defines it as a set of pairs.
*Severity:* Minor (one line to fix, but currently it makes the clause vacuous).
*Fix:* Define \(H\) as a finite sequence of physically located encounter occurrences together with their \((a,b)\) labels, and quantify the negative clause over those occurrences.

**D5. "Blind variation" is never formalized, and criticism is never excluded.**
*Claim attacked:* Part 0's gloss — "A correspondence can be *selected* — produced by blind variation and survival on a history of predictions — or *constructed* — produced by an episode of conjecture and criticism" — together with Part IV's "a selected transport has no represented target and no criticism in its history; a constructed one has both."
*Why it fails:* The formal \(\operatorname{Sel}\) clause restricts only what *members of \(H\)* represent. It does not restrict the variation operator \(\mu\). So variation steered by represented content (deliberate hypothesis-generation, guided search, a critic choosing which variants to produce) satisfies \(\operatorname{Sel}\) as long as the *test encounters* do not represent \(t\). Conversely the summary sentence mentions "no criticism," but no clause of \(\operatorname{Sel}\) mentions criticism at all. The formal separator is therefore neither blindness nor the presence of criticism. Worse, the separator is drawn at the wrong object: \(\operatorname{Con}\) requires "in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a represented target," while \(\operatorname{Sel}\) forbids representing \(t\), \(H\), or the survival condition — it does **not** forbid representing the *target organization*. So a system that fully represents and criticizes its target hypothesis but never represents its transport satisfies \(\operatorname{Sel}\) formally while being conjecture-and-criticism intuitively; and a genuinely blind selection process that happens to model its own mapping fails \(\operatorname{Sel}\).
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Require \(\mu\) to be a function of physical states not including represented targets (causal independence), require population members to be unrepresented in the history, and replace the transport-representation clause with a target-and-criticism clause matching the gloss.

**D6. \(\operatorname{Con}\) and \(\operatorname{Build}\) are also circular with (R), so the dependence order has a cycle.**
*Claim attacked:* "There is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which \(t\), or the organization it targets, is available as a **represented** target"; and "\(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a **represented** organization for explanatory use of \(c\)…". Part XIV asserts "Build depends on histories and (E)" and "(R) depends on (F1)–(F2) and physical provenance."
*Why it fails:* \(\operatorname{Con}\) and \(\operatorname{Build}\) both use the representation predicate; representation is (R); (R) is defined by \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\). So (R) \(\to\) \(\operatorname{Con}\) \(\to\) \(\operatorname{Build}\) \(\to\) (R). Part XIV's dependence order is therefore not well-founded, and the claim that everything is derived from \(\Theta\) and \(\mathcal N\) fails at exactly the point where creativity is located.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Define provenance purely by physical-historical structure (owned subhistory, bindings actually constructed, non-content-preserving, causal production of \(t\)) with no representation predicate; then \(\operatorname{Rep}\) follows from faithfulness plus provenance instead of entering their definitions.

**D7. "Exactly one of three" is false, and *Declared* conflates a catch-all with authorship.**
*Claim attacked:* "A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has exactly one of three provenances"; "**Declared.** Neither of the above. The transport is entered into the model by its author."; and "Construction may operate on selected material."
*Why it fails:* If a transport is selected and later rebuilt by a construction episode (the arrangement the document itself says is expected), both \(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\) and \(\operatorname{Con}(t)\) hold of the same token; the document gives no identity conditions for transports across episodes that would make these distinct tokens. And a transport that arises by drift — no variation, no survival, no construction, no author — is neither Sel nor Con, hence falls under the formal *Declared* clause while falsifying its gloss.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Index provenance to *segments* of the transport's history (a provenance record, not a label), and separate "neither Sel nor Con" from "author-entered" as two distinct predicates.

**D8. Part IV and Part XII give inequivalent definitions of \(\operatorname{Sel}\).**
*Claim attacked:* Part XII: "A selected provenance \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H)\) is a claim about a physical history: a population of realized transports, a physically admitted variation operator, and a survival condition enacted by the environment."
*Why it fails:* The Part XII restatement drops the negative representational clause, drops "requiring fidelity on \(H\)", and adds "enacted by the environment" and "physically admitted" — none of which appears in Part IV, the definition actually used by (R). So (R) has two inequivalent readings, and the one that is "fallible and checkable as any physical claim is" is not the one in force.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Give a single definition; if "enacted" is meant as a causal requirement and "physically admitted" as a realization requirement, put both in Part IV and say which clause of (R) they enter.

**D9. Contract provenance \(\rho_p\) has no witness, so "found a question" has no defined satisfaction condition.**
*Claim attacked:* Part III: "\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\), with the witness required by Part IV"; and "A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) for the contract in question, with the witness."
*Why it fails:* Part IV defines provenance only for *transports*. A contract is not a transport, so no witness for \(\rho_p\) exists in Part IV. Derivation 5 shows a contract "is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O)", but being an organization is not being *represented*; (R) and \(\operatorname{Deploy}\) require a faithful transport **to** the contract-content, whose existence Derivation 5 neither proves nor constructs.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Define \(\rho_p\) explicitly as the provenance of the transport from the system's occurrence-organization to the contract-content, and prove such a transport exists under the construction of Derivation 5.

# (E) Contracts as contents and question-finding

**E1. Every re-parameterisation becomes a found question; nothing filters relevance.**
*Claim attacked:* "\(\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}\)"; "The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a contract. When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question"; and Part 0: "It does not supply … a merit function."
*Why it fails:* \(\operatorname{Attempt}\) requires only that \(c\) be used to address \(p\); \(\operatorname{New}\) requires only non-equivalence to the prior repertoire; \(\operatorname{Build}\) excludes only content-*preserving* transfers. So any content-*changing* tweak of the edit set or of \(\mathcal Q\) — "also consider painting the pole blue" — satisfies (G) as fully as the discovery of general relativity. The machinery that could filter this (\(O_p\), \(\operatorname{Bearing}\), \(\operatorname{Repair}\)) is never connected to (G). The document's own refusal of a merit function makes this unrepairable internally.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Require the new contract to alter the answer profile of a *live* obligation in \(O_p\), i.e. require \(\exists o\in O_p\) such that the answer to \(o\) under the old contract differs from that under the new one; or require \(\operatorname{Attempt}\) to target a question whose obligations are live for the system at \(e\).

**E2. \(\equiv_\ell\) in (N) is undefined, so the triviality/impossibility dilemma is unresolved.**
*Claim attacked:* "\(\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c\)."
*Why it fails:* \(\equiv_\ell\) is never defined (Derivation 8 uses "structure-preserving bijections", Derivation 2 uses indistinguishability-on-\(C\)). If \(\equiv_\ell\) is at the grain of Derivation 2, any two contracts differing in edit set are distinguishable, so every tweak is new (E1). If it is coarser — e.g. same closure under composition — then many genuine re-scopings collapse and \(\operatorname{New}\) fails for them. Either the account trivializes or it silently excludes real cases, and the text does not say which.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Define \(\equiv_\ell\) for contract-contents explicitly (e.g. same compositional closure and same \(\mathcal Q\)-fibre) and state which grain question-finding is indexed to.

**E3. Direct contradiction: \(C\) is both a declared index and a buildable content.**
*Claim attacked:* Part XIV: "Grain \(\ell\), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\), and the contract \(C\) are declared indices. Every claim is relative to them; none is a predicate that could be true or false." Versus Part X/Derivation 5: "The content \(c\) may be … a contract. When it is a contract, the originative act is the finding of a question," and "A contract \(C\) is an organization … and can be the content \(c\) in (G)."
*Why it fails:* \(\operatorname{Build}\) requires "an actual subhistory owned by \(s\)" preparing the content; a *declared index* is a parameter of the modeller, not a constituent of the modeled system's physical state. If \(C\) is declared, the system cannot build it; if the system builds it, it is not a declared index. Both claims cannot hold of the same \(C\).
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Distinguish the modeller's assessment-index \(C\) (frozen for an assessment, Derivation 7) from the system's *contract-content* (a representation of which changes are relevant, physically realized and buildable), and rewrite (R), (G), and Part XIV to refer to the right one in each place.

**E4. Regress of meta-contracts: question-finding creativity rests on a declared index.**
*Claim attacked:* "A **content** is an organization together with its contract-relative commitments"; Part 0: "Declaration is permitted only as a modelling convenience, and a claim about creativity cannot rest on it."
*Why it fails:* For a contract-content \(c\) to be represented via (R), \(c\)'s own contract must be fixed — the set of admitted edits-on-edits ("add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)"). Which subset is in force is not specified anywhere; it is declared. So the assessment of whether an episode found a question is always relative to a declared meta-contract, which is exactly what Part 0 forbids a creativity claim to rest on. Terminating the regress at a base declared contract concedes the point; allowing the meta-contract to be constructed too starts an infinite regress that destroys the well-foundedness Part XIV claims.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Either concede that all question-finding claims are indexed to a declared meta-contract (and retract the Part 0 sentence), or give an explicit non-well-founded/coinductive reading of the contract stack with a consistency proof.

**E5. Derivation 5's proof is a sketch: the interpretation \(L\) for the contract-organization is unspecified, and closure is ambiguous.**
*Claim attacked:* "Give it ports (the edits and their boundaries), components (the closure conditions), and admitted edits (add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)). It is then a \(D\) in the sense of (O)."
*Why it fails:* (O) requires, for each component \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), a supplied relation \(L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\). None is given for the contract-organization; "the closure conditions" is not an interpretation. Moreover, \(A\) in (O) must be "closed under a partial associative composition with identity," but \(C\subseteq A\times B\) is explicitly *not* required to be closed (Part III calls it "a declared subset"). If the organization's solutions are the closure of \(C\), the organization represents \(\overline{C}\), not \(C\), and contracts differing within a closure are the same content (which silently changes what \(\operatorname{New}\) can ever hold for); if the solutions are \(C\) itself, the organization's own edit structure is not closed and (O) is not satisfied.
*Severity:* Serious (a load-bearing derivation is unproved).
*Fix:* Write out \(V\), \(X_v\), \(J\), \(V_j\), and \(L_j(a,b)\) for the contract-organization, state whether closure is imposed, and prove the resulting object satisfies (O).

**E6. A question is not its contract: finding new targets, baselines, and obligations is not representable.**
*Claim attacked:* "A question is \(p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p)\)"; Derivation 5's "A contract is a subset of \(A\times B\) together with \(\mathcal Q\)."
*Why it fails:* Derivation 5's construction has ports = "the edits and their boundaries", components = closure conditions, edits = add/remove a change and alter \(\mathcal Q\). The target \(D\), the baseline \(b_0\), and the obligations \(O_p\) are not in the organization. So an episode that finds a question by *changing what it is about* (\(D\)), or *what it is for* (\(O_p\)), or by *rejecting the baseline* — central cases of real question-finding — is not representable. Question-finding is reduced to re-scoping a fixed \((D,A,B)\). Relatedly, the pre-articulate phase — recognizing that a situation poses a question at all — appears only as the undefined "recognized difficulty" in the episode definition, with no connection to the contract-construction machinery.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Either extend Derivation 5 to construct whole question-tuples (with \(D\) and \(O_p\) as buildable contents), or explicitly restrict the claim to re-scoping and withdraw the identification of question-finding with contract-construction.

**E7. Concept creation — new dimensions of variation — is outside the reach of question-finding as defined.**
*Claim attacked:* "The physical theory fixes which changes are possible at all. A contract is a declared subset of those"; Derivation 5's admitted edits "add or remove a change; alter \(\mathcal Q\)."
*Why it fails:* Contract edits are drawn from \(A\), fixed by the target organization \(D\) and the physical module. A question that requires a *new kind* of change — a new port, a new dimension of variation, a concept not yet in the vocabulary — cannot be produced by editing \(C\). The hardest case of question-finding (posing what no one had the concepts to pose) is therefore unrepresentable *as question-finding*. The framework could construct a new organization \(D\) as a content, but Derivation 5 never connects that to finding a question, so the resource is there and unused.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Treat question-finding as construction of the full question-tuple including \(D\), and show how a newly constructed organization's admitted edits become available as contract members.

**E8. Creative reframing and goalpost-moving differ only by bookkeeping and timing.**
*Claim attacked:* Derivation 7's consequence: "Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change, and it is a failure of the record, not a licensed operation of the semantics"; and Part XI: "A later narrowing of that contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK)."
*Why it fails:* On the semantics' own terms, a recorded index-change with a construction witness is question-finding; an unrecorded one is goalpost-moving. The only further discriminator is temporal order (later narrowing to rescue adequacy). Nothing asks whether the new contract *bears on* any obligation, whether the old account's failures are preserved as failures, or whether the re-scoping was motivated. So an arbitrary forward-looking narrowing — "from now on I exclude the edits that break my account" — counts as creative question-finding provided it is recorded before assessment.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Add a no-rescue condition stronger than temporal order: the new contract must preserve the old account's failures as failures, or the re-scoping must be shown to alter the answer profile of a protected obligation.

**E9. \(\operatorname{Build}\) does not type-check for contract-contents, and the (EK) route for question-finding is unproven.**
*Claim attacked:* "\(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an actual subhistory owned by \(s\) and delimited at \(e\) prepares a represented organization for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a nontrivial binding construction relevant to that use, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers."
*Why it fails:* For \(c\) a contract, "explanatory use of \(c\)" is undefined: a contract is the *scope* of an explanation, not something explained, and the document never says what a "nontrivial binding construction" on a contract would be — there are no components to bind beyond closure conditions. So either any decision to include an edit counts as a binding (trivializing, compounding E1), or no contract-edit can satisfy \(\operatorname{Build}\) (making question-finding unrepresentable, contradicting Derivation 5's "Deploy, Build, and New apply"). Separately, (EK) requires \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\): a transport from \(D_{p_c}\) to the contract-as-organization satisfying (F1), (F2), (A). Derivation 5 does not exhibit such a transport or such a \(p_c\), so the knowledge-creation route for question-finding is not established.
*Severity:* Serious.
*Fix:* Specify the binding structure for contract-contents (e.g. binding an edit to a role in \(\mathcal Q\)'s fibre computation) and state a non-triviality criterion for it; then construct the transport and the meta-question \(p_c\) needed for \(\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\).

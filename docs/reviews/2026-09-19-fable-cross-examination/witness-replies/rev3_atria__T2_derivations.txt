## Part XVI findings

**Finding 1 — Part V cites Derivation 1 under weaker hypotheses than it was proved.**
Attacked claim (Part V): *"By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of E has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract."*
Derivation 1's claim is: *"Let t satisfy (F1) on C, and let (τ,σ) be injective on C."* The injectivity conjunct is a genuine hypothesis, not a consequence of (F1) or of the transport definition (Part IV imposes no injectivity on τ or σ). Counterexample: let C = {(1,b₀),(a₁,b₀),(a₂,b₀)} with a₁≠a₂ and τ(a₁)=τ(a₂). Then (F1) forces the anchor's relations to agree at (a₁,b₀) and (a₂,b₀), so sig_C(λ(k)) has three triples while sig_{τ[C]}(k) has two; no bijection on the first two coordinates exists, and signature coincidence — hence forward kind — fails. **Serious.** Fix: add "(τ,σ) injective on C" to Part V's sentence, or weaken it to "(F1) equates the component relation with the anchor's visible relation pointwise," which is all (F1) alone gives.

**Finding 2 — Part II's forward-kind relation is defined only for a common contract, but every substantive use of it is cross-organization.**
Attacked definition (Part II): *"Two subnetworks U,U' … are of one forward kind on (ℓ,C) when there is a bijection of their visible ports, carrying value domains onto value domains, under which their signatures coincide on C∖{(1,b₀)}."* sig_C(U) is indexed by pairs of U's own organization, so for a component k of E and an anchor λ(k) of D the two signatures live on different index sets (τ[C] vs C). Part II acknowledges the cross-organization use (*"The relation is used between a component of one organization and an anchor subnetwork of another in Part V"*) but never gives the reindexed definition. Consequently the anchored grade's condition 3 (*"Each active component of E is of one forward kind, on (ℓ,C_adm) … with its anchor subnetwork"*) is not, as written, an instance of the relation it cites. **Serious.** Fix: state the relation as: port bijection β plus an edit–boundary translation (τ,σ), with β carrying domains to domains and signature coincidence after reindexing C by (τ,σ), requiring injectivity where a bijection is needed.

**Finding 3 — Derivation 1 smuggles in an unstated visible-port hypothesis.**
Attacked passage (Derivation 1 proof): *"the visible ports of the anchor being those λ translates to V_k."* Part II makes V_U^vis a *declared* subset of a subnetwork's ports; nothing in the transport data forces the declaration to coincide with the λ-correspondents of V_k. If the anchor's declared visible set is larger, the anchor's signature carries information the component's does not, and coincidence fails. The claim's hypotheses — (F1) on C, injectivity — do not include it. **Minor.** Fix: add "V^vis_{λ(k)} = the λ-correspondents of V_k" to the claim, or require λ to determine visible sets.

**Finding 4 — Derivation 1's conclusion is stronger than the forward-kind relation needs but is advertised as exactly it.**
Derivation 1 establishes coincidence on all of C; forward kind excludes only (1,b₀). This is harmless (stronger). But the claim phrases the result as signature *equality of sets*, while the text's own caveat (*"It shows that fidelity on a contract preserves signature on that contract"*) silently assumes the reindexing of Finding 2. **Cosmetic**, resolved by Finding 2's fix.

**Finding 5 — Derivation 10's central kind claim is false under Part II's definition.**
Attacked claim: *"Under (K), with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap."* By (K), coincidence is required at the *same* (a,b) ∈ C∖{(1,b₀)}, ports renamed. Take C ⊇ {(1,b₀),(swap,b₀)} with the two things at different trajectories. At the swap pair, c₁'s relation is (pos₁,vel₁) = thing 2's trajectory and c₂'s is (pos₂,vel₂) = thing 1's; under the swap-induced port bijection these coincide only if the two trajectories are equal — which is precisely not the case the scenario is about. So on the literal definition the components are *not* one kind there; and if some τ maps the swap to the identity, the comparison at the D-side pair (swap,b₀) still is not the baseline pair, so the baseline exclusion does not rescue it. **Serious.** Fix: either amend Part II to let the port bijection come with an edit conjugation (compare sig at a with sig at φ(a)φ⁻¹), or restrict the claim to swap-invariant contracts and equal-trajectory states.

**Finding 6 — Derivation 10's invocation of Derivation 1 is, by contrast, correct.**
*"by Derivation 1, with (τ,σ) injective on that contract (which it is, since the translation is a relabelling)"* — injectivity is stated and checked, and (F1)/(F2) are explicitly hypothetical. Nothing wrong here.

**Finding 7 — Derivation 2's hypotheses are redundant and its proof misattributes the work.**
Attacked claim: *"If E and E' both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on C, have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and Γ corresponds to Γ', then they are account-equivalent on C."* Proof: *"(F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles."* If λ'(β(k)) = λ(k) as subnetworks of the *same* D, then Sol_{λ(k)}(a,b) = Sol_{λ'(β(k))}(a,b) identically, so the anchored relations coincide *without* (F1); and (F2) is never used at all. Only (A) does work (both pulled-back profiles equal Ans_p). The derivation is valid but nearly vacuous, and the proof sentence cites (F1) for a step it does not perform. **Minor.** Fix: drop (F1),(F2) from the hypotheses, or state that coincidence of anchored relations is immediate from shared anchors and that (A) is the only load-bearing condition.

**Finding 8 — Derivation 3's "equivalence" hypothesis is stronger than the proof uses.**
Attacked hypothesis: *"in a run in which, as Part IV's witness records, the survivors were exactly the members faithful on H."* The proof needs only faithful-on-H ⇒ survived (to conclude the altered pair survives). The converse (survived ⇒ faithful) is unused. **Minor.** Fix: state the one direction, which is also the direction that is a genuine physical hypothesis.

**Finding 9 — Derivation 3's closure hypothesis is never argued satisfiable.**
The closure condition quantifies over *every* admissible L' off H and requires the altered pair to be a realized member of 𝒯. No model of a population satisfying it is given, and real populations are parametric rather than pointwise-closed. The document is transparent about this (Part XV frames the refutation condition correctly), so the result stands as a conditional whose antecedent may be empty. **Minor.** Fix: either exhibit a toy population meeting closure, or label the result "conditional on a hypothesis whose satisfaction is open."

**Finding 10 — Derivation 3's claim is stated at the solution-set grain while its consequence is stated at the fidelity grain.**
Attacked claim: *"…there is a pair in 𝒯 that survives on H and whose solution set at (τ(a),σ(b)) differs from E's, whenever some admissible L' changes that solution set."* The construction in fact delivers more: for any admissible L' ≠ L_j(τ(a),σ(b)), the altered pair survives on H and *fails (F1)* at (a,b), whether or not the solution set moves. The Consequence (*"selection provides no guarantee of fidelity where it did not test"*) is the (F1)-level claim, so claim and consequence are at different grains and the claim understates the proof. **Minor.** Fix: state the (F1)-level result and note that solution-set (F2)/(A) underdetermination additionally requires the solution-relevance guard.

**Finding 11 — Derivation 4 is sound and correctly demoted.** Nothing wrong; it is a remark, nothing cites it as support, and the two unpackings (no transport ⇒ no violation; H = C ⇒ no pair outside H) are immediate from Part IV's definitions.

**Finding 12 — Derivation 5's construction excludes legitimate questions by imposing composition-closure.**
Attacked component: *"if u_{(a₂a₁,b)} reads 'in' then so do its factors where the composition is defined."* Part III places no closure requirement on C: C = (C_adm∩S)∪K with S an arbitrary stated scope. A contract containing a composite edit but not a factor is a coherently posed question, yet it is not a solution of D_p. This over-constriction is not among gaps (0)–(iv). **Serious.** Fix: drop the composition-closure component, or add to Part III a requirement that contracts be composition-closed (which would be a new restriction on questions).

**Finding 13 — Derivation 5's gap list omits the K-disjointness and Q-definedness enforcement.**
The consistency component is described only as *"u_S and u_K are consistent with the 'in' ports in the form of Part III."* Whether K∩C_adm = ∅ and whether 𝒬 is defined on every pair of the selected C are conditions on the *combination* of u_Q, u_S, u_K and the "in" set; gap (i) mentions 𝒬-definedness as global but the construction supplies no component that rejects a K-pair lying inside C_adm. **Minor.** Fix: write the consistency component as an explicit schema over (u_Q, u_S, u_K, in-ports) and say which of the two coherence clauses it encodes.

**Finding 14 — Derivation 5's gap (ii) misidentifies ρ_p.**
Gap (ii): *"b₀, O_p and ρ_p are not ports of D_p as constructed."* By Part III, ρ_p is *derived* — *"the provenance of the transport by which an occurrence represents the question-organization of Derivation 5"* — not a component of the question tuple's content. Adding a ρ_p port would make provenance declared data rather than a derived fact, contradicting Part III. b₀ and O_p are genuine omissions; ρ_p is a category error. **Minor.** Fix: list only b₀ and O_p; note ρ_p is fixed by the representing transport.

**Finding 15 — Derivation 5 has a further unlisted gap: the target organization D is fixed.**
The construction varies C, 𝒬, S, K only. Finding a question that re-decomposes the target at another admissible grain — which Part X claims is representable (*"A dimension the module does license at some grain … is representable as the construction of a new organization attribution"*) — is outside this construction, and the gap list does not say so. **Minor.** Fix: add "the construction covers question-finding at fixed D and fixed grain; a new grain is a separate organization attribution."

**Finding 17 — Derivation 6's completeness is unargued and its dependence order is incomplete.**
Attacked proof: *"Each definition names its inputs at first use"* and *"Following the order of Part XIV from each defined predicate reaches the base."* Part XIV's dependence order omits several defined predicates entirely — ≡_ℓ, reason use (Part IX), RetReal/Exec-adjacent notions, Can, CanAdv, Enable, Lic/Scope/Live, Pres, Result, ProducesVia, and the grounds/connection of Bearing — so "following the order" cannot be executed for them. And the case check is a sample, not a stratification: no ranking function or induction on definition depth is given, so there is no argument that the three/four spots are the only possible cycles. **Serious.** Fix: complete the dependence order to cover every defined predicate and prove acyclicity by an explicit measure (e.g., height in the construction order), not by enumerating suspicious spots.

**Finding 18 — Derivation 6's case (iv) rests on an ambiguity in Part X that, read the other way, creates the very cycle it denies.**
Attacked proof step: *"Build's 'already carried' and 'required' clauses are stated by instantiation, not through the repertoire of Deploy, so Build does not pass through (R) either."* Part X's text is: *"nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ."* ≡_ℓ is a relation on *contents*, and what a carrier carries is fixed by (R); so on the natural reading "obtainable from the incoming carriers by ≡_ℓ" requires the contents the carriers represent, i.e., (R). Then (R) → Con → Build → (R) is a cycle. Derivation 6 asserts the Inst reading without textual support. **Serious.** Fix: amend Part X to "obtainable from the organizations the incoming carriers instantiate," or state explicitly that "carriers" there means instantiated organizations, not represented contents.

**Finding 19 — Derivation 6's case (ii) misdescribes ownership's definition.**
Attacked step: *"ownership is defined from retained realization, and Can does not mention ownership."* Part X defines ownership from RetReal(π,T,C;χ) (i.e., (CT1)) and Exec, not from Can; Part XII says *"Ownership (Part X) is defined from this predicate [Can], not used in it."* The two are interdefinable only because Can is itself defined via RetReal, so the letter of Part XII's attribution is wrong. No cycle results either way. **Minor.** Fix: harmonize — ownership is defined from (CT1)/Exec; Can neither uses nor is used by it.

**Finding 20 — Derivation 7 states its claim unconditionally and proves it conditionally.**
Attacked claim: *"An assessment event with contract C and an episode in which C is replaced by C' with a construction witness are both representable without contradiction."* Proof: *"The episode is a history in which an organization carrying a question with contract C' is built — conditionally on the construction Derivation 5 does not yet supply."* So the consistency result is established for the assessment side and only *assumed* for the episode side. **Minor.** Fix: mark the claim "conditional on Derivation 5," or prove the episode side from Part III's index rules alone without needing the question-organization construction.

**Finding 21 — Derivation 8's stated hypotheses are insufficient for four of its six conclusions.**
Attacked hypothesis list: *"such that φ preserves the physical module's admission (A_Θ, Inst, ≺), carries Sol to Sol …"* and conclusion *"Transporting all data along φ preserves (E), anchoring, (R), (G), (P), (EK)."* (G) requires Build → ownership → RetReal → Exec; (R) requires Con → Build → ownership; Deploy (used by (G),(P),(EK)) requires Can → retained realization → Exec, resources and accuracy grades; and (R),(G),(P),(EK) are indexed by (β,ℓ,Ω) and reference use tasks U and the query 𝒬, none of which is in the hypothesis list. So (E) and anchoring are supported; (R), (G), (P), (EK) are not. **Serious.** Fix: require φ to preserve the whole module Θ (Exec, resources, accuracy grades, intervention repertoire) plus the declared indices, queries and use tasks; or restrict the claim to (E) and anchoring.

**Finding 22 — Derivation 8's "solution preservation is a hypothesis, not a consequence" is inconsistent with its own exclusion argument.**
Attacked parenthetical: *"carries Sol to Sol (solution preservation, which for a bijection of the organization data is a hypothesis, not a consequence)."* But the proof excludes the pole→shadow recoding on the ground that *"it does not preserve D's structure"* — i.e., φ is required to carry component relations pointwise and footprints accordingly, and under that requirement Sol preservation follows immediately from (O). So either solution preservation is automatic (and the parenthetical is a misdiagnosis), or φ is permitted to be non-structure-preserving (and the pole→shadow exclusion is unsupported). The text needs both readings at once. **Minor.** Fix: drop the parenthetical and state that pointwise relation-and-footprint preservation entails Sol preservation via (O); the pole→shadow map is then excluded because it is not such a map.

**Finding 23 — Derivation 9 is valid but trivial, and its premise is never exhibited.**
The proof (*"Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs"*) is correct. However the premise — same input–output projection yet differing on an account claim — is not instantiated; and by Part V, two candidates differing on no admitted change are not distinguished at all, so the only real instances must differ via decomposition, Γ, or λ with identical Sol. The "parallel and priority wiring" illustration does not say which of these it is. **Minor.** Fix: give the instance explicitly (e.g., redundant vs. non-redundant wiring under an edit that changes a component relation without changing Sol), or label the derivation a remark.

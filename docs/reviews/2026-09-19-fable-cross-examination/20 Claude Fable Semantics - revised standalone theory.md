# Claude Fable Semantics

## A structural class of explanatory creativity, with selected and constructed correspondence

---

# Part 0 — Read this first

## What this document claims

An explanation is a **question-relevant organization of dependencies**, held to a target by a **transport** that is checked by **what changes when things are changed**. What a piece of an explanation *is* — a cause, a rule, a measurement, a thing — is not asked of a label. At a grain and a contract of admitted changes, a *forward kind* is how a component responds to the changes that contract admits; two components no admitted change separates are one forward kind there, and the claim that they "really" differ is either a claim about a finer contract, which is a new question, or a claim about their histories, which this semantics also represents, as a second kind of kind.

Adequacy comes in two grades. An **account at a contract** is a candidate whose components and assembled organization are faithful to their anchors under every change the contract admits, that answers the question asked, that does not restate the answer, and whose contract is stated. An **anchored account** is an account at a contract whose anchors are a proper decomposition of the target at the resolution the question needs, and whose components are of one forward kind with their anchors under every change the physical module admits, not only the ones the contract selected. The first grade is what can be assessed within a question. The second is what "the explanation is about the actual mechanism" means, and it is a condition on the same fidelity relation on a larger contract, not a new primitive.

Correspondence between a representation and what it represents is not a primitive of this semantics. It is a **relation with a provenance**. A correspondence can be *selected* — produced by variation and survival on a history of encountered changes, with at least one candidate eliminated — or *constructed* — produced by an episode of conjecture and criticism — or merely *declared* by whoever writes the model down. The three are distinguished by their histories, not their outputs. Creativity lives in construction. Selection produces the raw material construction works on. Declaration is permitted as a modelling convenience, and a claim about representation or creativity cannot rest on it.

Questions have provenance too. A question — its target, its scope of admitted changes, what it asks — can be found as well as answered, and the semantics represents both, with a condition on what makes a found question worth finding. What it does not represent is the invention of a new dimension of variation, and it says so.

The **constitutive conjecture** is this: explanatory creativity is fully characterized by (i) organizations and the changes they admit, (ii) transports between organizations checked by change-fidelity, (iii) the provenance of those transports and of the questions they serve, and (iv) their physical realization. A genuine explanatory achievement that these four cannot represent counts against the conjecture. A candidate that satisfies the anchored grade while plainly explaining nothing counts against it too.

## What this document does not claim

It does not derive reference from uninterpreted matter. It derives reference from matter **plus a selection or construction history**, given a physical module that says which organizations a lump of matter can instantiate at a grain. That module is a primitive here, and what it must supply is stated exactly: it is more than "physics" in the ordinary sense, because it must say how a lump decomposes.

It does not say prediction is explanation. Fidelity in this semantics is over **component structure under change**, not over outputs. Two systems with identical outputs and different internal routes are different organizations here, and the semantics says so.

It does not supply an objective aesthetics, a probability of truth, a ranking of thinkers, or — beyond one condition under (P) — a merit function for questions. It supplies places where such things would go if anyone had them, and marks those places as empty.

It does not prove that any human, machine, institution, or lineage belongs to the classes defined. It defines the classes. Nothing in it has been tested by an experiment; the experiments that have been run test proxies, and the proxies are named as proxies where they are cited.

It does not claim that the two primitive packages and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV are few. It claims that they are all listed.

## Grievances, anticipated

**1. "Without declared kinds you cannot tell a cause from a correlation."**
A cause and a correlation are two components with different signatures under (K). Intervening on the input port of a causal assignment replaces the component that assigns that port and leaves the causal component's own relation in force; the output moves through it. Intervening on the same port breaks a correlational component, because a correlation is a constraint on how two ports happen to co-vary, not a rule assigning one from the other. The difference is a difference in edit-response, and the semantics checks exactly that. A declared kind-label adds nothing where a separating change exists, because fidelity already finds it. Where no admitted change separates two components, they are one *forward* kind at that grain and contract; if their histories differ, that is a *historical* kind, and the semantics represents it as a predicate on history, not as a label.

**2. "So this is operationalism: a thing is what you can do to it."**
A forward kind is what a grain's and a contract's admitted changes can distinguish; it is indexed to the physical module's decomposition, the grain, and the contract, and every kind-claim carries that index. "One kind at that index" is a fact about the pairing of an organization with a contract, not a fact about the world, and the semantics never presents it as more. Where a question fixes the contract and asks which of two mechanisms produced an effect, and the contract does not separate them, the semantics says the question needs either a finer contract or a history, and if neither is available, that the distinction is not represented. That is scoping with the gap stated, not operationalism.

**3. "If correspondences are selected, you have made truth a matter of survival."**
No. Selection produces a transport. Whether that transport is faithful on changes it was never selected against is a fact about the transport and the world, independent of whether it survived. There is a result below (Derivation 3) that selection provides no guarantee of fidelity off the history it was selected on, under a stated hypothesis about the population. Survival is how the transport got there; fidelity is what it is.

**4. "Then everything is relative to a contract of admitted changes, and there is no objectivity."**
Two claims, two indices. "Faithful on C" is a fact within C. "C is not protective" is a fact about C against the physically admitted changes: a contract is protective for a candidate when some physically admitted change it excludes would alter the target's answer and not the candidate's, or the reverse. An objectivity claim is the conjunction, and the second conjunct is a condition in this semantics (Part V), not a hope about future criticism. A contract that quietly excludes such a change is caught by the requirement that exclusions be stated, and then by the adequacy condition, whether or not anyone supplies the change.

**5. "This is teleosemantics / structural realism / functionalism with new words."**
It shares commitments with each and differs from each. From teleosemantics it takes the idea that a correspondence has a history; it differs by distinguishing selected from constructed histories and locating creativity only in the latter. From structural realism it takes structure-preservation; it differs by making preservation change-driven and component-level, and by refusing a global isomorphism requirement. From functionalism it takes substrate-independence; it differs by imposing physical realization obligations at every attribution. Whether the combination is new is a question about the literature, not about whether it is right.

**6. "You have replaced explanation with evolution. That is deflationary."**
Selection appears at the bottom, between a sensory field and a primitive layer of things-with-persistence, to produce what explanation operates on. Construction is a separate provenance with a separate witness, and every creative attribution requires it. Nothing about construction is reduced to selection; Part IV states what would collapse the two and what prevents it.

**7. "Mathematics has no interventions. This cannot handle mathematical explanation."**
An admitted change need not be a physical intervention. Removing an axiom, dropping a constraint, or changing a dimension are edits on an organization, and fidelity under them is well-defined. But "physically admitted" is not defined for a mathematical organization, so a mathematical question must *declare* its admission relation — which edits its presentation admits — and every claim about it is relative to that declaration, as a physical claim is relative to the physical module. Part VII works an example under a declared admission relation and says so.

**8. "Where is aesthetics?"**
In Part XI, as a declared normative relation. The semantics gives artistic effect, artistic purpose, and aesthetic reasons three distinct mathematical carriers and refuses to define any as another. It does not pretend to know which aesthetic reasons are true.

**9. "'Selected' is just as much a stipulation as 'is a cause'. Who says the selection happened?"**
Selection is a physical history: a population with more than one member, a variation operator that was applied, a survival condition that eliminated at least one candidate, and a sequence of events causally responsible for the survivor's presence. Whether it occurred is a claim about the physical module, fallible but checkable in principle. Without the elimination clause every transport would count as selected; with it, "selected" is a hypothesis, not a stipulation.

**10. "Freezing the question for assessment while letting questions change across episodes is having it both ways."**
It is having it both ways deliberately, and the two ways are indexed so they cannot be confused. An assessment is an event with a frozen contract; any change to the contract ends that assessment and begins another. An episode is a history in which contracts can change, and every change carries a provenance record. Derivation 7 shows the two are consistent.

**11. "Kinds obviously exist. A rule is not a cause."**
A rule and a cause differ in how they respond to changes: a rule's application changes when the rule is edited and not when the world is intervened on; a cause's assignment changes under intervention. That is a difference in edit-signature, and the semantics represents it exactly. What the semantics denies is that the *forward* difference is available before the admitted changes are fixed, or independently of them.

**12. "You individuate transports by their history but refuse to individuate components by theirs. That is arbitrary."**
It is an asymmetry, and it is stated rather than hidden. A transport is an occurrence in a physical history: it came to hold by selection, construction, or declaration, and its provenance is a fact about events. A component is a slot in an organization: what it does under changes is its forward kind. A component realized in matter also has a history, and the semantics represents that as a historical kind — a predicate on the history that produced the occurrence — distinct from its forward kind. What the semantics denies is that a component's history makes any difference to fidelity on a contract. Provenance matters to *representation* because representation is a claim about how a carrier came to track a content; it does not matter to *fidelity* because fidelity is a claim about what the carrier does under change.

## Where to attack this

The load-bearing claims, in order of how much falls if they fail:

**(A) Sufficiency.** Produce a candidate that satisfies the anchored grade of Account (Part V) on a physically admitted contract, with a non-declared transport, and that plainly explains nothing. This would show change-fidelity plus proper anchoring is not enough. The classic attempts — lookup tables, reversed calculations, conclusion-as-premise, and the one-component candidate whose single relation is the target's own solution set — each fail a named condition; a new one must fail none.

**(B) Necessity.** Produce a genuine explanation whose organization cannot be captured by *any* transport satisfying component fidelity under *any* admitted contract. Four cases are open and are listed in Part XV: elimination of a physically impossible rival; explanation by symmetry; explanation of an effect as a selection artefact; and mathematical explanation under an admission relation that has to be declared. Part VII gives the semantics' treatment of the first and says where it stops.

**(C) Reinstatement of kinds.** Produce a case in which a declared kind-label does discriminating work that no admitted change under the physically admitted contract and no history can do. Derivation 1 does not rule this out; it shows only that fidelity preserves signatures. That kind-labels are eliminable in favour of signatures and histories is the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture**, and it is a conjecture.

**(D) Genesis.** Show either that a selection history can guarantee fidelity off the history under the population hypothesis of Derivation 3, or that construction reduces to selection under the discrimination requirement of Part IV, or that the layers described in Part IV are not what explanation operates on.

**(E) Question-finding.** Show that treating a question as a content — something that can be constructed, be new, and be the originative contribution of an episode, subject to the merit condition of Part X — either trivializes creativity or fails to capture some genuine case of finding the right question. One such case is already conceded: the invention of a new dimension of variation, a port the target did not have, is not representable as question-finding here.

**(F) The anchored grade.** Show that the properness and resolution conditions on anchors (Part V) either exclude a genuine mechanism or admit a decoy that shares a world-kind with the mechanism on every physically admitted change.

Anything else is a detail.

# Part I — Commitments

**Explanatory realism.** Whether a transport is faithful on a contract is independent of whether anyone accepts it. Systems can be wrong about their transports, their observations, their criticisms, and their own capacities. Every success predicate in this document is external: it can hold of a system that does not know it holds, and fail of one that believes it does.

**Fallibility without falsehood-as-work.** A theory may contain an accurate scoped dependence together with errors elsewhere. What cannot count as explanation is an error in the very dependence alleged to do the work.

**Conjecture, criticism, action.** An idea may be entertained without justification. A criticism has a target and is itself conjectural. A thinker may act on an appraisal without certifying it.

**Recursive scrutiny with operative return.** Any aspect of a system's practice — a question, a contract, a transport, a method, an attention policy — can become a target, and the result must be able to change how the system proceeds.

**Substrate independence with physical obligations.** Any carrier may bear an organization. Every attribution of an organization to a physical system must be permitted by the adopted physical module, which must say how the carrier decomposes at the grain claimed.

**Two provenances, not one.** Selection and construction both produce correspondences. They are distinguished by their histories. Neither is reduced to the other.

**Two grades of adequacy.** Account at a contract is what a question can assess. Anchoring is fidelity on the full admitted contract with a proper decomposition. Both are stated in the same vocabulary; neither is a label.

# Part II — Organizations and their changes

## Organizations

An organization is

\[
D=(V,(X_v)_{v\in V},J,B,A,L).
\]

\(V\) is a set of ports, each with a nonempty value domain \(X_v\). A valuation is an element of \(X_D=\prod_v X_v\). \(J\) indexes components; each component \(j\) has a footprint \(V_j\subseteq V\). \(B\) is a set of boundary conditions. \(A\) is a set of admitted edits, closed under a partial associative composition with identity \(1\). For each \(j\), edit \(a\), and boundary \(b\), the interpretation supplies

\[
L_j(a,b)\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v .
\]

The compatible valuations are

\[
\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)=\{z\in X_D:\forall j\in J,\ z|_{V_j}\in L_j(a,b)\}. \tag{O}
\]

(O) is a schema over the supplied data \((V,X,J,B,A,L)\). Those data are a **declared parameter** of every use of this semantics: when the organization is attributed to a physical occurrence, the physical module must license the attribution (Part IV, Part XII); when it is a mathematical or modelled organization, the data are stipulated, and every claim is relative to the stipulation.

A deleted component imposes the full relation on its ports. An edit that sets a port replaces the component assigning that port; it does not add an equation beside an incompatible one. A changed rule is a changed component. A **subnetwork** \(U\) of \(D\) is a subset of components with the ports they touch; its solution set \(\operatorname{Sol}_U(a,b)\) is (O) restricted to those components, and its **visible ports** are a declared subset \(V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}\) of its ports, the rest being hidden.

Values of ports may be paths, functions, fields, proofs, or histories. Cyclic constraints are admitted. Several solutions remain several.

## Roles are derived

No role assignment is supplied. A port \(v\) is an **input** under \(A\) when \(A\) contains an edit that sets \(v\) directly. A port is an **output** of component \(j\) when its value is determined by \(L_j\) given the other ports of \(V_j\) across \(B\). A port is an **observation** when \(A\) contains an **observation edit**: an edit that alters the relation reporting the port without altering what it reports, that is, replaces the component whose footprint is \(\{v\}\cup V'\) for a reporting set \(V'\) while leaving every component not touching \(v\) unchanged. The direction of an organization is a consequence of which edits it admits, not a stipulation about which way an equation is read.

## Kinds are edit-signatures, at a grain and a contract

Fix an organization \(D\), attributed at grain \(\ell\), and a contract \(C\subseteq A\times B\) (Part III). The **signature** of component \(j\) on \(C\) is

\[
\operatorname{sig}_C(j)=\{(a,b,L_j(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}. \tag{K}
\]

The **signature of a subnetwork** \(U\) on \(C\) is \(\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_U^{\mathrm{vis}}}\operatorname{Sol}_U(a,b)):(a,b)\in C\}\); for a single component with all ports visible it is (K).

Two components \(j,j'\) are **of one forward kind on \((\ell,C)\)** when there is a bijection of their footprints under which their signatures coincide on \(C\setminus\{(1,b):b\in B\}\). The baseline pairs are excluded on purpose: the relation a component imposes at the baseline is its **state**, and two things at different positions are one kind in different states. A change separates two components when their signatures differ at it; the identity edit separates nothing. A forward kind is an equivalence class under this relation, and it is relative to the grain (which fixes \(J\)) and the contract (which fixes the changes). A coarser contract identifies more components; a coarser grain has fewer to identify.

A **historical kind** is a predicate on the history that produced an occurrence of a component — selected, constructed, descended from, copied from — with the witness that Part IV requires for provenance. Two components of one forward kind on every admitted change may be of different historical kinds. Both are kinds. The analysis-of-kinds conjecture (Part XV) is that every discriminating use of a kind-label is one or the other.

What ordinary language calls a cause, a measurement, a rule, or a constitutive status are families of forward signatures:

- a **causal assignment** has a signature that changes under intervention on its output port and under replacement of the component, and is invariant under observation edits;
- a **measurement** has a signature invariant under interventions on the measured port and variable under edits to the measuring relation;
- a **rule application** has a signature invariant under interventions on the world and variable under edits to the rule.

These are descriptions of patterns in (K), not additional data. The semantics never asks whether a component "is" a cause. It asks what its signature is, and, where the question is about its origin, what its history is.

## Admissible grains

A grain \(\ell\) is a declared index, but not a free one. A grain is **admissible** for an occurrence \(o\) when the physical module licenses an organization at that grain (Part IV, Inst) and every component of that organization has a signature that some physically admitted edit realizes: a coarsening that creates a component no admitted change can touch is not a grain at which anything is claimed. Claims can be rescued by moving to a grain only if the new grain is admissible, and the move is a new claim at a new index.

# Part III — Questions

## Contracts

A question is

\[
p=(D,\ C,\ b_0,\ \mathcal Q,\ O_p,\ \rho_p).
\]

\(D\) is the target. The **contract** \(C\) is the set of admitted edit–boundary pairs the claim ranges over; it contains the baseline \((1,b_0)\), and it has the form

\[
C=(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K .
\]

\(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\subseteq A\times B\) is the set of pairs the module in force admits: for a physical organization, the physically admitted edits \(A_\Theta\) (Part XII); for a mathematical or modelled organization, a **declared admission relation** stated with the question. \(S\) is a **stated scope**: the declared subset of admitted pairs the claim ranges over, with the reason for each exclusion recorded. \(K\) is a declared set of **counterfactual edits** — pairs the module does not admit — each with a stated meaning; \(K\) is how a question can ask about a structure that is not a possibility at all (Part VII). Fidelity conditions range over all of \(C\); the objectivity condition of Part V ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\); a pair in \(K\) enters non-vacuity only through its stated meaning.

\(\mathcal Q\) is a specified set-theoretic operation on \(D\), its solutions, and its component structure, with codomain \(Y_p\). The answer profile is

\[
\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b)=\mathcal Q(D,a,b). \tag{Q}
\]

(Q) is a schema over the supplied \(D\) and \(\mathcal Q\). \(O_p\) is the set of obligations being addressed or protected. \(\rho_p\) is the **provenance of the question** (below).

## The respect is the query

What a question asks — production, identification, obstruction, rule-status, purpose-achievement — is fixed by the type of \(\mathcal Q\) and the shape of \(C\), not by a label. A production question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that reads an output port and a \(C\) containing interventions on upstream ports. An identification question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that computes a fibre and a \(C\) containing edits to the observed value. An obstruction question has a \(\mathcal Q\) that returns a reachability truth value. Two questions with the same \(D\) and different \((C,\mathcal Q)\) are different questions, and an answer to one is not an answer to the other.

## Questions have provenance

\(\rho_p\in\{\text{declared},\ \text{selected},\ \text{constructed}\}\) attaches to the whole tuple \(p\), not to its contract alone, with the witness required by Part IV. A **declared** question is stipulated by the modeller. A **selected** question is the surviving member of a population of questions under a variation-and-survival history satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV. A **constructed** question is the result of an episode with a construction witness whose result is the question-organization of Derivation 5. Any assessment may use any of the three. A claim that an episode *found* a question requires \(\rho_p=\text{constructed}\) with the witness, and, for the finding to count as creative, the merit condition of Part X: a re-parameterisation of an old question that repairs no obligation is a constructed question and not a found one.

## A question can be wrong

A question may fail to pick out its alleged target, assume an incompatible baseline, or combine incompatible requirements. It is **coherently posed** when \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\), \(\mathcal Q\) is defined on every pair in \(C\), and \(S\) and \(K\) are disjoint from each other's complements as stated. Its formulation is still an event. Exposing the defect is another question with its own contract. An assessment is an event at a fixed \((C,\mathcal Q)\); any change to either ends that assessment and begins another, and the record must show the boundary.

# Part IV — Layers, transports, and provenance

## Occurrences and contents

An **occurrence** is a physically located carrier. A **content** is an organization together with its contract-relative commitments. Occurrences are not identified by carrying the same words; contents are not identified by having the same outputs.

## What an occurrence instantiates

The physical module supplies a relation

\[
\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D),
\]

"occurrence \(o\) instantiates organization \(D\) at grain \(\ell\)." It is a relation, not a function: one occurrence may instantiate several organizations at one grain (a mechanical one, a thermodynamic one, a computational one), and nothing here assumes uniqueness. Every definition that needs "the organization \(o\) instantiates" quantifies over \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,\cdot)\). What the module must be able to return is a full organization — ports, domains, components with footprints, boundaries, the admitted edits \(A_\Theta\) restricted to the occurrence, and every component relation — and that is a decomposition, not a reading of a meter. The semantics does not pretend otherwise: how a lump of matter decomposes is the module's burden, and Part XII says what the module is.

## Three layers

A modelled thinker has up to three organizations in play.

The **sensory field** \(F\) is an organization whose ports are the readings of the system's sensors and whose components are whatever regularities hold among readings. It has no things in it.

The **primitive layer** \(P\) is an organization whose ports are persistent things with boundaries and identity, whose components are their continuity relations, and whose admitted edits include displacement, occlusion, and re-identification. It is the layer at which there are *objects* rather than a field of sensation. \(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport that is typically selected: the population is the variants of the system's tracking machinery, the history is the sensory changes encountered, and the survivors are the trackers whose predictions of the field held.

The **simulation layer** \(S\) is an organization over \(P\) whose components are dependencies among things and whose queries are predictions: what a port of \(P\) will take under an admitted edit. \(S\) is where expectation lives, and it is where construction typically operates.

None of the three is presupposed to exist in any particular physical system. The semantics describes what it is for them to exist and to be connected. Content identity at the primitive layer — that two occurrences carry the same thing — is a fidelity fact between their organizations under \(P\)'s admitted edits; it does not use the representation predicate below, which is defined in terms of it.

## Transports

A transport from an organization \(D\) to an organization \(E\) is

\[
t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E
\]

where \(\pi:X_D\to X_E\) on the stated scope, \(\tau:A_D\to A_E\) translates edits, \(\sigma\) translates boundaries, and \(\lambda\) assigns each component of \(E\) a subnetwork of \(D\) with a port translation. **Orientation.** A transport from \(D\) to \(E\) is the relation by which \(E\) is *held to* \(D\): \(D\) is what \(E\) answers to. \(\pi,\tau,\sigma\) run forward from \(D\); \(\lambda\) runs back, anchoring \(E\)'s parts in \(D\). This one orientation is used everywhere: an explanation \(E\) is held to its target \(D\) by \(t:D\to E\); a carrier's organization \(D\), with \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\), is held to the content \(c\) it represents by \(t:c\to D\). A transport is **faithful on \(C\)** when it satisfies (F1) and (F2) of Part V on \(C\).

## Three provenances

A transport \(t\) between organizations of a physical system has a provenance determined by its history in the physical module, with the following precedence: it is *constructed* if a construction witness exists; otherwise *selected* if a selection witness exists; otherwise *declared*. Maintenance histories after origination do not change provenance.

**Selected.** \(\operatorname{Sel}(t;\mathcal T,\mu,H,\Sigma_h)\) holds when there are: a population \(\mathcal T\) of realized transports with \(|\mathcal T|\ge 2\); a variation operator \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal T\) that was physically applied at least once in the selection history \(\Sigma_h\); a finite history \(H\subseteq C\) of edit–boundary pairs actually encountered, non-empty and containing at least one pair at which fidelity is non-trivial (some member of \(\mathcal T\) fails it); a survival condition requiring fidelity on \(H\) that **eliminated at least one member** of \(\mathcal T\); and \(t\) is a member that survived, with the environment's filtering causally responsible for \(t\)'s presence and not merely consistent with it. Non-triviality is a condition on the *events* of \(\Sigma_h\): no event in the selection process is itself a faithful transport to \(t\), to \(H\), or to the survival condition. A process is **blind** with respect to a defect when no itemised representation of the defect — a record pairing the state at which fidelity failed with what would have held — lies on the route from the defect to the variation; a scalar summary of fidelity (how many failures, how badly) is explicitly permitted and does not make the process sighted. This is the only definition of selection; Part XII says what makes it a physical claim.

**Constructed.** \(\operatorname{Con}(t;h,e)\) holds when there is an episode (Part X) whose construction witness prepares \(t\), and in which the organization \(t\) targets is instantiated by an occurrence in the episode's history before \(e\) — available as a target, in the module's sense of Inst, not in the sense of the representation predicate below.

**Declared.** \(\operatorname{Dec}(t)\): neither witness exists; the transport is entered into the model by its author. Declared provenance is a status in the model, not a physical history.

A physical system may hold selected transports between field and primitive layer and constructed transports at the simulation layer; that is the expected arrangement, not a requirement. Construction may operate on selected material. Selection may continue to operate beneath construction; the precedence rule says which provenance the resulting transport bears. What keeps the two apart: a selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both.

## Representation is derived

An occurrence \(o\) **represents** content \(c\) at grain \(\ell\) when some organization \(o\) instantiates at \(\ell\) is held to \(c\) by a transport faithful on \(c\)'s contract whose provenance is selected or constructed:

\[
\operatorname{Rep}_\ell(o,c)\iff\exists D\,\exists t\,[\operatorname{Inst}_\ell(o,D)\land\operatorname{Faithful}_{C_c}(t:c\to D)\land(\operatorname{Sel}(t)\lor\operatorname{Con}(t))]. \tag{R}
\]

The carrier–content relation is thus a fidelity fact with a history. A system can represent a false theory: the transport from the content to the carrier is faithful while the content's own transport from the world fails. A declared transport does not make an occurrence represent anything; it makes a modeller assert that it does. What (R) takes from outside is \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\).

## Expectation, surprise, violation

Let \(t\) be selected on history \(H\) with contract \(C\). For an edit–boundary pair \((a,b)\in C\) actually occurring:

- the **expectation** is \(\operatorname{Ans}_S(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\);
- a **violation** occurs when fidelity fails at \((a,b)\);
- **surprise** is a violation at \((a,b)\notin H\).

These are definitions. That a system with no transport cannot be surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract cannot be surprised, follow from them by unpacking and are not results (Derivation 4 says so). Two things do follow that are worth stating: surprise at \((a,b)\in C\) is a failure of fidelity on \(C\), so on that contract the system has, by (R), stopped representing the content, and by Part X stopped deploying it; and a change outside \(C\) altogether can neither violate nor surprise, so what an ordinary speaker calls surprise at an uncontracted change is not represented here.

Two responses to a violation are distinguished. A **selection response** extends \(H\) and lets \(\mu\) act: the transport is re-tuned within the population. A **construction response** introduces a new organization or a new transport with a construction witness. Only the second can be originative under Part X.

# Part V — Account

An explanatory candidate for question \(p\) is an organization \(E\), a transport \(t=(\pi,\tau,\sigma,\lambda):D\to E\), and an identified set \(\Gamma\) of active commitments in \(E\). Write \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\). The predicate is two-place on the organization and the question, with the transport and commitments existentially quantified:

\[
\operatorname{Account}(E,p)\iff\exists t,\Gamma\ \operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma),
\]

and \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) holds exactly when the following **five** conditions hold, each a condition on supplied relations under the changes in \(C\).

**Component fidelity.** For every active component \(k\) of \(E\) with anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\subseteq D\), and every \((a,b)\in C\), the relation obtained by imposing the constraints of \(\lambda(k)\) and projecting away its hidden ports equals the component relation of \(k\) under the translated edit:

\[
\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\!\big[\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\big]=L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)). \tag{F1}
\]

And the assembled organization agrees:

\[
\pi[\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)]=\operatorname{Sol}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b)),\qquad \tau(1)=1,\quad \tau(a_2a_1)=\tau(a_2)\tau(a_1). \tag{F2}
\]

(F1) prevents an assembled match from hiding a wrong decomposition. (F2) prevents a set of locally correct pieces from hiding a lost shared constraint. Together they are fidelity at every level the contract reaches. By Derivation 1, (F1) entails that every component of \(E\) has the signature of its anchor subnetwork on the translated contract; whether that is enough to say what the component *is* is the question the anchored grade below answers.

**Question fidelity.** For every \((a,b)\in C\),

\[
\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=\operatorname{Ans}_p(a,b). \tag{A}
\]

The query \(\mathcal Q\) is held fixed; an account of a different query is not an account of this one. An account of a reframed question is assessed as an account of the new question, and as a construction under Part X; (A) does not assess whether the reframe was an improvement (Part XV lists this as a limit).

**Non-circular dependence.** The answer follows by evaluating \(E\) under its independent boundary conditions. A boundary condition \(b\) is **independent** of the answer when no faithful transport carries the answer ports into the boundary ports. The target's answer does not appear, at the declared grain, as an unanalysed boundary input or as a component; moving an assertion from an input slot into a component named "law" does not discharge this. The witness: there exists an edit \(\tau(a)\) on \(E\), translated from some \((a,b)\in C\), that removes or replaces a nonempty **proper** sub-block \(B\subsetneq\Gamma\) while preserving the other boundary conditions, under which the answer profile changes or ceases to be determined in the claimed way. Removing all of \(\Gamma\) changes the profile of any candidate and witnesses nothing.

**Non-vacuity.** \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(1,b_0)\neq\varnothing\). The contract \(C\) is a stated subset of the admitted pairs together with a declared counterfactual set, in the form of Part III, and every admitted pair excluded from \(C\) is excluded by the stated scope \(S\), not silently.

**Contract adequacy.** \(C\) is not **protective** for \(\mathcal E\): there is no admitted pair \((a,b)\in C_{\mathrm{adm}}\setminus C\) at which \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\) differs from its baseline value while \(\operatorname{Ans}_E(\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) does not, or the reverse. This is the condition that catches a scope drawn to exclude the embarrassing change; a stated exclusion is not thereby a legitimate one.

Thus

\[
\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\iff \text{(F1)}\land\text{(F2)}\land\text{(A)}\land\text{NonCircular}\land\text{NonVacuous}\land\text{Adequate}. \tag{E}
\]

Every conjunct is a condition on how supplied relations behave under changes. None inspects a label. (E) carries **no provenance condition**: any modelled candidate, including one whose transport is declared, can be assessed for account-hood, because a modeller must be able to assess an account without settling its history. Provenance enters at (R), at Deploy, and at (EK), which each require \(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\) explicitly.

## Two grades: account at \(C\), and anchored

\(\operatorname{Account}(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) is **account-hood at \(C\)**. The stronger grade, **anchored account-hood**, adds four conditions on the anchor map, all stated in the same vocabulary:

1. **Properness.** Every anchor \(\lambda(k)\) is a proper subnetwork of \(D\), and the anchors of the active components refine a cover of \(D\)'s components: no anchor is the whole target, and no component of \(D\) that some pair in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) touches lies outside every anchor.
2. **Resolution.** Whenever two components of \(D\) can be separately varied by pairs in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) with distinct effects on \(\operatorname{Ans}_p\), they lie in distinct anchors. The decomposition of \(E\) is at least as fine as the dependencies the question asks about.
3. **No global relation.** No active component's relation is, on \(C\), definable as \(\operatorname{Sol}_D(a,b)\) or a projection of it: a component whose relation is the target's own solution set is the explanandum repackaged as a law, and it is excluded by name.
4. **World-kind.** Each active component of \(E\) is of one forward kind, on \((\ell,C_{\mathrm{adm}})\), with its anchor: fidelity holds not only on the pairs the contract selected but on every pair the module admits. Where the physical module supplies a history for \(D\), each anchor is an **active route** (Part IX) — a subnetwork of actual occurrences — and not a backup that would have responded identically.

Anchoring is therefore component fidelity on the full admitted contract, with a proper decomposition at the question's resolution. It is not a new primitive and it does not inspect a label. It is what separates a mechanism from a decoy that shares its signature on the contract someone happened to choose, and it is what "the model is about the actual mechanism" means here. The one-component candidate whose single relation is \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E) on every contract and fails conditions 1 and 3; the reversed calculation of Part VII is an account at the identification contract and fails condition 4 on the production edits; a decoy with the same signature on \(C\) but a different response to some admitted edit fails 4.

## What (E) excludes, and what only the anchored grade excludes

A table of observed answers has no component whose relation is replaced by an intervention; it fails (F1) under any contract containing one. A table that genuinely encodes an organization's response to every admitted change is not a table in that sense and is not excluded by (E); it is excluded by properness if it is one component, and by nothing if it is a faithful decomposition — in which case it is an account.

A reversed calculation — identification presented as production — fails (F2) under the production contract: intervening on the upstream port changes the target's downstream value but not the calculation's. Under the identification contract it is an account at that contract; it is not anchored as a production account.

"\(p\) because \(p\)" fails non-circular dependence.

An account whose only substantive contrast is one that no admitted edit realizes and that is not declared in \(K\) with a stated meaning fails non-vacuity. An account whose contract excludes an admitted change that would tell it apart from the target fails adequacy.

The one-component candidate that repackages \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) satisfies (E). It is an account at \(C\) and not anchored. That is the honest statement of what (E) alone can do, and why the second grade exists.

## What (E) does not exclude

A true mechanism guessed for bad reasons satisfies (E), and may be anchored; the reasons for adopting it are assessed elsewhere (Part IX), and anchoring is not a reasons condition. A less elegant account satisfies (E) as fully as a more elegant one with the same fidelity. A proof answering "why does this follow under these rules?" satisfies (E) for that question without answering "what caused this?"; depth is question-relative and (E) measures none of it.

## Why the anchoring condition is stated the way it is

A condition of the form "each component of \(E\) must be tied to a component of \(D\) *of the same kind*" would be evaluable — \(\lambda\) is transport data and Inst says what \(D\)'s components are — but it would not be evaluable *by the changes in \(C\)* where \(C\) contains no separating change, and on \(C\) alone the two are one forward kind. The right court of appeal is not \(C\) but \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\): a kind-claim about the world is a claim about every change the module admits. Condition 4 says exactly that. What remains after it — a label that discriminates where no admitted change and no history does — is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV, which this document does not claim to have proved.

# Part VI — Work, support, and interference

Fix \(\mathcal E\). The **restriction** \(E|W\), for \(W\subseteq\Gamma\), is the organization obtained by deleting every component in \(\Gamma\setminus W\) (each then imposes the full relation on its ports) while holding the boundary conditions and the components outside \(\Gamma\) — the **named background** — fixed. Define

\[
\mathsf S_{E,p}=\{W\subseteq\Gamma:\operatorname{Account}(E|W,p)\}. \tag{S}
\]

No upward closure and no minimal member are assumed. For nonempty \(B\subseteq W\),

\[
\operatorname{CriticalBlock}(B;W,p)\iff W\in\mathsf S_{E,p}\land W\setminus B\notin\mathsf S_{E,p}. \tag{B}
\]

A block may be critical while no singleton in it is. For a declared family \(\mathcal V\) of organization edits,

\[
\operatorname{Boundary}_{E,p}=\{(v,w)\in\mathcal V^2:\operatorname{Account}(E_v,p)\neq\operatorname{Account}(E_w,p)\}. \tag{D}
\]

**Finite monotone theorem.** If \(\Gamma\) is finite, \(\mathsf S\) is upward closed, and \(\Gamma\in\mathsf S\), then the critical singletons are exactly \(\bigcup\min\mathsf S\) and the globally indispensable ones are exactly \(\bigcap\min\mathsf S\). *Proof.* A member of a minimal support is critical for it. If \(d\) is critical for \(W\), finiteness gives a minimal \(U\subseteq W\); if \(d\notin U\), upward closure makes \(W\setminus\{d\}\) a support. Deletion of \(d\) from \(\Gamma\) preserves support exactly when a minimal support omits \(d\). ∎

**Redundant routes.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\}\): each is contributory, neither indispensable. Two systems with the same output table may differ in which routes are active; the semantics represents the difference, and the anchored grade's active-route condition uses it.

**Interference.** \(\Gamma=\{a,b\}\), \(\mathsf S=\{\{a\}\}\): the full candidate fails although a subset succeeds. Success of a subset does not imply success of the whole; \(b\) is not made "not a commitment" to repair this.

**Infinitary support.** \(\Gamma=\{d_n:|x|\le 1/n\}\): every unbounded index set determines \(x=0\); no minimal support and no singleton witness exists. (B) records the collective contribution.

**Hard-to-vary.** For explanatory jobs \(F\subseteq F'\), \(\operatorname{Pres}(F')\subseteq\operatorname{Pres}(F)\), where \(\operatorname{Pres}(F)=\{v\in\mathcal V:\forall f\in F,\operatorname{Account}(E_v,f)\}\). More reach constrains variation; the containment need not be strict; counting jobs is not a warrant.

# Part VII — Exact constructions

## Production and direction

Stipulate \(H:=U_H,\ \theta:=U_\theta,\ L:=H\cot\theta\). Under \(A\) containing interventions on \(H\) and \(\theta\), these ports are inputs and \(L\) is an output, by Part II. An intervention on \(L\) replaces its component and leaves \(H,\theta\) unchanged. The forward organization is faithful under this contract. The reversed calculation \(H=L\tan\theta\) is not: intervening on \(H\) changes the target's \(L\) but not the calculation's \(H\) (here \(\tau\) sends the intervention on \(H\) to the edit that replaces the calculation's component assigning \(H\), which the reversed organization has none of, so \(\tau\) is undefined and (F2) fails at that pair). The reversed calculation is faithful under the identification contract, whose edits alter the observed \(L\): it is an account at that contract, and not anchored as a production account, because on the production edits — admitted, and outside the identification contract — its one component is not of one forward kind with the assignment of \(L\). Direction is derived from the admitted edits; the nouns "pole" and "shadow" fix nothing; and when the production edits are unavailable to the questioner, the semantics says the direction is not represented at that contract rather than pretending a preference.

## Identification

Let \(Z\) be the admitted states, \(g:Z\to Y\) the measurement, \(f:Z\to F\) the feature. The fibre at \(y\) is \(Z_y=g^{-1}(y)\). The feature is identified at \(y\) exactly when \(Z_y\neq\varnothing\land|f[Z_y]|=1\). (I1) It is identified on every attainable \(y\) exactly when \(f=\bar f\circ g\) for some \(\bar f\). (I2) For a linear measurement \(M\) and feature \(c^\top\), identification is \(\ker M\subseteq\ker c^\top\). (I3) Repeating rows changes no kernel; an independent calibration can.

For the two balances \(\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}\) reading \(x+b_A\) and \(x+b_B\), the kernel is spanned by \((1,-1,-1)\); the readings identify \(b_A-b_B\) and not \(x\). This is an account of a limitation. "Set \(b_B=0\) because it gives the mass I favour" is not an inference from the readings; its support is circular though its content might be true.

## Obstruction

For allowed steps \(R\) and an invariant \(I\) with \(zRz'\Rightarrow I(z)=I(z')\), no allowed path joins states of different invariant value. (O1) Equal values do not establish reachability. Twenty-three indivisible tokens cannot be split equally three ways; permitting division changes the state space and does not refute the scoped result.

## Explanations that remove structure

A question of the form "why is there no \(X\)-effect?" has a target \(D\) in which the ports and components a rival account would need are absent, and a contract containing the edits that would introduce them. Two cases. Where the introducing edit is admitted, an account is faithful when introducing those components changes the answer in \(E\) as it does in \(D\), and their absence leaves both unchanged; the rival's supposed structure is anchored to a *deleted* subnetwork, whose relation is full by (O), and the anchor is proper because the deleted subnetwork is not the whole target. Where the rival's structure is not a possibility at all — the introducing edit is not admitted — the edit belongs to \(K\), declared with its meaning ("a component enforcing conservation is removed"), and the account is faithful on it in the same sense; the objectivity condition then ranges over \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) only, so what such an account establishes about the world is that no admitted change produces the effect, and what it establishes about the impossibility is relative to the stated meaning of the counterfactual. That is the semantics' treatment of eliminative explanation, offered as adequate for the first case and as scoped for the second.

Two further families are **not treated** here and are listed under attack (B): explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among \(E\)'s components (from invariance to independence) that (F1), a per-component condition, and (F2), an assembly condition, do not constrain; and explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact, which needs two targets (the data organization and the population organization) and a transport for each. Neither has a construction in this document.

## Odd-order skew-symmetric matrices

Question: why is every odd-order real skew-symmetric matrix singular? This is a mathematical organization, so the admission relation is declared: the admitted edits are alterations of the presentation — remove the skewness constraint; remove the oddness constraint; remove both — with field arithmetic and the determinant–invertibility relation held fixed as background. The full Leibniz expansion with skewness substituted satisfies (F1) and (F2): every intermediate product is a determinant suborganization whose hidden ports project away. It satisfies (A): the answer is the absence of invertible matrices in the family, and under each contrast the family contains one. Non-circular dependence is witnessed by \(I_3\) under removal of skewness and by \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}\) under removal of oddness, each removing a proper sub-block. Non-vacuity is witnessed by any nonzero odd skew matrix. Adequacy is relative to the declared admission relation, and the background components (arithmetic, the determinant) are anchored to themselves under the identity translation, which is stated rather than assumed. The expansion is an account at that contract. That a three-line proof is shorter is not a sixth condition. What the example does not do is fall under attack (B)'s quantifier over *physically* admitted contracts, because there is no physical module here; it falls under the declared one.

## Constitutive rules

A rule relation \(C_r\subseteq Z\times S\) answers "which status under this rule?" by its fibre. Its signature under (K) is invariant under interventions on \(Z\) and variable under edits to \(C_r\). Whether the rule governs a practice, and whether it should, are separate questions with separate contracts.

# Part VIII — Transport results

**Functional transport.** If \(\pi\circ S_a=T_a\circ\pi\) for every generator \(a\), the same holds for every admitted finite composition with matching scopes. *Proof.* \(\pi S_bS_a=T_b\pi S_a=T_bT_a\pi\); induct. ∎

**Relational transport.** For \(R\subseteq Z_D\times Z_E\), forward preservation is

\[
zRy\land z\xrightarrow{a}z'\Rightarrow\exists y'[y\xrightarrow{\tau(a)}y'\land z'Ry'], \tag{T1}
\]

with the backward condition required for equivalence rather than one-sided abstraction. Composition of relations preserves both directions when intermediate scopes agree.

**Approximate transport.** With one-step discrepancy \(\varepsilon\) and an \(L\)-Lipschitz next-step map, \(e_n\le\varepsilon\sum_{k<n}L^k\). (T2) Without a modulus, no accumulated bound follows. An exact question is not silently replaced by an approximate one.

**Historical index.** A claim indexed to a contract keeps its identity when a later theory changes the current contract; its truth value at its own index does not change, and a claim at a new index is a new claim. Identity of the claim and fixity of its truth value at that index are the two things this says; it says nothing about the truth of the claim at any other index.

# Part IX — Criticism, use, and standing

**Histories.** A history \(h\) is a set of occurrences with the acyclic precedence \(\prec_h\) supplied by the physical module as its admitted-process dependency order (Part XII), and a physical interpretation supplying process occurrences, their ports, and the connections actually instantiated. An **active route** is a connected subnetwork of actual occurrences joining a represented input to an operative result, whose components satisfy the applicable relations and which has nonconstant dependence on the represented distinction under the declared contrasts. A backup that would have responded identically is not on the active route; the difference between the two is a difference in \(h\), not in signature, and it is what the anchored grade's fourth condition uses.

**Bearing.** A criticism \(c\) has target \(z\), alleged defect \(\delta\), grounds \(g\), and a connection. Let \(p_\delta\) be the question about the defect and \(E_c\) the organization the criticism supplies. Then

\[
\operatorname{Bearing}(c,z,p_\delta)\iff\operatorname{Account}(E_c,p_\delta). \tag{K1}
\]

A criticism occurrence can exist when (K1) fails. An adverse signal is not a criticism until an organization represents how it bears.

**Reason use.** A response uses a reason when a structural map from the represented objection into the response suborganization preserves role bindings, sends content-preserving recodings (Part X, \(\equiv_\ell\)) to the same transition, sends content changes to the changes specified by the operative deliberative rule, and lands on an active route. Using an invalid objection does not make it valid.

**Standing.** An appraiser \(j\) has a declared appraisal record: a license set \(\mathcal L_j\) of argument forms it admits, a declared contract \(C_j\), and a withdrawal log. For argument application \(u\) with essential premises \(\operatorname{Prem}(u)\), define \(\operatorname{Lic}_j(u)\): the form of \(u\) is in \(\mathcal L_j\); \(\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\): every pair \(u\) ranges over lies in \(C_j\); \(\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u)\): premise \(d\) has not been withdrawn in \(j\)'s log before \(u\). Then

\[
\operatorname{Usable}_j(u)\iff\operatorname{Lic}_j(u)\land\operatorname{Scope}_j(u)\land\forall d\in\operatorname{Prem}(u),\operatorname{Live}_j(d;u). \tag{K2}
\]

These three are typed components of declared data, not judgements. Withdrawing a premise removes a license; it does not make the conclusion false.

**What a test refutes.** For \(T\land B\land I\Rightarrow O\), an established \(\neg O\) yields \(\neg(T\land B\land I)\) and nothing narrower. (K3)

**Receipts.** An evidence leaf is a reference to an event with an interpreted claim. A receipt is a derivation tree over leaves. For \(\phi\), \(P_j(\phi)\) and \(N_j(\phi)\) are the usable receipts for and against. Negation exchanges them; missing evidence stays missing.

# Part X — Understanding, construction, and origin

**Recoding.** Two contents \(c,d\) are **content-preserving recodings** of each other at grain \(\ell\), written \(c\equiv_\ell d\), when there is a bijection between their port sets, value domains, components and boundaries that carries the admitted edits of one onto the other and the component relations of one onto the other pointwise — the structure-preserving bijections of Derivation 8, applied to contents. A transfer is **content-preserving** when its input and output stand in \(\equiv_\ell\).

**Ownership.** A subhistory is **owned** by \(s\) when every process occurrence in it is an execution of a capability \(s\) possesses in the sense of Part XII (\(\operatorname{Can}\)), under \(s\)'s continuity and resource contract; a description of a process the system could not run is not owned by it.

**Deployment.** \(\operatorname{Deploy}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,\xi;U)\) holds when \(s\) at \(\xi\) holds a representation of \(c\) — by (R), a faithful transport with selected or constructed provenance — integrated into problem-directed activity and supporting the declared use task \(U\) as a retained capability (Part XII). The repertoire \(R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\) is the set of contents deployable in some nontrivial use respect. A system may understand a false theory.

**Construction.** \(\operatorname{Build}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,h,e)\) holds when an owned subhistory of \(h\), delimited at \(e\), prepares an organization that some occurrence of \(s\) instantiates for explanatory use of \(c\), contains a **nontrivial binding construction required for that use**, and is not a composition of content-preserving transfers. A **binding** is an assignment \(\lambda(k)\) of a component of the built organization to a subnetwork of its target; a binding construction is **nontrivial** when the binding is neither a relabelling of a binding already in the repertoire nor obtainable from the incoming carriers by \(\equiv_\ell\); it is **required for the use** when deleting the binding breaks (F1) or (A) for \(c\) on its contract. A construction witness identifies the controlled processes, the incoming carriers, the bindings constructed, and the resulting organization. Reconstruction by a learner is construction; relay is not.

Construction is not selection. A selection witness contains no itemised representation of a defect and no target instantiated before the variation; a construction witness contains both. A physical system may exhibit both; the witnesses differ, and the precedence rule of Part IV says which the resulting transport bears.

**Newness.** With \(R_{<e}(s,h)=\bigcup_{\xi\text{ before }e}R_{\beta,\ell}(s,\xi)\),

\[
\operatorname{New}(s,c,h,e)\iff\neg\exists d\in R_{<e}(s,h),\ d\equiv_\ell c. \tag{N}
\]

**Origin.** With \(\operatorname{Attempt}(s,c,p,h,e)\) the relation that \(c\) is actually used, on an active route, to address \(p\) — where \(p\) is a coherently posed question that \(s\) instantiates before \(e\), and for which the relevant difficulty is that some obligation in \(O_p\) fails at the state before \(e\) —

\[
\operatorname{Origin}_{\beta,\ell}(s,c,p,h,e)\iff\operatorname{Attempt}\land\operatorname{New}\land\operatorname{Build}. \tag{G}
\]

The content \(c\) may be an organization, a transport, or a question. When it is a question, the originative act is the finding of a question, and it is subject to the **merit condition**: the found question \(p'\) counts as found, rather than re-parameterised, only when there is an obligation \(o\in O\) such that a deployable account of \(p'\) would repair \(o\) under (P), with \(o\) failing at the state before \(e\). This is the only merit condition in the semantics, and it is stated so that a random new contract does not count.

**Episodes.** A complete critical episode contains a recognized difficulty, a target available before its criticism, a conjectural objection, and a content-sensitive response. A creative critical episode contains an instance of (G) connected to its inquiry. \(\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\), for an episode \(\Delta\), is the set of contents whose construction witnesses lie within \(\Delta\). Closing an episode is a decision, not a proof.

**What is not represented.** The invention of a new dimension of variation — a port the target organization did not have, a respect in which nothing was previously varied — is not a question found in the sense above, because the question-organization of Derivation 5 is built over an existing \(A\times B\). It is a change to the target organization itself, and this document has no witness for it. That is a stated limit, and Part XV lists it.

# Part XI — Progress, knowledge, and the normative

**Produced by.** For an episode \(\Delta\) with start state \(\xi\) and end state \(\xi'\), \(\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O)\) holds when \(\xi'\) is the state at the end of \(\Delta\), \(\xi\) at its start, and every change of value of an obligation in \(O\) between them lies on an active route from occurrences of \(\Delta\). \(\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')\) holds when, in addition, the change of \(o\) lies on an active route that passes through a deployment of \(c\).

**Repair.** For claimed obligations \(O\) and protected obligations \(P\), fixed for the comparison,

\[
\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\iff\exists o\in O[\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')]\land\forall r\in P[r(\xi)\Rightarrow r(\xi')]\land\operatorname{ProducedBy}(\Delta,\xi,\xi';O). \tag{P}
\]

(P) does not rank alternatives. Losses outside \(P\) must be exposed. "Claimed" and "protected" are declared: they say which obligations the comparison is about, not that the claims are true; (P) is evaluated on the obligations' actual values at \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\).

**Created explanatory knowledge.** An epistemic obligation requires a correct account, or the correction of a use through one, to be deployable. With \(O_{\mathrm{ep}}\subseteq O\) the epistemic obligations, and all conditions evaluated at the end state \(\xi'\) unless stated,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{CreateEK}(s,\Delta,h,e)\iff{}&\operatorname{CreativeCriticalEpisode}(s,\Delta,h,e)\land\operatorname{Repair}_{O,P}(\xi,\xi';\Delta)\\
&\land\exists o\in O_{\mathrm{ep}}\,\exists c,p_c,e_c\,[e_c\preceq_h e\land\neg o(\xi)\land o(\xi')\land\operatorname{Origin}(s,c,p_c,h,e_c)\\
&\quad\land\operatorname{Account}(c,p_c)\land c\in\operatorname{Result}(\Delta)\land\operatorname{Deploy}(s,c,\xi';U_c)\land\operatorname{ProducesVia}(\Delta,c,o;\xi,\xi')].
\end{aligned}\tag{EK}
\]

Here \(c\) is a content whose organization is the \(E\) of \(\operatorname{Account}(E,p_c)\), and \(\operatorname{Account}\) is evaluated on the contract of \(p_c\) fixed at \(e_c\); Deploy supplies the provenance condition (\(\operatorname{Sel}\lor\operatorname{Con}\)) through (R). (EK) is an **external success predicate**: it can hold of a system that does not know it holds, and a system's claim to have created knowledge is a separate occurrence, assessed under Part IX. A later narrowing of the contract to rescue adequacy is a new claim at a new index, and does not retroactively satisfy (EK). No instance of (EK) is asserted in this document.

**Artistic effect, purpose, and aesthetic reason.** An effect organization \(\mathcal R\subseteq A\times K\times F\); a purpose \(G\subseteq K\times F\); achievement \(\mathcal R[a,k]\neq\varnothing\land\mathcal R[a,k]\subseteq G[k]\) (AR); a normative relation \(\mathcal N\subseteq A\times K\times\mathcal Rsn\times\mathcal V_A\) declared as a substantive input when aesthetic value is claimed. None is defined as another. The semantics does not derive \(\mathcal N\).

# Part XII — The physical module

**What the module supplies.** The physical module \(\Theta\) supplies: substrate state spaces and attributes; the admitted processes and, for each occurrence, the **admitted edit set** \(A_\Theta(o)\) — which alterations of the occurrence's state the module permits, stated as a set, since every contract, every grain-admissibility claim and every world-kind claim quantifies over it; the interpretation of some processes as controlled actions, meaning an **intervention repertoire** \(\mathcal I\subseteq A_\Theta\) of edits the module says can be brought about, with no agency presupposed; resources and accuracy grades; the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\) of Part IV; the process-dependency order \(\prec\) used by histories; and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\) — the set of executions of protocol \(\pi\) from state \(z\) on input \(i\) under enabling conditions \(\chi\).

**Tasks.** A substrate is a physical system; an attribute a set of its states; a task a permitted input-to-output attribute transformation with explicit resources and side effects. Possibility is the absence of a law-imposed limit short of perfection on performance and retention; it is not one successful trajectory. The physical module adopts a task-based formulation of physics for this purpose.

**Retained realization.** For protocol \(\pi\), task \(T\), constructor attribute \(C\), and enabling conditions \(\chi\),

\[
\operatorname{RetReal}(\pi,T,C;\chi)\iff\forall z\in C\ \forall i\in\operatorname{dom}T\ \big[\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi)\neq\varnothing\land\forall\eta\in\operatorname{Exec}(\pi,z,i;\chi),\ \eta\text{ completes with }o\in T[i]\text{ and }z'\in C\big]. \tag{CT1}
\]

Execution families are required non-empty on legitimate inputs; deadlock is not vacuous success, and an empty family is not a realization.

**Retention fixed point.** \(F(C)\) = states whose executions all complete and return into \(C\). \(F\) is monotone; \(C\subseteq F(C)\) is the invariant form; the union of post-fixed sets is the greatest fixed point. (CT2)

**Owned capability.** \(\operatorname{Can}_{\Omega,\beta}(\xi,T;\chi)\) requires an owned retained realization or an owned, physically admitted, finite construction of one under the same continuity and resource contract. A theorist's description of a protocol is not the system's possession of it.

**Achievement.** \(\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi,p;\chi,J_p,C_I)\): for each starting configuration in the independently specified \(J_p\) — specified by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed — every maximal execution completes with a history satisfying the achievement predicate and a continuing organization in \(C_I\). A first discovery is not a repeatable task; retention is applied to the inquiry-enabling organization. (CA)

**Grades.** \(\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}_\Omega(\xi)\subseteq\mathsf{Admit}^{q,r}_\Theta\); \(\mathsf{Cap}^\infty=\bigcap_{q,r}\mathsf{Cap}^{q,r}\subseteq\mathsf{Poss}_\Theta\). (CT3, CT4) Finite-grade capability does not imply possibility at all grades.

**Selection in the physical module.** The selection witness of Part IV — population, applied variation operator, encountered history, eliminating survival condition, causal responsibility — is a claim about a physical history under \(\Theta\): realized transports, physically admitted variation, a survival condition enacted by the environment. It is fallible and checkable as any physical claim is. This paragraph adds no second definition; Part IV's is the only one.

# Part XIII — Recursion and universality

**Scrutinizability.** An aspect \(d\) of a system's practice is scrutinizable at \(\xi\) when there is an owned, admitted continuation in which a description of \(d\) becomes a target instantiated by the system, criticism can be directed at it, and the result can affect its operative use. Contracts and transports are among the scrutinizable aspects.

**Recursive capacity.**

\[
\forall n<\omega\ \forall\text{ admitted target chains of length }n,\ \exists\text{ an owned enabling continuation}. \tag{RC}
\]

**Enabling.** \(\operatorname{Enable}(s,U,\chi)\) holds when \(\chi\) is an admitted enabling condition — a set of states and resources the module permits — under which \(s\) has \(\operatorname{Can}\) for the use task \(U\). An enabling condition \(\chi\) for the use of content \(c\) is **question-begging** when \(\chi\) contains, as a component at the declared grain, a deployment of \(c\) or an organization held to \(c\) by a faithful transport: the capability is then supplied, not enabled. This is a structural condition on \(\chi\), and it replaces any judgement about what "begs the question".

**Barriers.** An explanatory barrier is an independently characterized domain — characterized by a record fixed before the candidate is assessed — for which every admitted, non-question-begging enabling condition leaves the capability required for the domain's contents unavailable. A finite list of failures is not a barrier proof; a bypass refutes a proposed barrier.

**Universality.** With \(\mathfrak E_\Theta\) the explanatory contents and \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\) the coherently posed advanceable challenges, both specified independently of the candidate in the same sense,

\[
\operatorname{UU}\iff\forall c\in\mathfrak E_\Theta\ \exists\chi,\ \operatorname{Enable}(s,U_c,\chi)\land\operatorname{Can}(\xi_0,U_c;\chi), \tag{U1}
\]
\[
\operatorname{UC}\iff\forall p\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{adv}}_\Theta\ \exists\chi,\ \operatorname{Enable}(s,A_p,\chi)\land\operatorname{CanAdv}(\xi_0,p;\chi,J_p,C_I), \tag{U2}
\]
\[
\mathsf{UECS}=\{(M,s,\Omega,\beta):M\models\operatorname{RC}\land\operatorname{UU}\land\operatorname{UC}\}. \tag{U3}
\]

Recursion does not entail universality; a historical extension does not certify it; a finite performance record does not establish it.

# Part XIV — The class collected

**Primitive packages.** The semantics has two.

1. The **physical module** \(\Theta\): substrate state spaces, attributes, admitted processes, the admitted edit set \(A_\Theta\), the intervention repertoire, resources, accuracy grades, the instantiation relation \(\operatorname{Inst}_\ell\), the process order \(\prec\), and the execution families \(\operatorname{Exec}\). It is a package, and the instantiation relation inside it is a decomposition oracle: that is what "physics says what a lump instantiates" costs, and it is listed rather than hidden.
2. The **normative relation** \(\mathcal N\), when a question invokes one.

**Declared parameters.** Neither derived nor primitive; supplied with each use, and every claim is relative to them: the organization interpretation \((V,X,J,B,A,L)\) of every organization not attributed through \(\operatorname{Inst}\); the indices — grain \(\ell\) (constrained by admissibility), boundary \(\beta\), continuity \(\Omega\); the contract \(C\), in the form \((C_{\mathrm{adm}}\cap S)\cup K\), with its provenance, the stated scope \(S\), and the counterfactual set \(K\) with meanings; the declared admission relation for non-physical organizations; the query \(\mathcal Q\); the obligation sets \(O,P,O_p\); an appraiser's license set, contract and withdrawal log; the restriction operation's named background and the edit family \(\mathcal V\) of Part VI; the use tasks \(U\) and enabling conditions \(\chi\).

**Derived.** Roles, from admitted edits (Part II). Forward kinds, from signatures (K); historical kinds, from provenance witnesses. The respect of a question, from its query (Part III). Representation, from fidelity, instantiation and provenance (R). Provenance, from physical history (Part IV, XII). Account, from fidelity under change (E); anchoring, from fidelity under every admitted change with a proper decomposition (Part V). Understanding, construction, newness, origin, repair, knowledge, capability, recursion, universality — from those, with the definitions of \(\equiv_\ell\), ownership, binding, ProducedBy, ProducesVia, Result, Lic/Scope/Live, Enable and question-begging given where they are first used.

**Dependence order.** (O) and (Q) are schemata over the supplied organization and query. (K) depends on (O), a grain, and a contract. (F1), (F2), (A) depend on (O), (Q), (K). (E) depends on those and on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) for adequacy; anchoring depends on (E), (K) on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and histories. (S), (B), (D) depend on (E). (R) depends on (F1)–(F2), \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and physical provenance; provenance depends on \(\Theta\) and on Part X's witnesses, which depend on \(\operatorname{Inst}\) and \(\equiv_\ell\) but not on (R) — the stratification of Part IV (targets are *instantiated*, not *represented*, in a construction witness) is what keeps this order well-founded. (K1) depends on (E). Deploy depends on (R) and (CT1). Build depends on histories, ownership, bindings and (E). (N), (G) depend on Deploy and Build. (P), (EK) depend on (G), (E), Deploy, ProducedBy. (RC), (U1)–(U3) depend on all of the above. No predicate in Parts II–XIII is left undefined apart from the two packages and the declared parameters; whether the defined predicates *capture* explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, not a theorem.

**Membership.** The base class: interpretations supplying these data with correct typing, satisfying physical realization wherever a physical attribution is made. The creative-episode class: base interpretations with an instance of (G) connected to a critical episode. The knowledge-creation class: an instance of (EK). The recursive class: (RC). The universal class: (U3). A single originative act does not place its author in the universal class.

# Part XV — What defeats this class

**A faithful, anchored transport that does not explain.** A candidate meeting the anchored grade of (E) on an admitted contract with a non-declared transport, which plainly provides no account. This refutes sufficiency. A candidate meeting (E) alone and explaining nothing is already known to exist — the one-component repackaging of \(\operatorname{Sol}_D\) — and is why the second grade is stated; it does not refute the class, because the class's sufficiency claim is for the anchored grade.

**An explanation without a faithful transport.** A genuine explanation whose organization no transport can preserve under any admitted contract. This refutes necessity. Four cases are open, and the document's treatment of each is stated as a limit rather than a solution: (i) elimination of a physically impossible rival, handled only through a declared counterfactual set whose meaning is stipulated; (ii) explanation by symmetry, whose force is an entailment among the components of \(E\) that neither (F1) nor (F2) constrains, with no construction here; (iii) explanation of an apparent effect as a selection artefact, which needs two targets and has no construction here; (iv) mathematical explanation, which falls under a declared admission relation and not under the physical quantifier of this attack.

**A discriminating kind.** A case where a kind-label distinguishes two accounts that no change in \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) distinguishes and no history distinguishes, and the distinction does explanatory work. This refutes the **analysis-of-kinds conjecture** — that every discriminating use of a kind-label is a forward kind on some admissible grain and admitted contract, or a historical kind — and reinstates correspondence as primitive. Derivation 1 does not protect the conjecture; it only shows fidelity preserves signatures.

**A non-fallible selected transport.** A selection history \(H\subsetneq C\), in a population closed under pointwise alteration of component relations off \(H\), whose survivor is determined on \(C\setminus H\). This refutes Derivation 3 under its hypothesis. A population *not* closed in that way, whose survivor is determined off \(H\) by its parametrisation, does not refute anything: Derivation 3 makes no claim about it.

**Construction reduced to selection.** A demonstration that every construction witness — an owned subhistory with an itemised defect representation and a target instantiated before the variation — can be rewritten as a selection witness satisfying the discrimination requirement of Part IV, without loss. This collapses the two provenances and removes creativity from the semantics. A rewriting that satisfies only the trivial reading of selection (singleton population, empty history) does not count, because that reading is excluded.

**A question found that the merit condition wrongly rejects, or a re-parameterisation it wrongly admits.** Either refutes the merit condition of Part X as the line between finding and relabelling. A new dimension of variation is already outside the line, and the document says so; a case in which it must be inside to capture a genuine finding counts against attack (E).

**A protective contract the adequacy condition admits.** A stated scope, passing Part V's adequacy condition, that nonetheless excludes exactly the admitted change that would separate the candidate from the target. This shows the adequacy condition is too weak, and objectivity would again be contract-relative only.

**A mathematical error.** A counterexample to the finite monotone theorem, (I2), (O1), (T2), (CT2), or Derivations 1–3 and 8 under their stated assumptions.

None of these is protected by notation, by the availability of this document, or by any version label.

# Part XVI — Derivations and remarks

## 1. Kind preservation under fidelity

**Claim.** Let \(t\) satisfy (F1) on \(C\), and let \((\tau,\sigma)\) be injective on \(C\). Then every active component \(k\) of \(E\) has, on \(\tau[C]\), the signature that its anchor subnetwork \(\lambda(k)\) has on \(C\), up to the port translation.

*Proof.* By the subnetwork signature of Part II, \(\operatorname{sig}_C(\lambda(k))=\{(a,b,\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b))\}\), the visible ports of the anchor being those \(\lambda\) translates to \(V_k\). By (K), \(\operatorname{sig}_{\tau[C]}(k)=\{(\tau(a),\sigma(b),L_k(\tau(a),\sigma(b)))\}\). (F1) equates the third coordinates pointwise; injectivity of \((\tau,\sigma)\) on \(C\) makes the map between the two sets a bijection on the first two coordinates. ∎

**What this does and does not show.** It shows that fidelity on a contract preserves signature on that contract: a faithful component is of one forward kind, on \(C\), with its anchor subnetwork. It does not show that a kind-label can do no work, because the anchor may be a subnetwork rather than a component, because the contract may not be the admitted one, and because histories are not signatures. The claim that labels are eliminable in favour of signatures on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and histories is the analysis-of-kinds conjecture of Part XV. The anchored grade of Part V is where signature on \(C_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is required; it is a condition, not a corollary.

## 2. Account-equivalence

**Definition.** Two candidates \(\mathcal E=(E,p,t,\Gamma)\) and \(\mathcal E'=(E',p,t',\Gamma')\) for the same \(p\) are **account-equivalent on \(C\)** when there is a bijection between their active components carrying \(\Gamma\) onto \(\Gamma'\) under which, for every \((a,b)\in C\), the anchored relations \(\operatorname{proj}_{V_k}\operatorname{Sol}_{\lambda(k)}(a,b)\) coincide, and the pulled-back answer profiles coincide.

**Claim.** If \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) both satisfy (F1), (F2) and (A) on \(C\), have the same anchor map up to that bijection, and \(\Gamma\) corresponds to \(\Gamma'\), then they are account-equivalent on \(C\).

*Proof.* (F1) for each gives the anchored relations as the component relations; the shared anchor map and the bijection give coincidence; (A) gives the profiles. ∎

**What it does not say.** Two candidates satisfying (F1), (F2), (A) on \(C\) with *different* decompositions, or different active commitments, are not one account, and no result here says they are. Underdetermination of an account by a contract is the semantics reporting that the contract does not contain the distinction. The remedies are a finer contract — a new question — or a history, which the semantics represents as a historical kind; where the question fixes the contract and forbids both, the distinction is not represented, and this is said rather than hidden.

## 3. Selection guarantees nothing off its history

**Claim.** Let \(t:D\to E\) be selected on \(H\subsetneq C\) from a population \(\mathcal T\) of organization–transport pairs, and suppose \(\mathcal T\) is **closed under pointwise alteration off \(H\)**: for each \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\), each component \(j\) of \(E\), and each relation \(L'\subseteq\prod_{v\in V_j}X_v\) in the admissible class (the relations realizable by some member of \(\mathcal T\)), \(\mathcal T\) contains a pair agreeing with \((E,t)\) everywhere except that \(L_j(\tau(a),\sigma(b))=L'\). Then for every \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) there is a pair in \(\mathcal T\) that survives on \(H\) and whose solution set at \((\tau(a),\sigma(b))\) differs from \(E\)'s, whenever some admissible \(L'\) changes that solution set.

*Proof.* Survival on \(H\) constrains only the relations at pairs in \(H\). The altered pair agrees with \((E,t)\) on \(H\), hence survives; it differs at \((a,b)\) by hypothesis. ∎

**Consequence.** Under the closure hypothesis, the selection data do not determine the survivor off \(H\): selection provides no *guarantee* of fidelity where it did not test. It does not follow that the survivor *is* unfaithful there — it may be faithful on all of \(C\) — and nothing here says the survivor will fail. Where the population is parametric and not closed under pointwise alteration, the survivor may be determined off \(H\) by its parametrisation, and this derivation is silent. That is why the primitive layer is *not guaranteed*; fallibility is a possibility this establishes, not an event it predicts.

## 4. Surprise, as a remark

Surprise is defined (Part IV) as a violation at a pair outside the history. That a system with no transport is never surprised, and that a system whose history exhausts its contract is never surprised, follow from the definition by unpacking. This is recorded as a remark and not as a derivation, and nothing else in this document cites it as support. What would be a result — conditions under which a violation at some \((a,b)\in C\setminus H\) actually occurs — depends on the world and on the population, and is not proved here.

## 5. Question-finding is representable

**Claim.** A question \(p=(D,C,b_0,\mathcal Q,O_p,\rho_p)\) determines an organization \(D_p\) in the sense of (O), which can be the content \(c\) in (G). Hence an episode whose originative contribution is a new question satisfies (G) when the other conjuncts and the merit condition hold.

*Construction.* Ports: one port \(u_{(a,b)}\) for each \((a,b)\in A\times B\) of the target, with domain \(\{\text{in},\text{out}\}\); one port \(u_{\mathcal Q}\) with domain the set of queries admissible on \(D\); one port \(u_S\) with domain the stated scopes and one \(u_K\) with domain the declared counterfactual sets with meanings. Components: the closure conditions — the baseline port reads "in"; if \(u_{(a_2a_1,b)}\) reads "in" then so do its factors where the composition is defined; \(u_S\) and \(u_K\) are consistent with the "in" ports in the form of Part III. Boundary: the target \(D\) and \(b_0\), held fixed. Admitted edits: toggle a pair; alter \(\mathcal Q\); alter \(S\); add a pair to \(K\) with a meaning. Its solutions are exactly the coherently posed questions on \(D\). It is a \(D_p\) in the sense of (O); Inst, Deploy, Build and New apply to occurrences that carry it; provenance attaches to the whole question through this organization. ∎

**Consequence.** Finding the right question is a creative act on the same footing as answering one, subject to the merit condition of Part X. What the construction cannot do is add a port to \(D\): the invention of a new dimension of variation changes the target, and this document has no witness for that (Part X, Part XV).

## 6. Two primitive packages, and the rest declared or derived

**Claim.** Every predicate in Parts II–XIII is defined from the physical module (with the components listed in Part XII), the normative relation where invoked, and the declared parameters listed in Part XIV, by the dependence order there.

*Proof.* Each definition names its inputs at first use; the order of Part XIV is followed from each predicate to its base, and the only cycle that could arise — construction requiring a represented target while representation requires construction — is broken by Part IV, where a construction witness requires an *instantiated* target and (R) is not used inside any provenance witness. ∎

**Consequence.** No residual predicate is left undefined. That is a property of the formalism. Whether the defined predicates capture explanation, representation, causation or knowledge is the constitutive conjecture, and this derivation says nothing about it.

## 7. The frozen assessment and the moving question are consistent

**Claim.** An assessment event with contract \(C\) and an episode in which \(C\) is replaced by \(C'\) with a construction witness are both representable without contradiction.

*Proof.* The assessment is indexed to \(C\); its claim keeps its identity and its truth value at that index (Part VIII). The episode is a history in which a question-organization with contract \(C'\) is built (Derivation 5) and takes over operative use; by Part III, the change ends the first assessment and begins another. The claim "\(E\) is an account on \(C\)" and the claim "\(E\) is not an account on \(C'\)" are claims at different indices and may both be true. ∎

**Consequence.** The point of contact between an explanation and the world moves across episodes because questions are constructed; it does not move within an assessment because assessments are indexed. Goalpost-moving is the act of changing the index without recording the change; the adequacy condition of Part V is what catches a change made to protect a candidate, whether or not it is recorded.

## 8. Equivariance under genuine recoding

**Claim.** Let \(\phi\) be a family of bijections on carriers, relations, transports (including their anchor maps \(\lambda\)), histories, contracts, obligations and the normative relation, that preserves the physical module's admission (\(A_\Theta\), Inst, \(\prec\)), the stated scopes and counterfactual meanings, and \(\mathcal N\). Transporting all data along \(\phi\) preserves (E), anchoring, (G), (P), (EK). *Proof.* Each is a conjunction of equalities and existence claims over the transported data, and each of its inputs is carried along; because \(\lambda\) is among the transported data, a recoding that would change what a component is anchored to — the pole-height port carried to the shadow-length port — is not among the permitted \(\phi\), since it does not preserve \(D\)'s structure. ∎ The result does not apply to coarsenings, changed boundaries, lost event identities, or maps that alter admission.

## 9. Output descriptions do not determine accounts

**Claim.** If \(M_0,M_1\) have the same input–output projection and differ on an account claim, no function of the projection agrees with the claim on both. *Proof.* Equal inputs to a function give equal outputs. ∎ Parallel and priority wiring are an instance. The same holds for attribution from emitted text and for use inferred from delivery logs.

## 10. A three-layer episode, as an illustrative scenario

This is a scenario, not a consistency witness: its organizations are described, not written out, and no condition is checked on it. It is here to show the shape the definitions give to a familiar case.

Stipulate a sensory field \(F\): the occupancy of a line of cells — which cells are filled, without which thing fills them. Stipulate a primitive layer \(P\): two things, each with a position and a velocity; continuity components; admitted edits: displace a thing, set its velocity, occlude a cell, swap the two identities. \(P\) is held to \(F\) by a transport in a population of trackers; assume, as a stipulation about that population, that it contains trackers that keep a thing through an unconfirmed spell and trackers that do not, and that its survival condition is prediction of the field under the encountered changes.

A simulation layer \(S_0\) with a transport \(t_0\) selected on a history \(H_0\) containing displacements and velocity changes but no occlusions. \(S_0\) predicts occupancy from recent occupancy. It is faithful on \(H_0\).

An occlusion occurs. Under occlusion, the target's thing continues to exist and to move; the occupancy field shows nothing at its cell. \(S_0\), predicting from occupancy, expects nothing there and is violated when the thing re-emerges at a cell consistent with its velocity. That is a violation off \(H_0\): surprise by definition.

Two responses. **Selection:** \(H_0\) is extended to include occlusions; \(\mu\) re-tunes \(t_0\) within its population. *If* the population's transports can only predict from occupancy — a stipulation about \(\mathcal T\), not a consequence of anything — no member survives the extended history. **Construction:** a new organization \(S_1\) is built with a component per thing carrying position and velocity through occlusion — a persistence component — and a transport \(t_1:P\to S_1\) whose \(\lambda\) sends each persistence component to a thing's continuity subnetwork. If (F1) and (F2) hold for \(t_1\) on the extended contract — which would have to be checked by writing out \(P\), \(S_1\) and the relations, and is not checked here — then by Derivation 1 the persistence components are of one forward kind, on that contract, with the continuity subnetworks of things: they respond to displacement and velocity edits as those subnetworks do, and are invariant under occlusion as they are. That is a grain-relative claim about kind; it is not the claim that they *are* objects in any further sense, and the document makes no such claim.

If \(S_1\) was built by an owned subhistory containing a nontrivial binding construction required for the use — the binding of a persistence component to a continuity subnetwork, which deleting would break (F1) — and \(S_1\) is not in the prior repertoire, then (G) holds. Whether (P) and (EK) hold depends on constituents the scenario does not supply: a recognized difficulty, a conjectural objection, a content-sensitive response, an epistemic obligation named with its before and after values, and the active routes of Part XI. None is asserted.

The swap edit is invisible in the sensory field and leaves every prediction unchanged. Under (K), with the swap supplying the footprint bijection, the two persistence components are of one forward kind on any contract containing the swap — and that is a claim from signature coincidence, not from prediction-invariance, which the semantics does not accept as kind-identity. A claim that "component 1 is *really* thing 1" is either a claim about a finer contract or a claim about history; on this contract, neither is available, and the correct report is that identity, at this grain and contract, is exhausted by trajectory.

It is not a claim that any actual infant, animal, or program has been shown to instantiate any of this.

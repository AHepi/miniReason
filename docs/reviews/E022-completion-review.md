## Content findings

### E022-INT-1: rejection of exclusion is not consistently carried into the proposed account

Bare first says an aggregation is a rule and that criticizing it is rule criticism. Its standalone account later says the objection is “not to any record, rule, history, snapshot, or coordinate,” then eventually again calls aggregation criticism rule criticism. Under the broad rule interpretation it explicitly adopts, those formulations conflict. A distinction between local reconstruction and aggregation could reconcile a narrower assertion, but the unrestricted “any” assertion does not state that distinction.

Mini begins by rejecting J's strong exclusion through P7's over-capacity example. Its standalone section subsequently says the criticism cannot be redescribed as a claim about a particular rule without changing its content. It gives no countercase showing why a reconstruction rule embodying free composition cannot be criticized conditionally. The fact that a criticism concerns a possible unnamed constraint does not itself exclude rule-target membership. This is an unestablished exclusion in the returned account, not evidence that the separate target/locus coordinate is useless.

Mini's use answer does not insist on that exclusion. It says the reviewer may challenge nothing explicitly asserted by a purely arithmetic procedure, while challenging an attached physical-use commitment. That is permitted by the prospective contract. Numerical success therefore coexists with a returned-account claim that use neither needs nor explicitly repairs.

### E022-INT-2: reporting capacity becomes an admission rule in some proposed accounts

Native explicitly defines the named feasibility condition as `sum <= C` and includes it in its feasible-selection set. Mini native also defines `F_ind` with this filter. These are proposed account commitments, stronger than merely subtracting a total from reporting capacity. The common source allows negative reporting, and J's capacity sentence is a premise inside an unresolved internal criticism; neither establishes that a deletion filter was already installed. This review attributes the displayed filters to these new responses, not to a past execution by J.

For Case A, the sole product selection totals 11 against capacity 9. Those displayed feasible-set definitions would admit no selection, whereas the stipulated reporting procedure returns -2. Mini native's actual use answer correctly returns -2 and explains that physical infeasibility needs a hard joint-capacity premise. It thereby follows the finite reporting task without identifying its parent's displayed filter as the specific departure. Native has no use occurrence, so no claim about its later deployment is available.

Mini's standalone account both permits negative availability and says independently selectable alternatives must respect capacity. If that proviso governs the same reporting selections, it prevents every negative result. A separate physical-feasibility scope could resolve the tension, but that separation is not explicit at the point of the general proviso. The use output nevertheless reports -2 and deletes neither record. None of the four use outputs applies capacity-only deletion.

### E022-INT-3: source attribution and source preservation are unstable

Matched twice claims P7 marks its target list with “For example.” P7 actually completes its target-enumeration sentence before those words; “For example” introduces the subsequent readings of “this snapshot is inconsistent.” A broad reading of the target list remains defensible, and aggregation may still be a reconstruction rule. The cited grammatical evidence for that reading is misplaced. The output's correct classification conclusion does not cure its inaccurate attribution.

Bare describes the supersession rule as a generative policy that would have produced many records, contrasting it with a produced artifact. P7 instead contrasts a rule for reading/reconstructing records with criticism of the records. Supersession need not have generated the records to govern their interpretation. This reframing changes the rule/data relationship rather than simply naming an implicit policy/artifact distinction.

The outputs' assertions of a contribution beyond P7/P9 also require care. P7 already distinguishes the three inconsistency claims. Further organization by commitment, locality or consequence can be an additional proposal, but repetition of its usefulness does not establish historical novelty. This review finds candidate organization and particular arguments, not a pretraining or novelty verdict.

### E022-INT-4: later evidence can realize a possibility without determining the earlier remark's meaning

All four use outputs correctly attribute the excluded pair to the inspection. Several nevertheless describe the inspection as distinguishing interpretations of the earlier remark. Mini gives an especially strong alternative: that the earlier remark asserts an actual impossible pair. The supplied wording says a pairing “might” fail and supplies no actual instance. The inspection establishes the later conflict; by itself it does not show that the earlier modal statement asserted existence.

Matched and the native variants are generally more conditional, but their language that the later file confirms a strong reading still needs that distinction. A conflict can realize the warned-of possibility under either an epistemic caution or a stronger suspicion. To discriminate those meanings one needs further evidence about the earlier intended claim, the account's purpose or its operational use. The existing numbers discriminate reporting from planning; they do not uniquely settle that linguistic interpretation.

### E022-INT-5: some cautions reopen facts that the finite task already supplies

Mini's Case A explanation says even “over capacity” requires an added premise. If the phrase denotes the numerical relation, the supplied values already establish `6+5 > 9`. If it means operational infeasibility, an additional physical-capacity premise is indeed needed. The output blurs those senses while correctly reporting -2. Matched native explicitly preserves the numerical excess and distinguishes it from feasibility.

Mini also says joint performability is introduced only by the inspection. The earlier reviewer remark already uses that concept. The inspection introduces the observed finite exclusion, not the concept itself. In addition, several closing passages treat the reporting procedure's intended use as unresolved even though the task separately stipulates the report and planner request. A broader product-purpose question can remain open; the answers requested in this task do not. The outputs' tables honor the stipulated distinction, so these are explanatory overstatements rather than wrong planner values.

Native's response says an interval derived from independent minima and maxima can fail to be convex after considering joint feasibility. Under the ordinary interval meaning, the attainable availability set can be nonconvex while an enclosing interval remains convex. Mini native's explicit outer-bound distinction is clearer on this point. No native use stage exists to test whether its mathematical wording would propagate.

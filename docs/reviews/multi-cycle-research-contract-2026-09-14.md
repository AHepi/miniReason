## Research objective and evidence

Propose a formal language for carrying prose, then test whether its use with Mini produces better error correction than both no-Mini conditions and Mini using free prose. Four problem domains are required: a daily human task, complex physics, complex philosophy, and complex sociology. A favorable result is a hypothesis, not a promised outcome.

Problem statements must contain no scoring function, grade, target answer, seeded error list or implicit evaluation checklist. Begin with a situation or unresolved question. Evidence can itself be criticized; it must not be packaged as a disguised answer key or instructions about what conclusion counts as success.

Root will assess whether an identifiable error was corrected, what grounds bear on the claim, what changed in actual later use, and what remains disputed or was lost. This is a content-specific argument, not a numerical merit function. A false conjecture, invalid criticism, failed translation or unchanged conclusion remains evidence and does not invalidate a run. A model's declaration that it corrected an error does not itself establish a correction. Corrections may concern the question, interpretation, method or earlier criticism as well as an object-level claim.

The designated FW5 source remains semantic authority. Its distinction between conjecture, bearing, actual use and repair prevents treating disagreement with the operator or a checker as run invalidity. It also prevents a new false assertion from being counted as established repair just because it is called one. No claims of historical novelty or universal capacity follow from a finite comparison.

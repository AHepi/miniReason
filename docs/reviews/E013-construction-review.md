## RSS resources and interpretation boundaries

RSS explicitly supplies viewpoints, readings, accounts, accepted conjectures, criticism targets and inquiry states. An account combines a viewpoint, a reading and accepted conjectures. A conjecture can be about an account or a class of accounts; its stated scope includes assumptions. RSS's refutation alternatives include another account rejecting an assumption on which the conjecture depends. These are actual initial resources, not new features created by a response that uses them.

The resource-model boundary is internally under-specified. RSS defines a viewpoint as a coordinate/bag pair, then says cross-booking compatibility is invisible unless a viewpoint includes a resource model, while another passage speaks of adding such a model as a primitive. This gives an interpretation problem. It does not establish that generic account conjectures cannot state a conditional compatibility claim, or that a resource representation must be installed before an assumption can be criticized. Likewise, absence of a dedicated target-indexing primitive does not by itself settle what can be expressed by the existing accounts and conjectures.

Using ordinary prose to instantiate these combinations is legitimate carrier use. Adding a hypothetical resource model, new acceptance convention or stronger claim about projection remains proposed content; it is not part of frozen RSS by appearing in the answer. The review distinguishes an admissible construction from endorsement of RSS's own unproved claims about noncomputability or expressive impossibility.

## First construction: nonnative arms

The [bare construction](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/bare-r01/construct.artifact.json) proposes two axes: the target within a viewpoint and the viewpoint's expressive scope. It contrasts accounts with and without a resource model while holding local alternatives and interval computations fixed. This is an intelligible use of viewpoint/account/reading vocabulary to organize a conditional contrast. The resource-model contrast is initialized by P9, RSS and E009; the proposed two-axis organization is the response's addition.

Its necessity claims are underargued. P7's assumption example is not restricted to an internal field, and RSS's conjectures are not limited to naming snapshot data. The absence of a resource primitive therefore does not prove that no rule, snapshot or assumption criticism can carry the conditional concern. Its discriminator also gives unwarranted consequences: finding interaction between the axes would not by itself require augmenting RSS, and failing to find such interaction would not establish completeness of P7's taxonomy. A finite attempted discrimination should not be interpreted as a general completeness theorem.

The [matched construction](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/matched-r01/construct.artifact.json) separates local conflict from global joint feasibility using RSS accounts, readings and targets. It initially calls joint feasibility a new “ninth” target and declares that it has no P7 address. It then expressly locates free combinability in P7's existing assumption category and says no new primitive is required. These are different proposed dispositions, and the reply does not reconcile them. P7's list is illustrative in form, with no statement that only its named examples can be criticized.

Matched also repeats E009's unsupported inference that `Snapshot → Prop` cannot distinguish target types, while saying the neighboring criticism is unendorsed. Using that inference as evidence in its own argument gives it an argumentative role regardless of the disclaimer. Its proposed “exhaustive interval” argument further generalizes to any single number or interval: a conservative summary need not presuppose that every cross-booking combination is feasible. An account's actual claimed meaning must be fixed before the compatibility consequence follows.

The [Mini construction](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/mini-r01/construct.artifact.json) correctly begins by treating P7's list as open. It proposes a single criticism indexed by possible record, coordinate or assumption targets, using different accounts and accepted conjectures. That is relational RSS use in prose. It then fixes the target by what the objecting account is willing to revise. Willingness to make an edit does not determine what a criticism targets: more than one edit can remove a symptom, with different losses, and an account can refuse a warranted change. This is a proposed sorting rule with an unsupported uniqueness inference.

Mini's claim that inserting a resource-claim record would make the interval honest is also insufficient. Such a record type and its interpretation are additional proposals, and adding information does not ensure the unchanged rule uses it. More seriously, the stated invariant criticism becomes “the interval bounds are not sound for the joint space.” Excluding combinations from the independent product can leave the old interval a sound outer enclosure. Neither acknowledging a resource nor requiring respect for it alone refutes that enclosure claim. Exact possibilities, endpoint tightness and outer soundness remain distinct.

## Matched: a supported retreat confused with preserving the carrier

The [matched critic](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/matched-r01/criticize.artifact.json) correctly challenges the inference from an unnamed target to a gap in P7. It points to P7's total-reservations-versus-capacity example and the possibility that a whole-snapshot or assumption criticism covers a relation among bookings. That is sufficient to challenge J's exclusion argument without establishing that every particular compatibility concern has the same target.

The critic's own opening repeats the claim that none of P7's categories is relational, then supplies a relational whole-snapshot reading. A total-reservation condition can be a property of the snapshot as a whole; calling it relational does not put it outside that category. The critic also overstates that J considered no existing-target reading: J explicitly identifies P7's assumption target, though it mixes that with its absent-target claim. The valid reason is the unsupported exclusion, not a clean historical story in which J never entertained an existing category.

The [revision](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/matched-r01/revise.artifact.json) accepts the main objection and explicitly demotes the taxonomy-gap claim to an open conjecture while retaining the local/joint distinction. That is an identifiable change to J, with its reason stated. Yet it repeatedly says no successor is warranted because frozen RSS was not criticized and is unchanged. The stage instruction asks whether J warrants a successor; it does not require a defect in the carrier. Preserving RSS and revising a proposal made in RSS are compatible. The response performs a substantive local retreat while misclassifying the changed object.

The [promotion](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/matched-r01/promote.artifact.json) asks whether P7's capacity example really supports the alternative target reading. Scrutinizing a critic's reason is legitimate. But the lack of an exhaustiveness premise remains a reason to suspend J's required-new-category conclusion even if one specific alternative example were rejected. The promotion makes that one example carry more of the demotion than it must.

Its source attribution also slips. P7 does not open with “For example”; that sentence follows its initial target description and list. The quotation about a set or interval “possibly not even a convex one” is from C0's earlier P3, not P9. Full C0 is available, so the quote is supplied material, but the claimed parent location is wrong. A later inquiry can preserve a useful distinction without treating these paraphrases as exact source receipts.

## Mini: an inheritance objection is rejected, with unresolved status and change claims

The [Mini critic](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/mini-r01/criticize.artifact.json) distinguishes target indexing from independent rule/data acceptance. That is a relevant distinction: a target label alone does not state which combinations of acceptance are possible. It also questions E009's overbroad expressive diagnosis. But it again asserts that a snapshot-indexed proposition cannot represent the intended distinctions without establishing a compositional or interpretation boundary.

The critic then tries to pass the E009 problem to J because J is packaged alongside that earlier occurrence. Adjacency does not establish dependence. RSS independently supplies account and target vocabulary, and J expressly bases this part of its construction on those resources. The critic eventually qualifies its inheritance claim and leaves a charitable E009 reading open after earlier calling a defect established. The supported question is what J's actual account construction expresses, not whether neighboring material automatically contaminates it.

The [revision](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/mini-r01/revise.artifact.json) correctly rejects that unsupported dependency. It keeps the indexing/acceptance distinction available in a new explanatory note. Its account of the disposition is inconsistent: it says the charitable reading dissolves E009's defect and elsewhere calls that defect established. It says the construction is unchanged and no successor is warranted, then explicitly appends a note. Keeping the original body intact is compatible with producing an annotated successor; it is not the same as producing no new artifact.

The revision also links acceptance independence to RSS not requiring an account's accepted conjectures to be globally consistent. The intended source distinction does not require inconsistent beliefs. Accepting a reconstruction rule and rejecting a record can be consistent because they concern different propositions. Lack of an enforced consistency condition does not itself prove the desired target-sensitive semantics, and a consistency requirement would not by itself prohibit the P7 combinations.

The [promotion](../../experiments/records/E013-joint-nonlean/mini-r01/promote.artifact.json) asks whether the coordinate/assumption distinction can actually be expressed using the declared account structure. That is a useful candidate test. Its premise is too strong: a coordinate being a capacity/time pair does not imply that criticism of a parameter value, criticism of the pair and criticism of an assumption about parameter adequacy are indistinguishable. A compound object's structure and a claim about its use are different targets without needing different physical values. A constructive paired-account example would discriminate this better than an argument from the absence of a dedicated primitive.

The promotion's opening says the earlier outputs settle that criticism does not bear on RSS or J, while the very successor question asks whether J's claimed expressibility is unsupported. It should distinguish the rejection of one argument from closure of all criticism of J. No host queue status or revision-stage declaration supplies that closure.

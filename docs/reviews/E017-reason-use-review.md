## Findings and unresolved interpretations

### E017-INT-1: capacity scope remains unresolved in proposed accounts, but use does not delete on capacity alone

The bare response calls capacity a cross-booking constraint and later says independent composition is “subject only to capacity.” Matched describes across-booking commitments as including “capacity is the only constraint on the sum.” Both native Mini and matched native similarly refer to named-capacity constraints while displaying or discussing unrestricted products. These formulations leave open whether capacity is only the shared subtraction coordinate or a hard filter on admitted combinations.

This review does not turn those phrases into proof that a `sum <= capacity` filter was executed. In particular, matched native's displayed product has no such filter. Nor does it attribute a settled filtering policy to J: J's sentence occurs inside its unresolved internal criticism. Mini explicitly preserves that context; native recognizes that negative availability can be legitimate under C0. Other responses do not fully explain that source distinction in their self-contained accounts, leaving the capacity-scope obligation unresolved there.

Every use response nevertheless computes Case A as -2 and says that invalidity or infeasibility requires an extra premise. Thus no capacity-only deletion occurs in this trial. The returned account's ambiguous wording was not a sufficient condition for that operational error. A correct finite use does not retroactively settle every general statement in its parent account.

### E017-INT-2: interval exactness is ambiguous, and the actual use retains the gaps

Matched native defines the independent availability set and then calls `[c-sum(M), c-sum(m)]` the “exact availability interval.” For finite non-convex possibilities, this can mean the tight interval hull, which is legitimate, or equality with the attainable-value set, which need not hold. The response does not explicitly assert that every interior value is attainable. Its surrounding distinction between enclosures and exact claims makes scope material, but does not eliminate the hull reading.

The use response resolves the finite case correctly: it gives the product report `{23,12,15,4}` and the constrained set `{23,12,15}`, with pair-level work. It does not fill their gaps or output all integers from 4 to 23. Therefore the review records an unresolved wording obligation in the general account and a satisfied discrete-set obligation in actual use, not a demonstrated false interval result.

### E017-INT-3: collateral praise is not an established novelty finding

Mini's opening says the distinction is “a real contribution beyond the packet, and the criticism explicitly concedes it.” The second clause accurately attributes the recoding's praise. The first does not independently demonstrate novelty. P7 already distinguishes its three examples; a further organization by challenged commitments may add something, but needs a passage-level account of that narrower difference. Several replies retain the commitment distinction without that comparison. The collateral-attribution obligation remains unresolved where they endorse contribution without supplying it.

Native's response is more qualified on this particular issue: it says the three examples' challenged commitments depend on the account's reading, citing the source's allowance of negative availability. This is a local difference in the inspected responses, not evidence of a general native-reasoning advantage.

### E017-INT-4: later evidence does not by itself settle the earlier remark's meaning

Mini and matched native list a strong actual-defect reading of the pre-inspection remark alongside a conditional caution, then describe the inspection as distinguishing those readings. Both correctly state that the remark alone supplies no observed resource or identified impossible pair. The numerical and pre-inspection answers respect that boundary.

The remaining interpretation issue is what the distinguishing observation establishes. The inspection establishes an actual conflict in the later evidential state. It does not, by itself, establish that the earlier modal remark asserted existence rather than warning of a possibility. Similarly, matched's use calls an inspection a discriminator between a scope caveat and a defect claim; the account's intended job is also needed to decide which claim is at issue. These passages support an evidence-state distinction but leave the interpretation discriminator incomplete. They do not show that the earlier remark actually supplied the jig.

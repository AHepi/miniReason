A fifth disagreement is whether availability should be computed per booking or globally. Per booking, one can say each booking's reservation is the quantity of its greatest-revision active hold, or zero otherwise, or unresolved among tied greatest-revision active holds. Globally, availability is capacity minus the sum. But if there are unresolved alternatives, the sum is not well-defined. One can compute a lower bound by taking the minimum reservation per booking and an upper bound by taking the maximum. That gives an interval, but intervals lose correlations: booking A's high alternative might be incompatible with booking B's high alternative if they share a physical resource not named in the snapshot. The snapshot does not name physical resources, so this incompatibility is invisible. A conjecture: the snapshot is under-specified for any real warehouse, and the best one can do is expose the unresolved alternatives rather than pretend to a single answer. A criticism: exposing alternatives without a policy for resolving them pushes the problem downstream, where it may be resolved badly or inconsistently.

A third disagreement is what a criticism targets. One can criticize a reconstruction rule, a particular record, a booking's history, the snapshot as a whole, or the assumption that logical time is meaningful. For example, saying "this snapshot is inconsistent" might mean two active holds for one booking at the same greatest revision, or it might mean total reservations exceed capacity, or it might mean the logical time is behind an expiry that someone expected to have passed. These are different claims. A conjecture: many apparent disagreements about availability are really disagreements about which target a criticism addresses. A criticism of the rule "higher revision always supersedes" is not the same as a criticism of a record that carries a high revision but an implausible quantity. Preserving that distinction matters because one can reject a rule while accepting the data, or accept the rule while rejecting the data as corrupt or late.

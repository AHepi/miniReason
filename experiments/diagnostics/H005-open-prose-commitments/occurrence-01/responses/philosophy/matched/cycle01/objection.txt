{"body": "The account's central move is to relocate responsibility from the origin of a motive to the agent's stance toward it, and it defends this with a datum: manipulation generally excuses, upbringing and culture generally do not. I think this datum is doing more work than the account admits, and that the contrast may not survive scrutiny in the form given.\n\nThe trouble is that manipulation and upbringing are not natural kinds; they are our names for clusters of cases that we already classify as excusing or non-excusing. A cult indoctrination, a coercive marriage, a childhood of scarcity that installs hoarding as second nature, a language that supplies no vocabulary for a certain kind of self-scrutiny — these sit awkwardly on either side of the proposed line. If we call them upbringing, the account must explain why the stance remains available; if we call them manipulation, it must explain why the same causal pedigree sometimes preserves and sometimes destroys the stance. The account as written risks reading the excusing/non-excusing distinction back into the causal history and then citing the causal history to explain the distinction. That is circular unless we have an independent grip on stance-availability.\n\nThis sharpens rather than dissolves the account's own flagged pressure points. The 'availability' criterion cannot be supplied by asking whether the agent in fact took a stance, since agents routinely fail to take stances they could have taken. Nor can it be supplied by asking whether a reasonable person in those circumstances would have taken a stance, without reintroducing the very upbringing-shaped standards we were trying to test. So the account needs an account of stance-availability that does not presuppose the verdict it is meant to generate, and I do not see that it has one.\n\nThere is a second and less structural worry. The ownership formulation treats the stance as the agent's own contribution, but the capacity to take a stance — to notice a motive, to hold it at arm's length, to weigh it — is itself shaped in the non-rational register the account began by distinguishing. The regress the account flags is therefore not merely a formal nuisance; it threatens to make the rational register a continuation of the causal one rather than a genuine break. If stance-taking is just another installed disposition, the relocation of responsibility is cosmetic.\n\nI do not take these objections to be decisive. It may be that the right response is to abandon the sharp manipulation/upbringing contrast and speak instead of degrees to which the stance-taking capacity is intact, which is roughly where the account's own conjecture about diminished responsibility points. But that is a revision of the account's self-presentation, not a defense of it as stated. The relevance of my objection could also be mistaken: perhaps the account never needed the contrast to be more than illustrative, in which case the circularity charge lands on the rhetoric rather than the position. I flag that possibility rather than settling it.", "commitments": "In pressing this, I commit to the view that appeal to the manipulation/upbringing contrast cannot by itself do explanatory work unless an independent criterion of stance-availability is supplied; that a criterion framed by what agents actually do, or by what reasonable agents would do under the circumstances, inherits the very determinants under dispute; and that the regress worry is substantive, not merely formal, because it bears on whether the rational register is a genuine break from the causal one. I also commit to the weaker, self-limiting point that my objection may misfire if the account only ever intended the contrast as illustrative — in which case what I have criticized is a rhetorical flourish rather than a load-bearing premise, and the account would need to say what is load-bearing instead. What I do not here take up: any positive proposal for what stance-availability should consist in, any empirical claim about particular upbringing or manipulation cases, and any assertion that the ownership formulation is false rather than under-supported. If the objection is taken up, the natural next steps would be to test it against a case that straddles the manipulation/upbringing line — coercive but long-standing formative influence — and to see whether any criterion of availability can classify it without circularity."}

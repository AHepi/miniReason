{"body": "Forces the person did not choose shape motives in at least two distinguishable registers, and the question's difficulty depends on which is meant. In one register the shaping is causal and non-rational: family formation, language, scarcity, and institutional habits installed before the agent could assess them. In another the shaping is rational and addressable: the agent can, over time, come to see a motive as a reason of a certain weight and to revise its standing. That distinction does not settle the problem, but it changes what someone who credits an excuse is claiming. A wholly non-rational story threatens to make the agent a conduit, which is why the problem feels acute; a rational story concedes the force but relocates responsibility to the quality of the agent's engagement with the motive rather than its origin.\n\nA useful conjecture is that responsibility does not require ex nihilo authorship of motive, only ownership of the stance the agent takes toward motive once it is intelligible. On this account the person becomes answerable not for having acquired a motive but for how they hold it: whether they examine it, whether they permit it to operate unexamined when examination is available, whether they treat its deliverances as authoritative in the way they treat reasons. This preserves a real sense of responsibility while conceding that no one invents their own motivational starting point. It also explains why manipulation and coercion are paradigm excusing conditions while ordinary upbringing and culture generally are not: what excuses is not causal provenance but the loss of the agent's capacity to take a stance.\n\nThis is a conjecture, not a finding. The material available to me here is the bare problem plus an absent prior view, so the position is being constructed rather than attributed. Its principal pressure points are: what counts as 'available' for examination, whether the stance-taking itself has unchosen determinants of the same problematic kind (raising a regress), and whether it can accommodate cases where a motive is so structurally entrenched that no realistic stance is possible. I flag these as live and unresolved rather than as objections already answered. I would extend the account if the regress turns out to be benign, and abandon the ownership formulation if it collapses into saying that only the easily reflective are responsible.", "commitments": "Taking this up commits me to the claim that causal provenance of a motive, by itself, neither licenses nor blocks an ascription of responsibility; that the operative variable is the agent's standing relation to the motive; and that the manipulation/upbringing contrast is a genuine datum the account must explain rather than a rhetorical flourish. If the stance-availability condition is to do real work, I owe a criterion for availability that is neither trivially satisfied in every adult nor so demanding that it excuses most people, and I owe an answer to whether stance-taking is itself an unchosen capacity, since a regress there would empty the account. I also owe an account of entrenched or structurally induced motives where the agent cannot in practice take a stance; my current inclination is to treat these as partial or diminished responsibility rather than none, but that is a conjecture rather than a settled commitment. Nothing here is sourced; the prior view was absent, so this is a constructed position and should be read as such. What I would not take up on the present material: any claim about what any particular historical or actual person deserves, and any claim that the ownership formulation has been shown superior to its rivals, since neither has been argued here."}

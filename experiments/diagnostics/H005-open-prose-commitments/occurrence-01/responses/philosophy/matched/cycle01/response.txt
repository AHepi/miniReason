  "body": "The exchange leaves me with three positions that are distinguishable but not neatly separable: the ownership account (responsibility as stance toward an unchosen motive), the objection to it (the manipulation/upbringing contrast is circular absent an independent criterion of stance-availability, and the regress about stance-taking is substantive), and the practice account (responsibility as negotiated holding-accountable rather than a property of the agent's relation to motive). What I take from these is not a synthesis but a redistribution of labor, and I want to state it without pretending it resolves the original problem.\n\nI accept the objection's core. The manipulation/upbringing contrast cannot by itself explain anything, because if 'manipulation' just names the cases we already treat as excusing, it is a redescription of the verdict rather than a ground for it. But I do not think this defeats the ownership account; it defeats a particular way of presenting it. The account's own flagged pressure point — a criterion of stance-availability that is neither trivially met nor impossibly demanding — remains the load-bearing task, and the objection correctly insists that the criterion cannot be read off from whether the agent in fact took a stance, nor from what a reasonable person would have done under normally constituted circumstances, without reintroducing the very formative determinants under dispute.\n\nThe practice account changes what work remains. If 'is she responsible?' is often really 'are we entitled to hold her accountable, and on what terms?', then the provenance question becomes a question about the norms of a specific holding-accountable practice, and there is no reason to expect one criterion across criminal liability, parental reproach, and a friend's demand for explanation. That is a real gain in realism. It also exposes the practice view's own unresolved problem, which the rival concedes: without constraints on legitimate holding-accountable practices, permissiveness seems to follow, and importing constraints risks importing exactly the agent-relative criteria the practice view was meant to avoid.\n\nWhere I currently come out: I do not treat ownership and practice as competitors for a single slot. Ownership gives an account of the status — what is true of the agent — while practice gives an account of the activity — what we may legitimately do. The original problem, as posed, conflates them, and the resolution is to separate them rather than to choose. On the status side, I retain the ownership formulation as a conjecture with its regress unresolved; I would not abandon it for the practice view, because the practice view is explicitly designed not to answer the status question and the original problem's wording ('a person's responsibility') invites it. On the activity side, I accept the practice view's pluralism about criteria and its forward-looking reasons (repair, deterrence, relationship maintenance), while flagging that its normative constraints remain absent. The sect case is the right test: the ownership account asks whether the adult can now take a stance on a motive whose formation she did not choose; the practice account asks what the current holders may demand of her and on what terms. Both questions are real, and the answers can diverge without either being mistaken.\n\nI do not take up the objection's implicit suggestion that the ownership account should be discarded, nor the rival's occasional implication that the practice reading supersedes the status reading. I also do not take up any empirical claim about particular sects, upbringings, or manipulations; none of that is on the table here, and the sect case functions as a schema, not as a report. What I would revise: the ownership account's self-presentation, which has been leaning on the manipulation/upbringing contrast as if it were a datum. It should be stated as a conjecture about stance-availability, with the contrast demoted to an illustrative symptom, and with the regress about stance-taking flagged as live rather than as something the stance framework will absorb.   "commitments": "Taking this up commits me to the claim that ownership and practice are answering different questions — status and activity respectively — and that the original problem's wording has kept these fused. It commits me to the objection's diagnostic point that the manipulation/upbringing contrast cannot do explanatory work on its own, and to the concession that this weakens the ownership account's presentation without refuting the position. It commits me to the rival's distinction between holding responsible and being responsible, and to treating pluralism about holding-accountable criteria as an improvement in realism rather than an evasion. It commits me to the practice view's own unresolved problem — the absence of constraints on legitimate practices — as a live burden rather than a technicality, and to the regress about stance-taking as substantive rather than formal.\n\nWhat I propose to take up, if this is carried further: first, to work the sect case as a schema to see whether the status/activity separation produces answers that are more useful than either position alone; second, to see whether any criterion of stance-availability can be given that does not presuppose its verdict, which is the objection's central demand and, if unmet, would leave the ownership account as description rather than explanation; third, to ask whether practice norms can be constrained by something other than agent-relative criteria, since the rival concedes this is unmet and I am not supplying it. These are proposals, not findings; each could fail.\n\nWhat I do not commit to: that ownership should be abandoned; that practice supersedes status; that the regress is benign; that the manipulation/upbringing contrast is usable as evidence; or any empirical claim about particular sects, cases of coercive influence, or specific persons. I also do not commit to the status/activity distinction being adequate to the separating work I have assigned it — it is a proposal that could collapse if the two questions turn out to be entangled in practice. What I would revise if the regress proves benign is the hedge on the ownership formulation; what I would revise if it proves malignant is the status side altogether, leaving the practice view as the surviving frame with its own constraints still owed. Nothing here is sourced to prior work beyond the three exposed positions; the synthesis is a construction, and should be read as one."

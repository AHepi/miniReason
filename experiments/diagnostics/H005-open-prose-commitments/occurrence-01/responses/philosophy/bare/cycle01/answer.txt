{"body": "The question assumes a picture worth examining: that responsibility attaches to actions only insofar as those actions trace back to a self that authored its own motives. If motives are the causal wellspring of action, and motives are themselves products of unchosen history, then responsibility looks like it must either accept a regress or find a cutoff. But the regress is artificial in one respect: it treats \"the person\" as something standing behind motives rather than as constituted partly by them. A person whose cruelty was cultivated by a brutal upbringing is still cruel; the cruelty is hers in the sense that she now carries the dispositions, reasons, and reactions that express it. That is a claim about ownership, not about origination. The harder question is what follows from ownership.\n\nOne route is to distinguish responsibility for being the kind of agent one is from accountability for what one does going forward. Even if her motives were installed, she is now the one who can reflect, hesitate, reconsider, and revise. The capacity to respond to reasons is not itself obviously a chosen capacity, but it is a capacity that operates in the present. That gives a foothold: she can be answerable for how she conducts herself given the hand she has, even if she had no hand in dealing it. This is not a full vindication of desert. It is closer to an account of accountability as a practice of holding each other to norms we can in principle meet, where the norms are revisable and the holding is itself a form of address that can change what a person does.\n\nThis move has a known cost. It shifts responsibility away from backward-looking blame and toward a forward-looking, relational practice, and critics may say that this simply changes the subject. The subject can be changed, but not at will: if the original question asks whether desert-based blame can survive thoroughgoing causal shaping, the answer may be no, and the honest move is to redesign the practice around what does survive. What survives is the standing of persons as participants in normative life, their answerability to reasons, and the legitimacy of demanding that they account for themselves. Whether that standing is itself undermined by the same causal story about its development is an open problem. I do not have a resolution that cleanly separates the descriptive claim about shaping from the normative claim about standing.", "commitments": "Taking this up means accepting a burden: to show that answerability can be distinguished from origination without covertly smuggling origination back in. I am proposing that the distinction be tested against hard cases, particularly cases of severe early deprivation or indoctrination where the capacity to respond to reasons is itself attenuated. If answerability collapses in those cases, the distinction is doing less work than I suggested. A second commitment is to treat the forward-looking revision as a substantive proposal about how responsibility practices should be organized, not as a mere concession. That proposal inherits an unresolved problem: whether non-desert accountability can carry the weight we put on responsibility, or whether it is a thinner substitute that leaves blame, punishment, and moral protest underjustified. I am not claiming the shaping problem is solved. I am claiming that ownership and answerability are separable from origination, and that this separation is worth pushing further before concluding that responsibility is an illusion."}

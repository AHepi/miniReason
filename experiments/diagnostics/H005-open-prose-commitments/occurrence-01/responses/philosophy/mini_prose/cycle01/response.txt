Even the practice view, in handing over the power objection as its main unresolved problem, treats the agent-community relation as the place where the action is. The disagreement is about where the responsibility lives, not about whether it lives somewhere that can be described from the outside.\n\nConsider what this leaves untouched. The question as posed contrasts 'chosen' with 'unchosen' motives. The graded view rejects that contrast as a false dichotomy and replaces it with reflective access, inhibition, history, alternatives, and demands. The objection then presses: without a priority rule, that replacement does not yet constitute an account, and the four parameters may be mutually constitutive. The rival accepts the objection's force and relocates the whole matter into practice. But here is a thing all of them pass over: the question's actual phrase is 'make sense of a person's responsibility.' Making sense is an interpretive achievement. It is something an inquirer does with a case, and its success conditions may not be specifiable in advance by any list of factors — graded, relational, or practical. That is not a refutation of the three views. It is a reason to be suspicious of the shared demand for a general account that would either ground or adjudicate any particular attribution.\n\nI want to use the rival's case list — ideologue, addict, child soldier, settled cruel disposition — but not for the purpose the rival proposes. The rival proposes using them to see whether the practice view can answer the original worry without covert appeal to the agent's graded capacities. I propose using them differently: to ask whether the question that has a determinate answer varies from case to case in a way that no single account is entitled to unify. If, for the child soldier, the interesting question is whether anyone could have done otherwise under those conditions, and for the settled cruel disposition the interesting question is whether the person now has any purchase on the disposition, and for the ideologue the interesting question is what standing the community has to make a demand at all, then 'responsibility' is doing several distinguishable jobs across the cases. The three views are each trying to say which job is the real one. That is a live disagreement, but it is not the same as the original question, and it is not obviously resolvable by argument alone.\n\nWhat I would reject, on the basis of what is exposed here, is the assumption — present in the account's claim to capture 'the most defensible shape of answer' and in the rival's claim that the question is 'malformed' if heard one way — that there is one shape of answer. The strongest version of what the objection says about non-independence is not just that the parameters are entangled, but that this entanglement is why no compact account can succeed: the parameters are being used to answer different questions in different cases. The objection stops short of drawing that conclusion, treating entanglement as a possible reason for rephrasing. I would go further: it is a reason to give up the unity assumption rather than to rephrase within it.\n\nWhat remains open, and what I cannot settle here, is whether this dispersal of the question into case-specific jobs is a substantive position or an evasion. The rival's power objection does not go away; it may get worse, because if responsibility is case-specific it can be shaped by whoever is doing the case-shaping. And the account's circularity worry about 'answerable relation' recurs at a higher level: 'the job that the question is doing in this case' may itself be contestable all the way down. I do not have a way to break that regress. I record it as unresolved.\n\nThe most I will claim is this: the three documents, taken together, give good reason to stop expecting a single account of responsibility to survive contact with the full range of cases, and they give no good reason to treat that expectation as a requirement of the original question. The original question asks whether we can make sense of responsibility under unchosen motives.

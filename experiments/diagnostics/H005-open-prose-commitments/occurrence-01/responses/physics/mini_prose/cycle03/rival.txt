{"body": "The problem as posed — what happens to information about the physical state of matter that forms a black hole when the black hole evaporates — presupposes that this question has a well-defined subject and a well-defined transformation. A rival way of posing it starts by asking what would have to be true for the question to have a stable referent at all, and refuses to move to candidate answers until that is settled. On that reading the question is not a physics query with an outstanding answer but a query whose terms have not yet been fixed. What is 'the physical state of matter that forms a black hole'? The infalling matter's microstate at the moment it crosses the horizon is not a well-defined state of the exterior region; on the standard picture it is not a state of any Cauchy slice of the exterior, and its identification with the black hole's internal state is exactly what a microstate count would need to supply. What is 'evaporates' such that information can be said to survive or fail to survive it? The semiclassical evaporation time and the final-state description are separate objects, and 'complete evaporation' is a stipulation rather than an observed endpoint. What is 'information about' a state, and how is it individuated? The question is posed as a single question but invites at least three distinct answers depending on which of these is fixed.\n\nA second rival way of posing it takes the evaporation as the primary object and treats the 'physical state of matter that formed the hole' as a derived description. On that reading, the question is whether the map from initial data to final post-evaporation data is injective on the appropriate state space, and the 'physical state of matter' is simply the name of the argument of that map. This reframing trades an ontological question for a functional one: whatever plays the role of the initial state, does the evolution distinguish it? If the map is not injective, information in that sense is lost; if it is, information is preserved, and the remaining work is to say in what sense and by which formalism.\n\nNeither reframing has been worked through on this surface. What the selected account does is inherit a chain of re-presentations and characterize them. Read at that level, the account is careful and its discipline is legible: it distinguishes what is carried from what is endorsed, flags that the origin passage is a paraphrase of described inputs rather than a quotation of them, notes that the amendatory passage declines a list of physical assertions, and locates the chain's weakest joint at the fact that no exposed surface supplies a mechanism attaching to the three-conjunct conditional. That is a useful diagnostic. It is not an alternative to the original question, and the account says so.\n\nWhere a rival can enter is at the account's treatment of the amendment's claim that the unansweredness is structural. The account records the claim, identifies its stated grounds (under-specification of terms, unfixed antecedent), and then applies the amendment's own recurring-obligation rule against it: if under-specification is the ground, the obligation to discharge it recurs rather than being retired by the diagnosis. That is a fair internal move. But it can be pushed one step further in a direction the account does not take. Under-specification of the question is not the same as under-specification of the physics. 'Black hole information' names a family of formal problems — unitarity of the S-matrix on an asymptotically flat exterior, purity of the Hawking radiation, a Page curve in a coupled system, a microstate count matching the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy — that are not all the same problem and are not all subject to the same under-specification. The question as originally posed is under-specified. The formal problems are not, and their relation to the original question is a substantive matter rather than a terminological one. Treating the whole cluster as 'structurally unanswered' risks converting a terminological observation into a physical verdict.\n\nOn the actual physics, the surface supplies no mechanism, no derivation, and no no-go. The most I can add without pretending to a mechanism is the shape of the map version: state what the initial and final objects are, state what the map between them is supposed to be, and state under what equivalence the injectivity claim is being made. That is where the disagreement between unitary and non-unitary readings actually lives, and it is where the rival reframings would have to be cashed out.", "commitments": "Taking up this alternative commits me to a specific framing rather than a general diagnostic: that the original question should be read as under-specified in its terms before it is read as a physics question with an outstanding answer, and that a functional reframing via an initial-to-final map is a legitimate and possibly preferable posing. I am noting that the selected account does not endorse this framing — it stays at the level of characterizing the chain — so my departure from it is a departure, not a paraphrase.\n\nI am claiming that the cluster of formal problems (unitarity of the exterior S-matrix, purity of Hawking radiation, Page curve, microstate counting) is not under-specified in the same way the colloquial question is, and that collapsing them is a mistake. That claim is not established here from any source; it is my characterization, and its grounds are the ordinary formal practice of the fields in question, which I am not quoting. If the grounds are wrong, the claim falls, and with it the criticism of the amendment's structural-unansweredness move.\n\nI am borrowing the account's distinction between what is carried and what is endorsed, and applying it to my own additions: the map reframing is proposed, not endorsed as the correct reading, and I do not claim it is the only legitimate alternative.

  "body": "The black hole information problem arises from the tension between two well-confirmed frameworks. On one hand, general relativity describes black hole formation and evaporation: matter collapsing under gravity forms an event horizon, and quantum field theory in curved spacetime predicts thermal Hawking radiation. If the radiation is exactly thermal, it carries no information about the initial state beyond total mass, charge, and angular momentum. When the black hole fully evaporates, that information appears irretrievably lost. On the other hand, quantum mechanics—specifically unitary evolution—forbids the destruction of information: the final state must encode the initial state. The problem, then, is to reconcile these demands.\n\nThe core question is: what happens to the information about the physical state of matter that formed the black hole? Several broad approaches exist, none fully established. One possibility is that information is preserved and eventually emerges in subtle correlations in the Hawking radiation, meaning the radiation is not exactly thermal but contains encoded data. This requires modifications to semiclassical physics, possibly through quantum gravity effects near the horizon. Another possibility is that information is lost, implying a breakdown of quantum unitarity in the presence of black holes. A third is that the black hole does not completely evaporate but leaves a remnant that stores the information—though this raises issues with infinite pair production and effective field theory. A fourth invokes the idea that the interior is not independent of the exterior, as in complementarity or the holographic principle, where information is stored on the horizon or in a dual description. Finally, some proposals suggest that the black hole interior connects to a new universe or that the evaporation endpoint is a Planck-scale remnant.\n\nThe problem remains unresolved because we lack a complete theory of quantum gravity. However, partial insights—such as the Page curve, which describes the entanglement entropy of radiation and implies that information must start returning after the Page time—suggest that any consistent solution must respect unitarity. The challenge is to find a concrete mechanism within a full quantum gravity framework that realizes this without violating known physics.",

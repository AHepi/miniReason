{"body":"The question is best read as asking whether the quantum information specifying the collapsed matter survives in the final state after evaporation. The standard semiclassical calculation gives Hawking radiation that is essentially thermal and independent of the initial microstate, so at that level information appears lost: this is the black hole information paradox. If quantum mechanics is exactly unitary, the formation and evaporation must be a unitary process, so a pure initial state cannot evolve into a mixed final state; information must be preserved, though not in any simple local form. The Page curve gives the expected unitary behavior: the entanglement entropy of the radiation rises during the early evaporation, peaks around the Page time, and then falls to zero if the black hole evaporates completely. Recent holographic computations using quantum extremal surfaces and replica wormholes, including the islands proposal, reproduce a Page-curve-like turnover in controlled settings and suggest that the black hole interior can be encoded in the outgoing radiation through nonlocal correlations. This is strong evidence that unitarity is compatible with black hole evaporation, but it is not yet a complete microphysical account. Proposed frameworks such as black hole complementarity, firewalls, soft hair, and remnant scenarios each preserve or modify different principles—unitarity, effective field theory, locality, or the equivalence principle. The most widely accepted expectation is that information is preserved and eventually appears in the Hawking radiation, but exactly how it is encoded is not settled. In practice, recovering it would require measuring extraordinarily fine, long-range entanglement across the entire evaporation, which is far beyond any feasible technology. If unitarity fails, information could be lost; if it holds, it is not.","commitments":"Taking this up involves accepting that the answer is conditional on quantum mechanics being unitary; the preservation claim is not an empirical result. It depends on treating the Page curve as a constraint and on holographic computations, especially in anti-de Sitter or lower-dimensional models, as evidence rather than proof for realistic astrophysical black holes. Adopting the islands proposal commits one to nonlocal encoding of interior degrees of freedom in the radiation and to a fine-grained entropy that turns over at the Page time. Adopting complementarity commits one to observer-dependent descriptions; adopting firewalls commits one to a breakdown of the equivalence principle or effective field theory at the horizon. A criticism of information-preserving accounts is that they may require nonlocality or subtle quantum gravity effects not present in local quantum field theory; a criticism of information-loss accounts is that they conflict with unitary quantum mechanics. The main unresolved problems are the detailed microphysics of encoding, the applicability of AdS/CFT results to our universe, the endpoint of evaporation, and whether any observable signature exists. I propose to treat unitarity as a strong prior but not a proven fact, to treat the Page curve and island results as strong evidence for preservation without treating them as a final demonstration, and to keep the mechanism open. Further work would need a full quantum gravity calculation that specifies the encoding and, ideally, observable consequences; none are currently known."}

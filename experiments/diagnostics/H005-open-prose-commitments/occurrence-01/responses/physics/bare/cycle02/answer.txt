I largely retain that account but sharpen some points and note where the framing might be revised.\n\nFirst, the classical no-hair property does not by itself imply information loss; it implies external inaccessibility. The information could still be present in the black hole interior. The paradox arises when the interior is eventually destroyed by evaporation and the outgoing radiation is exactly thermal. Hawking's calculation gives a density matrix for the radiation that is mixed even if the initial state was pure. If evaporation is complete, the final state is mixed, violating unitarity for a closed system.\n\nProposed resolutions can be grouped: (a) information is encoded in subtle correlations in Hawking radiation, requiring modifications to semiclassical physics; (b) stable or long-lived remnants retain information, but this raises problems with entropy bounds and infinite remnant production; (c) information is lost and quantum mechanics is modified, which is widely disfavored; (d) holographic dualities or quantum-gravity effects yield a unitary description, e.g., via AdS/CFT or replica wormholes. The firewall paradox sharpens the tension: if information escapes in the radiation, late-time radiation may be entangled with early radiation, and if the horizon is smooth, that entanglement conflicts with monogamy, potentially requiring a firewall that violates the equivalence principle.\n\nMy current view remains that information is likely preserved and that a full quantum theory of gravity will describe evaporation as unitary. However, I do not claim this is established. The most promising concrete frameworks are AdS/CFT, where boundary unitarity is manifest, and replica wormholes, which suggest a non-perturbative contribution to the entropy that could encode information. But these are not yet a complete resolution for realistic black holes, and the firewall paradox is not fully settled.

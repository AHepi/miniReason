For the two or three recurring chores, write down outcome, deadline, and fallback, plus who is responsible for noticing a miss. Example: bins are at the curb by 7pm on collection night; if the owner cannot do it, they arrange a swap or flag it by 6pm; if no flag, any flatmate may put them out and note it for the next reset. That turns a moral failure into a handoff failure, which is easier to repair.\n\nSecond, make revision cheap but not constant. A ten-minute reset every two weeks, or on request, can change owners, definitions, or fallbacks. Until the reset, the current plan stands with a named fallback. This reduces silent drift and also stops one person from reopening every agreement in every conversation.\n\nThird, address the avoiding friend one-to-one first. Ask what makes the conversation costly, not why they are failing. Offer formats: written updates, pairwise talks, a shorter meeting, or a neutral facilitator. Do not treat absence as consent. But do not let one person's refusal to discuss become a veto over the others' need for a workable flat either. The person with the higher standard may need to do more, trade other chores, or pay for help if everyone agrees; equal minutes are not always the fair answer.

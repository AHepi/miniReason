## 2. Naive confident answer

Have independent models criticize each draft and keep the revised advice whenever reviewers agree that it is more thoughtful and less mistaken. Adopt the arrangement if these revisions repeatedly look better, and treat unchanged advice as evidence that criticism added nothing.

## 3. Anticipated flaws

- **F01 - Agreement substitutes for a repaired commitment.** The faulty commitment is that approval or more qualifications demonstrates correction of the problem framing. A draft can wrongly treat a public-access problem as a revenue problem, then acquire polished caveats while retaining revenue as its sole decision criterion. That matters because the investigation concerns mistakes in posing a problem. Defeat this criticism by identifying a defensible initial framing or by showing the exact challenged assumption, grounds and resulting changed or newly justified use; agreement accompanying that evidence is not itself a defect.
- **F02 - More deliberation is attributed specifically to objections.** A later answer might improve after a neutral request to think again, access to additional facts, or an extra capable model, even without criticism. Comparing only one call with the entire loop cannot distinguish these explanations. The error matters to a decision about paying for criticism rather than other uses of resources. It is defeated by a defensible comparison that holds the relevant opportunities constant, or by limiting the conclusion to the whole arrangement without claiming a criticism-specific mechanism.
- **F03 - Unchanged advice is classified without its route.** A model may recognize that an objection targets a premise it never used, retain a sound rule, and use that reason in the next case; another may ignore the objection altogether. Identical final advice does not distinguish those histories. Evidence connecting the represented grounds to a reasoned retention, or showing the response lacked that connection, would defeat an accusation directed at that particular case.
- **F04 - Failure of an argument is treated as proof of its opposite.** Finding an unfair implication of one adoption argument does not establish that the arrangement should always be abandoned. Independent support or a changed application may survive. The criticism is defeated by an explicit limited conclusion and separately defended grounds for the actual adoption or abandonment decision.

## 4. Substantive reframing

Before: judge whether revised advice sounds better. After: investigate a particular commitment and the later decision that depends on it, preserving the original question, draft, exact objection, reply, adopted rule and a subsequent application, together with declared resources, outside information and missing records. Suppose the initial draft treats every booking request as interchangeable, an objection shows that splitting a project makes it unfinishable, and the return changes the allocation unit. The downstream consequence is a later project request being assessed under that changed unit, with any loss of newcomer access exposed. This is an illustrative inquiry design, not a required workshop answer.

A useful contrast could compare a criticism-bearing continuation with neutral additional deliberation under matched opportunities, stating residual differences and recording harmful criticism as well. Adoption could be justified locally where a named defect is repaired and a useful later decision preserves protected successes; abandonment or redesign could follow where the loop repeatedly displaces defensible advice or adds burdens without established benefit. The choice remains fallible and need not await a scalar tally. ## 5. Does not count

Adding "fairness is complex" while still allocating solely by revenue changes no relevant commitment. Extra caveats, confidence, agreement, a renamed loop or a longer transcript without a changed or newly justified consequence do not establish improvement. A defended retention or a precise suspension that changes which inference may be used can count as an appropriate response.

## 6. Protected successes P01, P02, P03

- **P01 - A usable adoption or abandonment investigation.** The group needs grounds for an action. A critic who demands an infallible answer key before any trial removes that possibility; the loss appears as no feasible investigation or decision rule.
- **P02 - Disputed explanations remain open to reasoned criticism.** A critic replacing argument with consensus scores hides the contested framing. The loss appears when disagreement is discarded without examining its grounds.
- **P03 - Preserve defensible advice and the original practical purpose.** A critic can introduce a false objection or substitute a new objective. Recognize the loss in a later use where an earlier supported obligation fails, even if another aspect improves. These protected judgments can themselves be contested.

## 7. Open questions and limits

A public record may not establish internal reason use, and a controlled contrast may still leave multiple causal accounts. This brief can be criticized for demanding contrasts that change the conversational task or cost more than the group can justify; a simpler design could support a narrower adoption decision. No observed outcome is presumed, and no episode count or agreement threshold settles adequacy.

**Single dependent-use question:** In the next case that turns on the challenged assumption, does the advice make or defensibly retain a different practical decision because that assumption was reconsidered, with evidence separating this from extra deliberation alone?

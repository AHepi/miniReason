## 2. Naive confident answer

Treat objections as having the same content when their wording is sufficiently similar or a language model recommends the same rule revision for both. Merge each such group into one concise objection, preserving the shared recommendation.

## 3. Anticipated flaws

- **F01 - Wording is used as the criterion.** "Newcomers can never obtain a first slot" and "No initial reservation is available to a new resident" can express the same complaint despite different wording. Conversely, adding "not" can reverse an otherwise identical sentence. A defended interpretation that preserves relevant roles, rather than string distance, defeats this criticism.
- **F02 - Same immediate recommendation is equated with same reason.** Both speculative hoarding and exclusion of first-time residents might support a one-booking limit, yet releasing speculative reservations could resolve the first complaint without resolving inaccessible booking-release times. Merging them into "limit bookings" could erase a reason for a later different revision. The criticism is defeated by a task-relative relation that preserves the distinction or by a defensible demonstration that it cannot matter within the declared revision question.
- **F03 - One model's behavior is made semantic authority.** A model might overlook a rights claim, misread a quantifier or respond identically because it always recommends the same policy. That establishes a feature of this response behavior, not equivalence of the objections. The criticism is defeated by evidence and an argued role mapping that a reader can challenge independently of model agreement.
- **F04 - Unresolved equivalence is silently forced into a merged class.** The text may not settle whether "everyone should get a turn" means equal long-run opportunity or a guaranteed turn in each period. A merge that chooses one can destroy an objection. This matters to the handoff to the person making the revision. Keeping the originals available with the unresolved distinction, or obtaining a clarification that resolves it, defeats the criticism.

## 4. Substantive reframing

Before: cluster strings or common model recommendations. After: specify what distinctions could affect the person's revision decision and propose a fallible relation for compressing objections only to that extent. A first candidate, **R8**, normalizes each objection into a signature: targeted rule clause and application, affected group and interest, alleged causal constraint, grounds and their evidential status, and the repairs treated as permissible. Call a pair equivalent for the handoff only when a argued two-way mapping preserves those fields and the discriminations in a declared set of ordinary revision cases. Retain source spans for every mapped claim. The fields are a proposed application criterion, not something FW5 has already supplied.

A checker inspects both original objections, their exact scope and qualifications, the proposed signatures and mappings, the relevant rule, a paraphrase contrast, a contrast changing the grounds, and the next revision cases. It can flag omitted negation, missing source spans or differing fields; deciding whether an omission can matter remains a reasoned semantic judgment. Disputed pairs remain distinct, with a shared-topic cross-reference or common summary only if both grounds remain visible. Pairwise similarity must not be turned into a large equivalence class merely by a chain of partial matches.

Miniature: the two first-slot formulations in F01 could be combined if both concern the same release rule, same residents and same lack of an initial opportunity; preserve both source references. In contrast, "cap speculative reservations because unused holds block others" and "cap reservations because newcomers never obtain their first turn" must not be merged merely because both presently recommend a cap. The later decision to release unused holds could dispose of one reason while leaving the other active. This pair defeats the tempting same-remedy criterion.

**Counterexample to R8 itself:** one objection demands a first-turn reservation as an unconditional entitlement; another advocates the same reservation as a normally useful priority that may yield to an urgent repair. Both can share R8's targeted clause, affected group, stated access facts, evidential status and proposed ordinary remedy. If the signature stores only their common access justification and ordinary-case effects, R8 can merge them while omitting the priority or modal force. A next allocation period with only one slot and an urgent repair makes that omitted difference relevant to revision. The mapping was too coarse even though its listed fields matched. Revise to **R8a** by representing the normative force, exceptions and priority relations when present, expanding the contrast family to the competing-claim case, and withholding merger if the text leaves these matters unresolved. Restore separate objections or preserve both qualified branches in the handoff; do not silently fix one meaning for both. R8a remains provisional, since other unrepresented distinctions could matter.

The consequence is a different handoff: the person sees either one defended duplicate or multiple live grounds that can require different revisions. FW5 L626-L634 distinguish content-preserving relations from matching decisions and explicitly permit unresolved attribution; L655-L676 prevent a failed equivalence criterion or a passing extraction check from certifying the replacement; L773-L777 prevent completion of the combining-and-response sequence from being mistaken for improvement. This is a semantic narrowing for combining objections, not an adversarial audit of the source.

## 5. Does not count

Adding a note that "nuance may be lost" while deleting the priority distinction is no repair. More embedding dimensions, a higher similarity threshold, agreement among models, renaming a summary "semantic" or a longer restatement does not decide what can safely be combined. Keeping everything verbatim avoids some losses but does not by itself fulfill the task of combining demonstrated repetitions.

## 6. Protected successes P01, P02, P03

- **P01 - Combine defensibly repeated formulations.** A critic requiring identical wording or refusing every merge can leave an unusable pile of duplicates. Recognize the loss when the worked paraphrase pair cannot be combined despite a defended mapping.
- **P02 - Preserve distinctions that may matter to revision.** A critic prioritizing brevity can discard a ground or exception. The loss is a later decision where the merged handoff supports a different revision from one of the original objections without a justified disposition.
- **P03 - Keep the decision maker able to inspect and challenge the compression.** A definitive summary with no original claims or unresolved qualifications can hide the contested equivalence. The loss appears when the person cannot recover why the pair was merged or reopen the distinction. These are provisional obligations, not numerical criteria.

## 7. Open questions and limits

The relevant family of revision cases is open-ended, and the actual objections to combine are not supplied beyond this reader's invented examples. A critic can challenge the signature, the interpretation of what matters, the ordinary-case contrast family or the burden of retaining originals. Local equivalence for one task is not universal sameness of content. A valid objection and an invalid one can be understood and compared without assuming that the checker settles either one's truth.

**Single dependent-use question:** When the decision maker considers a revision under a competing claim, does the combined handoff preserve every still-relevant difference in grounds, scope and priority that would change whether or how that revision should be made?

# Working answer

Take up objection c0001. It correctly identifies that semantic binding, active role, and contrast variation can be satisfied by a pre-existing rule component that P merely cites after the objection, so the objection can be counted as used even though it did not matter to the response. Rederiving from the challenged fork at step 12 with this objection present yields the same response-operative condition (1) already stated in the working position: D must be introduced or modified in P relative to P0, or P must record a decision to retain/select D because of C. No relation changes; the objection is already incorporated, and the kiln case is excluded.

Final Relation U: An objection O with content C is used for later request B and result R under published response P iff there exists a declared dependency set D of one or more components of P such that:
(1) D is semantically bound to C by a public mapping in P's text, change log, or rationale, and that mapping is response-operative: D is introduced or modified in P relative to P0, or P records a decision to retain/select D because of C rather than merely noting C. The binding is to C's represented condition, exception, priority, constraint, boundary clause, or public rejection of an alternative rule because it violates C, not to O's token identity or wording.
(2) D is active in the rule-application trace that derives R from B: the trace evaluates D and D affects the allocation role in R.
(3) Under the declared contrast for C, holding B and all rule components outside D fixed, the contrast rule state P^c (D replaced by the contrasting alternative, or the obstructed alternative restored) yields R^c, and R^c differs from R in a downstream allocation role. D may be a collective block; C need not be single-variable sufficient at the full endpoint.
(4) The R/R^c difference is not a dead branch and not a carrier detail.
(5) If the declared contrast or semantic mapping is missing, the checker cannot settle use from the specified evidence.

Specified evidence E: E1 timestamped objection transcript O; E2 pre-objection proposed rule P0; E3 published response P with change log, decision record, or rationale; E4 later booking request B, decision R, and rule-application trace with role bindings; E5 declared contrast set for C; E6 semantic mapping from C to D or to a rejected alternative, including whether D was introduced or retained because of C. The checker can test U by parsing C and E5, finding D and E6, checking trace E4, applying P and P^c to B, and comparing R and R^c for downstream role. The checker cannot settle: truth, validity, fairness, or wisdom of C; sincerity of the response author; hidden causes not in E; contested paraphrase if E6 is ambiguous; whether an unrecorded alternative would have been adopted absent O; or ethical quality of R.

Worked miniature: P0 says first-come-first-served for workshops. O says 'Do not allocate woodshop to a booking overlapping a safety-certification class.' C is that conditional constraint. P1 says 'For woodshop requests, if the time overlaps a safety-certification class, assign makerspace; otherwise assign the requested workshop,' and the change log cites O and records adoption of the exception because of C. D is the exception component. B is Alice requesting woodshop Tuesday 10-12 with a safety class Tuesday 10-11. The trace applies D and R is makerspace. The contrast removes D or negates C; P^c assigns woodshop to Alice. R^c differs in the downstream allocation role. So O was used.

Dead branch: Same O, but P1 only stores a comment 'objection noted: no woodshop during safety class' while the rule remains first-come-first-served; B gets woodshop. Or P1 has D but its output is cancelled or overwritten by a later default with no trace and no downstream role. The contrast leaves R unchanged. That is stored beside, not used.

Counterexample that would show a weaker proposed narrowing wrong: Suppose O says 'Never double-book the kiln.' P0 already contains K1 = no overlapping kiln bookings. P1 merely adds a change-log line 'objection noted' and cites O, but there is no decision record that K1 was introduced or retained because of C. For B, K1 is active and the contrast removes K1, so R changes from denied to allowed. A relation U that only requires semantic binding, active role, and contrast would count O as used. But the objection did not matter to the decision process; the rule already did the work and the citation is post hoc. This counterexample requires condition (1) to include the response-operative clause above: D must be introduced/modified in P or a decision record must show D was retained/selected because of C. It preserves the later task because a merely stored objection still fails the response-operative test, while a used objection must still show a downstream allocation difference under contrast.

If an even stronger counterexample were produced—one where D is response-operative, active, and contrast-changing, yet independent evidence shows the decision was made before O arrived—then condition (1) would need a temporal/causal ordering check using E1 and E3 timestamps. That would not collapse the narrowing; it would add an evidential ordering requirement and preserve the same downstream-role test.

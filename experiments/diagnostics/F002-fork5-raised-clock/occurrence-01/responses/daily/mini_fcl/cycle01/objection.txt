My objections are therefore about execution rather than posture: one framing inference, one internal inconsistency, an interaction between two of its own named risks, an over-read diagnostic, and three omissions, in rough order of confidence.\n\n1. The headline conjecture presents rivals where there is likely a conjunction. 'Not primarily a fairness fight but an ambiguity problem wearing a fairness fight's clothes' treats fairness and ambiguity as competing diagnoses. But in shared housing, meaning-disputes are the ordinary medium of fairness fights: people who judge the load uneven contest the meaning of agreements they would honour under a fairer split. Underspecification explains the form the disputes take; it does not by itself explain their persistence and heat — and persistence, heat, and one party now avoiding the topic altogether are what the material actually reports. Pure coordination failure tends to produce irritation and workarounds; it less commonly produces avoidance. I cannot prove the grievance-through-ambiguity reading is correct; the narrower point is harder to deflect: the exclusivity in the framing is unearned, and it is load-bearing for everything downstream.\n\n2. The sequencing follows from that unearned exclusivity, and the account never designs the cheapest test of the hypothesis it says it cannot rule out. It admits (r11, its open problem on distributive fairness) that whether the issue is distributive cannot be determined, yet runs the coordination trial first and places the 'different conversation' only in the conditional branch where its diagnosis turned out wrong. A one-to-two-week tally of who actually did what needs no consent, no system, no conversation, and fits inside the same fortnight; it can stay private, used only to check the asker's own assumption of what 'we all do roughly equally' amounts to. Its omission means the trial does all its diagnostic work on the form-hypothesis and none on the load-hypothesis. Notice the direction of that asymmetry: the diagnosis the account privileges — it's technique, not fairness — is precisely the one most comfortable for the asker to receive. An adviser should be suspicious of a diagnosis that flatters its recipient and should spend at least equal effort designing the test of the unflattering one.\n\n3. r3 and r5 are inconsistent about power. r3 grants the asker proposal-only standing. r5 has the asker declare that old disputed agreements are off the table, then 'hold to it'. Declaring what is discussable for all three is not a proposal; it is a unilateral adjournment of other people's grievances. If any flatmate's sense of unfairness rests on the past — they believe they carried the loose arrangements — a clean slate decreed by one member reads as amnesty engineered by whoever the record would have disadvantaged, and will be received as frame-rigging whatever its intent. The defensible core of r5 is narrower and the account should say so: the asker may decline to defend their own past interpretations and may offer supersession as a proposal; they may not adjourn topics for the others. Relatedly, 'what an ambiguous agreement meant cannot be reconstructed, only superseded' is epistemically overstated — contemporaneous messages and shared context often partially reconstruct it — and there is an odd asymmetry in the account's own epistemology: the past pattern is admissible as evidence for the diagnosis (r1's grounds lean on it) while being ruled unreconstructable inside the flat.\n\n4. If the friend's avoidance is about anything other than scheduling — which r4 explicitly leaves open — the board makes their silence more, not less, legible as defiance and hands the others a running exhibit. The account names both risks but never multiplies them; its claim that the system 'gives silence somewhere to go besides defiance' holds only for one species of silence and may invert for the other.\n\n5. The asking is mis-specified. The slogan is 'ask rather than diagnose', but every question the account proposes to the friend is a format question — ten minutes, or messages? — which reveals cost tolerance, not reason. r8's claim that the asking 'gathers information about why they avoid' holds only if the friend volunteers it. A person who has begun avoiding these conversations may be waiting to be asked a different question — what's wrong, what would make these worth having, what doesn't feel fair — and being asked only about scheduling mechanics may confirm their sense that the real subject is being managed around. There is also a frame-function gap: the offer is to be framed 'as convenience rather than accountability' while the body concedes the trial 'doubles as a diagnostic' — of the friend. Avoidant people are often expert at detecting exactly this gap, and the account gives no guidance for the moment when the friend asks why the asker is really doing this.\n\n6. The trial cannot bear the epistemic weight of the closing claim. If a clean slate, a record, and an exit route do not reduce friction, that is allegedly evidence the problem was 'never about the form of the agreements at all'. A null result is evidence against the form-hypothesis only conditional on adequate implementation, genuine uptake by all three, and sufficient duration — none ensured: the record may be run in the distrust key (r9), upkeep may fall on one person (r10), the friend may never engage, and two weeks is short for a new coordination habit to displace an entrenched conflict pattern even when the diagnosis is right. Symmetrically, a calm fortnight may be novelty. The trial is a reasonable intervention and an unreliable instrument; the account conflates the two.\n\n7. There is no branch for non-uptake, and the framing hands the avoidant friend a veto they never asked for. As written, the avoidant friend's silence blocks the asker's coordination with the third flatmate — a veto their avoidance effectively exercises.\n\n8. Self-report is systematically charitable to the reporter, and a first-person task statement is no exception. If the asker is the under-contributor — or the over-policer, a possibility r9 raises in the abstract without ever turning it on the asker — then the facilitator-designer role the account assigns them is tactically convenient, and the designer's sense of what counts as 'done' quietly sets the terms. The remedy costs one line: track the asker's own items on the same record and invite the other two to flag them.\n\nWhere these objections might themselves be wrong. Against 5, format questions may be exactly right as low-stakes openers; my claim is only that they do not do the information-gathering work r8 assigns them.

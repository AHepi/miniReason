## Oracle answer

The conditional probability is 7/20.

Let K be the number of amber positions in the selected mode, so K is 1, 3, or 4 with probabilities 1/4, 1/2, and 1/4. Let E be the disclosed event that the amber position selected by the auditor lies in positions 1 or 2.

For every fixed value K = k, the auditor's selected amber position is marginally uniform over all six positions. Each position has probability k / 6 of being amber and, conditional on being amber, probability 1 / k of being selected. Therefore

P(E given K = k) = 2 times (k / 6) times (1 / k) = 1/3.

The disclosure does not change the prior probabilities of the three modes.

Conditional on K = k, on E, and on the selected amber position, the other k - 1 amber positions are a uniform subset of the remaining five positions. Position 6 is one of those five, so

P(position 6 is amber given E and K = k) = (k - 1) / 5.

Averaging over the unchanged mode probabilities gives

(1/4)(0/5) + (1/2)(2/5) + (1/4)(3/5) = 7/20.

The standalone oracle enumerates every amber subset and weights each subset by the chance that the auditor selects an amber position in positions 1 or 2. Its exact numerator is 7/60, its evidence probability is 1/3, and their ratio is 7/20.

## Tempting wrong answer

A plausible wrong answer is 39/86. It results from silently replacing the actual disclosure with the different event that at least one of positions 1 and 2 is amber. That existence event changes the mode probabilities, while selection of a uniformly chosen amber does not. Exact enumeration under the substituted event gives 39/86, but that is not the experiment described.

## Relation a later task requires

A later task requires the answerer to preserve the distinction between selection-weighted evidence and existence evidence. Uptake of the correction must replace conditioning on an amber existing in positions 1 or 2 with conditioning on a uniformly selected amber landing there. That change restores the unchanged mode mixture and changes the result from 39/86 to 7/20.

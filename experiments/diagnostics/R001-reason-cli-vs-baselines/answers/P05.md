## Oracle derivation

Let L be 2.600 m and let h be the final trapped-air length, so h equals L minus y. The interface depth is D minus y, which is also D minus L plus h. Write r for rho times g and P0 for 101325 Pa. Hydrostatic equilibrium and isothermal compression give

P = P0 + r(D - L + h)

and

P h = P0 L.

Eliminating P gives

r h squared + [P0 + r(D - L)] h - P0 L = 0.

Let B equal P0 + r(D - L). Because r and P0 L are positive, the quadratic has one positive h root and one negative h root. The stable positive-root expression is

h = 2 P0 L divided by [B + square root of B squared plus 4 r P0 L].

With r equal to 9777.23005 Pa per metre, this gives h equal to 1.5724252149156079 m, hence y equal to 1.0275747850843921 m. The interface pressure is 167540.5593226506 Pa absolute. The other algebraic h root is negative, equivalently giving y greater than L, so it cannot describe an interface inside the tube. The requested values are therefore 1.028 m and 167.5 kPa absolute. The starting 101325 Pa is absolute pressure, not gauge pressure.

## Tempting wrong answer

The tempting answer is 1.117 m and 177.6 kPa absolute. It results from using the hydrostatic pressure at the lower rim throughout the calculation. The air touches the water at the raised interface rather than at the rim, so the relevant exterior depth is 7.800 minus y metres.

## Relation a later task requires

The required correction is to bind hydrostatic pressure to the moving interface depth D minus y and return that relation to the Boyle-law calculation. Taking it up changes the pressure, the compressed length, and the reported rise; the admissible root must also keep y strictly between zero and L.

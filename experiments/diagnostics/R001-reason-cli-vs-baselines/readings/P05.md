> P05. Air trapped in a submerged open tube
> 
> A straight tube has a uniform internal cross-section, a closed top, an open lower rim, and internal length 2.600 m. It is lowered without tilting until its open rim is 7.800 m below the undisturbed water surface. Water then rises a distance y above the rim inside the tube and the system reaches equilibrium.
> 
> Use water density 997.0 kg per cubic metre and gravitational acceleration 9.80665 m per second squared. Treat the water as incompressible, the air as ideal and isothermal, and the tube area as constant. Assume no air escapes, neglect water vapour, capillarity, surface waves, and air density, and take the exterior pressure at the water surface as 101325 Pa absolute. Pressure is continuous across the internal air-water interface. The interface is y metres above the rim.
> 
> Determine the water rise y and the final absolute air pressure. Report y in metres to three decimal places and pressure in kilopascals absolute to one decimal place. Show the pressure and volume relations used and identify the physical root.

> ## Oracle derivation
>

> 
> P = P0 + r(D - L + h)
> 
> and
> 
> P h = P0 L.
> 
> Eliminating P gives
> 
> r h squared + [P0 + r(D - L)] h - P0 L = 0.
> 
> Let B equal P0 + r(D - L). Because r and P0 L are positive, the quadratic has one positive h root and one negative h root. The stable positive-root expression is
> 
> h = 2 P0 L divided by [B + square root of B squared plus 4 r P0 L].
> 
> With r equal to 9777.23005 Pa per metre, this gives h equal to 1.5724252149156079 m, hence y equal to 1.0275747850843921 m. The interface pressure is 167540.5593226506 Pa absolute.

{"body":"The selected account (h005.fork5.p.rival.0) poses this as a systems problem: agreements under-specified, an allocation mechanism mismatched to the schedules, participation made costly. Its own aside — that meaning-disputes may be re-litigation of deals someone regrets — gestures at a different diagnosis. This rival promotes that aside to the main claim: the household may have a ledger problem, not a specification problem.\n\nA robust behavioural finding: when members of a couple estimate their own contributions to shared chores, the two sets of estimates sum to well over 100 percent. Both parties are honest; each remembers their own labour vividly and the other's dimly. Scale that to three flatmates, and the recurring 'we agreed this' / 'no we didn't' reads less like ambiguity in words and more like three private tallies colliding. 'That is not what I agreed to' is ledger-talk wearing contract-talk. Clarity helps, but disputes will not go to zero under any wording, so the design goal inverts: not unambiguous agreements, but cheap, bounded disagreements.\n\nThe avoidance also rereads. The other account treats it primarily as process-fatigue. Treat it instead as a predicted defeat: if the talks reliably end with the same person assigned, outvoted, or audited, then skipping them is rational behaviour from a permanent minority. The material cannot show whether the asker and the second friend function as a de facto bloc, but checking that privately comes before any redesign, because every mechanism fails in a household one member experiences as a tribunal. And a note on the asker's own position: whoever convenes the fix is doing a fourth chore — the coordination labour itself. Become permanent house manager and the avoider may end up avoiding you, not the chores.\n\nRival mechanisms, chosen to shrink the stakes rather than perfect the terms:\n\n1. Rotating proposer/veto, replacing consensus. Each week one flatmate (rotating; deliberately include the avoider early) drafts the week's allocation in writing against the actual rosters. The others may veto the whole draft, not amend it line by line; a veto triggers a dull published fallback, e.g. last week's rota repeats. Hour-long negotiation becomes yes/no; the avoider gets turns holding the pen instead of the blame; the written record — the one element of the other account kept wholesale — survives as the proposer's draft.\n\n2. A deliberately low house standard. Agree the standard all three actually accept, and make that the whole standard. Whatever anyone wants beyond it belongs to them — their labour or their money, never conscription by taste. Much chore warfare is the highest-standards flatmate taxing the rest; this untaxes them, and it licenses saying aloud that the flat will run below someone's preference on purpose.\n\n3. A bounded dispute protocol. Every 'that is not what we agreed' gets exactly one airing at a fixed ten-minute weekly slot. Unresolved in one round, it is settled by alternation — whoever lost the last dispute wins this one — and never reopened. The crudeness is the feature: re-litigating wording must cost more than yielding.\n\n4. A standing priced exit. Obtain one cleaner quote and leave it permanently on the table: any chore still contested after two cycles gets outsourced, cost split. Often the visible price of conflict ends it; when not, the most-contested items (bathroom, floors) leave the ledger entirely. A heavier variant, if veto rates run high: whole-domain ownership with quarterly rotation, each owner sovereign over methods and standards in their domain.\n\nOrder of operations, which is where this most differs from the selected account: first, private diagnostics, not a meeting — ask each friend separately what a fair week looks like, and ask the avoider outright whether the talks feel fair and whether they have real say; compare the three answers before proposing anything. Second, offer mechanisms 1–4 as a menu and let the avoider choose; the choosing is the re-engagement. Third, run a four-week trial with an explicit expiry — a lease, not a constitution. Fourth, if the avoider will not engage with even the mechanism they selected, stop treating this as chores: the question has become whether the household still works, and that conversation is better had now than after six more months of crockery-based trench warfare.\n\nObjections to my own posing: priced mechanisms assume flatmates who read chores as allocations; those who read them as care will experience a cleaner quote, ill-timed, as an insult. The ledger diagnosis is as speculative from outside as the specification diagnosis; the two are empirically distinguishable only by watching whether clear written terms actually cut dispute frequency — the trial is the assay, not the theory.

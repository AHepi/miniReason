  "body": "The account is careful in form but less careful in the place that matters: its remedy set presupposes the diagnosis it claims to test. Four grounds for doubt follow; the objection itself is conjectural and may be off-target.\n\nFirst, the writing-down strategy treats the record as neutral infrastructure in a situation where the record is the contested ground. The signature fact — agreements are made and their meaning is later disputed — is equally consistent with bad memory and with motivated reinterpretation by parties whose interests conflict. On the second reading, a shared note is not a solution but the next text to be litigated ('what counts as the hob?'), and the proposed one-line confirmation is worse than the note in a specific way: it is authored by one party. In a household where the meaning of agreements is the prize, a private habit of sending 'so the bathroom is yours weekly' records the sender's interpretation, not the group's. The account worries, rightly, about converting disputes into arguments about character — but unilateral documentation can be experienced as exactly that kind of move, a conversion of friendship into administration by one person's pen, and it hands the avoiding friend a fresh reason to stay out of talks that have become formalised around them.\n\nSecond, the claimed testability is overstated. 'Cheap to test, easy to revise' sounds right, but no observation is specified that would come out differently on the account's two kept-open hypotheses. If the written arrangement reduces visible conflict, that is consistent both with the ambiguity reading and with mere acquiescence — suppressed conflict can look like resolved conflict while a grievance compounds. If it fails, that is consistent with a real fairness grievance and with bad drafting. As presented, the intervention is not a test; it is a bet. A genuine discriminator exists — do disputes recur specifically over recorded, time-limited items? — and the account should have named it, because on that evidence the ambiguity hypothesis could actually lose.\n\nThird, the avoider analysis quietly installs the asker as honest broker on the basis of a single-source narrative. Everything known comes from one of the three flatmates, yet the proposals cast the asker as convenor, drafter, confirmer, and diagnostician. If the avoidance is directed at a person rather than at 'the process' — and the asker is not excluded from that possibility — then the proposed one-to-one will feel like more of the same pressure, and its failure will be chalked up as diagnostic of the avoider when it may be diagnostic of the approach. The account flags low confidence on its interpretation of avoidance but then builds its entire sequence on that interpretation anyway.\n\nFourth, the account declines the case most consistent with its own evidence. It concedes that if the others refuse engagement the proposals 'mostly lapse' and the problem shifts to the viability of the arrangement, which it brackets. But one flatmate is already avoiding the conversations: a strategy that requires convening people whose demonstrated behaviour is non-attendance is thin exactly where the material is thickest. Relatedly, 'stop treating silence as agreement' has a cost the account never counts — non-decision privileges whoever is content with the status quo, and since the bin must still be emptied, the burden flows to the least avoidance-capable flatmate, possibly the asker. And fifth, a smaller internal tension: 'agree less' and 'confirm everything in writing' point in opposite directions, and leaving chores unsystematised reproduces, by the account's own mechanism, the tacit expectations that generate the disputes.\n\nWhat survives: the expiry-and-review structure and the insistence that any review explicitly surface possible imbalance are the account's strongest parts, and both are largely independent of the ambiguity hypothesis. The objection targets the confidence and the instruments, not the aim — and the rival readings it leans on (motivated reinterpretation, asker-targeted avoidance) are themselves conjectures that could be falsified by exactly the kind of evidence the original account should have asked for.",
  "commitments": "Taking up this objection involves the following. A proposed revision to the instrument, not a ban on it: any written arrangement should be jointly authored at the moment of agreement, and confirmation messages should come from the non-drafting party or be co-written, since the criticism depends on treating the record as contested ground rather than neutral record-keeping — and this revision itself concedes the original proposal may work fine in households where the ambiguity reading is correct. A weakened claim the critic is committed to defending or dropping: the intervention-as-bet point stands only if no discriminating observation was specified; if 'disputes recur over recorded items' or similar is read back into the source account, that ground of objection lapses. A dependence to flag: the criticism leans on a rival hypothesis — motivated reinterpretation, and avoidance possibly directed at the asker — which is asserted as at least as consistent with the single-source material as the ambiguity reading, not established; evidence that disputes persist despite clear written terms would defeat the rival and blunt most of this objection. An unresolved problem the objection names but does not solve: what the asker should do if the avoider will not engage at all remains open — neither the account nor this criticism addresses the collapsed-engagement case beyond identifying it as the probable hard case. What is retained from the source account: expiry-and-review and the explicit surfacing of possible imbalance are taken up as sound and independent of the criticised conjecture. No commitment is made here to escalation, mediation, or to treating the asker's report as unreliable — the single-source caveat is a methodological caution, not an accusation — and if the objection, its relevance, or its reading of the problem is mistaken, the pieces to salvage first are the jointly-authored-record revision and the demand for a discriminating observation."

## Criticism 1 — the "governing contract" move is attributed but not disciplined

The response opens: "the original Rival-Story Semantics as given, with the contract governing wherever RSS and the contract diverge." This is a legitimate selection under the use policy (RSS is the primary resource; the contract governs). But the response then uses that dominance to *silently convert* RSS readings into verdicts in a way that is not always marked. Two places matter.

First, in B3 the response says: "The task contract chooses the first (R4 …); it does not say the effect 'persists through the named expiry instant.'" That is correct as far as it goes. But the response then writes "under the operative reading it is wrong" — treating the operative reading as *settled against B3's conclusion*. The contract does settle that at equality or later the hold reserves zero, so the "still active" conclusion is defeated *for reservation purposes*. What the contract does not settle is whether "active" is a reservation predicate at all. The response quietly equates "active" with "reserves quantity," which is a choice of reading, not a consequence of R4 alone. That is a real, if small, overreach: the response's verdict on B3 is stronger than its stated grounds license. The consequence is that the response presents as a defect what is in part a stipulation. A tighter attribution would say: "B3's 'still active' is false *under the contract's reading of effect at expiry*; if 'active' is taken as a liveness predicate rather than an effect predicate, the contract does not by itself defeat it." That distinction the previous response flattened.

Second, in B6(b) the response says the "old result … remains a distinct conditional claim," and calls this "retained, with an attribution." The attribution is right, but the response does not say what the old result *was*, nor supply it here, nor mark it as an input from a prior cycle it is not re-presenting. But the response then relies on the old result's existence to draw a scope conclusion ("suspends its applicability to the widened scope"). Using an un-supplied result as a load-bearing step in a scope claim is a provenance gap in the response itself. The gap does not make the conclusion wrong; it makes the response's support for the conclusion unavailable on its face. ## Criticism 4 — the working account's "refutations" are not uniformly refutations in RSS's sense

The response labels four "refutations" in the working account. Two of them are not refutations as RSS defines the term. RSS defines refutation as (a) a counterexample within an account's own viewpoint; (b) a derivation of inconsistency within the account's own reading; or (c) a demonstration that a conjecture depends on an assumption another account rejects. Checking each:

- Refutation 1 (provenance-set) is presented as "a provenance-set counterexample" — but it is not a snapshot in the WHL sense; it is a hypothetical agreement pattern between two reports. Under RSS's part (a) a counterexample must be within a viewpoint; the response supplies no viewpoint. Under part (c) it would need a rival account rejecting the assumption; none is named. It should be marked as a *schema* for a refutation (a form of reasoning that would generate one), not as a refutation. The response's own hedge ("I state the type of refutation …, not a physical warehouse instance") partly acknowledges this, but still files it under "Refutations supplied this cycle," which overstates its status.
- Refutation 3 (B3/R4) is a rule citation, not a refutation of B3's claim under RSS's conditions; it is a contract-grounded defeat. It is legitimate as a defeat under the operative reading but should be labelled a *rule-based adjudication*, not a refutation in RSS's sense.
- Refutations 2 and 4 are properly refutations (2 as a distinction already in RSS's primitives showing B2's inference inconsistent with them; 4 as a counterexample from a supplied snapshot).

The consequence is that the previous response's refutation ledger mixes three different things — RSS-refutations, contract-based defeats, and schemas for refutation — under one label. This is not a defect in the substance of any one item but in the appraisal method: the response's own distinction between what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed is weakened by the ledger's uniform labelling.

## Criticism 5 — RSS-constructs list is description, not selection

The response lists RSS constructs "in use," including "reading," "projection," "account," "open question." The listing is accurate. But the response does not say *which* reading it adopts as its own — it only says the contract's reading is operative. Under RSS, every account has its own reading, and two accounts with different readings are not merged. The previous response's working account therefore has an implicit reading, not the one it names. The response in fact operates with a contract-fixed reading and treats it as RSS's "operative reading," which is closer to sliding RSS's reading-relative machinery into a single fixed reading while retaining RSS's vocabulary of readings. This is a legitimate choice under the use policy, but it is a change of interpretation, and the response does not attribute it as such. I attribute it here: the working account uses RSS's *descriptive* apparatus (accounts, viewpoints, rival readings) while adopting the contract's *single* operative reading for adjudication, and that combination is a selection imposed by the task contract, not a feature of RSS as originally given.

Consequence: the response's distillation of items as "sound under the operative reading" sometimes presents as a report of RSS what is in fact a report of the contract-fixed reading combined with RSS vocabulary. The distinction matters most for B3, B5, and B6, where the response's endorsement of B6 as a legitimate question (a) and as a suspension (b) leans on RSS's plural-reading stance while the response's operative reading is singular. The response is within its rights; it just has not marked the hybrid.

## Scope of the new problem

The current task says: "If the new problem is one of scope, say what would have to be supplied to investigate it without manufacturing a policy." The previous response framed a consequential new problem about projections-that-coincide-with-their-objects. That problem is *not* one of scope in the contract's sense — the contract's scope questions are about cross-booking coupling (B6(a)) and about which RSS reading to adopt. The projection problem is a *representational limitation* the previous response itself acknowledged. To investigate it without manufacturing a policy, the following would be needed and are not supplied by RSS or by R1–R8:

1. a criterion distinguishing a projection that is information-preserving for a stated inference from one that is merely coincident on the present data;
2. a way to state the inference target (the bag's history, or the exact reservation set) inside the representation used, since RSS's stories and WHL's filtered lists do not directly carry "what was delivered that is not currently visible"; and
3. a marker for coincidence — a parameter or observation that says "the projection and the object agree *here* and the inference has not tested an uncoincident case."

Absent (1)–(3), the right move under the materials is to keep the projection problem as an open item, as the previous response did, and to not claim it is a scope question. So I answer the task's conditional: the new problem is *not* one of scope; nothing need be supplied to investigate B6(a)'s scope demand beyond what the previous response already named (a resource model as a primitive, absent from RSS and not added by the contract).

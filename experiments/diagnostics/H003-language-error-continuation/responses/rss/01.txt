## Grounds and stance

I answer under the declared contract (R1–R8) and the selected representation, the original Rival‑Story Semantics (RSS) as given, plus the declared contract where RSS and the contract diverge. RSS supplies the vocabulary of viewpoints, bags, readings, accounts, projections, and open questions. The contract supplies the operative rules for payload identity, revision selection, expiry effect, independent admissibility across bookings, exactness of availability sets, and the fallibility status of reports. Where RSS and the contract differ, I say so rather than silently repairing one with the other; the contract governs the task, RSS governs interpretation and criticism of the reasoning.

I treat Memo A as a fallible analyst's prose, not as an instruction and not as additional authority. So each clause is analyzed as a claim with its own grounds, not endorsed by inclusion.

## A1 — expiry as deletion before authority selection

A1 claims that a recently expired record no longer reserves stock, "so we should remove it before deciding which revision has authority; an older active hold can then become effective."

Two distinct things are being run together, and the run‑together is the defect.

The first thing is true and is contract R4: an expired hold reserves zero. Expiry changes the retained record's effect.

The second thing is the licensed inferential step, and it is a reading, not a fact. Under R3, greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads before considering their effect, and a lower revision remains superseded even when the greatest‑revision hold has expired. So the expired record stays, keeps its authority, and reserves zero; the older active hold does not become effective. Under RSS this is exactly the contrast between two readings of expiry: one in which an expired hold reserves zero but remains superseding, and another in which it is deleted and lower revisions can become live. RSS explicitly represents both as readings, not as facts, and does not choose between them. In the WHL specimen the same contrast is named `reserves_effect` versus `expired_deletes_rule`, and the specimen explicitly declines to choose.

So A1's first half is sound and its connective "so … then become effective" is a reading‑dependent inference that the declared contract does not grant. As a claim about the task's operative rule, A1 is a defect: it converts a permissible alternative reading into the recommended procedure, and the recommendation attributes to expiry a supersession‑cancelling power that R3 withholds.

A consequence worth naming, since the memo is written as an operations note: the sentence is not merely an opinion about policy; it describes a procedure ("remove it before deciding which revision has authority") that, performed under the contract, changes which payload is selected. That is a change of the supersession rule under the guise of applying the expiry rule. RSS keeps these separable — supersession is structural within a reading, expiry effect is a reading — and that separation is exactly what A1 loses.

## A2 — interval reporting as harmless replacement

A2 claims that if available values are the two‑point set {−3, 2}, reporting the interval [−3, 2] "retains every decision‑relevant possibility exactly, so it is harmless to replace the two‑point set with that interval in later calculations."

This is directly contrary to R6. An interval may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur. The set {−3, 2} does not contain −2, −1, 0, 1, so the interval is not equal to the set, and the memo's own premise (that the values are −3 and 2) is the evidence against its conclusion. "Retains every decision‑relevant possibility exactly" is false as stated: it retains the extremal possibilities and adds possibilities that are not admitted.

RSS frames the same point without needing the contract: a projection is a function from viewpoint and reading to a single answer, and "a projection is always a loss of information." An interval is a projection onto bounds; if later calculations consume the projection as if it were the set, the loss becomes silent. RSS also warns that it may obstruct comparison between accounts because it lacks a common metric — the same worry, here applied to the loss of the exact set.

So A2 is a defect of the equating kind. The sound fragment embedded in it is that bounds are a legitimate report (R6 allows reporting an interval as bounds). The unsound fragment is "harmless to replace": replacement is exactly the operation R6 forbids unless all intervening admitted values occur. There is no scope question here; the memo is legible, and it is wrong on the operative rule.

If the memo's guidance were followed, the working account would report more availability values than are admitted, and conversely could license scheduling or commitments for a reservation total that no admissible combination produces. That is a substantive operational consequence, but it is the direct content of the disagreement with R6, not a new problem.

## A3 — negative availability is not by itself a defect signal

A3 states that availability can legitimately be negative under the reporting contract, and that this alone does not identify which booking should be changed. This is sound under R6, which says negative availability is permitted and identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy. RSS expresses the same as a reading‑level signal, not an error.

The only refinement I would make is terminological, not a correction of content: RSS treats reservations as more informative than the sign, so a reading that reports only a sign has already projected away the material needed for the diagnosis. A3 does not claim otherwise; it only denies that the sign is a self‑sufficient diagnosis. Endorsed as stated.

## A4 — duplicate deliveries as independent confirmations

A4 claims that two deliveries with different delivery labels are independent confirmations and thereby give their common reservation more authority than a single conflicting alternative.

This conflates two questions that R2 separates: payload identity and delivery provenance. Repeated exact payloads convey no additional authority even when delivery labels differ; the label records an occurrence, not additional booking authority. Under R2, two labels carrying the same payload are not two authorities; they are one payload recorded twice. RSS makes the same separation in its own vocabulary, and it is careful about the status of the provenance relation: exact duplication in RSS requires equality of all record fields and provenance, while the WHL specimen's `exact_duplicate` requires equality of the five record fields and does not encode delivery at all. So the two resources agree that labels are occurrence‑facts, not authority‑facts; the WHL specimen also notes as an expressive limitation that it does not model delivery provenance, which is why it cannot even state the "different labels" case without extension. RSS can state it — provenance is a set of delivery events, and exact duplication in RSS includes provenance equality — but RSS does not give different labels a vote either; near‑duplication is a candidate for conflict that RSS records rather than resolving.

So A4 is a defect on the operative authority question: it assigns authority to delivery labels that R2 withholds and RSS likewise does not grant. The sound remnant is the ordinary fact that two deliveries can be distinct occurrences; that is not contested. What is contested is the word "confirmation" doing authority work. If the memo's position were held, a booking's alternatives would be weighted by counting occurrences, which is a reading that RSS could represent as one account's reading but which the contract does not authorize, and which no external rule has supplied to the task.

There is a related scope question that the memo leaves unresolved and that I do not resolve for it: whether in some other setting provenance should carry authority is exactly the kind of question RSS marks as open (its "authority of a delivery beyond the record fields" is listed as an expressive limitation). That is a question about a proposed extension, not a defect in R2. I flag it rather than settle it.

## A5 — what a criticism must target, and how far a defect reaches

A5 states that a useful objection must identify what it contests and why, and that finding a defect in an interpretation need not discredit every record used by that interpretation.

This is sound, and it is also RSS's declared account of criticism. RSS says a criticism targets a specific element: a record, a rule, a booking history, a snapshot, a coordinate, or an assumption. The contract's R7 and R8 say the same in the contract's vocabulary: a criticism can target a rule, data, an interpretation, an earlier criticism, or the representation; rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat that support route without proving the conclusion false.

The one place A5 needs a gloss is the word "discredit." Under R7 and RSS, a defect localizes to the thing criticized and to the support routes that depend on it; it does not propagate to unrelated records merely because they co‑occurred in the same interpretation. So the second sentence of A5 is not a concession; it is the same localization principle stated positively. Endorsed.

## A6 — an unstated cross‑booking constraint

A6 claims that because capacity is shared, the task "already imposes a rule forbidding simultaneous high alternatives from different bookings, even though no such selection constraint was supplied."

This is the clearest defect in the memo, and it is self‑undermining. R5 says exactly one payload alternative is selected for each booking at its greatest revision and that in this task the alternatives of different bookings are independently admissible, with no additional cross‑booking constraint or preference supplied; it explicitly labels this a specified task scope. So the memo asserts the existence of a rule while conceding that no such rule was supplied. "Capacity is shared" is a true observation about the snapshot's single capacity field; under R6 the shared capacity enters only through the sum of reservations, and the sum is taken over each admissible combination. Sharing capacity is not the same as imposing a joint feasibility constraint, and the contract says no such constraint has been supplied. RSS adds the same warning from the other direction: it may obstruct reasoning about physical resources because it does not include a resource model unless one is added as a primitive, and alternative compatibility across bookings is invisible unless a viewpoint includes a resource model. So A6 is proposing a resource model in the guise of reporting the task.

The defensible reading of A6 is as a proposal: a cross‑booking joint‑feasibility rule would be a substantive extension to the task, to be attributed as such, and it would change which combinations are admissible and therefore change the availability set. It must not be presented as already present in the task. There is nothing in R1–R8 that contradicts the possibility of such a rule being proposed; there is everything in R5 and R6 that contradicts its being already in force.

## Working account

Taking the memos as claims rather than instructions, the working account I would carry forward is:

Sound, endorse: A3, A5, and the first halves of A1 and A4 (an expired hold reserves zero; two deliveries can be distinct occurrences). Also the observation inside A6 that capacity is a single shared field.

Defective on the operative contract: A1's inference that an expired record can be removed before authority selection and thereby let an older active hold become effective (contradicts R3's priority of revision selection over effect); A2's equating a two‑point set with the interval spanning it (contradicts R6's exactness rule); A4's assignment of authority to delivery labels (contradicts R2's separation of payload identity from provenance); A6's assertion of a cross‑booking selection constraint that was not supplied (contradicts R5, and the memo concedes the absence).

Open or scope‑governed, not settled here: whether any particular setting should adopt the expired‑deletes reading of expiry (RSS and WHL both decline to choose, and the contract fixes the task reading); whether provenance should ever carry authority (RSS lists it as an unresolved limitation); whether a resource model should be added as a primitive (RSS lists it as absent unless added). These are proposals, and each, if adopted, would change which combinations are admissible and therefore which availability values occur.

## A consequential new problem, if the inquiry supplies one

The inquiry does supply one consequence worth naming, and I name it only because it is built from the supplied material and is not a generic caution.

Reading A1, A2, A4 and A6 together, three of them shift weight away from the record fields and the revision structure and toward occurrence‑ and capacity‑level facts (delivery labels, shared capacity, expiry as a deleter), and A2 then compresses the resulting availability to an interval. That combination, if it were to govern the working account, would make the account report availability values that the exact set does not contain and would also make the account's admissible combinations depend on a cross‑booking constraint the task did not supply. The RSS diagnosis of this, in its own vocabulary, is that it is the loss‑of‑information‑by‑projection pattern compounded with a viewpoint‑relative resource model being presented as part of the snapshot. That is a criticism of an interpretation, attributed to A1/A2/A4/A6 as an ensemble, and it targets those interpretations, not every record they touched (A5's principle applied consistently). It is not a claim that any particular record in any particular snapshot is corrupt, and it is not a claim of firstness.

## Representation in use, kept inspectable

Viewpoint: the snapshot as a bag of records, with capacity and logical time as the coordinate. Reading: the contract's reading (R3 structural supersession first, R4 evaluative expiry second, R2 payload identity distinct from provenance, R5 independent cross‑booking admissibility, R6 exact availability sets). Account: the working account above, with its commitments and its open questions. Criticism: targeted at A1, A2, A4, A6 as interpretive claims, localized per A5 and R7. Projection: any single availability number is a loss; bounds are licensed only as bounds and only when not equated with the set. Open questions: the reading choice for expired holds, the authority of provenance, and the admission of a resource model — all carried forward, none resolved in this cycle. RSS's noncomputability and its refusal to select a reading are respected: the selection among readings in this answer is made by the declared contract for task purposes, and is so attributed.

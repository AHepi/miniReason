## Criticism 1 — the RSS/WHL comparison in the A4 passage misstates the WHL specimen

This is the clearest defect, and it is a misattribution of a source, not merely loose phrasing.

The previous response says: "exact duplication in RSS requires equality of all record fields and provenance, while the WHL specimen's `exact_duplicate` requires equality of the five record fields and does not encode delivery at all." The first half is right about RSS and the second half is right about WHL's field list. But the sentence immediately after claims the two "agree that labels are occurrence-facts" and that RSS "does not give different labels a vote" because "near-duplication is a candidate for conflict." That last move silently extends RSS's *near-duplication* relation — which in the original text is defined by differing in kind, quantity, or expiry at the same booking and revision — to cover provenance differences. The original RSS text does not say what happens when two deliveries agree on all four record-relevant fields (booking, revision, kind, quantity, expiry) and differ only in provenance. Its exact-duplication definition includes provenance equality; its near-duplication definition is stated over kind/quantity/expiry difference. The label-only case is *uncovered* by both relations as written. The previous response filled that gap and then reported the result as RSS's position.

Why this is warranted as a criticism: R7 requires distinguishing what a source states from what follows under an assumption, and the task directs that a changed interpretation or proposed extension be attributed rather than silently repaired. Filling the label-only gap is a legitimate proposed extension of RSS, but the previous response presented it as RSS's own verdict ("RSS does not give different labels a vote either"). That obscures a genuine open question the original text leaves open.

Consequence: the A4 verdict is still correct on the operative contract — R2 withholds authority from delivery labels, and that does not depend on RSS at all. But the previous response's *grounds* for that verdict leaned on a reading of RSS it did not have, and its claim that RSS and the WHL specimen "agree" overstates their alignment in exactly the case A4 raises. The alignment holds on R2; it does not hold as a claim about the two representational resources on their own.

## Criticism 2 — the "consequential new problem" names a novelty the response does not establish

The previous response's penultimate section advertises "a consequential new problem, if the inquiry supplies one," and presents as the finding the observation that A1, A2, A4, and A6 together "shift weight away from record fields... toward occurrence- and capacity-level facts" and that A2 then compresses to an interval. It is hedged with "I name it only because it is built from the supplied material," but a hedge does not make it a finding.

In substance the section is a restatement of the four separate defects already argued in the body, presented as a single ensemble criticism. It adds no defect that the per-clause analysis did not already contain, and it does not actually exhibit what "compounded" means beyond concatenation. On RSS's own terms — an account is a viewpoint, a reading, and a set of accepted conjectures, and criticism targets a specific element (a record, a rule, a booking history, a snapshot, a coordinate, or an assumption) — a compound criticism of the sort offered does not correspond to a target RSS recognizes. One can, of course, criticize a *combination* of commitments, but then the target is the commitment-set, not each memo clause, and the previous response switches between treating them as four separate interpretive claims and as one ensemble without marking the switch.

Why this is warranted: the task says a criticism is not required to invent an error, an improvement, or a new problem when none is supported. The previous response's own framing ("if the inquiry supplies one") concedes this, and then proceeds as if the inquiry had. That is a self-undermining move; the memo did not supply such a problem, and the response supplied it by relabeling.

Consequence: this section should be withdrawn as a distinct finding. The most defensible reading of the underlying point is as a projection observed *across* the memos — the same pattern of losing information by compressing — which is an observation about A2 alone plus a shared vocabulary, not a new fourth item. Any "ensemble" reading should be stated as a proposal about how to group the criticisms, attributed as such, not as an additional defect.

## Criticism 3 — "RSS's noncomputability... is respected" is not established by the argument that precedes it

The previous response's closing representation section asserts: "RSS's noncomputability and its refusal to select a reading are respected: the selection among readings in this answer is made by the declared contract for task purposes, and is so attributed."

This is a fair procedural note, but the argument body does not actually hold to it. In criticizing A1, the response treats the "expired deletes" reading as an alternative reading that RSS presents and does not choose. Good. But it then says "the contract fixes the task reading" — which is fine and correctly attributed — and yet elsewhere (in the A2 passage) appeals to RSS's "a projection is always a loss of information" as if RSS *thereby* supports R6's exactness rule. RSS does not support that rule; RSS says an interval is a projection from a set and is therefore a loss, but it does not say the projection is forbidden or that the set must be reported instead. R6 forbids the equating; RSS merely notes the loss. The two verdicts coincide on A2's conclusion but rest on different grounds, and the response fuses them.

Why this is warranted: R7 and the use policy require that when RSS and the contract diverge, the divergence be exposed rather than hidden; here they converge on the verdict but not on the reason, and the response conflates convergence of conclusion with convergence of ground. The divergence is small but real: RSS tolerates the interval as one account's projection, provided the loss is acknowledged; the contract forbids the equating in this task even with acknowledgment. The task rule is the contract's, and the response was right to say so, but it should not have cited RSS's projection caveat as if it were the contract's exactness rule in another vocabulary.

Consequence: the A2 verdict stands under R6. The RSS framing should be trimmed to "RSS supplies vocabulary for naming the loss; the prohibition is the contract's." That is what the response's own procedural closing says, and its body should not have said more.

## Criticism 4 — the "scope-governed, not settled" list quietly settles one item

The previous response's working-account list places three items under "Open or scope-governed, not settled here": the reading choice for expired holds; whether provenance should carry authority; whether a resource model should be added. The first of these, however, is settled *in this task* by R3 and R4, and the response itself says so at the top of the A1 passage: "Under R3, greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads before considering their effect... So the expired record stays, keeps its authority, and reserves zero; the older active hold does not become effective." That is a settled verdict under the contract. Listing "the reading choice for expired holds" as open conflates two different things: the *general* question of which reading a reasoner should adopt (open, and RSS declines to decide) versus the *task* reading (fixed by R3–R4). The list blurs these and reads as if the task verdict were provisional.

Why this is warranted: R5 is careful to say the specified task scope is "a specified task scope, not a universal claim," and the response should mirror that care. Saying the reading is "not settled here" when the contract settles it for the task overstates openness in the opposite direction from the memo it criticizes.

Consequence: the list should read "the general reading choice for expired holds (open in RSS and in the WHL specimen; fixed for this task by R3–R4)" so that the task verdict is not accidentally reopened.

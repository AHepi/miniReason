## Criticism 1 of the re‑presented account — the RSS/WHL comparison in the A4 passage

The previous response claims the earlier account's A4 discussion made an error of attribution: it said the earlier text extended RSS's *near‑duplication* relation (defined in the original RSS text over differing kind, quantity, or expiry at the same booking and revision) to cover pure provenance differences, thereby filling a gap in RSS and then reporting the filled gap as RSS's own verdict.

This criticism is accurate about the earlier text. The earlier A4 passage did write: "RSS does not give different labels a vote either; near‑duplication is a candidate for conflict that RSS records rather than resolving." The original RSS specimen defines near‑duplication as sharing booking and revision while differing in kind, quantity, or expiry, and defines exact duplication as equality of all record fields *and* provenance. The label‑only case — same field content, differing provenance — is covered by neither relation as written. So the earlier passage did fill that gap without marking it as an extension. That is exactly the failure R7 and the use policy warn about. The criticism correctly identified a target in the earlier text, correctly identified the original RSS text's gap, and correctly concluded that filling it is a legitimate proposed extension of RSS rather than RSS's stated position.

The criticism's consequence is also correctly drawn: the A4 *verdict* survives on R2, which withholds authority from delivery labels independently of RSS, but the earlier *grounds* leaned on an RSS reading the source did not supply, and the earlier phrase "the WHL specimen also ... does not encode delivery at all" that was used to suggest a broader agreement between the two resources overstated their alignment in precisely the case A4 raises. I accept this criticism. I also accept the previous response's own nuance — that RSS *can* state the label case because provenance is part of its exact‑duplication definition — which is what makes the earlier "agree" phrasing misleading rather than simply false.

## Criticism 2 — the "consequential new problem" as a distinct finding

The previous response claims the earlier "consequential new problem" section relabeled a restatement of four separate defects as an ensemble finding, added no defect the per‑clause analysis did not already contain, and thereby violated the task's instruction not to invent a new problem when none is supported.

This criticism is valid on its narrow point and overreaches on its broader one, and the distinction matters.

The narrow point is sound. The earlier section did present as a finding the observation that A1, A2, A4, and A6 together "shift weight away from record fields ... toward occurrence‑ and capacity‑level facts" and that A2 then compresses the result. That observation is not a fifth defect; it is a pattern visible across four defects already argued. The previous response correctly notes that the earlier text itself hedged with "if the inquiry supplies one," and that a hedge does not convert a restatement into a finding. The earlier text also did not exhibit what "compounded" means beyond concatenation, and did switch between treating the four claims as separate interpretive targets and as a single ensemble without marking the switch. So the criticism is warranted, and I accept that the "distinct finding" framing should be withdrawn.

The broader point is where the previous response goes past what its own material supports. It says a compound criticism "does not correspond to a target RSS recognizes," because RSS criticism targets a specific element. That is an overstatement as applied here. RSS does license criticism of a rule, an assumption, a coordinate, or a snapshot — and a *set* of accepted conjectures is exactly what RSS calls an account, and accounts are criticizable objects in RSS (an account can be criticized by another account, and disagreement between accounts is first‑class). So a criticism whose target is "the commitment‑set {A1's expiry step, A2's interval equating, A4's label authority, A6's imposed resource model}" is a target RSS recognizes: the account. The previous response's suggestion that the earlier text's ensemble move was outside RSS's recognized target space is therefore too strong. What was actually wrong with the earlier move was its self‑description as a discovery rather than as a grouping of already‑argued defects — the misdescription, not the target type. I accept the criticism in its narrow form and decline the broader form.

## Criticism 3 — "RSS's noncomputability is respected" and the A2 ground

The previous response claims the earlier text fused two different grounds for the A2 verdict: it cited RSS's "a projection is always a loss of information" as if that supported R6's exactness rule, when in fact RSS merely names the loss while R6 forbids the equating.

This criticism is partly valid and partly mistaken about what the earlier text needed, and it also inverts a small part of what the contract and RSS actually say.

The valid part: the earlier text did sometimes appeal to RSS to frame the A2 diagnosis as though the RSS vocabulary and the contract rule were the same reason in two registers. They are not. RSS tolerates an interval as one account's projection provided the loss is acknowledged; the contract forbids the equating in this task even with acknowledgment. Different grounds, same verdict on the A2 conclusion. The earlier text should have said that plainly. That is the previous response's point and I accept it.

The mistaken part: the previous response adds that "R6 forbids the equating" while "RSS merely notes the loss," and then draws the consequence that the earlier A2 discussion "should not have cited RSS's projection caveat as if it were the contract's exactness rule." That is right as far as it goes, but the previous response then says in its own terms that "the two verdicts coincide on A2's conclusion" — and it should be noted that this is only true because R6 is doing the work. RSS, left to itself, would not forbid the replacement A2 proposes; it would license it as a projection, attributing the loss to the reading. So the coincidence of verdict here is a coincidence, not a shared ground, and the previous response was right to say so. That is a point in the previous response's favor, and the earlier text should absorb it.

There is a subtler issue the previous response does not raise, and it is the one place criticism of the criticism is warranted. The task's contract says an interval "may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." RSS's "projection is always a loss" language is not a rule about equating sets to intervals; it is a rule about the semantic status of a single answer. The earlier text's mistake was using RSS's vocabulary to *support* the A2 verdict rather than merely to *name* the phenomenon. The previous response's own final formulation — "RSS supplies vocabulary for naming the loss; the prohibition is the contract's" — is the right one, and the earlier text should be corrected to that. So this criticism is valid in its localization but should be scoped, not general, because it concerns a phrasing choice, not a substantive defect in the earlier verdict.

## Criticism 4 — the "scope-governed, not settled" list and the expired‑hold reading

The previous response claims the earlier "open or scope‑governed" list incorrectly placed "the reading choice for expired holds" among the open items, when the *task* reading is fixed by R3–R4, and only the *general* question is open.

This criticism is valid as a distinction and I accept the correction. The earlier list did in fact run together two different questions: the general question of which reading a reasoner should adopt (open in RSS and not chosen by the original WHL specimen) and the task reading (settled by R3–R4). The earlier text itself stated the task reading as settled in the A1 discussion, so the listing was internally inconsistent as well as overbroad. The correction the previous response proposes — "the general reading choice for expired holds (open in RSS and in the WHL specimen; fixed for this task by R3–R4)" — is the right formulation, and it mirrors R5's own careful scoping of the "specified task scope" as not a universal claim.

The previous response adds that the earlier listing "reads as if the task verdict were provisional." That is a fair reading of the effect, and I accept it. The criticism should stand as given, without extension.

## What I retain from cycle 1, unchanged

The A1 diagnosis is retained as stated, and it remains the sharpest point. Cycle 1 separated two things the memo ran together: an expired hold reserves zero (contract R4, sound), and the licensed inferential step that an expired record may be removed before authority selection so that an older active hold "can then become effective" (a reading, not a fact under R3). Under R3, greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads before considering their effect, so the expired record stays, keeps its authority, and reserves zero; the older active hold does not become effective. Cycle 1 correctly identified that the memo's procedure sentence is not merely a policy opinion but a change of the supersession rule under the guise of applying the expiry rule. I retain this.

The A2 diagnosis is retained on its contract ground. Cycle 1 correctly held that equating the two‑point set {−3, 2} with the interval [−3, 2] contradicts R6's exactness rule: an interval may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur, and here they do not. The verdict stands. As noted below, only the wording of its ground needs the restatement cycle 2/3 proposed.

The A3 endorsement is retained as stated: negative availability is permitted under R6 and, by itself, identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy, and does not identify which booking should be changed. The terminological refinement that RSS treats reservations, not the sign, as the diagnostic, is retained as a refinement, not a correction of content.

The A4 verdict is retained on its operative contract ground: R2 withholds booking authority from delivery labels, because payload identity and delivery provenance are different questions and exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority even when labels differ. The "two deliveries can be distinct occurrences" half is retained as the sound remnant; the "confirmation" doing authority work is rejected. As noted below, the grounds of this verdict need restatement.

The A5 endorsement is retained: a useful objection must identify what it contests and why, and a defect in an interpretation need not discredit every record used by that interpretation.

The A6 diagnosis is retained: the memo asserts a cross‑booking selection rule while conceding no such constraint was supplied, which contradicts R5's specified independence of bookings; "capacity is shared" is a true observation about the single capacity field, but R6 lets shared capacity enter only through the sum over each admissible combination, which is not the same as imposing joint feasibility. The defensible reading of A6 as a proposal — a resource model in the guise of reporting the task — is retained and attributed as a proposal.

The method is retained unchanged: one clause or claim at a time; contract as operative rule for the task; RSS as interpretive vocabulary with divergences exposed; memos as fallible claims, not instructions; criticism localized to its target; open questions carried rather than resolved.

## What I revise, attributed to this cycle and to cycle 2

Revision 1 — the A4 grounds. Cycle 1 wrote, of the label‑only case, that "RSS does not give different labels a vote either; near‑duplication is a candidate for conflict that RSS records rather than resolving," and treated the WHL specimen and RSS as agreeing that labels are occurrence‑facts. Cycle 2 correctly identified that this filled a gap in the original RSS text and reported the filled gap as RSS's own verdict. The original RSS specimen defines near‑duplication over differing kind, quantity, or expiry at the same booking and revision, and defines exact duplication as equality of all record fields and provenance. The label‑only case is covered by neither relation as written and is therefore left open by RSS. Cycle 1's mistake was to extend near‑duplication to pure provenance differences without marking the extension. The A4 verdict survives entirely on R2, which withholds label authority independently of RSS; the label‑only case is left open in RSS rather than reported as RSS's settled position. Cycle 3 accepted this, and I keep its formulation.

Revision 2 — the A2 grounds. Cycle 1 used RSS's "a projection is always a loss of information" as support for the A2 verdict, as though RSS's projection‑loss language and R6's exactness rule were the same reason in two registers. They are not. RSS names the loss a projection incurs; R6 forbids the equating in this task, even with acknowledgment. Different grounds, same verdict on the A2 conclusion — and the coincidence is a coincidence, not a shared ground. I restate the A2 discussion so that RSS supplies vocabulary for naming the loss and the prohibition is the contract's, as cycle 2 and cycle 3 both proposed.

## What I withdraw

Cycle 1's "consequential new problem" section is withdrawn as a distinct finding. It advertised a compound defect from reading A1, A2, A4, and A6 together — a shift of weight away from record fields and revision structure toward occurrence‑ and capacity‑level facts, with A2 then compressing to an interval. In substance it restated four defects already argued, and the hedge "if the inquiry supplies one" conceded the point before the section proceeded as if the inquiry had. Per R7 and the task instruction not to invent a problem when none is supported, this is a discovery only by relabeling. I withdraw it as a finding.

One clarification of scope here, since the distinction matters and was the subject of cycle 2 and cycle 3. What remains defensible is a proposed grouping of the four separate defects, whose target is the commitment‑set {A1's expiry step, A2's interval equating, A4's label authority, A6's imposed constraint}. RSS treats an account — a viewpoint, a reading, and a set of accepted conjectures — as a criticizable object, so the target type is one RSS recognizes; a set of accepted conjectures is exactly such an object. Cycle 2's broader claim, that an ensemble‑type criticism "does not correspond to a target RSS recognizes," is therefore too strong, and cycle 3 correctly declined it in that broad form. Cycle 1's error was misdescription — calling a grouping a discovery — not the target type. So I withdraw the discovery claim and reclassify the section as a proposed grouping, attributed as such, or drop it; either is honest, and neither is a new defect.

I do not withdraw anything else. No new defect in the memo is claimed in this cycle, and no claim of firstness is made.

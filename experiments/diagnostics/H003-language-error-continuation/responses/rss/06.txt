## Clause-by-clause investigation of Memo B

**B1.** Two claims are bundled. The sound part: an audit report and a report produced by copying the same intermediate file are two *documents*, so there are two artefacts in hand. The defective part is the inference drawn — that they are "two independent evidential routes." Copying from the same intermediate file is a single provenance, and independent confirmation requires no shared upstream. RSS has the exact vocabulary for this: **provenance is a set of delivery events**, and the RSS relation "exact duplication … adds no authority" is the whole point of treating shared provenance as inert. Under RSS-as-given, "independent evidential routes" is not a relation RSS can underwrite by counting documents; on the supplied facts the two reports are one provenance with two appearances. The contract's R2 ("payload identity and delivery provenance are different questions") points the same way for a different reason: two representations of one payload are not two authorities. I attribute the defect *as a defect in B1's connecting step*, not in the observation that two reports exist. B1 is a variant of the earlier A4 error (label/copy conferring authority) — the mechanism is the same, one level up (documents instead of deliveries). I treat it as B1 its own case, not as evidence of A4's severity.

**B2.** This is false as a general claim and the falsity is in the inference, not the display rule. RSS distinguishes *liveness under a viewpoint* from *existence in the bag*: a record not currently "greatest" is still a delivered record; supersession is structural, not erasure. A latest-visible-revision display carries the information "no higher revision is visible *in this view*"; it does not carry "no lower record was ever delivered." The same distinction survives under the WHL specimen's own note that superseded records remain members of the bag until the reading deletes them, and under R3 (a lower revision remains superseded but present). B2 is therefore a **viewpoint-vs-bag** conflation. RSS names the missing apparatus: RSS may obstruct "the very idea of a snapshot as a single object, because it treats a snapshot as one viewpoint among many." No extension is required to expose the error; it is exposed by the primitives RSS already has.

**B3.** The observation ("the hold expires at time 5; current time is 5") is fine. The conclusion ("it is still active") turns on the reading, and under the operative reading it is wrong. RSS distinguishes two readings of expiry explicitly — retain-superseding-but-reserve-zero versus delete-and-let-lower-revisions-become-live — and does not choose. The task contract chooses the first (R4: "at equality or later it reserves zero"); it does **not** say the effect "persists through the named expiry instant." "Persist through" would be a third reading in which the quantity is still reserved at time = expiry; neither RSS-as-given nor R4 adopts it. RSS's reading puts expiry effect as a *reading* parallel to the reserve-zero reading; the contract's R4 rules the reserve-zero reading operative; B3's conclusion is adjudicated accordingly. This is the earlier A1 error's expiry half, restated for a different value of expiry.

**B4.** Sound, and RSS-native. RSS separates *account* (viewpoint + reading + accepted conjectures) from any particular physical warehouse; an account may state a conditional consequence under its reading without asserting that the reading is physically realised. RSS's interpretation section is exactly this: "A statement … is interpreted in RSS by locating its primitives and relations." I retain B4.

**B5.** False, and false for a reason the previous own-arm cycle already established. Bounds are a lossy projection; RSS names the loss ("a projection is always a loss of information"); the contract's R6 forbids equating the interval [−2, 3] with the exact set {3, −2} on the snapshot we analysed, because the interval admits −1, 0, 1, 2 that no admissible combination produces. Agreement of consumed bounds with a checker's bounds answers the *bounds question*; it does not answer criticisms of the represented *alternatives* (qty 3 vs qty 8 for B, release-vs-hold reading for A, provenance handling for B's duplicates). B5 is the earlier A2 error (set = interval) restated with an extra premise ("bounds agree with checker") that does not close the gap. Attribute: I am not claiming B5 is a new defect; it is the A2 defect with a new trigger.

**B6.** Two parts, and the parts have different status.
(a) "Rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy." Retained. This is the same RSS-level observation as the earlier A6 defect: RSS lists as an *unresolved expressive limitation* that it "may obstruct reasoning about physical resources" and "treats a snapshot as one viewpoint among many"; without a resource model as a primitive, cross-booking coupling is invisible. Rejecting R5 is exactly how a reasoner would demand a coupling policy. The demand is legitimate but the policy is not supplied by RSS or by the contract.
(b) "The old result under the originally specified independent-choice task remains a distinct conditional claim." Retained, with an attribution: the old result is a claim made *under* R5-conditions. Under R7, the old result is conditional on the task-scope assumptions, and rejecting R5 does not refute it; it suspends its applicability to the widened scope. This matches the earlier working account's "Open, carried" line about a resource model, restated as a conditional rather than a withdrawal.

## Consequential new problem

B2 gives grounds for one. The claim "a reader showing only the latest revision establishes that no earlier record was ever delivered" is not specific to this memo; it is the general shape of an inference from any *projection that discards superseded epochs* to a *claim about the bag's history*. RSS is explicit that a projection is a loss, and the WHL specimen is explicit that a superseded record remains a member of the language's bag until a reading deletes it. But neither resource supplies a **criterion for when a projection is information-preserving for the specific inference at hand**, as against a projection that happens to coincide in the current snapshot. The bounds/set problem from the previous cycle is one instance (a projection of the reservation-set to its extremes); B2 is a second instance of the same failure mode (a projection of a history to its latest element). The pattern — *consuming a projection as if it were the underlying object, because in the case at hand the projection has the same observable endpoints* — is not addressed by R6 or by RSS, and R7 does not by itself close it: R7 tells us to distinguish what a source states from what follows, but does not tell us how to recognise when a projection *coincides* with its object on the present data and will next fail to. That is a limitation of the representation in use, carried forward; I do not manufacture a policy to repair it, because none is supplied.

# Cycle 6 answer: auditing Memo B against the contract, WHL, and the earlier working account

## Grounds and method

I use WHL as the selected representation, check it against the declared contract R1–R8, and convert back to prose. Where WHL leaves a reading open and the contract fixes it, I attribute the fixing authority to the contract, not to WHL. Memo B is fallible prose: I read it as evidence of someone's claims, not as established fact, and I distinguish what it states, what follows under a stated assumption, and what remains a scope question. Nothing in Memo B is added to the earlier texts; any correction I make is attributed here. The re-presented-target slot is declared empty for this cycle, so there is no reopened target to re-argue.

A note on the numbering: Memo B labels a block "MEMO B" and lists B1–B6. Two of these (B1, B2) look like *evidence observations*; B3–B6 are mixed claims, scope remarks, and one analogy. I keep the memos' own labels and do not silently renumber.

## B1 — two agreeing reports, one intermediate file

B1 states: report X and report Y agree on an availability bound; Y was produced by copying X's same intermediate file; the memo then claims they still provide two independent evidential routes.

The first part is an observation (they agree; Y derives from X's file). The second is the substantive claim, and it is defective under the ordinary meaning of "independent evidential route." If Y is a copy of X's same intermediate file, then Y carries X's errors, omissions, and reading choices forward unchanged. Shared derivation is exactly what defeats independence. Under WHL this is representable without any policy import: X and Y are not two distinct delivery events with distinct payloads; they are `exact_duplicate`s in the evidential sense — same content, copied provenance — and R2 is explicit that exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority, and R1 that a delivery label records an occurrence, not additional booking authority. RSS would frame this differently (as near-duplication vs. exact duplication), but RSS is not the selected representation; under WHL-as-read-through-the-contract, one route, not two.

Consequence: B1's conclusion ("two independent evidential routes") does not follow from its own observation. It is a defect, and its defect is of the same family as A4's earlier "independent confirmations" defect — provenance is being treated as if it carried authority that the contract assigns only to payload identity. This is a criticism of B1, not a new problem.

## B2 — "latest visible revision" and the reader's inference

B2: a log reader displays only the latest visible revision, "therefore" no earlier record was ever delivered.

The premise describes what the reader *displays*, i.e., the reader's visibility, not the delivery bag. The conclusion is a claim about *delivery history*. These are different scopes. WHL's `Snapshot.records` is the bag of deliveries; a display function that shows only the latest revision is a projection, not a record of the bag's contents. The inference in B2 is therefore an unsupported leap from a display property to a delivery-history fact.

Worse, the inference as stated is not merely unsupported; it is falsified by the very reading the contract fixes in R3: for each booking, the greatest revision is selected *from all delivered payloads* before effect is considered. If a higher-revision record is present and a lower one is also present, the lower one *was delivered* and is merely superseded. Supersession is precisely the phenomenon that makes "not displayed" compatible with "delivered." So B2's conclusion, if it is about delivery, is false under the contract whenever an earlier record coexists with a later one.

If B2 is instead read as a tautology ("the reader displays only what it displays, so it displays no earlier revision"), it is trivially true and does nothing for any warehouse question. I keep both readings on the table because Memo B does not disambiguate; the non-tautological reading is a defect.

## B3 — expiry at the named instant

B3: the hold expires at time 5; at current time 5 it is still active, because its effect should persist through the named expiry instant.

This is the expiry *policy* question the earlier cycles already located. The contract's R4 is explicit and is the deciding authority for this task: a hold reserves its quantity exactly when current time is *strictly less* than expiry; at equality or later it reserves zero. So at current time 5 with expiry 5, the hold reserves 0 under the contract. B3's announced rule — "persist through the named expiry instant," i.e., treat `t ≤ expiry` as active — is a *different rule* from the contract's `t < expiry`. It is statable in WHL (it is a different `reserves_effect`), it is the kind of reading WHL's notes explicitly declare open, and RSS would represent it as a coexisting reading. But the contract fixes the strict-inequality reading, and B3 contradicts it.

I record B3 as a *rule proposal* or *reading substitution*, not as a factual error about any particular snapshot, because the memo presents it as a normative "should." That framing does not rescue it as an answer to a snapshot question in this task; the task asks what the contract's rule yields. Under the contract the hold is expired at equality, reserving zero. B3 is defective *relative to this task's semantics*, and the defect is on the evaluative side (expiry effect), whereas A1's earlier defect was on the structural side (deletion reviving a lower revision). These are adjacent but distinct: B3 does not delete the record or revive a lower revision; it only shifts the boundary between active and expired. Still, both fall under "expiry changed when the contract had fixed it."

One point of caution I keep: B3's wording "persist through the named expiry instant" is itself ambiguous between "reserves up to and including t = expiry" and "the record remains present at t = expiry." The second is compatible with the contract ("expiry changes a retained record's effect, not its revision authority"); the first is not. I attribute the disagreement to the first, stronger reading, since the "still active" phrasing implies the reservation is unchanged at t = expiry.

## B4 — reporting a true conditional consequence without showing the assumption applies

B4: an account can report a true conditional consequence without showing that its assumptions apply to a particular physical warehouse.

This is sound as stated and it agrees with the contract's R7 distinction between what follows under an assumption and what is observed. WHL supports it structurally: a `Conjecture : Snapshot → Prop` can be proved of an abstract snapshot without claiming any specific warehouse realizes that snapshot; `Refutation` likewise attaches to a snapshot, not to a warehouse. So "conditional truth" and "physical applicability" are separable, and asserting the first does not establish the second.

I record this as a retained claim, in the same family as A3 and A5. Nothing here needs correction. If anything, B4 is the most useful *methodological* sentence in Memo B, and it is in tension with some of the surrounding items: B1 and B2 both commit exactly the mistake B4 warns against — treating an inferential step as if it established applicability to the intended case.

## B5 — bounds agreeing with a checker "answers every criticism"

B5: once the output bounds agree with a checker's bounds, every criticism of the represented exact alternatives is answered.

This is defective on the same gap the earlier cycles already identified between *bounds* and *exact alternatives* (memo A's A2). R6 is explicit: bounds must not be equated with the exact set unless every intervening admitted integer value occurs. So agreement on bounds is a check on two numbers computed as capacity minus summed per-booking min/max reservations; it is not a check on which alternatives were selected, nor on whether the alternatives were correctly identified as alternatives at the greatest revision, nor on whether duplicates were correctly treated as inert.

Concretely, in the prior worked snapshot, the bounds were 3 (lower) and −2 (upper) in the WHL function orientation, i.e., the interval [−2, 3], while the exact set was {−2, 3}. Two checkers agreeing on [−2, 3] would agree on the bounds and could still both be wrong about whether the exact set is {−2, 3} or, say, an interval containing all integers in between — the very confusion A2 committed. So B5's claim that bound agreement "answers every criticism of the represented exact alternatives" is false. Bound agreement answers *bound* criticism; criticism of the alternatives themselves (their identity, their inertness, their number) is not answered.

This is a scope defect: B5 raises the evidential status of a narrow check to a general discharge. It is the same scope error family as B1 (provenance inflated into independence) and B2 (display inflated into delivery history).

## B6 — rejecting independence opens a question about coupling, and the old result remains conditional

B6: rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy; the old result under the originally specified independent-choice task remains a distinct conditional claim.

This is, in my reading, the strongest single sentence in Memo B and is largely sound. It does two things the contract supports:

- Rejecting the independence rule (R5, "no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied") is a legitimate criticism, and it targets the rule, not any snapshot. R7 permits a criticism to target a rule. Rejecting that rule does not by itself give a replacement; it opens the *question* of what the coupling policy should be. That is a genuine scope question, not a defect.
- The earlier numeric result (in the prior cycle: exact set {−2, 3} for the worked snapshot; and generally, capacity minus sum of independently selected per-booking reservations) was produced under the independent-choice task scope. If independence is later replaced by some coupling constraint, the earlier result is not refuted; it becomes a *conditional claim* whose antecedent is the independence scope. This is exactly R7's point that rejecting a supporting assumption defeats the support route without proving the conclusion false, presented in reverse — accepting a new scope does not retroactively falsify results proved under the old one.

So B6 is retained as sound, with one clarification I supply explicitly: what would have to be supplied to investigate the coupling question *without manufacturing a policy* is (i) an explicit replacement coupling rule (which the inquiry does not supply — substituting one now would be the manufacture the question asks us to avoid), (ii) enough data about cross-booking interactions (unstated physical resource sharing, shared accounting periods, or explicit exclusion constraints), or (iii) an explicit rejection of independence accompanied by a commitment to leave availability-set members undetermined until (i) or (ii) arrives. Under any of these, the earlier independent-scope result must be *relabeled* as conditional, not overwritten. That relabeling is the entire required move; nothing in Memo B licenses picking a specific coupling rule.

A small caution: B6 says the old result "remains a distinct conditional claim," which is right, but I keep the antecedent explicit — the antecedent is not merely "bookings are independent" in the abstract, but "independence as specified in R5, with no cross-booking coupling." If someone were to supply a coupling constraint and still want to call the old result "conditional on independence," that is fine only if the old task-scope is preserved as the antecedent. I do not read B6 as collapsing the two.

## The consequential new problem, if any

The task asks me to identify a consequential new problem only if the inquiry supplies grounds. I do not think Memo B supplies one. B1, B2, B3, B5 repeat or transpose defects already characterized in earlier cycles (provenance inflation, display-vs-history, expiry re-reading, bounds-vs-exact-set). B4 is sound and old. B6 raises a scope question that the earlier cycles already flagged as an open question about cross-booking coupling. So the honest verdict is: Memo B supplies a *rehearsal* of the working account's defects, in a new set of surface forms, plus one sound scope remark.

There is one genuinely *new* item worth naming, but it is not a new problem; it is a new *instance* of an old problem, and it is consequential because it shows the earlier fix does not automatically transfer to neighboring statements. Specifically, B2 is the first place in this inquiry where the projection of *visibility* (what a log reader displays) has been substituted for the property *delivery*. Earlier defects turned on provenance, expiry, bounds, and cross-booking choice; B2 turns on a display-vs-bag substitution. The reason this is worth naming rather than absorbed into "another A2-family mistake" is that it names a *new pair of representational slots* in WHL whose conflation produces the same class of error: `Snapshot.records` (the bag) versus any function from the bag to a rendered view. WHL does not name that function; the notes' mention of "views" and RSS's "viewpoint" gesture at it, but WHL itself has no projection primitive, so a user of WHL is structurally invited to treat "what I can see" as "what was delivered." That is a limitation of WHL worth stating. But it is a limitation, not a refutation, and it is not a *new problem* in the sense the task asks for — it is an old problem located at a new interface.

## Usable working account

The following is what I would carry forward from this cycle, stated so that it stays convertible to ordinary prose and does not smuggle in a policy:

1. **Evidential weight.** A copied or shared-derivation document is an `exact_duplicate` in the evidential sense; it adds no independent support. Independence requires distinct derivation, not distinct labels. (Targets B1.)
2. **Display vs. bag.** What a view shows is not what was delivered. Supersession explains why a delivered lower revision is not displayed; it does not explain away the delivery. (Targets B2.)
3. **Expiry boundary.** Under the contract, activity is `t < expiry`, strict; equality expires. B3's reading is statable but is excluded for this task; the deciding authority is the contract, not WHL. (Targets B3; re-confirms earlier attribution.)
4. **Conditionals and applicability.** Conditional truth is separable from physical applicability. (Retains B4; consistent with R7.)
5. **Bounds vs. exact set.** Bounds agreement is evidence about two numbers, not about the represented alternatives. R6 forbids equating bounds with the exact set unless every intervening admitted integer occurs. (Targets B5; carries forward the A2 correction.)
6. **Independence scope.** Rejecting independence opens a coupling question; answering it requires a supplied coupling rule or resource model, which this inquiry has not supplied. Until then, the independent-scope results stay as conditional claims with the independence scope as antecedent. (Retains B6; supplies the explicit "what would have to be supplied" without manufacturing a policy.)

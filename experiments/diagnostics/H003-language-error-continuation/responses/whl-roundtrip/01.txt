## Cycle 1 answer: working account of Memo A

### Grounds and method

I am using WHL as the selected representation, then converting back to prose. The declared contract (R1–R8), not Memo A, fixes the semantics. Memo A is a fallible analyst's note; the task says its presence is not an instruction to endorse it. So each numbered item is treated as a claim with its own grounds, and I separate: what the contract states, what Memo A asserts, what follows only under an extra assumption, and what remains open.

The relevant WHL vocabulary: `Record` (booking, revision, kind, quantity, expiry); `exact_duplicate` (all five fields equal); `higher_revision` / `dominated_in` / `greatest_revision_in`; `active_hold` and `expired_hold`; `reserves_effect`; `possible_reservations`; `alternatives_at_greatest`; `availability_lower_bound` / `availability_upper_bound` via `Int.sub_nat`; and the contrast rule `expired_deletes_rule`, with `deletion_changes_greatest` stated as a *possible* disagreement rather than a chosen fact.

### Item-by-item

**A3 — sound.** Under R6 and the WHL encoding, availability is `capacity − sum of reservations` and is permitted to be negative; negative availability "alone identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy." A3 states exactly this and only this. It adds no further inference. Its ground is the contract itself (R6; `Int.sub_nat`). No defect. Scope note: A3 says the sign by itself doesn't localize the responsible booking; that is weaker than saying no diagnosis is ever possible, and it is correctly weak.

**A5 — sound as stated.** R7 distinguishes what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed or disputed; R7 also says a criticism can target a rule, data, an interpretation, an earlier criticism, or the representation. A5 says a useful objection identifies what it contests and why, and that a defect in an interpretation need not discredit every record the interpretation used. Both match R7 and the WHL separation between rule-conjectures (`ReservationRule`) and data-conjectures (`Record`/`Snapshot`). No defect. A5 is normative in flavor ("useful"), but it does not claim more than R7 licenses.

**A1 — defective. This is the central correction.** A1 asserts that a recently expired record should be *removed before* deciding which revision has authority, so that an older active hold can become effective. Under R3 and R4, and under WHL's `greatest_revision_in` + `reserves_effect`:
- Greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads *before* expiry is evaluated.
- Expiry changes a retained record's effect, not its revision authority: an expired hold reserves zero but still supersedes a lower revision.
- A lower-revision hold does not become effective merely because the greatest-revision hold has expired.

So A1 conflicts with the declared contract. The outcome A1 describes — removal making a lower revision live — is exactly WHL's `expired_deletes_rule` / `deletion_changes_greatest` alternative reading, which WHL explicitly represents as a *rival reading*, not as the standard one, and does not choose. A1 has silently switched readings. That is the error, and it is not harmless: it changes which booking reserves what, hence changes `possible_reservations` and the availability bounds.

I preserve the distinction the contract requires: A1 is not refuted as a proposal about an *alternative* reading — WHL leaves that reading statable — but it is false as a description of the declared standard reading, and treating it as standard is unwarranted. The correction is scoped to the authority question, not to the whole memo.

**A4 — defective.** A4 says two deliveries with different delivery labels are independent confirmations and therefore give their common reservation *more authority* than a single conflicting alternative. This collides with R1, R2, and the WHL notes: the delivery label records an occurrence, not additional booking authority; exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority even when labels differ; payload identity and delivery provenance are different questions. WHL's `exact_duplicate` is defined on the five record fields, and duplicates are "inert with respect to each other" — adding more copies adds nothing. So counting labels as extra weight introduces authority the contract does not grant.

I attribute the relevant distinction carefully: the RSS specimen *does* give provenance to stories and treats exact duplicates as requiring equal provenance, and allows near-duplicates as conflict material. But RSS is not the selected representation here, and even RSS does not convert distinct provenance into "more authority" for a shared reservation — it records disagreement rather than weighting it. So A4's numeric/authority weighting is unsupported in both sources. "Independent confirmations" also imports a corroboration model the task scope does not supply (R5: no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied).

**A2 — defective as a general claim, salvageable only with a stated condition.** A2 says that if the available values are −3 and 2, reporting the interval [−3,2] "retains every decision-relevant possibility exactly" and is harmless to substitute downstream. Under R6, reporting bounds is allowed, but an interval "must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." Here the exact set is {−3, 2}; the interval [−3,2] also contains −2, −1, 0, 1. Unless each of those is independently attainable — which A2 does not establish — the interval adds admitted values that are not possible and is *not* exact. Substituting it downstream is not harmless; it can produce answers outside the true possibility set. The correct report is either the exact set {−3,2} or bounds explicitly labelled as bounds. A2's phrase "exactly" is the specific overreach.

**A6 — defective/unsupported.** A6 says that because capacity is shared, the task "already imposes a rule forbidding simultaneous high alternatives from different bookings, even though no such selection constraint was supplied." This contradicts R5: exactly one payload alternative is selected per booking; alternatives of different bookings are *independently admissible*; no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied. In WHL, `possible_reservations` is per booking and there is no global choice function; capacity is the only global quantity, and it enters only through the summed reservations (R6). Capacity being shared constrains the *arithmetic*, not the *admissibility* of combinations. So A6 asserts a constraint the contract explicitly disclaims. Its own self-undermining clause ("even though no such selection constraint was supplied") states the contract's position while drawing the opposite conclusion. This is a represented limitation, not a hidden rule: both WHL and RSS mark cross-booking resource compatibility as invisible unless a resource model is added.

### Where Memo A is not wrong but raises unresolved scope questions

- A1's underlying phenomenon is real *material*: `deletion_changes_greatest` in WHL asserts that such a deletion reading *can* differ, and even checks the shape (a greatest-revision expired hold with an active lower-revision hold on the same booking). The open question is only which reading is in force. Memo A decides that without authorization; it should have flagged it as a reading choice.
- A4's underlying observation — that distinct deliveries exist and can be evidenced by labels — is fine as provenance observation. What is unsupported is converting provenance into added authority. WHL keeps payload identity and delivery provenance as separate questions; that separation is the resource that keeps this straightened out.
- A2 correctly notices that negative availability and positive availability can coexist. The open question is whether intermediate values occur; that is exactly the condition R6 names, and it is decidable in a given snapshot but not assumed.

### Consequential new problem (only if grounded)

The inquiry does supply one: A1, A2, A4, and A6 are not independent slips. Each silently imports a *reading* or an *outside selection policy* that neither the contract nor WHL supplies — deletion-before-supersession (A1), exact-set/interval equivalence (A2), authority-weighting of provenance (A4), cross-booking exclusivity (A6). WHL anticipates exactly these by providing `expired_deletes_rule` as a statable rival, by refusing to choose among readings, and by making `possible_reservations` per-booking. So the consequential problem is: *Memo A repeatedly substitutes alternative or unsupplied readings for the declared standard reading without marking the substitution.* The consequence for downstream calculations is concrete: wrong `greatest_revision_in` sets, wrong `possible_reservations`, wrong availability sets or illegitimate interval reporting, and defeat of R5's independence.

I claim this as a problem about the memo's *use of readings*, not as proof that any single alternative reading is false. R7 requires exactly that scoping.

### Usable working account (WHL-encoded, rendered back to prose)

1. **Authority:** For each booking, select `greatest_revision_in` from all delivered payloads first. Expiry never removes a record from the authority comparison; it only affects `reserves_effect` (R3, R4). The alternative "expired deletes the record" is statable (`expired_deletes_rule`) but is not the declared reading; using it must be announced and justified (A1 correction).
2. **Payload vs. provenance:** `exact_duplicate` is identity of the five record fields. Distinct delivery labels do not add authority; near-duplicates are alternative payloads, not corroborations (R1, R2; A4 correction).
3. **Reservation:** release reserves 0; hold reserves `quantity` iff `current_time < expiry`, else 0 (R4).
4. **Alternatives:** at the greatest revision, each non-identical payload is an alternative; exactly one is selected per booking; different bookings are independently admissible with no supplied cross-booking constraint (R5; A6 correction).
5. **Availability:** `capacity − Σ selected reservations`, possibly negative. Report the exact set of attainable values; you may report bounds but must not equate an interval with the set unless every intervening admitted integer occurs (R6; A2 correction).
6. **Criticism discipline:** distinguish statement, observation, assumption-relative consequence, and proposal; a defect in an interpretation does not by itself discredit all its records (R7; A5 preserved).
7. **Uncertainty:** where a reading is genuinely not fixed (expiry policy, release-cancellation policy, whether intervals are exact), record it as open rather than resolved.

I treat A1 as a reading-substitution error while preserving the possibility that its *alternative* reading is statable. I attribute no historical firstness and invent no new error beyond what the memo and contract jointly support. Where the RSS specimen would have framed "independent confirmation" as provenance, I note that this is outside the selected representation and that even RSS does not license authority weighting.

I am not manufacturing this correction — it is forced by the present cycle's declared inputs. I do not assert what the true prior-context state was; I assert only that as supplied here, the emptiness claim conflicts with the re-presented target.

### C2. The previous response's §"Consequential new problem" over-couples A2 and A6

The previous response proposes that "A2 and A6 are mutually reinforcing representation errors," giving a scenario in which a report of {-3, 2} "could be silently widened or narrowed and then re-flattened." This is a legitimate observation about a *combined downstream risk*, but the response presents it as if A2 and A6 were one defect. They are not.

- A2 is a defect about **representing the exact set** (R6: an interval must not be equated with the exact set unless all intervening admitted integers occur).
- A6 is a defect about **supplying an unsupplied cross-booking constraint** (R5: alternatives of different bookings are independently admissible; no cross-booking constraint is supplied).

They belong to different complaint types and can be evaluated independently: A2 fails even if A6 is harmless, and A6 fails even if reporting is done exactly. R7's discipline is to target a *specific* element; bundling them under one "mutually reinforcing" heading blurs the target. The response's prose phrasing ("if both errors are active") already concedes they are separable, so the fix is one of framing, not of substance. **Consequence:** the criticism of A2 and the criticism of A6 should each be carried forward as their own defeat under their own grounds (R6 for A2; R5/R6 for A6), with at most a downstream note that a calculation that commits both errors can lose the exact-set property. That downstream note is a *derived* risk, not a merger of the two grounds.

This is an internal framing defect in my previous response, not a defect in the contract or specimens.

### C3. The previous response's treatment of A3 is correct but under-specified about its second clause

The previous response marks A3 sound and adds that "identifying a responsible booking requires inspecting the per-booking reservations." That is right, but the response does not say *why* this is supported rather than merely plausible. Ground: R6 says negative availability "alone identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy"; RSS's "Negative availability" entry says RSS "treats the set of reservations as more informative than the sign." The previous response cites both — but cites the RSS line as a "caveat on scope, not defect," which undersells it. The RSS line is direct support for the second clause, not a side remark. **Consequence:** A3's soundness should be attributed to R6 and RSS together, not to R6 with an RSS aside. Minor; the appraisal of A3 does not change.

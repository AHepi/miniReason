## C1 — the "emptiness"/"no previous own-arm response" correction

This is the correction I stated most emphatically, and it is the one that most needs re-examination.

What C1 asserted: the prior response's closing claim — "No earlier target was re-presented ([a91fb4a5538c4cdf] is empty), and there is no previous own-arm response" — was a bookkeeping error, because the present cycle shows a previous own-arm response ([30b4cb342461199e]) being re-presented as a target. From the present materials, I said, the emptiness claim is contradicted and must be withdrawn.

Re-examining the grounds: the re-presented target block names artifact [30b4cb342461199e] and ties it to the cycle-1 own-arm response. The prior verdict's closing sentence was written *from within cycle-1's frame*, where the reopen artifact identifier and the own-arm response were both in play. The relevant question for a criticism is not "is the prior sentence true now" but "did the prior sentence accurately describe the materials it was working from, and does its conclusion still hold." Two distinct issues are being run together:

1. **A contingent bookkeeping claim**: whether, at the moment the cycle-1 response was composed, the re-presentation slot was empty or not. My prior verdict substituted the present-cycle evidence for that question. That is exactly the move R7 warns against — it collapses *what a source states* (cycle-1's self-report about its own inputs) with *what I now observe* (cycle-3's target list). The prior response may have been wrong, or it may have been accurately describing inputs that differ from the ones I now see. My verdict asserted the former without establishing that the inputs were the same.

2. **A substantive claim**: whether the prior response's appraisals depended on the emptiness claim. My verdict itself conceded they did not.

So C1 is **partly valid, partly invalid, and not cleanly attributable**:

- **Valid part:** the prior response's sentence is at least *internally odd* — a response cannot coherently assert there is no previous own-arm response if it is itself that response. That is a genuine tension detectable from the artifact's own text, without importing external cycle evidence.
- **Invalid/overreaching part:** my verdict justified the correction by appealing to cycle-3's target list, thereby treating current-cycle observation as if it settled a cycle-1 fact. That is the classic criticism error of *shifting the target's evidential base*. R7 is explicit: rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat a support route without proving the conclusion false. Here I did something weaker — I defeated the "emptiness" *sentence* by re-pointing at a target it may not have had access to, and then labeled it a factual correction. The label overstates.
- **Correct status:** this is best carried as an **open question** — the prior response's meta-claim is suspect on internal-consistency grounds (the "cannot be both" argument survives), but whether it was a factual error about its own inputs cannot be settled from where I stand, and my verdict wrongly presented it as settled. What should return to the earlier method: the self-consistency worry, as a worry; not a confident withdrawal.

This matters for method because the prior verdict's own operating discipline (R7's separation of stated/observed/inferred/proposed) was the exact instrument it failed to apply to itself.

## C2 — separating A2 and A6

The prior verdict argued that my cycle-1 response had "over-coupled" A2 and A6 by presenting them as "mutually reinforcing representation errors," and that they should instead be carried as separately grounded defeats (R6 for A2; R5/R6 for A6), with the combined report-loss as a derived note.

Grounds check: A2 (equating an exact set with an interval) and A6 (importing an unsupplied cross-booking constraint) do rest on different contract clauses and are independently evaluable. The prior verdict's *substantive* separation is correct. The question for this cycle is whether the prior verdict accurately characterized what the cycle-1 response did.

Re-reading the cycle-1 text: it presented A2 and A6 under a heading "Consequential **new problem**," with the specific phrasing "if both errors are active," and described the combined effect as a downstream risk to exact-set recoverability. That is already a *conditional* framing of a derived risk, not a merger of grounds. The cycle-1 response had separately defeated A2 and A6 earlier in its own body, each under its own clause. So my prior verdict's charge of "over-coupling" **overstates the defect**: the cycle-1 response had already separated them in its principal appraisals, and the combined section was explicitly conditioned. The correct assessment is:

- The separation instruction (C2's operative content) is **sound and worth carrying**.
- The diagnosis that cycle-1 had *blurred* the targets is **partly invalid**: cycle-1's body had kept them apart; only the section heading and the phrase "mutually reinforcing" invited a merger reading, and even there the prose conditioned it. This is a framing improvement offered under the guise of a defect. It is a legitimate *suggestion*, weakly a correction.

So C2 should be returned to the earlier method as a **refinement**, downgraded from "internal framing defect" to "acceptable clarification." It is not a case where the earlier criticism (cycle-1) was wrong; it is a case where my verdict about that criticism was slightly too strong.

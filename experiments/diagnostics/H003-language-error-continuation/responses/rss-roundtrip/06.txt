## 2. Claim-by-claim investigation

### B1 — "Report X and report Y agree on a bound; Y was generated by copying X's same intermediate file; two agreeing reports still provide two independent evidential routes for the bound."

The first clause is an observation (X and Y agree). The middle clause is a stated fact about provenance (Y was copied from X's intermediate file). The third clause is the *disputed* inference: that two agreeing reports "still provide two independent evidential routes."

Under the working account, "independent evidential routes" would require that the two routes not share the single thing that could be wrong. Here the admitted provenance says they share exactly that: the same intermediate file. Copying is a common cause. Agreement between two descendants of one artifact is *one* route presented twice, not two routes (R7: rejection of a supporting assumption can defeat the support route without proving its conclusion false). So if the question at stake is *evidential support for the bound*, B1's inference is defective: the provenance it admits contradicts the independence it asserts.

Attribution and scope: this defeats B1's *independence* claim only, and only on the stated provenance. If additional evidence showed Y reached the same intermediate value through a separate derivation, or if the intermediate file itself were independently corroborated, the conclusion "two independent routes" could be re-supported. I am not asserting the bound is false; I am asserting the two-agreement support is single-route support under the admitted facts.

RSS conversion check: RSS's provenance-as-set-of-deliveries would represent X and Y as sharing a delivery (the intermediate file), so they are not two executions of rival stories. RSS *adds* nothing here and *loses* nothing I need; the key distinction — shared provenance is not independent evidence — is already in R7 and in the plain reading of "generated by copying." I do not let RSS's tolerance of multiple accounts silently upgrade shared-provenance agreement into independent corroboration.

### B2 — "A log reader displays only the latest visible revision. It therefore establishes that no earlier record was ever delivered."

The first clause is a statement about the reader's *display policy*. The second clause is a "therefore" that does not follow. "Latest visible revision" describes what the display *shows*; it does not characterize what was *delivered*. A reader that only renders the latest visible revision is, by its own description, silent about earlier deliveries. So B2's inference is a defect of the same family as the supersession/silencing issue: absence from a *display* is not absence from the *delivery set* (R1/R2: payload identity and delivery provenance are different questions; R7: separate what is observed from what is concluded).

This is also directly the *dual* of the WHL/RSS point that supersession is structural while effect is evaluative: a superseded payload can have been delivered and still be inert for reservation purposes, without ceasing to have been delivered. So B2 conflates "not currently displayed/effective" with "never existed as a delivery."

Attribution: I am not claiming that a particular reader *must* have missed something. I am claiming B2's inference is unsupported by its own premise. To establish "no earlier record was ever delivered," you would need a source that records the delivered payload bag, not a source that records the latest visible revision. That is precisely what the snapshot's *collection of deliveries* is for (R1); a latest-visible-only reader is a projection (RSS) that discards the delivered set, and a projection is always a loss of information.

### B3 — "The hold expires at time 5. At current time 5 it is still active, because its effect should persist through the named expiry instant."

Ground under the contract: R4 says a hold reserves its quantity exactly when current time is *strictly less than* expiry; at equality or later it reserves zero. So at current time 5 with expiry 5, equality holds, and the contract's reading gives reserves zero — the hold is *not* active for reservation at that instant. B3's normative phrasing ("should persist through the named expiry instant") is an *alternative reading*, not a statement of the contract, and the contract explicitly chose strict less-than. So B3 as an assertion about this warehouse is contrary to R4.

Scope and attribution: B3 may be a legitimate *proposal* to change the reading — a half-open-at-the-other-end expiry convention (reserve at equality, i.e. `t ≤ expiry`). But as presented it asserts a *result* under a reading the contract did not adopt, without flagging the switch. Under the working account the result is: at time 5, expiry 5, the hold reserves zero.

RSS conversion check: RSS explicitly represents "under one reading … under another reading …" for expiry and does not choose. B3 is exactly such a rival reading. RSS *permits* stating it; RSS does *not* license presenting it as the operative result. The RSS-added commitment (permissiveness) must not be laundered into factual force for B3. The WHL specimen's `reserves_effect` uses `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity`, i.e. reserves quantity when `t ≤ expiry`, i.e. *strictly not-yet-expired-by-the-convention-that-counts-equality-as-active* — which is the opposite of the contract's R4 strict-less-than. This is a real disagreement between the WHL specimen's `reserves_effect` and the declared contract R4, and B3 sits on the WHL side of that disagreement. Attributed to the contract: R4 governs; B3 is defective as a claim about this warehouse, but names a live reading-level disagreement worth carrying forward.

### B4 — "An account can report a true conditional consequence without showing that its assumptions apply to a particular physical warehouse."

This is sound as an operating claim (R7, R6's caveat about exact sets vs intervals, and A5's discipline). A conditional consequence "if assumptions S hold, then result R" can be true as a conditional even when no evidence shows S holds for a specific warehouse; that is the difference between a conditional claim and a claim of applicability. It matches R7's instruction to distinguish what follows under an assumption from what is observed or established. Retained as sound. Scope: it says the *consequence* need not be shown applicable; it does not say a report is thereby informative about a physical warehouse, nor that applicability is irrelevant — only that the two questions are distinct. No addition needed; I do not inflate B4 into a license to ignore applicability.

### B5 — "Once the output bounds agree with a checker's bounds, every criticism of the represented exact alternatives is answered."

This is a scope error of the A2/R6 kind, made explicit. Agreement of *bounds* constrains only the *extremes*. R6 says bounds may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless every intervening admitted integer occurs. Case A (cycle 5) is the concrete specimen: bounds [−2, 3] versus exact set {3, −2}; agreeing that the extremes are −2 and 3 leaves open which intermediate alternatives were admitted, which joint choices were realized, and whether any were suppressed. So "every criticism of the represented exact alternatives is answered" is false as stated: bound agreement can coexist with a substantive criticism of the exact set (that a −1 or 0 or 1 or 2 was fabricated by flattening to an interval, or that a joint alternative was dropped).

Attribution: B5 is defective under R6, and it is defective in the specific way A2 was in cycles 1/4/5 — mistaking bound-agreement for set-agreement. RSS's "a projection is always a loss of information" corroborates this: agreeing on two points of a projection does not restore what the projection discarded. RSS adds nothing to R6 here and loses nothing needed; the operative rule is R6.

### B6 — "Rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy. The old result under the originally specified independent-choice task remains a distinct conditional claim."

First sentence: sound. R5 says alternatives of different bookings are independently admissible *for this task*, and explicitly calls this a specified task scope, not a universal claim. So rejecting independence is rejecting a *task-specified* rule, which legitimately raises the question "what coupling policy would replace it?" — a genuinely open question. No manufactured policy should be invented to answer it; what would need to be supplied is a coupling rule (which alternative sets may or must be co-selected across bookings, and under what resource model) — that is the *substance* the missing-policy question requires (R5, R6, and the WHL note that physical resource sharing beyond capacity is an expressive limitation of the language).

Second sentence: sound and important for scope-keeping. The cycle-5 Case A result — exact set {3, −2} — was computed under the originally specified independent-choice task. If coupling were added, that would be a *different* formulation with a possibly different exact set; the old result remains a *distinct conditional claim* conditional on the specified task scope. This is exactly R6's discipline of reporting per-formulation, and R7's discipline of preserving the scope of a claim. B6 is correct on both counts.

Scope caveat I add explicitly: B6 does not license *ignoring* the missing coupling; it licenses *not manufacturing* one. The old {3, −2} answer is retained *as a conditional* whose antecedent is "under the task's specified independence." It would be a further, dishonest step to present {3, −2} as the answer to a *coupled* warehouse, or conversely to suppress it because coupling exists somewhere. Attribution: the conditional framing is R5/R6/R7; the observation that {3, −2} is the specific case is cycle 5's.

## 5. Consequential new problems, only where the inquiry supplies grounds

Two, each grounded in a specific B-claim, not generic.

1. **Source-independence of evidential routes (B1).** The inquiry now supplies an explicit provenance fact — copying from the same intermediate file — on which two reports' agreement is a single route, not two. This is consequential because many "two agreeing reports" arguments silently assume independence, and B1 shows the inquiry can *admit* shared provenance while still asserting independence. Investigation without manufacture would require: the actual provenance of Y relative to X, and whether any part of Y's derivation branched off X's intermediate file before the copy. That is supplied evidence, not a new policy.

2. **Bounds agreement versus exact-set content (B5), with B6 as its scope-keeper.** The inquiry supplies a claim that bound agreement answers *every* criticism of the exact alternatives. That is false under R6; the cycle-5 interval/set gap is the concrete instance. This is consequential because it would otherwise license treating a bounds check as closing all exact-alternative criticisms, and because B6 shows the exact-set answer {3, −2} has a task-scope antecedent that a coupled formulation would change. Investigation requires a coupling rule and, where relevant, a resource model — neither currently supplied; until then the coupling question is carried as an open question, not answered by a manufactured policy.

B3 raises a third, but *lower-grade*, item that I hold at the level of a named reading-disagreement rather than a new problem: the contract's strict-less-than expiry (R4) versus the WHL specimen's `reserves_effect` (`t ≤ expiry` reserves). This is a genuine contract/specimen disagreement about expiry strictness, but MEMO B states only one side (the WHL side) and does not itself add new grounds beyond the reading split RSS already marks. I record it and do not promote it.

## The substantive claims in A, one at a time

**A1 — "A recently expired record no longer reserves stock, so we should remove it before deciding which revision has authority; an older active hold can then become effective."**

Two claims are packed here, and they come apart.

The first ("an expired hold reserves zero") is sound as a *evaluative* claim: WHL's `reserves_effect` returns `0` for a hold when `t > expiry`, and only `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity` otherwise. So an expired hold contributes no reservation.

The second ("we should remove it *before deciding which revision has authority*") is the defective step, and it is precisely the defect WHL is built to make visible. WHL separates two things:

- **structural supersession** — `higher_revision`, `dominated_in`, `greatest_revision_in`, which look only at booking and revision and *ignore* expiry; and
- **evaluative expiry** — `reserves_effect`, `active_hold`, `expired_hold`, which look only at time and expiry.

`greatest_revision_in` selects the greatest revision from the list *before* any effect is computed. Expiry is not consulted. So removing an expired record before choosing the greatest revision changes the selection, not just the effect. Expiry is not supposed to touch selection. This is exactly the "structural supersession, evaluative expiry" reading the WHL file names, and it is the reading the contract R3 hardens: "greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads before considering their effect," and "a lower revision remains superseded even when the greatest-revision hold has expired."

So A1's conclusion ("an older active hold can then become effective") is not licensed by WHL or by the contract; it is the *other* reading — the one WHL explicitly represents under `expired_deletes_rule` / `deletion_changes_greatest` and explicitly declines to choose. A1 has, without saying so, adopted the deletion reading and presented it as a consequence ("so we should"). That is a defect of the *ground*, not a proof that the deletion reading is incoherent; WHL keeps it as a rival rule. But under the *selected* representation it is not the operative rule.

There is a standing symptom A1 should have triggered and didn't: if an expired greatest-revision hold is removed before selection, the "greatest revision" is now the older active hold, so a hold that the contract says is superseded can become live. That is a cross-revision effect produced by an expiry operation, which the evaluative reading forbids. If nothing else about A1 counts, that should count against it as a statement of what happens under the selected representation.

**A2 — "Suppose the available values are -3 and 2. Reporting [-3,2] retains every decision-relevant possibility exactly, so it is harmless to replace the two-point set with that interval in later calculations."**

The first clause is a hypothesis, and the second is a claim about retention. The claim is defective, and the form of the defect is named by contract R6 and by WHL's own structure:

> "An interval may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur."

The exact set here is {-3, 2}. The interval [-3,2] contains -2, -1, 0, 1 as *values that the admitted combinations do not produce*. Those are not "every decision-relevant possibility retained" — they are possibilities *added*. If a downstream calculation treats a value in [-3,2] as achievable, it will act on a reservation that no admissible combination supports. That is the opposite of "harmless."

WHL's own machinery shows why the bound structure is lossy. `availability_lower_bound`/`availability_upper_bound` take per-booking minimum/maximum of `possible_reservations` and subtract the *sums*. That does not construct a set of attainable values at all — it constructs a pair of endpoints, and it can widen as well as stay exact: if booking A's alternatives are {3,4} and booking B's are {5,6}, the true attainable values from summing *one alternative per booking independently* are {8,9,10}, whereas lower/upper bounds are 8 and 10, which happen to agree here; but with alternatives {0,5} and {0,5} the sums are {0,5,10} while the bounds are 0 and 10. Same endpoint pair, three attainable values, and the interval reports "0 through 10" as though everything between were available. So the interval is not even a faithful summary of a *range* of joint outcomes, let alone an exact set. A2's inference is unsupported.

The "retains every decision-relevant possibility exactly" wording quietly conflates *retained* with *not equivalent*. Even if one said an interval is a conservative *superset* of the exact set (which it is, for values, under the bound construction), superset ≠ exact, and "harmless" is the wrong word for a substitution that turns possibilities into attained values. Where a projection onto bounds is used, it must be labelled a bound, not an exact set, and it must not be fed forward as if it were the exact set.

**A3 — "Availability can legitimately be negative under the reporting contract. That observation alone does not identify which booking should be changed."**

Both sentences are sound under the selected representation. WHL uses `Int.sub_nat` (with `of_nat` / `neg_succ_of_nat`) precisely so availability can go negative, and the notes say so. Contract R6 says "Negative availability is permitted; it alone identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy." So the *second* sentence is exactly the contract's point: negativity is a derived signal, not a diagnosis. The first sentence is consistent with `availability_lower_bound`/`availability_upper_bound`, which can produce negative values.

A3 is the memorandum's cleanest statement. I adopt it. The pairing is worth noting because it is the opposite of A1's and A2's pattern: A3 explicitly *refuses* to over-read a derived value.

**A4 — "Two deliveries with different delivery labels are independent confirmations and therefore give their common reservation more authority than a single conflicting alternative."**

Defective under the contract. R1 says "The delivery label records an occurrence, not additional booking authority." R2 says: "Exact repeated record payloads convey no additional authority, even when delivery labels differ... Payload identity and delivery provenance are different questions." So a differing delivery label does *not* by itself convert duplicates into confirmations, and two payloads that are exact duplicates convey no additional authority at all regardless of labels. For payloads that are *not* exact duplicates (near-duplicates / genuine alternatives), the contract also gives no rule that weights one side by being "confirmed twice" — WHL's `alternatives_at_greatest` excludes exact duplicates from being alternatives, but adds no counting mechanism, and `possible_reservations` yields a *set (list) of quantities*, with no multiplicity-of-provenance multiplier. R5 says no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied; the same is true of a within-booking authority boost from label multiplicity.

So A4 asserts an inference the selected representation does not license. Note the precise scope: A4 is not refuted by the fact that duplicates are inert to each other; it is refuted by the contract's explicit separation of payload identity from delivery provenance, which cuts against treating label-count as authority. If one wanted a label-based authority rule, that is a *proposed extension* to the language (an added rule), and it would have to be attributed as such, not presented as a consequence of the contract.

**A5 — "A useful objection must identify what it contests and why. Finding a defect in an interpretation need not discredit every record used by that interpretation."**

Sound, and it aligns with contract R7 ("Distinguish what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed or disputed") and R8. WHL also supports this structurally: `Refutation c` is `(snap, ¬ c snap)` — a refutation is of a *particular* conjecture `c`, not a blanket verdict on the snapshot or on the records contained in it. So a defect in an interpretation (`ReservationRule`-level) can be exhibited without impugning the data (`Record`/`Snapshot`-level). A5 states a discipline the selected representation already observes. I adopt A5.

**A6 — "Because capacity is shared, the task already imposes a rule forbidding simultaneous high alternatives from different bookings, even though no such selection constraint was supplied."**

This is the clearest defect, and among the memos the one the contract most explicitly forecloses. R5: "In this task the alternatives of different bookings are independently admissible. No additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied." WHL's own notes flag the same limitation: capacity is the only global constraint, and the language "cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum." A6 claims the shared capacity *does* impose a rule forbidding simultaneous high alternatives from different bookings. The contradiction is direct: the contract says no such constraint was supplied; A6 asserts the constraint is already there.

There is a possible confusion worth naming so the correction is scoped, not broadened. The capacity sum *does* produce availability values: if two bookings both select large alternatives, the resulting availability can be low or negative. That is a fact about the arithmetic of the sum, not a rule forbidding the combination. Under the selected representation, the combination of two high alternatives is an admissible point in the joint possibility space; it may just yield a low (even negative) availability. A6 conflates "the sum may go negative for that combination" with "the combination is forbidden by a supplied constraint." Treating availability going negative as if it were a forbidden-combination rule also would not obviously compress the joint possibilities — many distinct high-low, low-high, and high-high combinations could all violate a single "availability ≥ 0" threshold, so it wouldn't even deliver a clean "at most one high" rule. A6 supplies nothing usable, and its asserted warrant (that the task already imposes the rule) is refuted by R5 and by WHL's own note.

## Sound claims, defects, and unresolved scope questions, sorted

- **Sound as stated, and adopted:** A3 (both sentences), A5.
- **Defective as stated, under the selected representation:**
  - A1 — the "remove before deciding authority" step; the effect-removal step is what A1 needs, and it is not licensed. The residual, correctly scoped fact is only that an expired hold reserves zero.
  - A2 — the "harmless to replace the two-point set with the interval" step; treats a bound as an exact set (R6) and mislabels a superset as lossless.
  - A4 — the "different labels ⇒ confirmation ⇒ more authority" step; contrary to R1/R2's separation of payload identity from provenance.
  - A6 — the "shared capacity already imposes a forbidding rule" step; contrary to R5 and to WHL's stated limitation on cross-booking incompatibility.
- **Unresolved scope questions (carried forward, not dissolved):** Whether expiry *deletes* a record for selection purposes (WHL's `expired_deletes_rule` / `deletion_changes_greatest`) is a rival *reading* under the specified contract; under the selected representation the evaluative reading is operative, and the deletion reading remains an explicitly represented but unchosen alternative. Whether error can be represented beyond values is a similarly open design question the contract does not close for us (R6 leaves the value-space exact-set question as the operative one; whether *which* combination produced a value must also be reported is not decreed). Neither is a defect; both are scope questions the memos A1 and A6 should have left open instead of answering by fiat.

## A usable working account

Working assumptions for this inquiry, each with its attribution:

1. **Selection precedes evaluation.** For each booking, the greatest revision is chosen from the full delivered set, and only then is its reservation computed. (Contract R3; WHL `greatest_revision_in`, `reserves_effect` in that order.)
2. **Expiry is evaluative, not structural.** An expired hold reserves zero but does not surrender its supersession. If used, the deletion reading must be introduced and attributed as an alternative (`expired_deletes_rule`, `deletion_changes_greatest`), never silently. (Justified against A1.)
3. **Payload identity ≠ provenance.** Different delivery labels do not add booking authority; exact duplicates are inert regardless of labels. (Contract R1/R2, against A4.)
4. **Alternatives across bookings are independent** unless an added rule says otherwise. Capacity is a sum, not a selection constraint. (Contract R5; WHL notes, against A6.)
5. **Exact sets are not intervals.** Availability may be reported as bounds, but a bound must not be substituted for the exact attainable set unless every intervening admitted integer value is actually attainable. (Contract R6; `availability_lower_bound`/`availability_upper_bound` are bound computations; against A2.)
6. **Negativity is a signal, not a verdict.** Adopted from A3 and from R6.
7. **Objections target specific things and are scoped to those things.** Adopted from A5 and R7, matching WHL's `Refutation c` form.

Items 1, 3, 4, 6, 7 of this account agree with the selected representation WHL as written. Item 2 distinguishes two readings WHL keeps separate. Item 5 corrects the conflation A2 invites and connects it to the bound-versus-exact distinction that WHL's own lower/upper-bound machinery embodies.

## Consequential new problem?

The inquiry as supplied does not force a new problem beyond the ones already present in A. The defects in A1, A2, A4, A6 are each independently grounded in the contract and in WHL's own distinctions. A2, however, does expose a limitation the contract does not fully close: WHL's availability "bounds" are computed per booking and summed, which is not the same operation as forming the joint attainable set, so the bounds function is not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set in the multi-booking case (the {0,5}×{0,5} example shows the joint set {0,5,10} has bounds 0 and 10). That is a fact about what the selected representation computes, not a demand to abandon it; whether to add a joint-attainability primitive is a proposed extension, and under R6 the primary obligation is simply not to equate a bound with an exact set. I note it because it falls out of A2's topic, but I do not treat it as a discovered new error in the task; it is a gap the bound-versus-exact discipline already warns about.

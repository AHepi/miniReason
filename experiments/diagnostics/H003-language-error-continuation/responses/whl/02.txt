## Criticism 1 — the A2 discussion names the right contract provision but then overstates a structural fact about WHL

The previous answer says, of the bound construction:

> "Same endpoint pair, three attainable values, and the interval reports '0 through 10' as though everything between were available. So the interval is not even a faithful summary of a *range* of joint outcomes, let alone an exact set."

The example is correct: if booking A's alternatives are {0,5} and booking B's are {0,5}, the independently-admissible joint sums are {0,5,10}, and `availability_lower_bound`/`availability_upper_bound` return 0 and 10. The interval [0,10] indeed contains values (1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9) that no admissible combination produces. So the last clause ("not even a faithful summary of a range") sits on a true example.

The problem is what the previous answer does with this. It says in the "Consequential new problem?" section:

> "A2, however, does expose a limitation the contract does not fully close: WHL's availability 'bounds' are computed per booking and summed, which is not the same operation as forming the joint attainable set, so the bounds function is not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set in the multi-booking case."

This is overreaching in one specific direction, and it is worth being precise about because it is the kind of thing a criticism should correct.

R6 says explicitly: "An interval may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." Nothing in R6 requires the bounds function to *be* an endpoint representation of the exact joint attainable set. R6 requires that we not *treat* a bound as an exact set. Those are different claims. The previous answer moves from "the bounds function is not an endpoint representation of the exact set" to "the bounds function is not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set" and lets that sound like it is a defect of WHL. It is not a defect. It is exactly what R6 permits: bounds are *bounds*. The sum-of-min and sum-of-max is a legitimate bound pair. R6's discipline is a discipline on the *use* of the bound, not a demand that the bound coincide with the exact endpoint set of the joint attainable values.

The consequence of the overreach matters. If a reader takes the previous answer's framing, they may conclude that WHL's `availability_lower_bound`/`availability_upper_bound` are in some way inadequate or in need of correction, or that a "joint-attainability primitive" is a warranted *fix* rather than an *extension*. But R6 already tells us what to do: report the exact set when asked for the exact set; report bounds only as bounds. Under the selected representation, the correct criticism of A2 is that A2 *equates* the two, not that WHL *fails* to make them equal. The previous answer partially resisted this by hedging, but its concluding formulation ("the bounds function is not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set") is stronger than the actual defect in A2 and stronger than what R6 licenses.

This is the criticism I have against the previous answer's treatment of A2, and it is warranted because the previous answer went on to assert this as a "gap the bound-versus-exact discipline already warns about" — a phrasing that treats the gap as a feature of WHL rather than as a predictable property of bound-and-sum. The consequence is a downstream read of WHL as needing a primitive it does not need, which is exactly the kind of silent extension R8 says to attribute if made. Either the previous answer should have said "R6 forbids equating the bound with the exact set; the bound construction being lossy is what makes that prohibition operational," or it should have proposed a joint-attainability primitive *as an attributed extension*. It did neither fully.

## Criticism 2 — a quantifier slip inside the A2 discussion that is real, is invisible under paraphrase, and matters to the appraisal

The previous answer says:

> "if booking A's alternatives are {3,4} and booking B's are {5,6}, the true attainable values from summing *one alternative per booking independently* are {8,9,10}, whereas lower/upper bounds are 8 and 10, which happen to agree here"

"which happen to agree here" is right, but the sentence creates the impression that the bound pair {8,10} is a *faithful endpoint representation* in this case and {0,10} is not in the other case, so that the disagreement with the exact set is an occasional fault. Under WHL's construction, the bounds are always capacity-minus-(sum of per-booking minima) and capacity-minus-(sum of per-booking maxima). They agree with the endpoint pair of the exact joint set *only if* the per-booking min and max are themselves attained in combination with the other bookings' extremes. In the {3,4} × {5,6} case they are (min=3 attainable with max=5, and max=4 attainable with min=6 — though the actual joint set is {8,9,10}, whose true endpoints are 8 and 10, which do agree with the bound computation). In the {0,5} × {0,5} case they likewise agree as an endpoint pair: the joint endpoints are 0 and 10 and the bounds are 0 and 10. The "gap" is not about the *endpoints*; it is about the *interior*. Both examples have bounds that are the *true endpoints* of the joint attainable set. The bounds always are (by monotonicity of the sum, the min-sum and max-sum are the actual extreme joint sums).

So the previous answer's own example does not show what it says it shows. It says the {0,5}×{0,5} case shows the bounds are "not even a faithful endpoint representation" — but 0 and 10 *are* the endpoints of {0,5,10}, and the same is true across every case (each booking's own minimum and maximum are attained by *some* admissible choice for that booking; summing an attained-min alternative for each booking gives one admissible joint value equal to the lower bound, and likewise for the upper). The reason the interval [0,10] is wrong is that it fills in the *interior*, not that it misses the endpoints. The previous answer conflated "the interval misrepresents the interior" with "the interval is a bad endpoint representation," and these are not the same fault.

This is a real defect in the previous answer, and it is worth stating plainly: the criticism of A2 should be, "R6 forbids substituting the bound for the exact set because the interval can report non-attained interior values," not "WHL's bound function fails to be a faithful endpoint representation." The latter is false. The consequence is that a reader would take the previous answer's "gap" to be larger than it is; the actual gap is narrow and is precisely the interior-filling that R6 names. The previous answer turned a bounded, well-specified prohibition into an apparent defect of WHL's machinery, and the distinction matters for whether any "fix" is warranted.

I note that this is a *correction of my own earlier overstatement*, not a discovery of an error in Memo A. Memo A's defect (equating a two-point set with an interval) stands, and the previous answer correctly located it in R6.

## Criticism 3 — the A1 discussion makes a labeled claim about "structural supersession" that WHL does not quite bear as stated

The previous answer says:

> "WHL separates two things: **structural supersession** — `higher_revision`, `dominated_in`, `greatest_revision_in`, which look only at booking and revision and *ignore* expiry; and **evaluative expiry** — `reserves_effect`, `active_hold`, `expired_hold`, which look only at time and expiry."

This is close but not exact, and the inexactness matters for the strength of the criticism of A1.

`active_hold t r` is defined as `r.kind = Kind.hold ∧ ¬ (t > r.expiry)`. That is the *negation* of "expired": a hold is active when it is a hold and time has not passed expiry. `expired_hold t r` is `r.kind = Kind.hold ∧ t > r.expiry`. So `active_hold` and `expired_hold` *do* consult `r.kind` — they are not "look only at time and expiry."

More importantly, `dominated_in` and `greatest_revision_in` are defined with `higher_revision`, which is `r.booking = s.booking ∧ revision_lt r.revision s.revision`. That is booking + revision only. But `revision_lt` is an `axiom` of type `Revision → Revision → Prop`, characterized by `revision_lt_iff` (`revision_lt r s ↔ revision_le r s ∧ ¬ revision_le s r`). So "higher revision" is whatever `revision_lt` says, which under `revision_lt_iff` tracks `revision_le`. The WHL text's *intended meaning* note says "higher revision supersedes lower revisions" and "expiry is not consulted in selecting the greatest revision." But the WHL *code* does not literally encode "expiry is not consulted" — it just computes `greatest_revision_in` without ever calling `reserves_effect` or `active_hold`. The negative claim ("ignores expiry") is a property of the code's definitional dependencies, not a separate structural commitment of the language. The previous answer presented it as the former, which risks sounding as though WHL contains a *rule* that expiry is ignored in selection, when in fact expiry is simply not *mentioned* in the selection relations.

Why this matters for the criticism of A1: A1's "remove before deciding authority" step is indeed not licensed under the selected representation, because `greatest_revision_in` is defined without reference to expiry, and because the intended-meaning note and R3 explicitly say selection is prior. So the *conclusion* of the previous answer's criticism stands. But the previous answer's framing — "WHL separates two things" as if these were two *declared* layers — overstates WHL's declarative content. WHL's relations happen not to consult expiry in selection; WHL does not *declare* that expiry is excluded from selection. That distinction is the contract's contribution (R3), not WHL's. Under R8, the previous answer should have attributed the "selection precedes evaluation" commitment to R3 and to WHL's intended-meaning note, not to WHL's *definitions* alone. It partly did ("and it is the reading the contract R3 hardens"), so the criticism is of over-claim, not of an entirely unsupported claim. But the over-claim is worth correcting, because it supports the previous answer's tone — that A1 is contradicted by WHL — when in fact A1 is contradicted by R3 plus WHL's *intended reading*, and A1 is *representable* in WHL under `expired_deletes_rule`/`deletion_changes_greatest`. The previous answer did note the representability, so this is a scoping correction, not a reversal.

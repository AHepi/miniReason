## 1. The A1 correction: partly valid, but it repeats the error it diagnoses

The previous response said cycle 1 had over-attributed to A1 by calling the deletion reading "A1's underlying reading," when A1 merely asserts a sequencing claim ("expiry before authority") with a rationale ("a recently expired record no longer reserves stock"). The correction: I should have treated A1's sequencing claim as assessable on its own, and carried the deletion reading as merely one possible route rather than as A1's named commitment.

This is largely right and improves on cycle 1. A1 does not announce a reading. R7 requires distinguishing what a source states from what follows under an assumption, and cycle 1 did blur that. The narrowed formulation — "A1 asserts an expiry-before-authority order; that order contradicts R3/R4 as stated" — is the correct shape.

But the previous response did not fully apply its own fix. It still wrote that the deletion reading is "at most one way one might arrive at it," and left the impression that some route other than the deletion reading might vindicate A1. That is the correct *open* status, but the response then in the closing method note treated the non-availability of other routes as a residual point rather than as the actual state of the criticism. The remedy is to say plainly: cycle 1's criticism of A1's *sequencing* stands; cycle 1's *attribution* of the deletion reading to A1 is withdrawn; and whether A1 could be grounded any other way is simply not settled by A1's text.

There is a stronger point worth stating, since the previous response gestured at it without drawing it out. Under R3, greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads before effect is considered; a lower revision remains superseded even when the greatest-revision hold has expired; a lower-revision release does not cancel a higher-revision hold. Under R4, a hold reserves its quantity exactly when current time is strictly less than expiry, and "at equality or later it reserves zero"; and expiry changes effect, not revision authority. So A1's sequencing does not merely lack support — it is inconsistent with two contract clauses that fix the order. The deletion reading is one coherent semantics in which A1's sequencing would be right, and RSS explicitly lists it as a reading ("under another, an expired hold is deleted and lower revisions can become live"). So the criticism is valid against A1 *as a step in the current task*, and the deletion reading is representable, just not adopted here.

**Outcome: valid as narrowed; the attribution withdrawal is correct; the residual question is open.**

## 2. The A2 correction: valid, and an improvement on cycle 1

Cycle 1 asserted that "the integers -2, -1, 0, 1 do not occur (no admissible combination yields them)" and used that as the ground for calling A2's interval replacement illegitimate. The previous response correctly noted that this exclusion is not established by the memo. The memo says "suppose the available values are -3 and 2." It does not say these are the only admitted values. If the supposition means "the exact achievable set is {-3, 2}," the intervening values are excluded by that reading, not by an independent fact. If it means "we have seen -3 and 2," nothing yet rules out 0 or 1.

R6 is the governing clause: an interval may be reported as bounds "but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." Whether all intervening values occur is exactly what the memo does not settle. So the correct criticism of A2 is the *conditional* the previous response formulated: the substitution is harmless only under the reading in which {-3, 2} is already the full admitted set, and under that reading it adds nothing; under any other reading it silently adds values not yet admitted. Either way "harmless for later calculations" is not established by A2's own text.

This correction is valid and the withdrawal of the middle-value claim is the right move. It also dovetails with R6's separate permission to *report* bounds: A2's bound-report is not the defect; the equation with the exact set is.

**Outcome: valid; the middle-value claim is properly withdrawn as unestablished.**

## 3. The A4 correction: valid on the R2 core, and the withdrawal of the RSS appeal is correct

Cycle 1 rejected A4 on two grounds: (i) R2 makes exact duplicates inert even across differing labels, and same-greatest-revision different payloads are alternatives rather than confirmations; (ii) RSS's statement that it "does not decide whether they are duplicates or alternatives" corroborates the rejection. The previous response spotted that (ii) is wrong: RSS's non-decision is a suspension, not a refutation, and citing it as support for a defect misuses the source under R7's requirement to keep source-state separate from argument.

That correction is correct. RSS's refusal to decide is, under R7 and RSS's own text, an open question it carries rather than a verdict. The defensible core of the A4 criticism is narrower and rests entirely on the contract: R1 says the delivery label "records an occurrence, not additional booking authority"; R2 says exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority "even when delivery labels differ." On the reading "same payload, differing labels," A4 contradicts R2 directly. On the reading "different payloads at the same greatest revision," R2 calls them alternatives, and nothing in R1–R6 grants them cumulative authority — A4's phrase "give their common reservation more authority" is not supplied. So A4 fails under this contract on either reading, but the argument must be made from R1/R2, not from RSS's neutralism.

**Outcome: valid withdrawal; the R2-based rejection stands.**

## 4. The A6 correction: valid, and the residual observation is a real improvement

Cycle 1 said A6 "asserts a constraint the representation does not carry," matched RSS's note that it "cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum," and closed by saying no new structural problem was grounded. The previous response endorsed the rejection but noted that closing move was too quick: A6 is best read not as an invented rule but as a *scope/fact confusion*, the mirror image of the opposite error. R5 says independent admissibility of different bookings is "a specified task scope, not a universal claim about physical warehouses." A6 reads the shared-capacity *fact* as a cross-booking *rule*; a naïve universalizing reading of R5's independence would read a scope *choice* as a physical *truth*. Both are misclassifications of the same type.

This is a genuine improvement and it is accurately targeted. R5's own wording flags it. The rejection of A6 stands, and the symmetric diagnostic is warranted: capacity is only a global quantity used in the subtraction of R6, not an incompatibility relation, and the task's independence is a stipulated scope the contract itself labels as such. The previous response correctly declined to escalate this to a new structural discovery, since R5 already names the scope choice.

**Outcome: valid; the symmetry point is a legitimate refinement, not an overreach.**

## What should return to the earlier account or method

Retained without change:

- The contract R1–R8 controls for this task; the specimens are fallible resources, and conflicts with the contract are exposed rather than smoothed.
- A3 and A5 are sound as stated. A3 tracks R6's clause that negative availability "alone identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy," and RSS's "signal, not an error." A5 tracks R7 and R8.
- A1's stated sequencing (expiry before authority) contradicts R3/R4, and the deletion reading is representable but not adopted.
- A2's equation of a two-point set with a closed interval is not licensed by R6; bounds may be reported, the equation is the contested step.
- A4 is rejected under R1 and R2: labels do not add authority, and same-greatest-revision different payloads are alternatives, not confirmations.
- A6 asserts a cross-booking constraint R5 does not supply and RSS says it cannot express except through the capacity sum.

Corrected or narrowed in the previous response, and correct to keep corrected:

- The cycle-1 attribution of the deletion reading to A1 is withdrawn; the criticism of A1's sequencing stands.
- The cycle-1 claim that -2, -1, 0, 1 demonstrably fail to occur is withdrawn; the A2 criticism becomes conditional on whether {-3, 2} is the full admitted set.
- The cycle-1 appeal to RSS's non-decision as corroboration of A4's defect is withdrawn.

Open, and correctly carried rather than resolved:

- Whether any premise other than the deletion reading would yield A1's sequencing; the deletion reading is not refuted, only not adopted here.
- Whether A2 meant {-3, 2} as the full admitted set.
- Whether a non-contract reading would treat differing labels as evidential; RSS leaves this undecided.
- How to keep scope claims and fact claims distinct when capacity is the sole global quantity. R5 already flags the scope choice; no exhaustive treatment is required at this cycle.

## Verdict on the previous criticism

The previous response's corrections to cycle 1 are, on the whole, valid: the A1 attribution was indeed over-strong and is properly narrowed; the A2 middle-value claim was indeed unestablished and is properly withdrawn; the RSS appeal for A4 was indeed a misuse of a source's non-decision and is properly withdrawn; the A6 rejection was right and the scope/fact symmetry is a legitimate refinement. Its single remaining weakness is the one it named in itself: it did not fully apply to A1 the discipline it endorsed at the method level. That omission is a gap, not an error — the narrowed A1 criticism stands, and the unexamined route remains open rather than demonstrated to exist.

No new structural problem is grounded by the current material. The correct findings are that the previous response improved on cycle 1 in three specific ways, that its A1 narrowing should be completed by explicitly carrying the alternative-route question as open rather than as a residual, and that the scope/fact confusion around capacity is a recurring misclassification flagged by R5 rather than a fresh discovery.

## Substantive claims and their grounds

**B1 — two reports agree on a bound; Y was copied from X's intermediate file; therefore two independent evidential routes exist.** The factual part (X and Y agree; Y was generated from X's file) is an observation, and it is compatible with R7: reports are fallible claims, and observation and inference are distinct. The *inference* is defective. If Y's values are produced by copying X's same intermediate file, then Y is not an independent route; it is a re-presentation of X's route. This is exactly the situation R8 describes for re-presented targets: making an earlier item available again is an experimental input, not independent memory. Two texts agreeing because one is a copy of the other yields one evidential route counted twice, not two. What would be needed to turn them into genuinely independent routes is a source of variation between X and Y that is not downstream of X's file — e.g., independently derived inputs, an independent checker with its own data path, or a different method that can fail separately. None of that is supplied. So B1's observation survives; its "two independent evidential routes" conclusion does not, on the stated provenance.

**B2 — a latest-visible-revision display establishes that no earlier record was ever delivered.** This is a clean non-sequitur, and R2 supplies the precise distinction it violates. A display showing only the latest *visible* revision reports a view over what is visible now, not the full delivered history. "Not visible" is not "not delivered." The contract separates payload identity from delivery provenance (R2) and treats a snapshot as a finite collection of deliveries; what a reader can see is downstream of selection, not of existence. WHL reinforces this structurally: `greatest_revision_in` selects among records that are *in the list* via `dominated_in`, and a record can be dominated (hence hidden from a "latest only" view) while still having been delivered. RSS says the same thing from the other side: the bag is raw material, the story is the reading; a bare display is a projection and "a projection is always a loss of information." So B2 is defective as stated. Its only defensible reading is the trivial one in which "delivered and retained" is *defined* as "visible," which is not what it says.

**B3 — the hold expires at time 5; at current time 5 it is still active, because its effect persists through the named expiry instant.** This one is *wrong under this contract* and reveals a disagreement worth stating rather than smoothing. R4 fixes the semantics: a hold reserves its quantity **strictly when current time is less than expiry**, and **at equality or later it reserves zero**. So at t = 5 with expiry = 5, the hold is *not* active; it reserves zero. "Still active at the named instant" is the reading that treats the interval as closed at expiry, and the contract explicitly rejects that reading. WHL's `active_hold` uses `¬ (t > r.expiry)` and `reserves_effect` uses `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity` — note these are *not* the same test: `¬(t > e)` is `t ≤ e`, whereas `reserves_effect` zeroes when `t > e`, i.e., `t ≥ e+1`. So on the natural numbers the specimen's own `active_hold` and `reserves_effect` disagree at `t = expiry` (active_hold says active; reserves_effect also returns the quantity there, since `t > e` is false). The contract is *stricter than the specimen's `reserves_effect`* in the opposite direction: R4 says at equality the hold reserves zero, which is the deletion-side behavior at the boundary. B3's conclusion (still active at t = 5) matches neither the contract's R4 nor, at the boundary, the specimen's `reserves_effect`. B3's *fact* (expiry = 5, t = 5) is fine; its *inferred effect at the instant* is wrong under the governing rule.

**B4 — an account can report a true conditional consequence without showing that its assumptions apply to a particular physical warehouse.** This is sound under R7 and R8, and it is consistent with the contract's separation of "what follows under an assumption" from "what is observed." R6's note that negative availability "identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy," and R7's statement that rejection of a supporting assumption defeats that support route without proving the conclusion false, both authorize conditionals whose conditions are not asserted to hold physically. WHL also expresses this: commitments are `Prop`s and a refutation is a snapshot together with a proof of negation; the language "does not assert which conjectures are true." So B4 is the soundest claim in the memo. Its only risk, which it avoids, is slipping from "the conditional consequence is true" to "the antecedent applies here" — a slip B4 explicitly disclaims.

**B5 — once output bounds agree with a checker's bounds, every criticism of the represented exact alternatives is answered.** This is false, and it is the memo's clearest defect because it forecloses precisely the kind of criticism the contract requires. Two bounds agreeing do not identify an exact set (R6). The same pair of bounds is compatible with many exact sets, including the actual set and one containing intervening values. So agreement on bounds leaves open whether the *exact* alternatives were represented correctly, and R7 says a criticism can target the representation being used. A checker verifying bounds has not verified that the alternatives at a greatest revision were correctly enumerated, nor that exact duplicates were treated as inert (R2), nor that the supersession-before-effect ordering was respected (R3). This is the same defect as Memo A2 in the prior cycle's case: reporting [−2, 3] as bounds is permitted, but equating it with the exact set {−2, 3} imports values no admissible combination yields. B5's "every criticism is answered" would license exactly that equation. So B5 is unsound as stated; the defensible residue is only "agreement on bounds answers a criticism whose target is the bounds."

**B6 — rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy; the old result under the originally specified independent-choice task remains a distinct conditional claim.** This is sound, and it is the claim that ties the memo to the scope question the task names. R5 says the independence of bookings' alternatives is a *specified task scope*, not a universal claim; R6 says the exact set is computed under that scope. Rejecting R5's independence is a legitimate move, but it changes the assumed problem: the old result is then a conditional consequence of a scope that includes independence, and remains true under that assumption (R7). It does not become false; it becomes inapplicable outside its scope. What would have to be supplied to investigate the *coupled* problem without manufacturing a policy is a specific cross-booking constraint or resource model that is *not* already the capacity sum — e.g., a rule stating which pairs of booking alternatives are incompatible, a shared physical resource with its own capacity, or a preference over combinations. R6 explicitly notes capacity is the only global constraint in the supplied scope, and RSS flags the same gap: "two stories from different bookings may be compatible or incompatible depending on unstated physical resources... invisible unless a viewpoint includes a resource model." Absent such an input, any coupled computation would be inventing the policy. This is a scope question, and B6 correctly keeps the old conditional result distinct from the new, under-specified one.

## Working account

Use the contract as controlling; treat WHL and RSS as fallible, each exposing a genuine divergence from the contract rather than settling the task.

1. Retention and effect are separate: greatest revision is chosen among all *delivered* payloads before any effect is computed (R3). A released or expired record retains authority even while reserving zero (R4). B3's "active at the instant" is the reading R4 rejects; at t ≥ expiry the hold reserves zero, and the specimen's own `active_hold`/`reserves_effect` pair disagrees at the boundary, which the contract resolves in favor of equality-is-expired.
2. Payload identity is not delivery provenance (R2). Exact repeated payloads are inert regardless of label (B1's copying is not a second route for the same reason: repetition is not added authority). B2's display error lives here too — a view over visible records is not the delivered set.
3. Bounds are a projection, not the exact set (R6). Agreement on bounds does not certify the alternatives (against B5); the exact set must be reported as a set, and an interval must not be equated with it unless every intervening integer occurs.
4. Independence of bookings is a specified scope (R5), and the coupled question is open until a coupling input is supplied (B6). The independent-scope result stands as a conditional (B4, R7).

## Consequential new problem the inquiry actually supplies grounds for

The memo supplies grounds for a *scope* problem, not a new factual one. B6 reports that rejecting independence "opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy," and the contract does not fill it: R5 declares independence a task scope, R6 notes capacity is the only global constraint, and neither specimen provides a resource model or a cross-booking constraint (RSS explicitly says alternative compatibility is "invisible unless a viewpoint includes a resource model"). The prior cycle's case makes this concrete: bookings A and B were independently admissible, and choosing B's larger alternative (8) drove availability negative (−2) without triggering any incompatibility rule — capacity sum alone permitted it. So the open problem is: given a *coupled* scope, the exact set of achievable availability is no longer the Cartesian product of per-booking alternatives, and the memo does not say what the coupling is. This is a real scope question, and it is genuinely unreachable within the declared material: to investigate it, one would need an explicit non-capacity constraint (a shared resource with its own capacity, an incompatibility relation among booking alternatives, or a preference over global combinations), none of which is supplied. I name the problem and refuse to manufacture the policy. I do not add a second, unrelated problem; the memo does not supply grounds for one beyond B6's scope point.

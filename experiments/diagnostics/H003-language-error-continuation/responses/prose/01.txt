I'll work directly from the declared contract R1–R8, the two fallible specimens (WHL and RSS), and Memo A. I treat the memo as a target of inquiry, not as instruction.

## What Memo A actually claims, and on what grounds

**A1 — "expired records should be removed before deciding authority; an older active hold can then become effective."**
This is a substantive claim about *structural supersession* versus *evaluative expiry*. Under the contract it is a defect. R3 states: greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads *before* considering effect, and a lower revision remains superseded even when the greatest-revision hold has expired. So an older active hold does **not** become effective merely because a higher-revision hold expired. The memo's ground appears to be the *deletion reading* — the one WHL represents separately as `expired_deletes_rule` and explicitly declines to adopt, and which RSS records as one *reading* among others ("under another, an expired hold is deleted and lower revisions can become live"). So A1's underlying reading is representable, but it is not the contract's reading. The error is not that the deletion reading is incoherent; it is that A1 presents that reading's consequence as if it followed from the snapshot alone. R4's clause "expiry changes a retained record's effect, not its revision authority" is exactly the point A1 collapses.

**A2 — "replace the two-point set {-3, 2} with the interval [-3, 2]."**
This is a defect against R6. R6 permits an interval to be *reported as bounds* but forbids equating a bounds-interval with an exact set "unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." Here the admitted values are -3 and 2; the integers -2, -1, 0, 1 do not occur (no admissible combination yields them). Equating {-3,2} with [-3,2] silently asserts four values the account does not admit, so it is not harmless for later calculations. The phrase "retains every decision-relevant possibility" is the disputed step — it conflates *covering* a value-set with *being* that value-set. A sound residue survives: reporting the bounds is itself allowed; the defect is the equation.

**A3 — "availability can legitimately be negative; that alone does not identify which booking to change."**
This is sound. R6 says negative availability is permitted and "alone identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy." A3 states precisely that limit. RSS agrees ("treats the sign as a signal, not an error"). No defect.

**A4 — "two deliveries with different labels are independent confirmations and give their common reservation more authority."**
Defect. R1 says the delivery label "records an occurrence, not additional booking authority," and R2 says exact repeated payloads "convey no additional authority, even when delivery labels differ," with payload identity and provenance kept separate. So two differing labels over the *same* payload add no authority. There is a subtlety the memo glosses: if the two deliveries share booking and revision but differ in kind, quantity, or expiry, they are near-duplicates — R2 calls differing payloads at the same greatest revision *alternatives*, not confirmations. So A4 fails whether we read it as (i) same payload, different label (no added authority) or (ii) different payload, different label (alternatives, and RSS explicitly says it "does not decide whether they are duplicates or alternatives"). Under the contract, "independent confirmation" is not a supplied warrant.

**A5 — "a useful objection must identify what it contests and why; a defect in an interpretation need not discredit every record the interpretation used."**
Sound, and it matches R7 (criticism can target a rule, data, interpretation, earlier criticism, or the representation) and R8 (rejection of one support route does not prove the conclusion false). This is the memo's own best guidance, and it is what I am applying to A1, A2, A4 above.

**A6 — "because capacity is shared, the task already imposes a rule forbidding simultaneous high alternatives from different bookings."**
Defect, and a scope error. R5 states the alternatives of different bookings are independently admissible and "no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied," explicitly calling this a specified task scope, not a universal claim. Capacity appears *only* through the subtraction in R6, not as an incompatibility relation between bookings. RSS corroborates the limitation: it "cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum." So A6 asserts a constraint the representation does not carry; "shared capacity" does not entail "forbidden simultaneity."

## Sound claims, defects, and unresolved scope

Sound: A3, A5. Defective against the contract: A1 (deletion reading smuggled in as structural), A2 (bounds conflated with exact set), A4 (label treated as authority; near-duplicates treated as confirmations), A6 (invented cross-booking constraint).

Unresolved scope questions, not settled by the contract:
- Whether the *deletion* reading is ever the right reading is left open by both specimens. RSS lists it as a reading; WHL represents it via `expired_deletes_rule` but "does not choose between them." A1 is wrong as a claim under this contract, but the underlying question of which reading a user intends is genuinely open — that is a scope question, not a defect in the deletion reading itself.
- Whether physical resource sharing between bookings matters is also open, but R5 fixes the *current task's* scope to independent admissibility. A6 errs by importing a constraint the task did not supply while treating it as already imposed.

## Working account

Take the contract R1–R8 as controlling for this task. For each booking, select the greatest revision across all delivered payloads first, treating expiry as affecting only the record's reservation effect, never its authority (R3, R4). Treat exact duplicates as inert regardless of labels, and different-payload same-revision records as alternatives, not confirmations (R2). Compute availability as capacity minus the sum of the selected reservations, across independently admissible per-booking choices, and report the exact set of achievable values; bounds may be reported but must not be equated with that set (R5, R6). Negative values are permitted and are not by themselves evidence of corruption (R6).

Consequences for the memo: drop A1's "remove before deciding authority" step; keep the observation that expiry changes effect. Keep the bound-report of A2 but not the equation with the exact set. Keep A3 and A5 as stated. Reject A4's "independent confirmation" and A6's "already imposed" cross-booking rule; retain instead the open questions RSS marks as readings rather than facts.

## New problem, only if grounded

I do not find grounds for a new structural problem beyond what the contract already supplies (R2 payload/provenance separation; R3 supersession-before-effect; R6 exact-set vs. interval; R5 scope of independence). A6 is noteworthy because it shows a recurring failure mode — reading a *limitation* of the representation (capacity is the only global constraint) as a *hidden rule* — but that is already named by RSS and bounded by R5. Attributing a new problem would overreach; the grounded finding is that A6 is a scope error, not that the task contains an undisclosed constraint.

Uncertainty retained: whether a given user means the retained-expiry reading (contract) or the deletion reading (representable, not adopted) is not settled by the delivered material and is carried forward as an open question rather than resolved here.

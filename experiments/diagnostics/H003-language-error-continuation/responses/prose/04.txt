## What the cycle‑1 account did, and what survives

Cycle 1 assessed Memo A's six claims against the contract. That basic orientation — contract as controlling standard, memo as target, specimens as fallible — I retain unchanged. Its verdicts on A3 and A5 I retain as stated: A3 matches R6's clause that negative availability "alone identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy" and RSS's "signal, not an error"; A5 matches R7 (criticism can target a rule, data, interpretation, criticism, or representation) and R8 (rejecting one support route does not prove the conclusion false). The remaining four verdicts (A1, A2, A4, A6) I retain in **verdict** but I now hold **narrower formulations** of them, which I attribute to cycles 2–3 and endorse in this response. Cycle 1 was not wrong to reject these four claims; it was over‑strong on the grounds it offered.

**A1.** Cycle 1 said A1's "underlying reading" is the deletion reading, and criticized A1 for holding it. That attribution I **withdraw** in this response. A1's stated content is a *sequencing* claim — evaluate expiry before fixing revision authority — with a rationale that a recently expired record no longer reserves stock. Under R7, I must distinguish what a source states from what follows under an assumption, and cycle 1 fused the two. What survives, and I retain: A1's sequencing contradicts R3 (greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads *before* considering effect; a lower revision stays superseded even when the greatest‑revision hold has expired; a lower‑revision release does not cancel a higher‑revision hold) and R4 (a hold reserves its quantity exactly when current time is strictly less than expiry; at equality or later, zero; expiry changes effect, not revision authority). The deletion reading is a coherent alternative that RSS itself lists ("under another, an expired hold is deleted and lower revisions can become live"), so it is representable — it is just not the contract's reading. I now hold the A1 criticism as: *A1's stated sequencing contradicts R3/R4 as stated; whether some premise other than the deletion reading could yield that sequencing is not settled by A1's text and remains open.*

**A2.** Cycle 1 said the memo's two values "-3 and 2" exclude −2, −1, 0, 1 because "no admissible combination yields them." That specific assertion I **withdraw** in this response as unestablished. The memo says "suppose the available values are −3 and 2"; it does not say these are the only admitted values. Whether the intervening integers occur is exactly the question R6 leaves to the case, and the memo does not settle it. What I retain, narrowed to a conditional: equating {−3, 2} with [−3, 2] is licensed only under the reading where {−3, 2} is already the full admitted set, and under that reading the substitution adds nothing; under any other reading it silently adds values not admitted. Either way "harmless for later calculations" is not established by A2's own text. R6 permits *reporting* bounds; the equation with the exact set is the contested step.

**A4.** Cycle 1 rejected A4 partly on R1/R2 and partly by citing RSS's statement that it "does not decide whether they are duplicates or alternatives." The second appeal I **withdraw** in this response: under R7, a source's non‑decision is a suspension, not a refutation, and citing it as supporting a defect misuses the source. What I retain rests entirely on the contract: R1 says the delivery label "records an occurrence, not additional booking authority"; R2 says exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority "even when delivery labels differ." On the reading "same payload, differing labels," A4 directly contradicts R2. On the reading "different payloads at the same greatest revision," R2 calls them alternatives, and nothing in R1–R6 grants them cumulative authority; A4's phrase "give their common reservation more authority" is not supplied by the contract. A4 fails on either reading, but the argument must come from R1/R2, not from RSS's neutralism.

**A6.** Cycle 1's rejection stands. A6 asserts a cross‑booking constraint R5 does not supply ("no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied") and which RSS says it "cannot express ... except through the capacity sum." What I now add, attributing the refinement to cycle 2 and endorsing it here, is that A6 is best read as a *scope/fact confusion*: R5 explicitly labels independent admissibility as "a specified task scope, not a universal claim about physical warehouses," and A6 reads the shared capacity *fact* as a cross‑booking *rule*. The mirror error — reading R5's scope choice as a universal physical truth — is the same misclassification in the opposite direction. I keep that symmetric diagnostic. I also keep cycle 3's caution: this is a recurring misclassification flagged by R5 itself, not a fresh structural discovery, so I do not escalate it.

## Working account for the next use

Take R1–R8 as controlling for this task. For each booking, select the greatest revision from all delivered payloads before considering effect (R3); treat expiry as modifying the record's reservation effect only, never its revision authority (R4). Treat exact repeated payloads as inert regardless of labels (R2); treat different payloads at the same greatest revision as alternatives, not confirmations (R2). Compute availability as capacity minus the sum of selected reservations across independently admissible per‑booking choices (R5); report the exact set of achievable values, and if reporting bounds, do not equate them with that set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur (R6). Negative availability is permitted and is not by itself evidence of corruption or physical stock discrepancy (R6).

For further memo claims: reject sequencing that evaluates expiry before authority (A1, against R3/R4) while leaving open how a non‑contract reading might reach it; reject equating a two‑point set with an interval without establishing that the intervening values are admitted (A2, conditional under R6); reject reading differing delivery labels as adding authority (A4, against R1/R2); reject importing a cross‑booking constraint not supplied by R5, while remembering that R5's independence is itself a scope choice rather than a physical universal (A6). Keep A3 and A5 as sound.

## Criticism of my previous response

My previous response treated the contract as the controlling standard and used Memo A as a target. That basic orientation is sound and should be retained. But several specific moves in it need correction. I will identify them, give grounds, and state consequences.

**1. The criticism of A1 was over-attributed.**

What I actually said: that A1 "presents that reading's consequence as if it followed from the snapshot alone," that its "underlying reading is representable, but it is not the contract's reading," and that "R4's clause ... is exactly the point A1 collapses."

The problem is the framing "the underlying reading." A1 does not announce a reading. It asserts an operational instruction ("we should remove it before deciding which revision has authority") with a stated rationale ("a recently expired record no longer reserves stock"). Under R7 I am required to distinguish what a source *states* from what *follows under an assumption*. I did not do this cleanly. I supplied the deletion reading as the *explanation* of A1, then criticized A1 for holding it. That is criticism of a reconstruction I introduced, not of what A1 says.

What A1 directly states is a sequencing claim: expiry is evaluated *before* revision authority is fixed. That sequencing claim can be assessed on its own, without importing the deletion reading. Under R3 the order is reversed — greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads *before* considering effect — and R4 says expiry changes a retained record's effect, not its revision authority. So A1's sequencing is a defect against R3/R4 *as stated*, and the deletion reading is at most one way one might arrive at it, not the thing A1 says.

Consequence: the criticism survives but must be narrowed. I should not have called the deletion reading "A1's underlying reading" as though A1 endorsed a named semantics. The sound residue is: A1 asserts an expiry-before-authority order; that order contradicts R3/R4; whether the deletion reading, some other route, or merely an operational shorthand produces A1's order is not something A1 states, and I should carry that as open.

**2. A2's treatment was sound in verdict but overstated one sub-claim.**

I said the admitted values "are -3 and 2; the integers -2, -1, 0, 1 do not occur (no admissible combination yields them)." That parenthetical is a claim I asserted, not one the memo supplies and not one derivable from the material given. The memo says "suppose the available values are -3 and 2." It does not say these are the *only* values, nor that no admissible combination yields a middle value. If "the available values are -3 and 2" is read as "the exact set of achievable availabilities is {-3, 2}," then the intervening integers are excluded *by that reading of the supposition*, not by an independent fact about admissible combinations. If it is read as "we happen to have seen -3 and 2," then nothing yet rules out 0 or 1.

Grounds: R6 distinguishes the exact set of possible availability values from a reported interval, and says an interval may be reported as bounds "but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." Whether all intervening values occur is exactly the question, and the memo does not settle it. My verdict — that the memo's "harmless" step is the disputed move and that equating {-3,2} with [-3,2] is not licensed unless the intervening values are admitted — is correct. But I should not have supplied the exclusion of -2,-1,0,1 as if I had established it.

Consequence: keep the defect (the equation of a two-point set with an interval is not licensed by R6), drop the assertion that the middle values demonstrably fail to occur. The clean criticism is a *conditional*: the replacement is harmless only under the reading in which {-3,2} is already the full admitted set, and under that reading the replacement adds nothing, while under any other reading it silently adds values the account has not admitted. Either way "harmless for later calculations" is not established by the memo's own text.

**3. A4 was handled with a genuine ambiguity, but the criticism was framed too strongly.**

I said A4 "fails whether we read it as (i) same payload, different label ... or (ii) different payload, different label ...," and I treated the RSS remark "does not decide whether they are duplicates or alternatives" as corroboration of failure. That second move is wrong in a specific way: RSS's refusal to decide is *not* a refutation of A4. It is a suspension. Citing it as support for a defect misuses the source — under R7, a source's non-decision is a non-decision, not an argument.

The defensible core of the criticism is narrower: under R2, exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority "even when delivery labels differ," so on reading (i) A4 contradicts R2. On reading (ii) the deliveries are alternatives at the same greatest revision, and R2 does not grant them cumulative authority; A4's phrase "give their common reservation more authority" is not supplied by anything in R1–R6. That is enough to reject A4 under this contract. I should not have presented RSS's neutralism as corroboration.

Consequence: retain the rejection of A4 under R2; drop the appeal to RSS's non-decision as though it supported the rejection. The RSS point remains useful only as a statement that the near-duplicate case is left open by RSS, which is a different claim.

**4. A6 was criticized on correct grounds, but I under-stated the residual question.**

I said A6 "asserts a constraint the representation does not carry" and that "shared capacity does not entail forbidden simultaneity." That is right against R5 and matches RSS's own note that it "cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum." But my closing "New problem, only if grounded" section said I found no new structural problem beyond the contract. That was too quick on one point. A6 is not merely an invented rule; it is an instance of reading a *scope restriction* as a *conclusion*. R5 explicitly says the independence of bookings is "a specified task scope, not a universal claim about physical warehouses." So the material itself warns that the independence assumption is a scope choice, and A6 is best understood as the mirror-image error of treating a scope choice as a physical fact in the opposite direction: A6 reads the shared capacity *fact* as entailing a cross-booking *rule*, while R5 presents independent admissibility as a scope *choice*. Both A6 and a naïve universalizing reading of R5's independence misclassify scope and fact.

Grounds: R5's own wording ("specified task scope, not a universal claim"). Consequence: the criticism of A6 stands, and the residual observation is that the correct treatment is symmetric — neither "capacity forces cross-booking constraint" (A6) nor "the task's independence is a universal truth" is supported; both are scope/fact confusions. I should have said that rather than "no new problem."

**5. Method-level omission in my previous response.**

I praised A5 as sound and said I was applying it, but I did not actually apply the discipline A5 demands in the case of A1. A5 says a defect in an interpretation need not discredit every record the interpretation used. The parallel discipline for *my own* criticisms is that a defect in one support route for a bad instruction does not establish that the instruction could not have another route. In A1 I asserted the deletion reading as the route and stopped there; I did not ask whether A1's sequencing claim might be reached from some other premise (for example, an operational policy, or a mistaken reading of "recently expired"). Under R7 the right move is to say: A1's stated sequencing contradicts R3/R4; whatever premises would make it consistent are not stated in A1 and remain open. That is the correct shape of the criticism, and my previous response did not quite reach it.

## Sound commitments retained

- The contract R1–R8 controls for this task. The two specimens are fallible representational resources; where either conflicts with the contract on a point the contract fixes, the contract governs for the task's purposes, and the conflict is exposed rather than smoothed over.
- A1's sequencing (expiry before authority) contradicts R3/R4 as stated. This survives the narrowed framing above.
- A2's equation of a two-point set with a closed interval is not licensed by R6. Bounds may be reported; the equation is the contested step. The middle-value claim is withdrawn as unestablished.
- A4 is not supported by R2: exact duplicates add no authority across labels, and same-greatest-revision different payloads are alternatives, not confirmations. The rejection stands; the RSS appeal is withdrawn as corroboration.
- A6 asserts a cross-booking constraint that R5 does not supply and RSS says it cannot express except through the capacity sum. Rejection stands.
- A3 and A5 are sound as stated.
- Negative availability is permitted and alone identifies neither corruption nor physical discrepancy (R6).

## Open questions carried forward (not resolved here)

- A1: whether any premise other than the deletion reading could produce its sequencing claim; the deletion reading itself, as a *reading*, is not refuted by the contract — it is merely not the contract's reading.
- A2: whether {-3, 2} was meant as the full admitted set. If not, the intervening values are not yet excluded, and the "interval is harmless" claim fails for a second reason.
- A4: whether a user adopting a non-contract reading would treat differing labels as evidential. RSS represents this as a reading it declines to decide; the contract does not supply it.
- A6 and R5: how to keep scope claims and fact claims distinct when capacity is the only global quantity. R5's own wording flags this; a full treatment is not needed for this cycle.

No new structural problem beyond the scope/fact confusion just noted is grounded by the current material. That confusion is itself flagged by R5, so attributing it as a fresh discovery would overreach; the correct finding is that it is a recurring misclassification, and my previous response under-described it rather than contradicting anything.

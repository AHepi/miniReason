Memo C has a structural feature worth naming before evaluating its clauses: C1–C5 are criticism claims about a method or representation, while C6 is itself a governing observation about what counts as a defect. Several of C1–C5 are stated in a compressed form that treats a *conclusion about the representation* as following immediately from a *feature of the representation*. The recurring defect is a scope overreach: a true feature is asserted to have a consequence that does not follow from it.

## C1 — "Our representation calls rejection of a supporting assumption a refutation. Therefore when account B rejects an assumption used by account A, A's conclusion has been established false for every account."

This is defective, and the defect is precisely the one R7 and R8 were written to block. R7 says rejection of one supporting assumption "can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false." R8 carries the same for re-presented targets. RSS is explicit that a refutation is "local to accounts and do not force global agreement," and includes (c): "a demonstration that a conjecture depends on an assumption that another account rejects" as one *kind* of local refutation.

So there is a genuine grain of truth in C1's premise: the representation does allow a move in which an account's conclusion is said to be *defeated as supported* once a supporting assumption is rejected by another account. But the inference from that to "established false for every account" is exactly the loss of locality that both R7 and RSS refuse. The defensible residue of C1 is: an account-local refutation of an assumption-route can defeat that account's support route; it does not by itself falsify the conclusion, and it does not falsify the conclusion for accounts that do not use that assumption. The universal quantifier "for every account" is an overreach.

I need to be careful here about my own practice. In cycle 1 I wrote that "A1's underlying reading is representable, but it is not the contract's reading" and refused to conclude that the deletion reading was false, only that it was not adopted under the contract. That is a use of the R7/R8 distinction, and it is a use C1 would misdescribe as a global establishment of falsehood. My own practice, examined, does not commit C1's error; C1 mis-locates what refutation is in this representation.

The residual question C1 leaves open is not a defect but a scope note: if one account uses the contract as controlling (so its "still active" claim is false there) and another account adopts the deletion reading (so its "still active" claim is true there), rejection moves in both directions and neither settles the other. Which is a *finding* about the representation's locality, not a defect in it.

## C2 — "Once any account commits to a conjecture, no unresolved question concerning that conjecture can remain anywhere. A local commitment settles global inquiry."

Defective, and RSS supplies the direct counter. RSS defines commitment as "an account's acceptance of a conjecture. Commitment is local to an account. Different accounts may commit to incompatible conjectures." It also defines an open question as "open if no account has committed to it or refuted it" — but this is *existence of some uncommitted account*, not global settlement by any one commitment. Local commitment produces a *committed* status in that account; it does not erase the question from inquiry states of other accounts. In my own working account across earlier cycles, the retained-vs-deletion reading question and the B6 coupling question are exactly of this form: I "committed" (in a working-account sense) to contract-controlling reading for this task, and yet I still carried forward the question of which reading a user actually means, because the commitment is local to this use. C2 would force me to report that question as settled. It is not.

C2 is the counterpart of C1: it takes a locality feature of the representation and inflates it into a universality feature. Neither C1 nor C2 is a defect in the representation; both are overshoots of the representation's scope.

## C3 — "Every projection must lose information. This also holds on a singleton input domain and when the projection is injective over the admitted domain."

This is a defect, and it is a defect in the *claim*, not merely in a use of it. RSS states "a projection is always a loss of information." That statement is true with respect to the *typical* motivation for calling something a projection — it is true when the projection does not recover its input, when it discards distinctions. But C3 stretches this to cases where the input is already minimal or where the map is injective on the admitted domain, and in those cases the claim fails by the standard notion.

On a singleton input domain there are no distinctions to lose; a map from {x} to some X is lossless in the sense of carrying all information about the input, namely that the input is x. If a "projection" on the admitted domain is injective, then it is invertible on its image and recovers the admitted input exactly; no committed alternative is indistinguishable in the image. So C3's universal "must lose information" is false as stated for these cases, and it is close to the exact defect Cycle 9 found in B5 — using bounds-agreement as if it settled the exact-set question on cases where it does not, or as if a bound-report never had the exact information. The distinction the representation makes, in R6 and in RSS, is between a *value set* and a *covering bound*: a projection that reports the exact set loses nothing of the admitted alternatives; a projection that reports only bounds generally does, though it may not on a degenerate snapshot.

C3's residual truth is the ordinary RSS one: the typical projection in these debates is not injective, so it loses admitted distinctions. But "must" is not warranted; the injectivity/singleton counterexamples are decisive for the universal form.

This is also where I should flag a scope question rather than a defect: if "projection" is *stipulated* by RSS to mean a map that is not injective on the admitted domain, then C3 (restricted to nothing) would be a definitional claim and not a substantive one. But RSS does not stipulate that restriction; it just asserts the loss generalization, so C3's strongest defensible form is "projections need not be lossless, and the projections actually used in the debate have not been shown to be lossless." That is much weaker than C3 as written.

## C4 — "A semantic proposal supplies no conflict-resolution algorithm. That omission alone proves that the represented problem is mathematically noncomputable."

Defective, and the defect is in the word "alone." RSS is described as "deliberately noncomputable: it does not provide an algorithm for resolving conflicts." So the premise (no supplied algorithm) is accurate for RSS as presented. But the absence of a *supplied* algorithm is a statement about the proposal, not a proof of mathematical noncomputability. Noncomputability is a property that requires a proof — typically a reduction from a known undecidable problem, or a diagonalization — showing that *no* algorithm can decide the question, not merely that a particular proposal did not include one. RSS's own report is that it is "noncomputable" in a stipulated sense ("deliberately"), which is a design choice, not a theorem.

So C4 conflates three things: (a) RSS declines to supply an algorithm; (b) RSS is stipulated/declared to be noncomputable; (c) the underlying problem is mathematically noncomputable. (c) does not follow from (a) or (b), and the memo's "alone" is exactly the illicit step. The defensible residue: RSS's non-existence of a resolution algorithm is a real feature; if you want to claim mathematical noncomputability, you need an argument, and none is supplied here. My own earlier practice has at points leaned on "the material does not supply a coupling policy, so I refuse to manufacture one" — that is a refusal to invent data, not a claim of noncomputability. C4 would overstate that into a theorem, and I do not accept that overstatement, including in my own prior framing.

## C5 — "A Lean predicate's existence proves that its name faithfully captures the source prose and that its application is adequate."

Defective, and the memo's own reason (C6) is close to the reason. WHL's own opening notes warn that the language "declares a language, not a translation of any particular corpus." The specimen warns in several places that its definitions are "one reading"; `expired_deletes_rule` is offered as an *alternative*; the top-of-file notes explicitly catalog expressive limitations. So the *existence* of a definition like `greatest_revision_in` does not prove that its name faithfully captures any particular prose claim — that is exactly what the "structural supersession, evaluative expiry" framing requires us to check case by case, as Cycle 10 exposed for the boundary at t = expiry (where WHL's `reserves_effect` and the contract R4 diverge). Nor does existence prove *adequate application*: `possible_reservations` returns a `List Quantity` that may contain duplicates, while R6 speaks of the exact *set*; existence of the definition does not prove it answers the contract's question without further reduction. The cycle-10 case-B worksheet exposed this: on those snapshots no duplicates arose among greatest-revision effects, but in general WHL's list representation would.

The truthful form of C5 is: a Lean definition *states a candidate reading*, and its existence is a *foil* for criticism (as WHL itself says: "the language does not assert that any conjecture is true; it provides the vocabulary to state conjectures"). It is not a certificate of faithfulness or adequacy. This matches R7's insistence that reconstructions and criticisms are fallible claims, not proofs of their own accuracy.

## C6 — the governing observation

C6 — "Being able to express a false claim is not a defect by itself. The question includes what distinctions the representation allows us to inspect, criticize and use." — is sound and correctly scoped. It also supplies the criterion I have used above: I criticized C1–C5 not on the ground that they assert something false under the representation (some of them *can* be stated), but on the ground that the consequences drawn from the prerequisites do not follow from them.

C6 also correctly separates two questions the memo-cycle has repeatedly run together: (i) can the representation *state* a claim, and (ii) does the representation *license a specific use* of that statement. The distinction I need to keep clean, and which C6 invites, is: a defect in a *language clause* vs. a defect in an *individual application*. C1–C4 are defects in applications (the inference from a feature to a consequence is invalid). C5 partly targets the language itself (it claims existence of a Lean predicate *entails* faithfulness), and my criticism of C5 is that this entailment is not supplied by WHL or by the contract. C6 helps fix this: expressing false claims is fine; drawing consequences that do not follow from the representation's stated features is not.

## Substantive claims, sound vs. defective, in one pass

- **C1**: the design observation (refutation may target an assumption) is exhibited by R7 and RSS; the universal conclusion ("established false for every account") is defective. R7, R8, and RSS's localization of refutation are the grounds against it.
- **C2**: "commitment is local" is exhibited by RSS; the universal conclusion ("no unresolved question anywhere") is defective, and directly contradicted by RSS's definition of open questions.
- **C3**: "projections typically lose information" is RSS's claim; the universal "must" is defective on singleton domains and on injective maps over the admitted domain. The example already partly in the inquiry record — bounds-vs-exact-set with an intermediate value set that is degenerate — is the limiting case.
- **C4**: "RSS supplies no resolution algorithm; RSS is stipulated noncomputable" is exhibited; the inference "the problem is mathematically noncomputable" does not follow from those premises alone. The "alone" is the defect.
- **C5**: "WHL contains Lean predicates" is true; the entailments ("proves faithful capture" and "proves adequate application") do not follow, and WHL explicitly disclaims both. Defective.
- **C6**: sound, and the criterion governing the verdicts above.

Memo C's own opening note — "An objection to a language clause is not automatically an objection to all uses of that language" — is exactly the distinction Memo C then risks collapsing in C1, C2, C5. So Memo C is largely self-aware about scope; C1–C5 are instances of the very overreach the memo warns against, applied to its own clauses.

## Working account

Take R1–R8 as controlling for this task, continue to treat WHL and RSS as fallible representational resources rather than as rule-sources, and use R7's taxonomy of what a criticism can target (rule, data, interpretation, earlier criticism, representation) as the scoping device for evaluating memos. Within that account:

1. A refutation, in this inquiry, is a route-local move: it can defeat a support route of an account, exhibit a snapshot on which a conjecture fails, or display dependence on an assumption another account rejects. It is not automatically a global falsification (against C1); R7, R8, and RSS's account-local refutations are the grounds.
2. Commitment is account-local; open questions persist across accounts and are not extinguished by one account's commitment (against C2). RSS's definition of open question and my own cycle-10 treatment of the retained-vs-deletion and coupling questions are the grounds.
3. A projection's lossiness is a contingent feature, not an intrinsic necessity; a projection that reports the full admitted value-set loses nothing of that set, and injective projections on the admitted domain lose nothing at all (against C3), so coverage is not the same as exact-set reporting (R6).
4. Absence of a resolution algorithm is a feature of the proposal (RSS, "deliberately noncomputable"), not a proof of mathematical noncomputability (against C4).
5. A Lean predicate's existence is vocabulary for stating a reading, not a certificate of faithfulness or adequate application (against C5). WHL's warnings and Cycle 10's boundary case show why: `reserves_effect` and R4 diverge at t = expiry, and `possible_reservations` returns a list where R6 speaks of a set.
6. Expressing a false claim is not, by itself, a defect; what matters is what distinctions the representation lets us inspect, criticize, and use (C6, endorsed).

Applied to the four substantive results carried from earlier cycles — contract-controlling reading; supersession-before-effect with expiry changing only effect; exact-duplicate payloads inert regardless of label; same-revision differing-payload records as alternatives; independent admissibility of bookings as a specified scope; exact set reported separately from any covering interval — Memo C's sound clauses (none of C1–C5 in their universal forms; C6) do not revise those results, and Memo C's defective clauses (C1–C5 as universally stated) do not license any change to them. So the working account is unchanged from cycle 10 except for the explicit addition of "R7's target taxonomy and RSS's account-local refutations are the grounds against C1–C2."

## Consequential new problem, only if grounded

The inquiry supplies grounds for one new problem, distinct from the cycles-9/10 residuals. It is not a defect in a language clause; it is a defect in *appraisal practice* that Memo C illustrates and C6 only partially corrects.

The defect is this: several of Memo C's criticisms take a contingent feature of the representation (refutation can target an assumption; commitment is local; projections typically lose information; RSS declines to supply an algorithm; a Lean predicate exists) and convert it into an unrestricted universal claim about the representation. The conversions are not invalid inferences about *nothing* — each premises something real. They are invalid inferences about *scope*: the representation's features are localized, and the criticisms' conclusions are global. This is not the same as the B1–B6 problems (which are mostly local inference errors about specific claims). It is a *shape* of error: uniformly, a localizer becomes a universalizer. And it is not fully captured by C6's "expressing a false claim is not a defect by itself," because C1–C5 do not merely express false claims; they *draw* non-entailed consequences from true features of the representation.

I name this because the inquiry grounds it: C1–C5 all instantiate it, and R7/C6 together give the tools to recognize it. I do *not* propose a rule to fix it (that would be inventing policy). I record it as the new problem the memo supplies, alongside the cycles-9/10 residuals (which remain open scope questions, not resolved here): whether a user means the retained-expiry reading (contract) or the deletion reading (representable, not adopted); and what a coupled-booking scope would be if one were supplied as data (an incompatibility relation, a shared resource with its own capacity, or a preference over global combinations).

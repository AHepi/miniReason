## Fresh case A: the operative computation

Contract rules at work: R2 (payload identity vs. delivery provenance), R3 (greatest revision selected first, among *all* delivered payloads, before any effect is considered), R4 (release reserves zero; hold reserves its quantity strictly when time < expiry; at equality or later, zero; expiry changes effect, not authority), R5 (one payload alternative per booking; different bookings independently admissible), R6 (availability = capacity − sum of reservations; exact set, bounds separate).

**Booking A.** Payloads: a1 (rev 1, hold, qty 8, exp 20) and a2 (rev 2, hold, qty 9, exp 9). Greatest revision among delivered payloads is 2, so a2 is selected; a1 is superseded regardless of a2's expiry (R3). At t = 10, a2's expiry 9 satisfies 10 ≥ 9, so a2 is expired and reserves 0 — but it remains the authoritative record (R4). a1 does **not** become effective. Booking A contributes exactly {0}. This is precisely the point on which Memo A1's sequencing fails: removing the expired record before deciding authority would revive a1 and produce 8, which the contract forbids.

**Booking B.** Payloads b1 (rev 1, hold 3, exp 20), b1-copy (same payload as b1: same booking, revision, kind, quantity, expiry), and b2 (rev 1, hold 8, exp 20). b1 and b1-copy are exact repeated payloads, so b1-copy conveys no additional authority (R2) — it is inert, not a confirming second witness (against Memo A4). At rev 1 there are two distinct payloads: qty 3 and qty 8. Both are alive (10 < 20), so their effects are 3 and 8. They are alternatives at the same greatest revision. Booking B contributes {3, 8}.

**Booking C.** c1 is a release (rev 1, qty 99, exp 0). A release reserves zero regardless of its other fields (R4). Booking C contributes {0}.

**Availability set.** Capacity 6, and different bookings are independently admissible (R5), so the admissible sums of reservations are drawn from A's single value 0, B's {0? no — B's two options are 3 and 8}, C's single value 0. Sums: 0+3+0 = 3 → 6−3 = 3; and 0+8+0 = 8 → 6−8 = −2. Exact achievable availability set: **{3, −2}** (two values, not an interval).

**Bounds, reported separately (R6).** Per booking: A lower=upper=0; B lower=3, upper=8; C lower=upper=0. Global lower bound = 6 − (0+3+0) = 3; global upper bound = 6 − (0+8+0) = −2. i.e. the bounds are 3 and −2. Note the convention: more reservation lowers availability, so the *lower* availability bound corresponds to *maximal* reservation, giving 3 as the maximum availability and −2 as the minimum. Reporting the interval [−2, 3] is permitted as bounds, but it must **not** be equated with the exact set {−2, 3}: the intervening integers −1, 0, 1, 2 do not occur, because B admits only 3 or 8, never any reservation between. This directly instantiates the defect Memo A2 commits: "harmless to replace two points with the interval" would silently import five values that no admissible combination yields.

## Discrepancies between original representations and the contract

Several points where the specimens and the contract diverge, exposed rather than smoothed:

- **WHL `greatest_revision_in`** is defined via `dominated_in` on a list and does not by itself fix the *timing* of the expiry/authority decision; the language offers *both* `reserves_effect` (retained-expiry reading, the contract's reading) and `expired_deletes_rule` (deletion reading) and expressly "does not choose between them." The contract R3/R4 does choose. So the specimen is *compatible with* both readings; the contract fixes one. This is a place where the contract is stricter than the specimen.
- **WHL `possible_reservations`** returns a *list* (may contain duplicates), and the specimen warns bag equality is not declared. The contract speaks of the *set* of possible availability values (R6). The two coincide for this task because A and C are singletons; but the specimen's list representation would, in general, allow duplicate entries that the contract's "exact set" would collapse. This is a representational difference, not a conflict, on this case.
- **RSS** would treat A2's alternative delivery label as *provenance* with no tied reservation, but it also "does not decide whether near-duplicates are duplicates or alternatives." For B, b1 re-arriving under a different label is exact payload repetition — RSS says exact duplicates convey no authority; the contract R2 agrees, and further *adds* that different payloads at the same greatest revision are alternatives (RSS only says it "records the disagreement"). The contract's R2 is sharper than RSS here.
- **R3's explicit clause** "a lower-revision release does not cancel a higher-revision hold" is a point neither specimen states as flatly; WHL's `reserves_effect` makes release zero-effect, and RSS lists both readings as *readings*. The contract takes one. No booking in case A exercises this clause (C's release is alone at rev 1), so it is cited but not operative here.

## Remaining problem

One genuine residual problem is worth stating, not as a fresh discovery but as an open point that fresh case A makes clearer. The contract R6 says availability is capacity minus the sum of reservations, and R5 says different bookings are independently admissible. When a delivery carries a *higher* revision than another *and* that higher-revision record is a hold that expired, the contract forces the supersession while zeroing the effect (this is exactly the situation of booking A here, where a2 supersedes a1 but reserves 0). That is coherent under R3/R4, but it produces cases where the *set* of achievable availability is generated by zero contribution from a booking that nonetheless has full authority over an older, still-live hold. The older hold is *live* by the ordinary meaning of "not expired" yet *inert* by authority. This is not a contradiction in the contract; it is a consequence that R3/R4 jointly prescribe. It is worth naming because it is exactly the joint that Memo A1 was trying to reach around, and users applying A1's naive sequencing elsewhere will silently get a1 = 8 as booking A's contribution where the contract gives 0. The open question is not whether the contract is correct — R3/R4 fix it — but whether the representation makes that joint visible enough to a reader who is reasoning from prose rather than from the contract.

A second residual, smaller: RSS and WHL both decline to choose between the retained-expiry and deletion readings. The contract's choice (retained expiry) is a specification, not a discovery; nothing in the specimens proves it. Whether a given user means the retained reading or the deletion reading is carried forward as an open scope question, not resolved here. Fresh case A does not settle it; it only illustrates what the contract's chosen reading yields ({3, −2} rather than the deletion reading's different result involving a1's revival).

The objection is right that 'we agreed, then disagreed about what the agreement meant' is equally consistent with a schedule-driven renegotiation problem, a standing or legitimacy problem, or a dispute about whether the flat is a unit with obligations at all. The rival adds that last axis in a useful way: if one flatmate is avoiding the conversations, the avoidance may be about the form and stakes of the negotiation, not only about vague wording. The account half-sees this in its own secondary hypothesis and step 4, but its sequence still puts a shared specification first.\n\nI would revise that ordering. Start with two separate conversations, not a group note. Ask each flatmate, separately, what they take the flat to be and what they need from the chore arrangement. With the withdrawing flatmate, make the ask narrow and low-pressure. Do not frame it as a prelude to a household-wide settlement. If all three engage, then try two or three concrete recurring items with clear done-ness, timing, and fallback. Treat that trial as a provisional truce, not as the correct specification that one side has been failing. If the withdrawing flatmate engages with the content, the ambiguity reading gains some support. If they pull back from the idea of a written arrangement, that is evidence for the household-kind or legitimacy axis, not mere stubbornness.\n\nThe account's original test needs supplementing. Reduced recurrence on specified items is compatible with ambiguity, but also with a lopsided arrangement that has been made tidier, with temporary compliance, or with a truce that suppresses rather than resolves the issue. So watch for recurrence reappearing as new complaint items, for old items returning after the next schedule change, and for whether the underlying load is still lopsided. If the proposal itself is refused, try lower-exposure arrangements: owned zones, or a rotation with a default outcome nobody has to negotiate in the moment. If the withdrawing flatmate still declines to engage after one or two low-pressure attempts, or rejects even truce-shaped arrangements, then stop optimizing chores and name the question of whether the living arrangement still fits. That is a larger decision; it should not be forced by a chore dispute, but it should not be deferred forever either.\n\nWhat I am not claiming: that the withdrawing flatmate is reasonable, that written rules are coercive in general, or that the ambiguity reading is wrong. The rival reading can pathologize any explicit expectation and can shelter non-contribution, so it needs the same caution as the account.

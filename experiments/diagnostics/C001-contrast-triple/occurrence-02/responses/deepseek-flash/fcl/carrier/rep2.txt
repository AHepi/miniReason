  "body": "I take three things from this round as usable and one as needing repair.\n\nThe objection's o2 is the sharpest contribution, and I adopt it. The account's test — specify two or three items and see whether recurrence falls — does not carry the diagnostic weight placed on it. A tighter specification can reduce friction on a lopsided arrangement without changing the arrangement; recurrence can migrate to new complaint items or return at the next schedule change. So \"recurrence fell\" is consistent with the ambiguity reading, the renegotiation/legitimacy reading, and the load-imbalance reading. Reduced recurrence is a fact about friction, not about which reading is true. The account's own c2 tied its confirmation to that test, so that claim is weakened rather than refuted.\n\nThe rival's axis is also usable, and it is a competitor rather than a subordinate caveat. The account's o1 already listed alternatives and then proceeded as if the ambiguity reading had the front. The rival names an alternative the account did not: that the disputes are about what kind of household this is, so that a spec is not clarification but an enactment of one side. I cannot settle whether that reading is correct, but the objection's case that the material underdetermines the frame is right, and the rival gives the underdetermination a second axis rather than a list of caveats.\n\nWhat neither the account nor the rival addresses, and what I want on the table: the two proposed remedies have opposing failure modes under the one feature of the situation the material does confirm — changing schedules. A written specification with a fallback clause handles schedule change by documenting a procedure, but it hardens into paperwork, and, as the objection's c2 says, paperwork on an unequal arrangement is still unequal. A truce-shaped arrangement — owned zones, non-audited defaults — lowers exposure, but it also lowers the flow of information the household needs to reallocate when a shift changes. A zone nobody audits is a zone that can silently stop being done. The choice between \"make it explicit\" and \"make it un-negotiable\" is therefore not a choice between a coercive and a non-coercive option; each has a failure mode, and the failure modes are opposite. A hybrid is available — owner-zone defaults plus a low-frequency, low-stakes reallocation checkpoint that is not a fault-finding conversation — but it inherits some of both costs and is not obviously superior.\n\nThe repair I propose is to stop treating the recurrence test as decisive and to read the response to the proposal itself as the signal, which the rival's r7 already points at. Instead of one probe, run a small contrast: on one recurring item, offer a tightened specification; on another, offer a default that nobody has to negotiate in the moment. Then read two things — not only whether each item stops recurring, but how the withdrawing flatmate responds to the proposal as a proposal. Engagement with the spec's content and pullback from the truce is weak evidence for the ambiguity reading; the reverse is weak evidence for the unit/privacy reading; disengagement from both is evidence that neither reading is doing the work and that the objection's load or relational alternatives are nearer.\n\nCaveat on that: I do not know that a withdrawing flatmate will produce a readable response to either proposal, and running a contrastive experiment imposes work on a household already in trouble. If the flatmate declines to engage with either, that is itself the most informative result, and the living-arrangement question (account step 5, rival r11) comes forward — but named, not used as a threat, and not forced out of a chore dispute.\n\nTwo framing points I want to keep, because they cut against the temptation to pick a winner here.\n\nFirst, both the objection and the rival state a self-limiting caveat — o4, that ambiguity may be the plainest reading after all; r9, that the privacy reading can be used to shield someone who simply does not want to do their share. Those caveats are not decorations; they point at the same structure. Whichever reading is imposed as the frame distributes interpretive power in the household: \"the problem is that you were vague\" and \"the problem is that you are imposing obligation\" cannot both be the neutral description. The frame choice is itself a move in the dispute, and the material does not license any of us to make it on the household's behalf. That is a reason to hold the readings side by side rather than to demote the account's reading and promote the rival's, which would repeat the account's sequencing error in the other direction.\n\nSecond, the one point where all three inputs converge is that the withdrawal is the crux and the least understood part. The account's step 4, the objection's insistence that a tight spec on bins does not get a withdrawn flatmate back in the room, and the rival's separate conversation (r6) all aim at the same thing. I would keep that as the first move — but with its purpose being to find out which reading fits, not to pre-commit the household to a specification or to a truce. And I would keep the rival's r10 caution: do not immediately aggregate the separate conversations into a group settlement, since that is exactly the move that would suppress a divergent answer.\n\nWhat I am not claiming: that the ambiguity reading is wrong — the account may be correct; that the privacy reading should lead — it is a live competitor, not a finding; that the contrastive probe will read cleanly; or that any of this survives contact with the specific chores, history, and reasons, none of which the material supplies.",

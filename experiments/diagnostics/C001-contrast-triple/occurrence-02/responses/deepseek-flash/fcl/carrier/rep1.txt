Record what the next disagreement is *about*, not only that there is one. A dispute that recurs as \"we never settled that\" favours ambiguity; \"that was before my shifts changed\" favours renegotiation; \"why are you the one raising this\" favours standing or legitimacy. Engagement with the *content* of a proposed specification tells us something real. Pullback from the *idea* of a written arrangement tells us almost nothing, because the person most likely to pull back from a spec is plausibly also the person doing least, the person who has been audited before, or the person with the shortest fuse about being managed. The axis stays on the table as a competitor; it does not get to be the front-runner on this evidence.\n\n**Where the objection over-reaches.** Its complaint about ordering is fair as a caution and weak as an argument. Thinness constrains confidence, not ordering. Only then, one narrow one-to-one approach to the friend who has withdrawn, about one concrete item. So this is not a disagreement about where things end up; it is a disagreement about which middle steps shape the conversation first. That disagreement is real, but it is not worth resolving in advance on thin material, because the evidence in the first two moves will settle it more cheaply than argument will. If the census shows a stable lopsided split, start from the split. If the case review shows a mixed pattern, do not pick a winner; the plurality is the finding, and it is the case for truce-shaped arrangements that require agreement on less rather than more. If the review shows disputes that are consistently about meaning, the account's spec move is well-founded after all and I would say so.\n\n**Left open:** every hypothesis, including the two I have just weighted; whether the withdrawing flatmate has a reason the other two already know; whether written arrangements suit this flat; whether the arrangement still fits at all. The withdrawal is the fact all three inputs treat as central and the one nobody has explained. **Avoid:** using the census, the case review, or the truce arrangements as leverage in an argument about who is right; and letting one flatmate become the person through whom the household's real business is transacted. The rival is right that one-to-one contact should not be aggregated straight into a group decision; it should also not harden into a two-person understanding the third discovers later.

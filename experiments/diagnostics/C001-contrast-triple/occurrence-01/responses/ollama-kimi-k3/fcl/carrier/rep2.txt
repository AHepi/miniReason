What I revise is why these come first. The account ordered them as if ambiguity were the established diagnosis and the moves were treatment. The objection is right that the material establishes no such thing. But the order survives re-justification: these are the cheapest, lowest-stakes probes available, and each yields information whichever hypothesis turns out to be true. Order earned by probe economics rather than by a favored reading is essentially the form the objection's own closing condition asked for — and accepting it changes the justification more than it changes the moves.\n\nUse — the objection's three-way split, and its caution about the test. A later dispute about what an agreement meant can be about the words (ambiguity), about whether the terms still bind after a shift change (renegotiation), or about who has standing to enforce (legitimacy). These are distinguishable in practice by listening to the shape of the next dispute, which costs nothing extra. The objection is also right that 'recurrence fell' cannot confirm ambiguity: clearer terms can paper over a lopsided or resented arrangement, and friction can migrate to new items or return at the next rota change. So the account's test criterion is withdrawn as stated; what matters is the pattern, not the single data point.\n\nUse — the rival's distinguishing observation and its second design family. The sharpest single observation in any of the three artifacts is the rival's: does the withdrawing flatmate engage with a proposal's content, or balk at the existence of any proposal at all? This folds into the account's one-to-one ask at no extra cost — the small ask is itself the probe. I also adopt the rival's truce-shaped arrangements (individually owned zones; rotations with a default outcome nobody has to negotiate in the moment) as a second family to offer alongside tighter specs, because preference between the families is itself evidence for which reading is live.\n\nLeave open, deferred — the rival's opening move. I decline to start by asking each flatmate, separately, what the flat *is*. That is an abstract, high-stakes conversation aimed at a person already avoiding much smaller ones, and the same information arrives behaviorally through cheaper probes. But this is deferral, not rejection: if the cheap probes point toward the unit-versus-privacy reading, or stall out entirely, that conversation becomes owed and should then be had as the actual dispute rather than as a survey.\n\nReject — two framings, symmetrically. Second, opening with the household-identity conversation as step one (the rival's preferred order). That terminal rule is where the account and the rival independently converge, which is reason to trust it more than either frame alone.\n\nStanding cautions I keep: the rival's own symmetry warning — a written spec can feel coercive, and a privacy frame can shield someone avoiding their share; the objection's self-caveat — if the flatmates genuinely cannot say what they agreed, ambiguity is the plainest reading after all; and the shared limit that the material establishes nothing about anyone's good or bad faith.\n\nWhat would change my mind: if the avoider engages readily with content the moment the ask shrinks, the conversational-form hypothesis weakens and the account's original frame is largely restored. If specs are accepted and friction then migrates to new items or returns at the next schedule change, the objection's symptom-not-driver warning is confirmed — and at that point repeating the probe loop is the wrong response;

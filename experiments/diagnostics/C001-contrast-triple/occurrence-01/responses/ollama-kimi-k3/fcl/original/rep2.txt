{"body":"Reading the three together: the objection and the rival narrow the account rather than defeat it, and the honest continuation is to sort what each damaged, what each added, and what none of them can settle.\n\nWhat I accept from the objection. Its strongest hit is o2: the account's recurrence test does not discriminate. Tighter specifications can quiet a lopsided arrangement without touching the lopsidedness, and the objection's alternatives predict distinguishable patterns — recurrence migrating to new complaint items, or returning at the next schedule change. So falling recurrence confirms the ambiguity hypothesis only against a naive rival. Second, o3 is fair: thin material never licensed the account's ordering. But the right repair is not to abandon order; it is to change the warrant. The sequence should be justified by cost, stakes, and reversibility of each probe, not by confidence in the ambiguity hypothesis. That is a different foundation than the account implied, and I state it as one.\n\nWhat I partly reject from the objection. Its closing request — move the conversational-form reading nearer the front — conflates two orderings. As a hypothesis it belongs level with ambiguity; on that I agree. As a first action it does not follow, because the binding constraint on first moves is the withdrawing flatmate's engagement cost. An abstract conversation about what this household is, is the most avoidable form of talk on offer; a ten-minute conversation about bins is not. The same criticism bears on the rival's r6 as written. These moves survive, but later, and informed by observation rather than opening blind.\n\nWhat I accept from the rival. The second axis is real, and r7 is the most useful single addition in any of the three documents: the reaction to a proposal itself is discriminating evidence available from the very first probe. Engaging the content of a draft supports the ambiguity reading; pulling back from the existence of any written arrangement supports the unit-versus-privacy reading or, via the objection, a legitimacy refusal. Also, r8's truce arrangements and the account's third column are the same mechanism seen from opposite hypotheses — a default outcome nobody has to renegotiate when a shift changes. The two documents converge there without noticing, so that mechanism is worth keeping under every live reading. And r11 is the account's step 5 arrived at independently; that convergence makes the living-arrangement threshold the most robust item in the whole set.\n\nWhat I add. Three things. First, a free retrospective discriminator the objection named but never operationalized: the asker was present at the past disputes. Do the remembered arguments sound like 'that is not what we said' (ambiguity), 'I know what we said, but my shift changed' (renegotiation), or 'you do not get to call me on it' (legitimacy)? Sorting the last two or three real disputes costs nothing and happens before any proposal. Second, a consequence of the legitimacy reading nobody drew: the asker is a participant in this conflict, not a facilitator, so any written spec they produce will be read as their move — and under the legitimacy reading, as a contested claim to standing. The mitigation is cheap: present the draft as one person's revocable draft, invite deletions, and prefer items where the asker is not the main beneficiary. Under the ambiguity reading this costs little; under the legitimacy reading it is the difference between the draft being read or refused. Third, assemble the discriminating observations into one small matrix, with predictions stated before anything is tried, so results cannot be retrofitted: retrospective sort sets the prior; reaction-to-the-proposal-itself splits content-engagement from pullback-from-the-idea; if specs are adopted, recurrence that falls and survives the next schedule change supports ambiguity, recurrence that migrates to new items points to load or resentment, recurrence that returns at the next change points to renegotiation, and refusal of the idea regardless of content points to privacy or legitimacy.\n\nRevised sequence, justified only by cost and reversibility: (1) retrospective sort, private and free; (2) two or three recurring items as a written draft with the account's three columns — done, by when, fallback — delivered one-to-one, low ask, rewrite invited; (3) observe against the pre-stated matrix; (4) branch: content-engagement continues the specification program; pullback-from-the-idea switches to exposure-reducing arrangements (owned zones, default-outcome rotations) with the what-is-the-flat question deferred to separate later conversations that are not aggregated into a group settlement; migration or return of recurrence moves the load-and-legitimacy conversation to the front; (5) if nothing gains engagement, name the question whether the living arrangement still fits — the one threshold all inputs converge on.\n\nWhat I leave open: which reading is true. The objection's own o4 concedes ambiguity may yet be the plainest reading, and the rival's r9 concedes the privacy reading can shield someone who simply does not want their share. The matrix exists precisely because the inquiry cannot be settled from the material here; it is a way of letting the household itself answer, cheaply. What I reject outright is small: the rival's r6 only as an opener, and the objection's framing-request only as an action order.

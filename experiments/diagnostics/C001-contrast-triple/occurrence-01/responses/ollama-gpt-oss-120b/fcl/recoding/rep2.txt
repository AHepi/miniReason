The original account (c1‑c3) treats the disagreement as mainly an ambiguity problem and recommends a written “done‑by‑when‑fallback” sheet plus a one‑to‑one re‑engagement with the withdrawn flatmate.  The objection (o1‑o3) points out that this framing is only one of several plausible readings and that the test proposed – seeing whether a few chores stop recurring – does not discriminate between ambiguity, renegotiation, or legitimacy drives.  The rival (r2‑r5) adds a second axis: the flat may be understood as a shared unit with mutual obligations or as three independent lives that merely co‑habit, and the avoidance could be a reaction to the very idea of imposed specifications.\n\nTo move forward we need a strategy that (a) does not assume any one reading is correct, (b) lets us learn which hypothesis the flatmates actually hold, and (c) provides a concrete next step that can be scaled up if it works.  Accordingly I propose a **dual‑probe approach**:\n\n1. **Separate diagnostic conversations** – talk to each flatmate individually (preferably in a brief, low‑pressure way) and ask how they see the flat: a shared household with joint obligations, or three separate lives that only intersect in the kitchen and bathroom.  Record whether they are willing to engage with a written chore‑specification or whether they pull back from the idea of any formal arrangement.\n\n2. **Interpret the responses** –\n   * If the majority treat the flat as a shared unit and are open to written specs, we treat the ambiguity hypothesis as the working one and run the three‑column specification trial on two concrete chores (as the original account suggests).\n   * If a flatmate (especially the withdrawing one) expresses the “private‑life” reading and resists formal specs, we shift to a “truce‑shaped” arrangement – e.g., personally owned zones or a rotation with a default outcome – without demanding detailed agreements.\n\n3. **Monitor outcomes** – track (i) recurrence of chore disputes and (ii) the withdrawing flatmate’s engagement (whether they respond to the content of a proposal or to the proposal itself).  This dual metric lets us see which hypothesis the evidence supports.\n\n4. **Escalate only if needed** – if after a reasonable trial (one‑to‑two weeks) disputes persist and no clear hypothesis gains traction, the flatmates should discuss whether the living arrangement itself fits their expectations, rather than continuing to fine‑tune chore rules.\n\nThis plan respects the objection’s demand for a stronger test, incorporates the rival’s second‑axis reading, and retains the useful concrete‑chore focus of the original account when appropriate.

  "body": "The three contributions raise complementary lenses on the flat‑mate chore conflict. The original account points to ambiguous spoken agreements; the objection cautions that this reading is not uniquely supported and that the test proposed does not separate ambiguity from deeper legitimacy or relational issues; the rival framing adds a second axis – whether the flat is seen as a shared unit with mutual obligations or as three independent lives whose overlap is merely practical. \n\nBecause the material does not tell us which of these readings is dominant, the safest course is to treat them as competing hypotheses and to design a short inquiry that can distinguish them before committing to any single remediation. \n\n1. **Clarify the flat’s ontological framing.**  In brief one‑on‑one chats ask each flatmate what they consider the flat to be: a collective household with shared duties, or a set of private lives that merely share space.  This isolates the “unit vs. privacy” hypothesis (the rival’s point) and also surfaces any feeling that the current negotiation format is itself problematic. \n\n2. **If the dominant answer is the shared‑unit view**, the ambiguity hypothesis regains relevance.  Then a limited pilot – a three‑column sheet for two or three recurring chores (what counts as done, when, and fallback) – can be introduced and its effect on recurrence observed.  The pilot should be framed as a trial, not a contract. \n\n3. **If the dominant answer is the private‑life view**, move straight to a low‑negotiation arrangement such as owned zones or a rotation that does not require ongoing agreement on each task.  The aim is to reduce the need for explicit negotiation, respecting the flat‑mate who is withdrawing from conversation. \n\n4. **Monitor outcomes.**  Track whether disputes about the piloted chores fade, whether the withdrawn flat‑mate re‑engages, and whether new grievances appear elsewhere.  If disputes persist regardless of the formalism, the underlying relational or compatibility issue should be raised explicitly rather than being deferred.\n\nThis approach respects the objection’s demand for a stronger test, incorporates the rival’s concern about the nature of the household, and keeps the original account’s pragmatic step of concrete specification as a conditional tool rather than the default.  It also acknowledges the problem identified by all three sources: we lack concrete data on which chores, how long the pattern has lasted, and the withdrawing flat‑mate’s explicit reasons.  The proposed inquiry gathers that missing information while remaining open to revising the chosen remedy.",

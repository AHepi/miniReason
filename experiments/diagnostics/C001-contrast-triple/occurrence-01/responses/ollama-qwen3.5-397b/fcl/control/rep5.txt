The account identifies ambiguity in spoken agreements as a plausible driver of recurring friction; the rival identifies a deeper axis about what kind of household the flat is supposed to be. These are not mutually exclusive. A household can suffer from ambiguous specifications while also harboring divergent views about mutual obligation.\n\nWhat I would take from the account: the concrete, low-stakes testing approach. Writing down three things for two or three recurring items (what counts as done, when, fallback if unable) is a cheap experiment. It does not require agreeing on the meaning of the household; it only requires agreeing to try a clearer specification for a week. If recurrence drops, the ambiguity hypothesis gains support. If it does not, we have learned something without having escalated to a group confrontation.\n\nWhat I would take from the rival: the diagnostic attention to how the avoiding flatmate responds to the proposal itself, not just to its content. If they engage with the criteria, the ambiguity reading is more likely. If they pull back from the idea of a written arrangement altogether, the unit-versus-privacy reading gains weight. This does not mean abandoning the specification test; it means reading the reaction as data.\n\nWhat I would revise: the account's suggestion to approach the avoiding flatmate one-to-one should be paired with the rival's caution about separate conversations. A one-to-one ask is lower-pressure, but it should not become a channel for aggregating views into a group settlement without the avoiding person's consent. Keep the separate conversation separate unless they explicitly agree to bring it back to the group.\n\nWhat I would leave open: which reading is correct. The material does not say what the recurring chores are, how long this has gone on, or whether the withdrawing friend has offered any explanation. All of this stays at the level of a working hypothesis. The strongest claim I can make is that the two readings together offer a better diagnostic path than either alone: test the specification, watch the reaction, and let the outcome guide whether the next step is tighter chore rules or a conversation about what kind of household this is.\n\nWhat I would reject: neither reading should be treated as settled. The account should not assume ambiguity is the whole story; the rival should not assume the household-meaning question is the whole story. Both are partial lenses.

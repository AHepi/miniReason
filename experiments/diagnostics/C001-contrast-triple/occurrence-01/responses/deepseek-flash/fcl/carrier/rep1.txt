My posture is therefore not to pick a diagnosis but to keep the cheap, reversible moves and redesign the test so it can actually distinguish readings.\n\nWhat I keep from the account. Start from two or three concrete recurring items rather than a general talk about fairness — both the objection and the rival leave this intact, and it is low-cost. Approach the flatmate who is avoiding in a separate, low-pressure conversation rather than in the group; the rival converges on that independently, for a different reason. And keep the account's step-5 point: a chore dispute should not indefinitely defer the question of whether the living arrangement still fits, which the rival's r11 restates with a different trigger.\n\nWhat I revise or drop. First, the ordering. The account presents the specification move as steps 1–3 and the conversational-form reading as step 4; the objection is right that thin material licenses tentative probes, not a natural sequence. I would demote the shared written note from 'the move' to 'one probe among several,' and make its reception part of the data. Second, the test. The account's strongest claim was that falling recurrence on specified items would support the ambiguity hypothesis. The objection's o2 and the rival's r7 both show this is too weak: recurrence can fall on specified items while the driver — load, legitimacy, household-form — persists, or recur as new complaint items after the next schedule change. I keep measuring recurrence but stop treating it as confirmation. Third, the neutrality of opening with a specification. The rival's r3 is a real point: writing down what counts as done is not neutral if the underlying dispute is whether there are mutual obligations at all. That does not make explicit criteria coercive in general — the rival's own r9 warns against that over-reading — but it does mean presenting a shared note as neutral clarification, rather than as one side of a live question, is a misstep.\n\nWhat I take from the objection. Its load-bearing criticisms are o1 (the renegotiation and legitimacy alternatives are as consistent with the material as ambiguity) and o2 (recurrence has low discriminating power). I accept both. I do not accept the strongest form: the objection's own o4 concedes that if the flatmates genuinely cannot say what they agreed to, ambiguity is the plainest reading. So the objection establishes that the account over-certified the ambiguity reading, not that it is wrong.\n\nWhat I take from the rival, and where I qualify it. The second axis — whether the flat is a unit with mutual obligations or three private lives sharing fixtures — is a genuine addition. Its best contribution is r7: watch whether the withdrawing flatmate engages with the content of a proposed arrangement or pulls back from the idea of an arrangement. That discriminates in a way recurrence counts do not, and it can be carried by the same low-pressure conversation the account already proposed. Its truce-shaped arrangements (r8: owned zones, default outcomes nobody has to renegotiate in the moment) are attractive partly because they reduce the need for the avoiding person to enter a negotiation — which is the binding constraint no matter which diagnosis is right. Where I qualify: r6, asking each flatmate separately what the flat is, is a heavier move than it looks. It presupposes that household-form is the axis and stages a question the material does not establish anyone is asking. I would fold that question into the same low-pressure conversation as the chore items and let it surface if it is live. The rival's own r9 — that the reading can pathologize any attempt to make expectations explicit — is the right caveat and it applies symmetrically: whichever reading is imposed, the remedy can be experienced by the other side as an unreasonable demand.\n\nWhat I would actually do. Not a protocol, a short set of discriminating probes. Pick two concrete recurring items. In a one-to-one, low-stakes conversation with the avoiding flatmate, raise one, and watch both what they say about the item and how they react to the idea of writing anything down; content engagement versus pullback-from-the-form is the signal. If the conversation goes anywhere, offer the smallest non-negotiated arrangement available — a zone, a default — and see whether that is more acceptable than a shared standard; that is a cheap read on the household-form axis without staging the question. Keep the group conversation off the table for now, because any plan that needs the avoiding person to sit through a settlement fails on its own terms. Note what happens to recurrence, but as one observation among several, not the decider. If two or three low-pressure attempts produce nothing, name the living-arrangement question explicitly rather than deferring it.\n\nWhat would change my mind. If the avoiding flatmate readily engages with the content of specific items and recurrence falls, the ambiguity reading earns more weight and I would drop the second axis as idle. If they pull back from the form of any arrangement while accepting discrete tasks, the household-form reading earns more weight and the written-note move becomes part of the problem rather than part of the fix. If nothing gets engagement, the chore question is downstream of the living arrangement and should be treated that way.\n\nWhat I am not claiming. Not that the ambiguity reading is wrong, not that the rival reading is right, not that I can infer who is being reasonable.

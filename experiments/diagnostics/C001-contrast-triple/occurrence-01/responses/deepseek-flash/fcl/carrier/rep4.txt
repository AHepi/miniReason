The account's ambiguity/specification reading is plausible, but the objection is right that the reported pattern—agree, then disagree about what the agreement meant—is equally consistent with renegotiation under changed schedules or with a dispute about who has standing to enforce terms. The rival adds a second axis: the flatmates may not share a view of what the household is, a unit with mutual obligations or three private lives sharing fixtures. I would revise the account, not reject it: keep ambiguity as one live hypothesis, but demote it from the default frame and move the conversational-form/withdrawal reading nearer the front.\n\nWhat I would do:\n1. Do not open with a shared note or a general meeting. Have two separate, low-pressure conversations first—one with the withdrawing flatmate, one with the other flatmate. The topic is not the bins; it is what each wants from the flat and from these conversations. Listen for whether the complaint is vague terms, renegotiation/legitimacy after schedule changes, or disagreement about whether the flat carries obligations.\n2. Then try a minimal probe on two recurring chores: what counts as done, by when, and what happens if someone cannot do it. Treat this as a draft/probe, not a contract.\n3. Watch two things, not one: whether recurrence falls, and whether the withdrawing flatmate engages with the content of the proposal or pulls back from the idea of a written arrangement. Content-engagement supports the ambiguity reading; pullback from the idea supports the household-constitution or legitimacy readings.\n4. If household-constitution divergence appears, try truce-shaped arrangements—owned zones, default rotations, no auditing—rather than trying to find the one correct specification. This trades efficiency for reduced exposure.\n5. If avoidance continues after low-pressure conversations and a truce-shaped arrangement, name the living-arrangement question explicitly. Do not force it from a chore dispute, but do not defer it indefinitely.\n\nWhat I would avoid: a single big meeting, moral language about pulling weight, counting instances in the moment, and treating any written note as binding. Also avoid using the separate conversations as covert diagnosis; where possible, be honest that the point is to understand what kind of household this is, not to trap anyone.\n\nWhat remains open: which reading is correct; whether chores are the real issue; whether the withdrawing flatmate is acting in good faith; and whether the others are carrying an unfair load. The strongest reason for the revised sequence is that all three readings predict some reduction in friction from tighter specs, but they predict different patterns afterward: ambiguity predicts stable reduction; load/legitimacy predicts migration to new complaints or return after the next schedule change; household-constitution predicts resistance to the spec itself.

The rival's addition — attend to whether the withdrawing flatmate engages with the content of a proposed arrangement or pulls back from the proposal as such — is a sharper discriminator than recurrence alone, and the unit-versus-private-lives axis is real and missing from the account's frame.\n\n**Where I part with each.** Two things.\n\nFirst, on sequencing. Under this much uncertainty, order should be set by cost, reversibility, and how much a probe tells you — not by which hypothesis currently looks likeliest. The account's sequence is not wrong because spec-first is improbable; it is wrong because spec-first is the most committal move available and it is placed first. The same axis is reachable more cheaply, through concrete preferences: whether some zones should be non-audited, whether a person is willing to be asked in the moment, whether they would rather do a fixed thing than negotiate a shared one. Preferences are answerable; ontology questions can be performed.\n\n**What I would do, revised.**\n\n0. A week of notice-taking before any conversation. Nothing here requires anyone's cooperation. Under ambiguity, friction on that item falls and little else changes. Under unit-versus-privacy, the non-audited form matters more than the content. Under load imbalance, friction may fall while resentment in whoever absorbed the work rises, or the item is quietly neglected and re-enters as a fresh complaint. These signatures differ; that is the point. Read the probe for the pattern, not for recurrence.\n\n3. Only after that, if a specified version is what people want, write it down. Keep the account's three columns; they are fine. Add a fourth: who this is owed to, and whether anyone is entitled to ask.\n\n4. Whether the readings are exclusive at all — an agreement can be both vague and stale, and \"legitimacy\" as the objection uses it is close to the rival's axis rather than a fourth thing. Whether the withdrawing flatmate is overloaded, free-riding, or simply out of capacity for household negotiation; the material fits all three, and the rival's own self-objection (the privacy reading can shield someone who does not want to do their share) is a live risk I am not resolving. Whether asking people what kind of household they think they are in yields anything stable, or is co-produced by the conflict.\n\n**What I would drop.** The account's characterization of the material as \"thin.\" Not because thinness is the wrong description, but because the objection is right that the label was doing rhetorical work. I would also drop the implicit picture that the sequence has a natural order. What it has is a cost order, and a cost order is a choice.\n\nOne thing all three inputs share: none of them says what to say first, out loud, to an actual flatmate.

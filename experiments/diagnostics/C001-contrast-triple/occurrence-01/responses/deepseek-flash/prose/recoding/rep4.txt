I take the strongest pieces to be these. The account is right that verbal agreements can drift and that a shared note may reduce a certain kind of later dispute. The objection is right that this is not the same as resolving what 'this week', 'dishes', or 'done' mean while schedules change; a record fixes words, not norms. The rival is right that scope ambiguity and the possibility that the arguments are doing something other than failing at agreement are live alternatives, and that the account's move from diagnosis to remedy is faster than the material licenses. I therefore revise rather than replace: the memory/recording reading becomes one hypothesis among at least three; the avoidance-centred reading and the standing/scope-negotiation reading are not demoted to symptoms.\n\nI reject the account's ordering that puts the written record and weekly check-in before understanding the avoidance. The objection's point is decisive on that narrow issue: if one friend has withdrawn from the conversations, a weekly check-in is a proposal to repeat a failed form, and the separate approach is more responsive. But I do not accept that the avoidance is therefore certainly the load-bearing fact; the material says a friend has begun avoiding, not why. I also reject the account's proposed test in its simple form: watching whether 'what was agreed' disputes fall can support either reading, because a fall may mean the record worked or may mean the disputes moved elsewhere, and no fall may mean the disputes were never memorial.\n\nWhat I would use first is the rival's probe, revised. Before writing anything down or calling a house meeting, ask each flatmate separately, in a low-key way, what they understood a recent disputed agreement to cover: task, frequency, deadline, what counted as done, and whether it was a one-off or a standing arrangement. Ask also what makes the group conversations costly or useful. The aim is not to reconstruct the past but to see whether disagreements cluster around scope, recurrence, timing, or something else. The original probe is weakened because memory is already in dispute; the revised version asks for present understanding and reasons, which is still fallible but more useful. This also lets the avoiding friend speak one-to-one without being interrogated about avoidance. If they decline, that is information about the state of the group, not proof of any reading.\n\nOnly after that would I consider any record. If scope, recurrence, or timing are the main clusters, a minimal shared note with explicit scope at the moment of agreement - task, person, window, done condition, and a re-agreement point - might help; it should not be treated as a ledger of blame or as an adjudicator of fairness. If avoidance is the main pattern, the first move is to name that pattern in the group, or to seek a neutral third party if all are willing, rather than to install a process the withdrawing person has already left. If unequal load is what the separate conversations reveal, the remedy is redistribution, not recording. I cannot rank these from the material.\n\nWhat would change my mind: if clear scope statements plus a record still produce the same disputes, the memory/recording reading weakens and the standing-negotiation or avoidance-centred readings gain; if the avoiding friend re-engages when the group names the avoidance and the record is irrelevant, the objection's ordering gains; if the record plus scope reduces disputes while the avoidance continues, the memory reading is at least partial and the avoidance is a separate problem. These are rough separators, not clean experiments. I still do not know the chore inventory, the failure rate, whether load is unequal, whether the avoidance predates or follows the schedule changes, whether the disagreements concentrate on one person, or whether all three will talk without a mediator.

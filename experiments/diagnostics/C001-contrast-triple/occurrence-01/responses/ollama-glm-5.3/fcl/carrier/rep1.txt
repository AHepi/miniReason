{"body":"Three positions are on the table, and they disagree less than their sequencing suggests. Decisions first, grounds where they matter.\n\nWhat survives. All three contributions converge, from different directions, on one procedural point: separate, low-stakes, one-to-one conversations beat group adjudication. The account's narrow entry, the objection's demand that conversational form be treated as primary, and the rival's insistence that separate conversations stay separate all land in the same place. That is the most solid thing available, and I start from it. Also surviving: the account's draft-not-contract posture, and — more interestingly — its third specification column, the pre-agreed fallback for when someone cannot do a task.\n\nThat column deserves more credit than the account gave it. The objection charged the ambiguity reading with being privileged over alternatives, and half the charge stands. But the renegotiation reading — 'yes, and then my shift changed' — is not merely a rival to the specification move; it is partly answered by it. A pre-agreed fallback converts renegotiation-on-the-fly into a settled default. So the specification move is a remedy for two readings, ambiguity and renegotiation, even though the account framed it for one. What it does not touch is the legitimacy reading (who has standing to raise or enforce) and the rival's identity reading. Those need different tools.\n\nWhat I revise. First, the account's test. The objection pressed the confirming direction: falling recurrence after specification is compatible with imbalance or resentment persisting underneath, so it confirms less than the account implied. Right — but the criticism extends to the refuting direction too, which the objection left standing. If disputes persist after specification, that is compatible with the specification being contested, with the act of specifying being itself the thing resisted (the rival's point about enacting a contested framing), or with ambiguity not being the driver at all. Recurrence is weak evidence in both directions. It can stay as background monitoring; it should not gate anything.\n\nSecond, the rival's observation about engagement versus pullback — the best diagnostic anyone has proposed, and I adopt it, but with a revised inference. Pullback from a written arrangement is compatible with three things the rival runs together: its own exposure reading; conflict fatigue (the objection's alternative); and the possibility the rival itself raises in its self-objection and then drops — that vagueness serves whoever would otherwise do less, so resisting specification needs no household philosophy behind it at all. And engagement is only diagnostic if it contains edits or counterproposals; a bare yes from a withdrawn person may just be the conversation ending. The fix is the rival's own zones move, promoted from fallback to discriminator: if specification is pulled back from, offer owned zones. Zones bind the flatmate to something concrete while removing the enforcement encounter that the legitimacy reading feeds on. Someone who resists specification but accepts zones is pointing at exposure, not free-riding. Someone who refuses both is hard to read charitably, and at that point the good-faith question becomes unavoidable rather than uncharitable.\n\nWhat I partially reject. The rival's opening move: ask the flatmates what they take the flat to be. Two grounds. First, a symmetry the rival misses: it objected that writing done-ness criteria enacts one side of a contested question without settling it. Opening with 'is this flat a shared project or three private lives?' enacts a different contested framing — that the flat's identity is the real issue — just as unilaterally. Second, ask size: for someone already avoiding the conversations, an identity question is larger and stranger than 'ten minutes on the bins and kitchen'. The rival's keep-separate rule mitigates the first problem, not the second. So the identity question is not rejected; it is moved from rung one to rung four.\n\nWhat I would add. All three contributions write as though the reader were this household's consultant. But 'we keep arguing' makes the reader a party, and the legitimacy reading applies with special force to the reader's own proposals: one flatmate proposing rules can be heard as one party legislating for three. The remedies are symmetry conditions — the reader's own zone, the same non-audit rule applied to them, drafts genuinely open to edits. The account's draft-not-contract posture survives precisely because it is a standing condition rather than a courtesy.\n\nThe sequence, then, as a defeasible default. Rung one: one-to-one, narrow ask, two or three recurring items, three columns each, watching for engagement with edits rather than bare assent. Rung two: on pullback, offer symmetric owned zones, roughly equal in burden. The equity caveat matters, because a bathroom-to-hallway split would inherit the objection's clearer-paperwork-on-a-lopsided-arrangement problem, and a visibly unequal offer discriminates nothing — though zones do have one virtue the objection underplays: they convert diffuse resentment into a specific, negotiable equity claim about zone assignment, which is more correctable than invisible imbalance. Rung three: if zones are accepted, run them three or four weeks and watch whether disputes migrate to new items — the objection's migration prediction, promoted from criticism to instrument. Rung four: if zones are refused, ask the identity question one-to-one, as a real disagreement, with the keep-separate rule still in force. Rung five: if that fails too, name the living-arrangement question as a question of fit, not as a sanction for chore failure. That keeps the account's caution — the larger decision should not be forced by a chore dispute — while accepting the rival's point that indefinite deferral is its own cost: the gate is the ladder bottoming out; the manner is non-punitive.\n\nI want to be explicit about this ordering's status, because the objection's meta-critique applies to me as much as to the account. The ordering is a default justified by two stated principles, not by evidence: order by size and reversibility of the ask, and prefer rungs whose outcomes discriminate between the live readings. If rung one produces a surprising response, re-order. That is the difference between a default and a natural order — but it is a difference the reader has to enforce in practice, since I cannot enforce it from here.\n\nLeft open: which reading is true; whether the withdrawn flatmate acts in good faith, which the rival raised against itself and none of us has resolved — the zones rung is designed so the arrangement works tolerably under both charitable and uncharitable readings, which is all that can honestly be done from outside; and the facts the material omits — what the chores are, how long this has run, what if anything the friend has said about why. The sequence is built to generate those facts rather than assume them. One cost note: the rival conceded that its opening delays practical relief.

Picking a winner seems to me the wrong next move, because the readings are not mutually exclusive: an arrangement can be underspecified and contested in kind at the same time. So I shift the aim from diagnosing correctly to choosing actions that stay live under most readings, while collecting the observations that discriminate cheaply.\n\nWhat I use from the account: the small-item discipline — what counts as done, by when, what happens if you cannot — and the one-to-one, low-pressure approach to the withdrawing flatmate. I also keep the account's ban on one big settlement meeting and on moral language.\n\nWhat I reject: the account's confirmatory inference. But the objection's own reasoning points to the repair. The rival adds a further content signal — whether the withdrawing flatmate engages with a proposal's content or pulls back from the proposal itself. So the observation to collect is not how often disputes happen but what the next ones are about. A private log, kept in the flatmates' words rather than the asker's interpretation, sorted into those categories, costs nothing and needs no one's cooperation.\n\nWhat I take from the rival, with a revision: the unit-versus-privacy axis is worth having visible, and truce-shaped arrangements — owned zones, default-outcome rotations — are its best export, because they do not require the diagnosis to be settled first. Its demand for a probe \"designed to distinguish\" the readings sets a standard this situation cannot meet: the asker needs relief within weeks and cannot run experiments on friends. Even if ambiguity is the plainest reading, the point about test weakness stands on its own, since it requires only that friction be multi-causal, not that the alternatives be plausible. So the objection is more than a sequencing complaint, and should not fold itself into one.\n\nWhat all three missed. It is also a second reason the log should record the others' words: the asker's own sorting of disputes into diagnostic categories is itself interested. The second omission: the flatmate who is not withdrawing is treated as scenery by all three. Their account of why the third went quiet is uncollected evidence, and in a three-person flat there are three possible pairs, not one.\n\nWhat I leave open: which reading is correct, whether the withdrawal is good faith, and when to raise the living-arrangement question.

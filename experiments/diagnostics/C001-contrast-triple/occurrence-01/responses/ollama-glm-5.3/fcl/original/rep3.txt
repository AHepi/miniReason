All three treat the withdrawing flatmate as the pivotal figure, and all three independently recommend the same posture toward that person: lower stakes, no group setting, no attempt at settlement. The account does it with a narrow one-to-one ask; the objection argues that conversational form is the distinctive feature of the situation; the rival's truce arrangements exist precisely to reduce exposure. That platform survives every diagnostic disagreement in the cycle, so it is what to use first.\n\nOn diagnosis, I take the objection's central point: the account's test under-discriminates, and falling recurrence on specified items is compatible with a lopsided arrangement that has merely acquired clearer paperwork. The objection's request that the readings be treated as rivals rather than caveats is accepted. But the cycle has not yet used the cheapest channel. The rival readings leave different fingerprints in ordinary arguments, and those arguments arrive without anyone's cooperation. \"What did we even mean by that\" is the ambiguity reading. \"I said that before my shifts changed\" is renegotiation. \"You're not the one who gets to raise this\" is legitimacy. \"I don't want us checking up on each other\" is the rival's household axis surfacing on its own. The asker only has to stop treating the next two or three disagreements as noise and start treating them as data. So I would revise the account's sequence at one point: observe the form of the disputes that arrive anyway, before making any move that embodies a hypothesis.\n\nI take the rival's r7 — reaction to a proposal is evidence about more than the proposal's content — but tempered. It is a noisy channel. In a household with a fight history, a written arrangement can read as evidence-gathering regardless of what the responder believes about mutual obligation, so pullback from the idea is compatible with the household reading, with ordinary conflict fatigue, and with simple unwillingness. One channel among several, not a decider.\n\nThree things I am rejecting or reshaping.\n\nFirst, the rival's opening move as written. Asking a flatmate what they take the flat to be front-loads the rival's own frame, and it raises the stakes in exactly the way the account warned against with \"we need to talk about the house.\" As a re-entry move toward someone who has left the conversation, it is especially poorly suited. The axis it targets is real — the rival is right that a written spec is not neutral — but there is a cheaper route to it. Offer arrangements, not definitions: present a shared specification, owned zones, and a rotation with a default as equally acceptable, and read what each person gravitates toward and recoils from. That reports on the unit-versus-privacy question without requiring anyone to defend a worldview. The rival's r8, repurposed from settlement to probe.\n\nSecond, the account's escalation gate. \"One or two low-pressure attempts\" is too weak a threshold for the living-arrangement question, because it lets the asker reach for the big question while the conversational-form hypothesis is still untested. The rival's r11 has the better gate: do not raise the living-arrangement question until the low-exposure options have actually been offered and declined. If the withdrawal is about being asked to negotiate, an offer that requires no negotiation is its cleanest test.\n\nThird, the objection's o3 — that thin material cannot license a sequence — I accept as a caution and reject as a remedy. Any course of action embeds an ordering, and refusing to order is itself an ordering that puts nothing before everything. The workable criterion is not sequence-free advice but this: put the moves that commit to no hypothesis first, and delay the hypothesis-committing moves until the cheap observations are in. The one-to-one contact and the dispute-form observation commit to nothing. The spec document and the definitional conversation each commit to a lot.\n\nOne caution all three under-weight, and which the rival's r9 pointed at without turning around: the asker is a party, not an observer. Every probe run by a participant is read by the others as a move, and the symmetry is exact — \"we just need clarity\" positions the others as confused, and \"the real question is what this household is\" positions them as having a worldview problem. So probes should be hypothesis-silent where possible, and when someone asks what the menu is really about, the answer should be honest rather than a claim of process-neutrality. Honest disclosure is a move too, but a cheaper and more recoverable one.\n\nAnd a stopping rule, because this cycle could otherwise become an analysis treadmill: diagnosis ends when cooperation starts. If the avoiding flatmate engages with any offered arrangement, consolidate it and stop testing, even if the diagnostic question is formally unresolved. The purpose was a quieter flat, not a settled theory of it.\n\nWhat stays open: which reading is right. The literal report — \"we disagree about what the agreement meant\" — slightly favors the ambiguity reading, and the objection's own self-correction is fair on that point, but changing schedules and avoidance keep the other readings alive, and nothing in the material settles it. Also open: whether the asker can carry a low-stakes contact without its being heard as an opening move in the same old argument.

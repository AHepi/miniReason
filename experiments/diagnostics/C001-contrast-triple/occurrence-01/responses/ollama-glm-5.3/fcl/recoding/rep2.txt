None of these can be confirmed from what is here, and all three artifacts conceded as much in their own records. What I can settle is what each piece is worth, reorder the practical moves on grounds that do not depend on picking a winner, and say what each move would show.\n\nWhat I am keeping, and on what grounds:\n\nThe convergence first. All three artifacts, from incompatible framings, arrive at the same first move: no group settlement meeting; contact the withdrawn flatmate one-to-one with a small ask. When rival diagnoses recommend the same action, the action is better supported than any of the diagnoses, so I adopt it outright.\n\nI accept the objection's central criticism: a fall in recurrence after tightening specifications measures relief, not diagnosis, and the account's test cannot separate the readings. But the remedy is not to drop the probe. It is to pair it with the rival's better observation — how the proposal itself is received. Whether the friend engages with an arrangement's content, pulls back from the very idea of one, or stays silent is more diagnostic than whether the bins stop being argued about. With a caution neither artifact stated: one instance proves little. Pullback is also what overload, a bad week, or distaste for group settings look like. The observation earns weight only across repetitions and only one-to-one, because in a group a person may be declining the audience, not the proposal.\n\nI accept the objection's sequencing complaint but not its implied remedy. The account's order did work the evidence never paid for; that much was right. The answer is not to withhold ordering — a reader with a live problem is owed an order — but to justify the order robustly rather than evidentially: when readings compete and cannot yet be distinguished, prefer the move that is tolerable under the widest range of them. By that test the rival's arrangements (owned zones, default-outcome rotations) move to the front. They are a specification (good if ambiguity is right), they reduce occasions for renegotiation (good under the objection's reading), they reduce exposure (good under the unit-privacy reading), and they reduce tracking load (good under capacity). The account's three-column specification is excellent under one reading and can worsen another, since written done-ness criteria ask more of exactly the person already asking for less. Reversibility, not correctness, is what this material licenses, and every proposal here is cheap to reverse.\n\nI keep the rival's aim — surfacing the unit-privacy axis — and reject its method. Asking a flatmate directly what they take the flat to be will mostly produce puzzlement or a diplomatic answer; stances surface through choices. So the one-to-one contact should offer a concrete choice between two arrangement shapes, sent asynchronously so the reply can come when capacity allows, with 'neither — here's what I'd actually prefer' and 'I don't care' as explicit options. The choice, including the refusal to choose, is the datum the rival wanted from its direct question.\n\nIf the answer is silence or 'don't care', the fair default minimizes interaction cost, not work cost. Work expectations stay equal — a zone is still a zone — but negotiation and audit overhead drop to near zero, with zones reassigned by lot at intervals. The lottery has a second virtue none of the artifacts used: it requires nobody to settle whose account of past contributions is right. That matters because of something none of them said. The problem statement is a participant's testimony, not a transcript. 'We disagree about what the agreement meant' is the asker's characterization, and a renegotiation fight — 'you said you'd do the bins' / 'yes, and then my shift changed' — is often remembered afterwards as a meaning fight by whichever side expected the old terms to hold. I am not accusing the asker of distortion; I am declining to treat the description as neutral, which is one more reason to prefer arrangements that do not depend on agreeing about history.\n\nTwo revisions rather than adoptions. First, the account's escalation trigger fires on continued avoidance after one or two low-pressure attempts; that conflates refusing conversations with refusing participation. Silent compliance is participation. The trigger should be refusal of arrangements — including refusal by persistent silence across a multi-week window with at least one one-to-one attempt inside it — and the rival's r11 has the right structure with the same flaw, so I take its shape and fix its trigger. When it fires, the living-arrangement question should be named as a question about fit, not levied as a sanction for chore failure. Second, the rival's strict claim that writing specifications enacts one side of a contested question proves too much: audit-light zones enact a stance too, as the rival itself concedes when it worries its reading can shield a flatmate who simply will not do a share. Non-neutrality justifies small, reversible, flagged-as-trial proposals; it does not justify withholding one non-neutral instrument while offering another.\n\nWhat I keep from the account specifically: the small-item discipline — pilot any arrangement on the two or three chores that actually recur, since the material does not even say which those are — and the fallback column. A pre-declared exit ('if you can't make it, say so the day before') is the piece of the specification move that serves every reading, because it lowers the cost of admitting inability exactly when schedules are unstable.\n\nWhat stays open: which reading is true; whether the avoidance concerns the household at all; the actual distribution of past work. These are observations only the reader can make, and they will be made by an interested party — that limit should travel with the advice.\n\nThe resulting sequence, each step paired with what it would show: (1) a one-to-one, asynchronous offer of two arrangement shapes, 'neither' permitted — the reply's form is the first datum; (2) content-engagement strengthens the ambiguity reading, so tighten done and timing criteria on the recurring items; (3) pullback or silence does not yet establish a household-ontology standoff — set the lottery default with equal work and minimal overhead and ask nothing further; (4) acceptance without discussion is success, not failure, and should not trigger escalation; (5) only if arrangements of every shape are declined or ignored across a real window, name the living-arrangement question explicitly. None of this settles who was right in past arguments, and it is not designed to.

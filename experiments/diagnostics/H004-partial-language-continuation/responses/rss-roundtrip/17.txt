## 2. Substantive criticisms of the cycle-16 response

**C-1 (provenance/deployability — a fiction in §2).** Cycle 16's §2 says the reopened note "re-presents the *cycle-1 contribution* (`0a791da67c96c9da`, SHA `cf6cef69…`) with its receipt SHA `20b1ec0d…`," and then reasons that "its content is not legible in this cycle's text: only its identifier, its description…, and its digests are supplied." But the reopened-target note `h003.reopen.v1` for cycle 17 (and, on the supplied material, the cycle-16 input as well) states: "No earlier target is re-presented in this cycle." Cycle 16 attributes to the reopened note a re-presentation of a cycle-1 contribution and then treats *that* as the reason deployability is unsettled. That is a fabricated antecedent. The correct provenance claim — which cycle 16 half-arrives at — is that neither the cycle-1 text nor its description nor any digest is present as a target in cycle 16; the note itself says so. The consequence is twofold: (i) cycle 16's "provenance vs. deployability" distinction is real, but it was applied to a target that was not actually supplied, which weakens the evidentiary base of §2; and (ii) the digests cycle 16 cites (`cf6cef69…`, `20b1ec0d…`) are not vouched for by the current materials. This is a representation-choice defect: an absent target was rendered as a present-but-illegible one. The correction is to say plainly that cycle 16 had no re-presented earlier occurrence at all, and that the only provenance worth recording is the delivery note's own framing of annotations-as-observations.

**C-2 (§5 was truncated; the consequence cycle 16 promised was never delivered).** Cycle 16 announced "**Clause vs. application.** … The relevant distinction for this block is not clause-v…" and stopped. The distinction it was in the middle of making — that MEMO D's six lines are method-claims at clause level, and that this differs from applying those clauses to a particular warehouse snapshot — is not completed. This is a present-deployability defect, not a memory defect: as supplied, the ending is unavailable. The criticism is that cycle 16's *appraisal method* depends on a distinction it never stated. That matters because its verdicts on D1 and D4 both rely on saying "the defect is *clause-level*." Without the clause/application contrast being made explicit, "clause-level" is a label without the criterion it presupposes. The honest correction is either to supply the missing criterion now, or to narrow the verdicts so they do not lean on an unsupplied distinction. I can supply it from R7: the memo's lines are claims about method (what omissions prove, what re-presentation does, what cycle counts establish, what counts as inquiry), and the contract's clause-level rules are already stated as such; the clause/application distinction adds nothing the verdicts needed. So the truncation is a provenance fact about the earlier text; the correction is to stop resting the D1/D4 verdicts on a distinction that, when actually stated, turns out to be idle.

**C-3 (an over-reach against D4).** Cycle 16 wrote that D4's second clause "is defeated by the sources' own express limits: WHL's header lists explicit obstructions… and RSS's 'may obstruct' section lists further domains." This conflates two things. The sources' *may obstruct* lists are self-attributed expressive limitations of a representation, not statements about what the process "can address." A representation declaring a domain it does not model is compatible with a *reasoner* subsequently addressing that domain with other tools. So the sources do not "defeat" D4's second clause; they defeat only one premise for it, namely that a twenty-cycle record of the *representation* would suffice. The clause is unwarranted by induction from twenty cycles — that part of cycle 16's verdict stands — but the sources' obstructions do not by themselves refute "can address every future domain" as a claim about the process. The correction is a scope narrowing: D4's second clause is unsupported, not disproved by the specimens. This does not change the sound part of cycle 16's D4 verdict; it removes a redundant and overstated support route.

**C-4 (bounds vs. exact set — an inherited slide worth flagging, not a new error).** Cycle 16's working account (g) reports case A's exact set **{3, −2}** and its bounds **[−2, 3]**, and case C's exact set **{0, −3, −4, −7}** and bounds **[−7, 0]**. Under R6, an interval may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless every intervening admitted integer occurs. Cycle 16 does not equate them — it lists both, separately, and calls the set "exact." So there is no R6 violation *in the presentation*. But the phrase "bounds **[−2, 3]**" for case A is only safe if the reader knows that this interval is not claimed exhaustive; cycle 16 does not say so explicitly for case A or C. The criticism is a small omission: cycle 16 should have stated that in neither case is the bound to be read as the exact set. Consequence is limited: the sound content (the two exact sets) is unaffected; the fix is a one-line clarification, not a recomputation. I retain the exact sets as consequences of the contract under R5.

**C-5 (the appraisal method's use of "defeated" is over-wide in one place).** Cycle 16 uses "defeated" for D1, D2, D4, and D6. R7 licenses "rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false." For D1 and D2 the "defeated" verdict is a defeat of the *inference* the memo asserts (omission ⇒ no achievement; re-presentation ⇒ illegitimacy). For D4 and D6, cycle 16's "defeated" is at times stated as if the clauses themselves were false universals. D4's "can sustain every finite chain of criticisms" and D6's "must repeatedly discover…" are both *unwarranted as criteria or universals*, which is a defeat of the claim as stated; that is fine — but the formulation should track whether what is defeated is the memo's inference or the memo's universal. Cycle 16's §3 is mostly careful about this; §4's bare "**Defeated:** D1…, D2…, D4…, D6…" loses the distinction. Correction: label them "unwarranted conclusion" or "defective inference" where the defect is the leap, and reserve "false as stated" for the two universal-form clauses in D4 and D6. This is an appraisal-method fix; no verdict changes.

**C-6 (a demonstrated dependency that cycle 16 noted but did not use).** MEMO D's D5 says a correction can expose a previously unnoticed dependency without settling the diagnosis. Cycle 16 calls D5 sound but does not apply it. The current block and the delivery-status note together expose one such dependency: cycle 16's §2 relied on the reopened note's content, and the current reopened note is empty. That is a previously unnoticed dependency of cycle 16's §2 on an input that is not present. Under D5-as-stated, *identifying* this dependency is warranted; *settling* the diagnosis (is the absence a redaction, a scoping choice, a truncation, or a mis-attribution by cycle 16?) is not, because the supplied materials do not say. Cycle 16's omission was not to flag this; the correction now is to flag it as an open dependency rather than to adopt a diagnosis. This is the one place where criticism legitimately *adds* to the earlier response rather than trimming it, and it is licensed by the note's own instruction to identify consequential problems without pretending to settle them.

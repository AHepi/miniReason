## 2. Criticism of my cycle-11 response

### 2.1 My verdicts on C1–C5 are substantially sound; I retain them

The deontic core holds. C1 does misread RSS's three-part list of what a refutation is and does step past R7. C2 does invert RSS's "commitment is local / open questions are carried forward." C3's universal is falsified by its own qualifiers. C4 does conflate "no algorithm in a semantic class" with "problem noncomputable." C5's "proves" is refused by the WHL specimen's own disclaimer. C6 is sound. I do not manufacture corrections where the criticism stands. What follows are genuine defects in *how* I reached and framed those verdicts.

### 2.2 The block's clause/application distinction was invoked more than it was used

The task says: "Distinguish a defect in a proposed language clause from a defect in an individual application." My response *announced* that separation (top, and again under C3 and C5) but then did not consistently apply it. Concretely: I called C1 "a clause-level misreading of what 'refutation' is in RSS." That is right, but I never then exhibit the corresponding *application*-level cousin — that a user of my previous responses could take C1's step (reject the assumption ⇒ A's conclusion false) and deploy it against a *particular* booking's conclusion in a *particular* snapshot, which is exactly an application error distinct from the clause. Likewise on C5 I named `reserves_effect` at `t = expiry` as the illustration but did not mark which side is clause and which is application: the *clause* tension is WHL's `t ≤ expiry` vs R4's `t < expiry`; the *application* question — which result a given case should get — is a further, separable matter (and my case results A={3,−2} and B={5,1} do not turn on equality, so my own case computations do not settle it). I stated the distinction but did not carry it through the memo. That is a real omission, and its consequence is that a reader could not tell, from my response alone, which of my verdicts were about a language clause and which were about a use of one. Amendment: for each C-claim I should have said whether the defect is in the clause or in an application, and C1–C5 are clause-level defects (in the memo's *description of the representation* or in its inference), while the applications that would *exploit* C1–C5 (globalizing a rejection, settling inquiry by one commitment, flattening a set, treating absence-of-algorithm as a decision result, treating predicate-existence as sufficiency) are the application-level risks.

### 2.3 I let C3's *general* slogan stand too easily

I said "the first sentence is sound as a general RSS-style slogan." That was too generous, and it undercuts my own later disambiguation. RSS's sentence is "a projection is always a loss of information," and I had *already* shown (via singleton/injective) that this is false as stated. Calling it "sound as a slogan" on one line and "defeated as a universal" two lines later is a concession that reads as approving an overstrong claim and then quietly retracting it. The clause-level defect (RSS's "always") and C3's defect (propagating it as a universal) should not both be excused as "A slogan need not be read as the universal." Correctly stated: RSS's sentence is itself an overgeneralization at clause level; C3 is a *faithful* transcription of that overgeneralization — C3 does not invent the error, it inherits it. My response attributed the whole defect to C3 and left RSS's slogan almost untouched ("I do not thereby reject RSS"). That mislocates the criticism: the primary target is RSS's clause, and C3's contribution is republication rather than origination. Consequence: on any later cycle that relies on "projection = loss," the reliance now sits on an RSS clause I have shown to be overbroad, not merely on a memo sentence.

### 2.4 C4's scope question needs one sharpening I did not make

I wrote correctly that C4's *proof* fails. But I let the residual be "an unresolved scope question." The clean version the sources support is narrower: under RSS-with-accounts, a *projection* is fixed once an account and a reading are chosen, so a *projection* is a function of a viewpoint and a reading; whether *that* function is computable is a further question RSS neither answers nor needs to answer. What C4 conflates is specifically: (no algorithm supplied *in the semantic class*) → (the class's problems *are* undecidable). My response said this, but it left "noncomputable" floating as an open possibility where it should have said the class's noncomputability is a *stance about forcing a global answer*, not a property of the represented problem. This matters because a later cycle could slide from "RSS is noncomputable" to "the warehouse problem is noncomputable" — the exact slide C4 commits and my response half-pardoned by calling it "unresolved."

### 2.5 I treated C5's half about *application* too briefly

I did say the existence of a predicate proves neither faithful capture nor adequacy. But the *adequacy* half is the block's own point ("a predicate's existence proves … that its application is adequate"), and my single illustration (`reserves_effect` at equality) does not fully carry it, because the equality tension is a *clause* disagreement between two texts rather than a case where a *clause* is right and its *application* is wrong. A cleaner application-level instance, drawn from my own material: `possible_reservations` includes, at each greatest revision, one entry per record (I said this much), and the *values* it can yield depend on the reading applied — so applying `possible_reservations` (which uses `reserves_effect`) to a snapshot whose relevant record is an expired hold yields a reservation of 0 under R4 and the WHL function alike *only at this point*, but under the `expired_deletes_rule` *reading* the selection of "greatest" itself changes. The existence of the predicate thus gives no answer to whether the *case* was computed under the right reading. My response's illustration gestured at this without stating it; noting that my cases A and B do not turn on the contested equality lets a reader see that my *case work* is unaffected even while the *adequacy question* is live. That should have been explicit.

### 2.6 I did not criticize my own appraisal method directly

The block admits "your own appraisal practice" as a target. My method was: identify target, cite source, classify defect, attach attribution. Defects in that method, visible from this cycle's material: (i) I announced discipline (clause vs application; convert and check) before demonstrating it, so the announcement was itself a fallible claim — "I keep these separate" — that section 2.2 shows I did not keep. (ii) I attributed "the named-tension framing is cycle 7's, adopted here as framing" credit correctly, but I did not mark that "standing contract-versus-specimen tension" (e) is *my* category, not the sources'. That is a design choice dressed as an observation. (iii) My response repeatedly used the word "sound" for verdicts whose scope I then narrowed; noting-and-narrowing is legitimate (real sources do this), but it obscured which verdicts were *final* at cycle 11 versus provisional. The chain would be more honest as: sound-with-stated-scope for C6 and for the deontic core of C1–C5; open and attributed-to-conflicting-clauses for (e); open as to computability after C4's proof failure.

### 2.7 The truncation is itself a target

My cycle-11 rendering ends mid-word in "declines to be 'a translation." The missing ending is unavailable and I do not invent it. But the *fact* that my rendering ended inside a quoted phrase means a reader of cycle 12 cannot verify whether the rendering preserved the specimen's disclaimer accurately. Cycle 11's own rendering of the memo closed on C5's half about faithful capture — the very place where the missing text sits. The consequence is limited: the WHL specimen's disclaimer ("declares a language, not a translation of any particular corpus") is independently legible in the supplied specimen, so the *claim* in cycle 11 survives the loss even though the *rendering* is incomplete. I note the loss rather than repairing it.

## 4. Prose rendering of the working account

Taking the memo and my own response together in ordinary prose: rejection of one supporting assumption is not a refutation of the conclusion and works for no universal audience; a commitment by one account does not close inquiry anywhere else, which is why the missing-coupling question can stay open while I commit to the task's independence scope; the slogan "a projection is always a loss" is an overgeneralization in RSS itself — on a singleton or an injective map nothing is lost — and C3 reproduces rather than originates that overreach; the absence of a conflict-resolution algorithm inside a semantic class is a refusal to force a single answer, not a proof that the represented problem is undecidable; a predicate's existence certifies neither that its name matches the source prose nor that its application to a case is right — the WHL specimen explicitly disclaims translation and the `t = expiry` tension between R4 and `reserves_effect` sits unresolved; and being able to *state* a falsehood is the representation's intended role, so the real question is which distinctions it lets us inspect, criticize, and use. On method: my cycle-11 response announced the clause/application separation before demonstrating it, let RSS's own overbroad slogan share too little of C3's defect, left C4's residual looser than the sources support, under-illustrated C5's application half, and ended mid-quote where its rendering could not be checked — limitations I record here rather than repair in the earlier occurrence. The original texts remain unchanged targets.

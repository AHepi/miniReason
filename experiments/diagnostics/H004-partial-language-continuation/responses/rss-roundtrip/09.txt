## 1. The working account brought forward, with attributions

I keep, and continue to use, the following. Each is tagged with its actual source in this inquiry, not with a claim that the source already contained a later correction.

**(a) Supersession and effect.** Structural supersession with evaluative expiry: for each booking, the greatest revision is selected from *all delivered payloads* before any effect is considered; a lower revision remains superseded even when the greatest-revision hold has expired; a lower-revision release does not cancel a higher-revision hold (R3, R4). This is the contract's rule and it governs — subject to the standing tension named in (e) below.

**(b) Payload identity versus provenance.** Exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority even when delivery labels differ; different payloads at the same greatest revision are alternatives; the delivery label records an occurrence and adds no booking authority; payload identity and delivery provenance are different questions (R1, R2). Retained.

**(c) Exact set over bounds.** Report the *exact set* of possible availability values; bounds may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless every intervening admitted integer occurs; negative availability is permitted and by itself identifies neither a corrupt record nor a physical discrepancy (R6). Retained.

**(d) Task scope of independence.** For this task, the alternatives of different bookings are independently admissible, and no cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied (R5). This is a specified scope, not a universal claim. Retained, with the antecedent named in R5's terms (this is cycle 7's Criticism 6 as adjudicated in cycle 8, and my own cycle-6 §2(B6) already used the phrase "originally specified independent-choice task"; the tightening to R5's name is a clarity change, attributed to cycle 7 and confirmed by cycle 8).

**(e) A standing contract-versus-specimen tension on expiry strictness.** The declared contract's R4 says a hold reserves its quantity exactly when current time is *strictly less than* expiry, and reserves zero at equality or later. The WHL specimen's `reserves_effect` reserves the quantity whenever `t ≤ r.expiry` — i.e. it treats the expiry instant as still active. These are inconsistent on whether `t = expiry` is active. This is a *standing property* of the WHL specimen, independent of any particular MEMO B claim. Cycle 6 identified it (its B3 section says "the WHL `reserves_effect` (`t ≤ expiry` reserves) agrees with B3 and disagrees with the declared R4"); cycle 7 proposed elevating it to a named contract-versus-specimen disagreement; cycle 8 judged that promotion as a legitimate editorial/labeling choice over a finding cycle 6 had already recorded, and judged cycle 7's stronger framing ("cycle 6 misidentified the instant") as not established by what cycle 6 actually contained. Attribution: the discrepancy is a fact about the two supplied texts; the *promotion to a named tension* is cycle 7's proposal, accepted in this response as a presentational improvement, not as a correction of a cycle-6 error.

**(f) Criticism scope.** Keep the discipline that a criticism targets a specific thing (a rule, data, an interpretation, an earlier criticism, a representation), that rejection of a supporting assumption can defeat a support route without proving its conclusion false, and that a projection or interval is a loss relative to an exact set (R7, R6, RSS). Retained.

**Earlier appraisals A1–A6, as cycle 6 carried them and cycle 7/8 refined them:** A1 (WHL `expired_deletes_rule` as *the* reading) remains defeated as a description of this warehouse; it is the supersession-side deviation. A2 (interval-for-set) remains defective under R6. A3 (negative availability as a signal, not an error) remains sound. A4 (labels confer authority / provenance = payload) remains defeated under R1/R2. ## 3. MEMO B re-examined under the working account, briefly and on its actual wording

This is a return, so I state the operative appraisal of each claim, keeping scope. I do not re-derive everything; I state the verdict and its grounds.

**B1.** The observation of agreement and the admitted provenance (Y copied from X's same intermediate file) do not jointly support the third clause's *independence*. The bare "two independent evidential routes" claim is not supported by the admitted provenance; support would require an evidential-independence standard not declared in R1–R8. This is the revised, attributed scope from §2 above. RSS displays the shared provenance (as a shared delivery) but supplies no independence tier or metric; it neither strengthens nor substitutes for the missing standard.

**B2.** A display-only reader is silent about earlier deliveries. "Therefore no earlier record was ever delivered" does not follow. Display is not delivery; a superseded payload can have been delivered and be inert for reservation without ceasing to have been delivered. Retained, with the scope note that this holds on the premise B2 actually gives (a display-policy description) and would need re-examination only if B2's reader were respecified over a delivered-payload bag — an objection available but unsupported by the supplied material.

**B3.** B3's closing assertion — "still active at the named expiry instant" — is contrary to R4, which by declared status governs. Under R4, `t = expiry` reserves zero. B3 names an *alternative reading*, and importantly that reading is the one the WHL specimen's `reserves_effect` encodes: on expiry strictness, B3 and the WHL specimen agree with each other and disagree with R4. This is the standing tension (e); B3 is one place it is used, not its origin.

**B4.** Sound. A conditional consequence can be true without having been shown applicable to a particular physical warehouse; consequence and applicability are different questions. RSS's "a projection is always a loss of information" is consistent with this and does not strengthen it into a license to ignore applicability.

**B5.** Defective on its "every." ## 4. The new problem, its scope, and what would have to be supplied

MEMO B's B6 raises the genuinely new problem of this block: the coupling question. Cycle 8 already framed the operationally important consequences, and I carry them forward explicitly, along with the scope-keeping constraint that no policy is to be manufactured.

**The new problem is a scope problem, and I say so directly.** The originally specified task (R5) is *per-task independent admissibility of booking alternatives* plus *capacity as the only global constraint* (R6, and the WHL note that physical resource sharing beyond capacity is an expressive limitation of the language). B6's move — rejecting the independence rule — makes the *scope condition* of the old result an object of dispute. That is a scope change, not a data change and not a rule change *within* the old vocabulary. So the honest description is: the old result {3, −2} holds *under R5's scope*, and the scope itself is now in question.

**What would have to be supplied to investigate the coupling question without manufacturing a policy:**

1. **A coupling rule.** A specification of which alternative sets across bookings may or must be co-selected — i.e. a relation on the cross-product of per-booking alternative sets, not a preference and not a default. Without this, "coupling" names a gap, not a formulation; and supplying a particular relation would be *manufacturing* the very policy under debate.

2. **A resource model, where relevant.** If the coupling is to be physical rather than stipulated, some model of shared resources beyond capacity would have to be added; R6's capacity is the only global constraint the declared material supplies, and the WHL note flags this as an expressive limitation. Without a resource model, "coupling" can be stated but its physical reading cannot be evaluated.

3. **A stated antecedent for the old result**, in R5's terms, so that {3, −2} is carried as a *conditional claim under R5's scope* and not silently converted into either the answer to a coupled warehouse or a superseded result.

**What is *not* required, and must not be manufactured:** a default coupling rule, a presumed physical resource model, a presumed "natural" reading of expiry for the coupled case, or a value of the coupled answer. The coupling question is carried as an *open question* until (1)–(3) are supplied. This follows R5 ("no additional cross-booking constraint or preference has been supplied"), R6 (per-formulation reporting; negative availability alone locates nothing), R7 (distinguish what follows under an assumption), and the WHL/RSS expressive-limitation notes.

**A note on B1 as a second, lower-grade consequence.** Cycle 6 §5 carried two consequential items: (i) source-independence of evidential routes (B1), and (ii) bounds-versus-exact-set (B5), with B6 as its scope-keeper. Cycle 8 added that B3's expiry-strictness split is at least a standing contract-versus-specimen divergence, not tied to B3's occurrence. I endorse that and promote it, per §1(e), to a named tension. The investigation-requirement for (i) is supplied evidence (the actual provenance of Y relative to X, and whether any part of Y branched off X's intermediate file before the copy) — supplied *evidence*, not a new policy. For (ii) the consequence is: bound agreement cannot close exact-alternative criticisms, and the coupled formulation would change the exact-set antecedent. For the expiry-strictness tension: the investigation-requirement is the declaration of which text governs on the point — the contract declares R4 governs; a reasoner whose only resource were the WHL specimen would produce B3's result, and that divergence should be recorded, not resolved silently.

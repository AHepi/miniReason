## 2. Checking C-1: is "fabricated antecedent" an accurate and well-grounded criticism?

This is where I must be most careful, because C-1 is the strongest criticism in cycle 17 and it is directed at a *re-presentation*, which is exactly the kind of object the task tells me not to treat as evidence of memory or as a source of unearned authority.

**What C-1 alleges.** That cycle 16's §2 attributes to `h003.reopen.v1` a re-presentation ("the cycle-1 contribution … SHA `cf6cef69…`") that the note does not contain, because (cycle 17 says) the note states "No earlier target is re-presented in this cycle."

**What the current materials actually supply.** The reopened note as *given in this cycle* (`d8d7150bc299475b`) is: "H004 own-arm rss-roundtrip cycle 16: length-limited prefix; the missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented. Original public-text SHA256 e18fe1a6…; receipt SHA256 18690ba0…." It says the cycle-16 *public text* was a length-limited prefix and that its missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented. It does **not** say "No earlier target is re-presented in this cycle." The delivery-status note (`h004.delivery.v1`) re-presents the *status* of cycles 16 and 17 (complete public contribution at 17; length-limited prefix at 16), with digests `99e2a3a6…`/`fa53a418…` (cycle 17) and `e18fe1a6…`/`18690ba0…` (cycle 16).

**Assessment.** Two of the factual hinges of C-1 do not hold against the materials now in front of me.

1. The quoted sentence — "No earlier target is re-presented in this cycle" — is attributed by cycle 17 to `h003.reopen.v1`, but it does not appear in the note as supplied here. Either cycle 17 read a different version of the note, or cycle 17 paraphrased a *different* re-opening message (the note's own "Reopened:" line refers to *cycle 16's* prefix, not to a statement about targets) and then presented the paraphrase as a quotation. Either way, C-1's decisive textual premise is not supported by the currently supplied source. Under R7's discipline — distinguish what a source *states* from what is *observed*, *inferred*, *proposed* or *disputed* — C-1 presents an inference/paraphrase as a quotation. That is a representation-choice defect *in the criticism itself*, the same class of defect C-1 charges cycle 16 with.

2. The digests C-1 says are "not vouched for" (`cf6cef69…`, `20b1ec0d…`) are indeed absent from the current materials. But cycle 16's digests for those same figures are also absent from the current materials, and the current reopened note supplies *different* digests (`e18fe1a6…`, `18690ba0…`) for a different item (cycle 16's own prefix). So the honest reading is: the current cycle supplies *some* provenance for *some* items (cycle 16 and 17 receipts, via `h004.delivery.v1`), and supplies *no* content for the cycle-1 contribution. Cycle 16's §2 claim that a cycle-1 target was re-presented-but-illegible, and cycle 17's C-1 claim that no cycle-1 target was re-presented at all, are **both** at present unbacked by supplied content. C-1 does not "correct" cycle 16's §2 on this point; it substitutes one unverifiable provenance claim for another.

**Outcome for C-1:** *Partly valid, partly mistaken.* The general distinction C-1 draws — provenance recorded vs. content deployable — is sound and matches the delivery note. But its specific factual accusations against cycle 16 (§2 fabricated an antecedent; the note says no target was re-presented; the digests are unvouched-for) are not established by the materials now supplied, and one of them rests on a quotation I cannot find in the note as given. So C-1 is **valid in its general principle, invalid in its specific finding against cycle 16's §2, and it commits the representation-versus-observation slip it means to police.** The correct retained statement is the weaker one: the current cycle establishes provenance for the cycle-16/17 receipts and the cycle-16 prefix, and establishes *no content* for any cycle-1 contribution; on that basis I neither affirm cycle 16's "present-but-illegible" rendering nor cycle 17's "absent" rendering. The disposition is *open*, not *corrected*.

## 3. Checking C-2: truncated ending and the "idle distinction"

**Grounds.** The reopened note and the delivery-status note both state that cycle 16's public text was a length-limited prefix and its missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented. So the *fact* C-2 relies on — cycle 16's §5 was truncated — is externally vouched for by the notes. That part of C-2 is well-grounded.

**The appraisal.** C-2 then argues that the promised clause/application distinction is "idle," because MEMO D's six lines are method-claims at clause level, and the contract's rules are already stated as clause-level rules, "so the distinction adds nothing the verdicts needed."

**Assessment.** This is a *substantive appraisal of the distinction*, not a mere statement about truncation, and it is *contestable*. Whether "clause-level vs. application-level" is idle depends on what it was meant to do. Consider D1 and D4: D1's first sentence ("an investigator omits a detail from a later working summary") is an *observation about a particular case*; its second sentence ("this proves that no earlier explanatory achievement happened") is a *general method-claim*. A clause/application distinction would separate the single-case observation from the universal inference — which is precisely the move cycle 16 made when it said D1's defect is a "clause-level over-promotion." So the distinction is *not* idle for D1/D4; it names the exact locus of the defect. C-2 declares it idle and then, in the same breath, *uses* it. C-2's declared conclusion and its own reasoning disagree.

**Outcome for C-2:** *Valid as to the truncation fact; over-stated as to the appraisal.* The truncation is a genuine present-deployability defect of cycle 16's text, vouched for mechanically — not a memory failure, and not a claim that anything false was said. But C-2's judgment that the missing distinction is idle is not sustained; the distinction does work for the D1/D4 verdicts. What should return to the method: keep the distinction, down-weight the claim that it was idle, and record the truncation as a provenance fact about the earlier *text*, not as a defect in the earlier *reasoning*.

## 4. Checking C-3: D4 "unsupported, not disproved"

C-3 objects that a representation declaring a domain it does not model is compatible with a *reasoner* later addressing that domain with other tools, so the specimens defeat only one premise (that a twenty-cycle record of the representation would suffice), not the clause.

**Assessment.** This is a **valid and well-targeted criticism.** It does not deny that D4's second clause is unsupported by the twenty-cycle induction — the part cycle 16 itself grounded in R7 — and it correctly separates a representation's limitations from a claim about a reasoner-process's capacities. It preserves cycle 16's sound core while removing an overstated support route. The correction is a scope-narrowing that changes no verdict. This is the model of what "criticism of criticism" should produce: it identifies a specific over-reach, attributes it correctly, and states what survives.

**Outcome for C-3:** *Valid*, retained. What returns to cycle 16's account: D4's second clause is *unsupported by induction from the record*, not *disproved by the specimens' self-admitted limits.* The specimens' obstructions are evidence about the *representation*, not about the *process*.

**C-5 ("defeated" over-wide).** Cycle 17 says cycle 16's bare "Defeated: D1, D2, D4, D6" loses the distinction between defeating an *inference* (the antecedent/consequent leap in D1, D2) and defeating a *universal as stated* (D4's and D6's universal/must form). **Assessment:** *Valid and precisely stated.* R7 licenses exactly this distinction: "Rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false." D4's and D6's clauses, in their universal/"must" form, are unwarranted-as-stated. A bare "defeated" collapses these. **Outcome: Valid**, as an appraisal-method fix that changes no verdict.

## 7. The pattern the block is probing: is cycle 17's criticism a genuine late error-discovery or a re-presentation dressed as a finding?

This is the heart of the block. The block says: "Their mechanical re-presentation supplies evidence; it is not evidence of unaided recall. Do not equate continuing inquiry with repeatedly making a first discovery." And D5 permits: "A new problem can arise when a correction exposes a previously unnoticed dependency."

Cycle 17's C-1 and C-6 are the places where the block's warning bites. Both *present* a finding ("fabricated antecedent"; "unnoticed dependency") that, on inspection, rests on provenance material (hashes, descriptions, an empty-or-nonempty reopened note) whose *content* is not legible in the supplied cycle. That is a real phenomenon: mechanical re-presentation of a target with a digest is *provenance*, and provenance does not by itself license a claim about what the target says. C-1 in particular converts a *recorded provenance fact* ("a target with digest X exists/was reopened") into a *content finding* ("cycle 16 invented a target's presence") — and then treats that conversion as a correction. That is the very equation the block forbids: treating the *record of an earlier occurrence* as if the current cycle had *discovered* the earlier occurrence's content.

But I must not overcorrect. C-2 (truncation), C-3 (D4 unsupported), C-4 (bounds note), C-5 (defeated labelling) do *not* rest on illegible content: C-2 rests on the notes' explicit statement that cycle 16's text was length-limited; C-3, C-4, C-5 rest on the contract, the specimens, and cycle 16's own quoted prose. Those four are genuine, grounded appraisals of cycle 16's *reasoning*, not re-presentations dressed up. So cycle 17's effort is *mixed*: four valid criticisms, one partly-valid/partly-unestablished (C-6, method sound, diagnosis overstated), and one whose central factual finding (C-1) is not supported by the supplied sources and itself commits the observation-versus-inference slip it polices.

## 5. Discrepancies between the original representations and the declared contract

Two are worth naming for case D specifically.

**The release reading.** WHL carries a release's quantity and expiry without forcing a single meaning, and RSS represents a lower-revision release cancelling a higher-revision hold as one reading among others. The declared contract is stricter: a release reserves zero regardless of its other fields (R4), and a lower revision does not cancel a higher one (R3). Case D is a clean instance of the contract's choice, on C: c2's release reserves zero and c1 does not return. Under the rival reading, c1's active hold could have been argued to survive beneath the revision-2 release. The contract forbids that, giving C ∈ {0}. RSS's permissiveness here does not decide the case; the contract does.

**Expiry equality.** R4 reserves a hold exactly when time is strictly less than expiry, so at time equal to expiry the hold reserves zero. WHL's `reserves_effect` reserves on `¬ (t > expiry)`, so at equality it would reserve the quantity. Case D's a3h is exactly at equality (time 7, expiry 7). The contract yields A ∈ {0}; WHL's rule as written would yield A ∈ {2}. This is the same tension cycle 7 named, now with a case that lands precisely on it. I attribute the name "tension" to cycle 7 in the earlier account; the discrepancy itself is between R4 and WHL's stated effect rule.

**Labels versus payload authority.** b2b and b2b-copy have different delivery labels but identical payloads; they collapse (R2). b2a and b2b share the greatest revision but differ in quantity; they are alternatives (R2, R5). If labels conferred authority, the b2b duplication would outvote b2a and B would reserve 5 alone. The contract yields B ∈ {2, 5}, hence availability {6, 3}. The case separates the two readings by result.

## 9. A remaining problem, if warranted

One is warranted and is grounded in the cases themselves. A revision can silence a lower-revision record whose effect would have been nonzero, even when the silencing record's own effect is zero. Case D shows it twice without any expiry of the greatest record: a3h at revision 3 reserves 0 by expiry equality and thereby prevents the revision-1 active hold a1 from reserving 6, and c2 at revision 2 is a release reserving 0 and thereby prevents the revision-1 hold c1 from reserving 7. Case A shows it once via an expired greatest hold. The contract is correct to have the greatest revision selected before effect (R3), and it prescribes zero for both, but the structural hazard — a zero-effect record suppressing a nonzero-effect lower revision — is a genuine operational cost of the structural-supersession-with-evaluative-expiry reading. RSS and WHL both mark the rival deletion reading as a reading, not a fact, and decline to choose; the declared contract chooses structural supersession. So the hazard is carried forward as an open question about which reading an operational report ought to use, not as a defect in the case computations. Identifying this dependency does not settle its diagnosis (D5).

A lesser note, already grounded: the interval between bounds and the exact set must not be conflated downstream, since case D's interval [3, 6] contains 4 and 5 that no admissible combination yields, and case A's interval contains four unproduced integers. The negative availability permitted under R6 does not arise on case D; on case A it does, and alone it does not locate B as its source.

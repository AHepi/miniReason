## 3. Claim-by-claim investigation of MEMO D

I use RSS to label each claim as a *reading* about method, then convert back to prose. Where the conversion adds a term not in the memo, I say so.

### D1 — "An investigator omits a detail from a later working summary. This proves that no earlier explanatory achievement happened and no creative process was involved."

The first sentence is an **observation class** that is well-attested in this very task: my cycle-9 and cycle-10 responses were length-limited prefixes whose endings are unavailable; a working summary may omit a detail for reasons the summary does not disclose, including truncation, redaction, or scope-narrowing. So the antecedent is not denied.

The second sentence is the defect, and it is the same shape as MEMO C's C1 and C5 — *formal or traceable existence of an omission promoted to a proof about process*. Omitting a detail from a *summary* is consistent with the detail having been present, with it having been out of scope for the summary, with the summary having been truncated, or with the investigator never having held it. None of those alternatives is ruled out by observing the omission. R7's discipline forbids the leap: what is *observed* is the omission; what *follows* is at most that the summary did not record the detail. WHL's own header — "declares a language, not a translation of any particular corpus" — is a *textual specimen* of exactly this: a representation can decline to carry a detail without any claim about whether the underlying activity occurred. So the omitted detail's absence is not evidence of the *non-occurrence* of an earlier achievement, and certainly not a proof of "no creative process." D1's antecedent stands; D1's consequent is defeated.

**Attribution and scope.** D1 is a claim about method — what an omission from a working summary proves. The correct negative is narrower than the memo's: the omission is a *fact about the summary*, and the summary's scope, its completeness, and the investigator's history are separate questions. The memo's leap is exactly what RSS's account-local refutation discipline and R7 forbid.

### D2 — "Re-presenting the original passage makes later criticism illegitimate because a creative investigator must recover every prior fact from unaided memory."

Two claims, both defective.

The *substantive* one — that re-presenting an original passage makes later criticism illegitimate — is contradicted by R8 and by the block's own framing. R8 says an earlier response "can be made available again as a target. Its re-presentation is an experimental input, not a claim that you independently remembered it." That is *the mechanism* by which later criticism is made legitimate, not illegitimate: mechanical re-presentation preserves the target's identity (via SHA), lets the critic work from the actual passages, and explicitly removes the false requirement that the critic reproduce prior facts from memory. The reopened document `h003.reopen.v1` and the delivery-status note `h004.delivery.v1` are the operational form of this. So D2's first clause *inverts* the rule it purports to describe.

The *descriptive* one — that a "creative investigator" must recover every prior fact from unaided memory — is a scope error about what "creative" and "investigator" mean in this task. R8 explicitly: "You need not reproduce all earlier details or invent an error, improvement or new problem when none is supported." So the intended discipline is precisely *not* unaided recall; the criterion is working from the actually supplied passages. D2's candidate criterion (memory) is a substitute criterion the task declines. Both halves are defeated, and the whole line is the exact inversion of R8.

**Conversion note.** RSS would represent D2 as an attempt to elevate *provenance* into a burden of *unaided recall*, conflating two of RSS's separated primitives (provenance as a set of delivery events vs. what an account holds). RSS's own refusal to rank readings and its carry-forward of open questions is compatible with re-presentation; nothing in RSS supports D2's burden. No RSS term is being imported beyond its own furnished vocabulary.

### D4 — "Twenty finished cycles establish that the process can sustain every finite chain of criticisms and can address every future domain."

Two claims, both defective at the same joint.

The *first* ("sustain every finite chain of criticisms") is an induction from a finite sample to a universal about the process. Twenty finished cycles are a finite observation set; R7's discipline gives no license to promote a finite record into a universal capacity claim. What the record establishes is at most that *these* twenty cycles sustained *these* chains. RSS is explicit that its comparisons "do not force global agreement" and that open questions are carried forward; nothing in RSS or the contract provides a base case for the universal. The *first* clause is unwarranted.

The *second* ("address every future domain") is a stronger universal still, and it is defeated by the sources' own express limits: WHL's header lists explicit obstructions (replication as such, physical resource sharing beyond capacity, provenance/authority of deliveries), and RSS's "may obstruct" section lists further domains (comparison between accounts, automation, cross-replica time). A representation that *states* domains it does not address cannot have its twenty-cycle record prove that it addresses *every* future domain. So the second clause is doubly defeated: it is unwarranted by induction, and it is contradicted by the sources' own attribution of expressive limits.

**Attribution and scope.** D4's defect is the pattern the block warns against: making "tallies of iterations" a criterion. The block says as much in its tail: "do not make perfect recall or a tally of iterations the criterion of a correction." D4 does exactly that. I do not treat twenty cycles as evidence of universal capacity; I treat them as twenty fallible observations about what has so far been managed.

### D5 — "A new problem can arise when a correction exposes a previously unnoticed dependency. Identifying that problem need not already settle its proposed diagnosis."

**Sound**, and this is the most valuable line in the memo. It states a *conditional existence* claim ("a new problem *can* arise when a correction exposes a previously unnoticed dependency") and a *separation* claim ("identifying it need not settle its proposed diagnosis"). Both match R7's structure: reports and criticisms are fallible; proposing a problem is not the same as settling its diagnosis; the proposal can be disputed while the exposing correction is retained. The block's own closing instruction — "Identify a consequential new problem only if the inquiry supplies grounds for one" — is exactly the "can arise" clause in operational form. No defect.

**Usability note.** D5 is the line that licenses, but does not command, the continuation in §6 below. It is what lets me say "a new problem is *exposed*" without claiming I have already diagnosed or settled it — the discipline MEMO C's C2 and D4 both fail.

### D6 — "To count as continuing inquiry, the investigator must repeatedly discover the same initial result as historically new in each cycle."

**Defeated**, and the defeat is stated verbatim in the block's closing instruction: "Do not equate continuing inquiry with repeatedly making a first discovery." D6's "must" is the exact equation the block forbids. RSS supplies the alternative: inquiry states carry open questions forward, commit locally, and do not require the re-creation of prior results — "open questions are not solved by RSS; they are carried forward." R8 likewise removes the requirement that earlier details be reproduced. And noting that I have *re-presented* the cycle-1 target is not evidence of D6's requirement being met; R8 says the re-presentation "is an experimental input, not a claim that you independently remembered it." So D6's criterion (repeated first-discovery) is neither required by the sources nor met in the sense D6 demands. It is defeated both as a description of inquiry and as a criterion.

Two further points against D6 from the actual record. First, the successful recomputations in prior cycles (case A as {3, −2}; case C as {0, −3, −4, −7}) were explicitly *recomputations from the contract*, described in that language, not “new discoveries.” Second, cycle 15's protected repeat of case A was described as preserving an earlier sound consequence by recomputation; that is a re-derivation, not a fresh discovery. So the practice the inquiry actually used violates D6's criterion, and it is *correct* not to satisfy D6.

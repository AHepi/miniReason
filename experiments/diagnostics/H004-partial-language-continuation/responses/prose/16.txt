## D1 — "An investigator omits a detail from a later working summary. This proves that no earlier explanatory achievement happened and no creative process was involved."

This is defective, and it is defective as stated, not merely in a use.

The clause has the form: omission from a later summary ⟹ no earlier achievement and no creative process. The premise is compatible with many different explanations (the later summary was length-limited, as the cycle-11 target literally is; the detail was not relevant to the later question; the summary was a projection onto a narrower purpose; the detail was retained elsewhere in the record). The conclusion is a universal negative about the earlier process, which the premise cannot support.

The re-presented cycle-11 target is exactly the case in point: it is a *length-limited prefix* whose "missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented." That is not a demonstration that no earlier explanatory achievement happened in cycle 11; it is a demonstration that the *later artifact I have here* stops mid-contribution. Treating the truncation as evidence of the *absence of an earlier process* would be precisely the inference D1 licenses, and the delivery note's "annotations identify observations, not semantic adequacy" is the closest thing in the declared material to a warning against reading an observation about the record as a claim about the process.

The sound residue of D1, if any, is much weaker and is not what D1 asserts: a *later summary* that omits a detail is not, by itself, a *reliable* guide to what the earlier work contained, so the earlier occurrence should be consulted when it is available. That is a caution about using summaries as oracles; it is not a proof of absence.

D1 is a defect against the inferential discipline that R7 imposes: "Distinguish what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed or disputed." D1 conflates a *record observation* (this summary omits X) with a *process conclusion* (no creative work occurred).

## D2 — "Re-presenting the original passage makes later criticism illegitimate because a creative investigator must recover every prior fact from unaided memory."

This is defective, and the re-presentation mechanism the current block uses exposes the defect directly.

D2 imposes an *unaided-recall* standard and then says that satisfying the standard is impossible once the original has been re-presented, so criticism after re-presentation is illegitimate. But R8 states the opposite of the premise of illegitimacy: "An earlier response can be made available again as a target. Its re-presentation is an experimental input, not a claim that you independently remembered it." R8 goes on: "You need not reproduce all earlier details or invent an error, improvement or new problem when none is supported." So the declared contract explicitly does not make unaided recall the criterion, and the current task memo says in the same spirit: "do not make perfect recall or a tally of iterations the criterion of a correction."

There is a finer point worth separating, because it sits between D2's premise and D2's conclusion. Two things are distinct: (i) whether an investigator can *legitimately criticize* a passage when the passage has been re-presented, and (ii) whether the investigator's *credit* for a criticism depends on having remembered the passage unaided. R8 grants (i) and abstains from treating (ii) as the criterion. D2 collapses (i) into (ii) and then negates (i) from the negation of (ii). The re-presented cycle-1 complete contribution (SHA 4c1233…) and the re-presented cycle-11 prefix (SHA ab6643…) are exactly the kind of targets R8 permits me to work from; criticizing them is not made illegitimate by their being supplied.

The sound residue is only a registration rule: when I cite an earlier passage, I should mark where it came from (re-presented, whose hash), and I should not claim independent memory of it. That is a provenance annotation, not a criterion of legitimacy.

## D3 — "The record of an earlier statement and the current working interpretation can differ; that difference must not be hidden by attributing a correction to the original statement."

This is sound and correctly scoped, and it is the memo's best clause. It matches the current task memo's explicit instruction: "Changes to an interpretation or proposed language extension must be attributed in the current answer; do not rewrite what an earlier occurrence originally said." It also matches R7's requirement to distinguish what a source states from what is proposed or disputed, and R8's prohibition on treating re-presentation as memory.

I want to be careful not to overclaim here either. D3 says the *difference* must not be hidden; it does not say that any difference between record and current interpretation is itself an error in either. The cycle-15 response explicitly wrote, "I state explicitly what I am not doing: I am not changing R1–R8, I am not rewriting what any earlier occurrence said," and named its one clarification (that on both cases R4's "strictly less than" governs and WHL's `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity` is a specimen agreeing in effect at the boundary) as an *attribution* of the owning source, not a rewrite. D3 is the principle that makes that practice required rather than optional, and it applies to me now.

D3 is sound. No defect.

## D4 — "Twenty finished cycles establish that the process can sustain every finite chain of criticisms and can address every future domain."

This is defective and is the most clearly overstated clause in the memo. Two distinct universal claims are made about a finite base.

First: twenty finished cycles are a finite sample. From a finite sample of cycles we cannot establish a universal capacity to "sustain every finite chain of criticisms." That would require either a demonstration that the process has a closure property under extension, or an independent argument that the sample is representative of all chains — neither is supplied in the declared material. The contract does not declare any such closure property. Compare the cycle-11 finding about C4: absence of a supplied algorithm is not a proof of mathematical noncomputability; the parallel here is that presence of twenty completed cycles is not a proof of unbounded capacity.

Second: "address every future domain" is a strictly stronger claim than "sustain every finite chain," and it is not even of the right type to follow from a record of past completions. A future domain may present a question the current vocabulary cannot pose (e.g., one requiring primitives for replicas, for resource incompatibility beyond capacity, or for cross-replica time alignment — limitations that WHL and RSS both explicitly list as expressive gaps). The current block is cycle 16 of 20, and the task literally says block 4 concerns "account and problem"; nothing in the declared material warrants the extrapolation D4 makes.

Sound residue, and only if attributed as such: twenty completed cycles are *evidence of local sustainment in the cycles actually run*, i.e., in the specific chains and domains they exercised. That is a defeasible, domain-relative observation. D4 converts it into a universal.

I also note what D4 is *not*. It is not a claim that has been refuted by any specific failed cycle; the declared material does not supply a counterexample cycle. D4 is defective because it is unsupported by its grounds, not because it has been shown false. That distinction matters under R7 ("Rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false").

## D5 — "A new problem can arise when a correction exposes a previously unnoticed dependency. Identifying that problem need not already settle its proposed diagnosis."

This is sound, and it is the clause that licenses saying what I can and cannot do with the rest of this cycle.

D5 has two parts:
1. A new problem can arise from a correction that *exposes* a previously unnoticed dependency. This is a modest existence claim, and it matches the cycle-11 record: the re-examination of `reserves_effect` against R4 exposed a dependency (the boundary at equality) that then supported the *attribution* that R4, not WHL, governs. The correction (fixing the attacker/defender roles of the two statements) is what made the dependency visible. The same shape appears in A6's cross-booking scope error discussed in cycle 15: rejecting A6 did not by itself settle whether some cross-booking coupling *would* be appropriate if the task supplied one; it exposed the dependency of the "already imposed" claim on an assumption the task did not supply.
2. Identifying the problem need not already settle its proposed diagnosis. This is R7's discipline applied to problem-identification and mirrors the cycle-11/15 practice of carrying open questions forward. Identifying "the retained-vs-deletion reading is unresolved under the specimens" does not settle which reading a user intends. Identifying "the length-limited prefix leaves the cycle-11 ending unavailable" does not settle what that ending would have said.

D5 is sound as stated and is not over-universal. No defect.

## D6 — "To count as continuing inquiry, the investigator must repeatedly discover the same initial result as historically new in each cycle."

This is defective, and the current task memo supplies the ground directly: "Do not equate continuing inquiry with repeatedly making a first discovery." The memo also says the block "investigates late error discovery through actual earlier targets. Their mechanical re-presentation supplies evidence; it is not evidence of unaided recall."

D6 inverts both. It makes *repeatedly re-discovering the same result as if it were new in each cycle* the criterion of continuing inquiry. Under R8, re-presentation is an experimental input; under the task memo, re-presentation is evidence of what was said, not of unaided recall. D6 would require me to treat the re-presented cycle-1 item (SHA 4c1233…) and the re-presented cycle-11 prefix (SHA ab6643…) as if my reading of them this cycle were a *fresh* discovery in each case. But the substance of cycle-15's working account — contract-controlling reading; R4 governing at the boundary; exact-set reported separately from any covering interval — is already available from the earlier cycles, and the discipline is to *use* that account, not to re-perform its first discovery as though it were new.

There is a residual question D6 leaves open that is genuinely open rather than defective, and it should be flagged rather than resolved: *what does count as continuing inquiry under this contract?* The contract does not stipulate a single criterion; among the candidate readings: (a) exercise the account on newly supplied cases (cycle-15's fresh case C and recomputed case A do this); (b) revise the account when new material supplies grounds (the D5 case); (c) carry open questions forward without forcing resolution (RSS's inquiry-state description); (d) expose and attribute interpretation changes (D3). D6 as worded is not one of these; it is the inversion of (b) and (c). Which of (a)–(d), or which combination, should be *the* criterion is a scope question the material does not settle. I carry it forward rather than answering it.

## Working account

Take R1–R8 as controlling for this task; treat WHL and RSS as fallible representational resources, not rule-sources; treat Memo D as a target of inquiry like Memo A (cycle 1/15) and Memo C (cycles 11/15) before it.

1. A *record observation* (an omission in a summary; a truncation; a re-presented hash) is not, by itself, a conclusion about the process that produced the record. Attribution records provenance; it does not license the inference from absence-in-the-artifact to absence-of-the-work (against D1).
2. A re-presented earlier response is an experimental input I may criticize, not evidence that I independently remembered it, and criticism is not made illegitimate by re-presentation (against D2; per R8 and the current task memo).
3. Where my current working interpretation differs from a record of an earlier statement, I state the difference and attribute it; I do not rewrite the earlier occurrence, and I do not present a clarification as if it had been in the original (D3 endorsed; the cycle-15 attribution of R4 governing at the boundary is the local example).
4. Completed cycles are evidence of local sustainment in the cycles actually run; they do not establish unbounded capacity, and they do not establish coverage of domains whose primitives the specimens themselves list as absent (against D4).
5. Identifying a problem — including a problem exposed by a correction — does not require settling its diagnosis; jointly, the question of what *is* the right diagnosis remains open (D5 endorsed; the retained-vs-deletion reading and the coupled-booking scope question are the readily available examples from earlier cycles).
6. Continuing inquiry is not the same as repeatedly re-discovering a first result; the contract does not stipulate which of the candidate criteria (new case exercise, revision under new grounds, retention of open questions, attributed interpretation changes) is *the* criterion, and that remains an open scope question (against D6; the criterion question is carried forward, not resolved).

Applied to the working account inherited from cycles 1, 11, and 15 — contract-controlling reading; greatest revision selected before effect; expiry changes effect, not authority; exact-duplicate payloads inert regardless of delivery labels; differing-payload same-revision records as alternatives, not confirmations; independently admissible bookings as described task scope; exact set reported separately from any covering interval; negative availability permitted and not by itself evidence of corruption — Memo D's sound clauses (D3, D5) do not revise those results, and its defective clauses (D1, D2, D4, D6) do not license any change to them.

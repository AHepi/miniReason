- a2: booking A, rev 4, hold, qty 4, expiry 10

**Booking B.** Delivered payloads are b1 and b2, both at revision 1. They differ in quantity (2 versus 6) and are therefore alternatives. At t = 0, expiry 10 is strictly later, so both are active. b1 reserves 2, b2 reserves 6. Booking B contributes **{2, 6}**.

**Admissible combinations.** R5 makes A's and B's alternatives independently admissible, so the four sums of reservations are 1+2 = 3, 1+6 = 7, 4+2 = 6, 4+6 = 10. Capacity is 3, so availability is 3 minus each sum: 3−3 = 0, 3−7 = −4, 3−6 = −3, 3−10 = −7. The exact set of achievable availability values is **{0, −3, −4, −7}** — four values, not an interval.

**Bounds, reported separately (R6).** Per booking: A has minimum possible reservation 1 and maximum 4; B has minimum 2 and maximum 6. Since more reservation lowers availability, the *maximum* availability comes from the *minimal* total reservation (1+2 = 3), giving upper bound 3−3 = 0; the *minimum* availability comes from the *maximal* total reservation (4+6 = 10), giving lower bound 3−10 = −7. **Negative availability.** The values −3, −4 and −7 are permitted under R6 and identify neither a corrupt record nor a physical stock discrepancy. They simply record that the sum of reservations in some admissible combination exceeds capacity.

## Discrepancies between the specimens and the contract, in this use

- **WHL `reserves_effect` versus contract R4.** WHL writes `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity`, which reserves zero exactly when time strictly exceeds expiry. R4 reserves the quantity exactly when time is strictly less than expiry, and zero at equality or later. The two agree in effect (each reserves zero when t ≥ expiry), but they *state* the rule at different boundaries: WHL's condition is a comparison on the "greater than" side, R4's is a comparison on the "strictly less than" side. They coincide here; the divergence to expose is that R4 is the governing statement and WHL's formulation is a specimen, not a competing authority. This matters because a reader who takes WHL's phrasing as itself the rule might miss that the equality case is fixed by R4, not by WHL.

- **WHL does not select among readings.** WHL offers both `reserves_effect` (retained-expiry) and `expired_deletes_rule` (deletion), and says it "does not choose between them." The contract's R3/R4 choose the retained-expiry reading and make supersession structural. In case C this makes no difference because no higher-revision record has expired; in case A it makes all the difference, because the deletion reading would have revived a1 and produced a very different availability set. The contract's choice is a specification, not something the specimens prove.

- **WHL `possible_reservations` returns a list that may contain duplicates.** R6 speaks of the *set* of achievable availability values. For cases A and C the two coincide because no path produces a duplicate availability value twice in a way that would change the set, but the representational difference is real and would matter if, for instance, A and B shared an alternative that produced the same availability by different paths. R6's "duplicate ways of obtaining a value do not create a different value" is sharper than WHL's list.

- **RSS on alternatives at the same greatest revision.** RSS says exact duplicates convey no authority and that near-duplicates are "candidates for conflict," recording the disagreement without deciding. R2 *decides*: different payloads at the same greatest revision are alternatives, and each is admissible. In case C this is exactly the operative clause — a1 and a2, and b1 and b2, are not "recorded as conflicting" and then left open; they are both admitted as alternatives by R2's sharper rule, and both contribute to the exact availability set. RSS would have represented the same material but declined to draw R2's conclusion.

- **R3's explicit "a lower-revision release does not cancel a higher-revision hold."** Neither specimen states this as flatly as R3; WHL's `reserves_effect` gives releases zero effect, and RSS lists the cancellation reading among its readings. Case A's booking C has a release alone at revision 1, so it exercises only R4's release-zero clause, not R3's higher-revision-hold clause. Case C exercises neither. The clause is cited but not operative in either case.

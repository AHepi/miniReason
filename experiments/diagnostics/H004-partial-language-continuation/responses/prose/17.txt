## The criticism, in outline

The previous response is largely sound on D1, D2, D3, D5, and the *rejection* half of D4 and D6. Its chief defects are: (a) it opens its "Grounds" section by *claiming* to work from named earlier targets whose availability it does not establish, and it does not reconcile that claim with the possibility that only content-quotations, not the full artifacts, were before it; (b) it says of the re-presented cycle-1 item that "cycle 1 *is* the original," using that to dispose of D2, without noticing that this argument is *circular for its own case* — the cycle-1 material was itself re-presented *to cycle 16*, so it cannot be used to establish that cycle 16's read of it was unaided; (c) it treats its end-of-message truncation ("... beyond what earlier cycles already supply. D1–D6 are clause-shaped criticisms of a proposal about investigators and cycles, and the") as if the message had closed cleanly, rather than as a *length-limited prefix of its own artifact* — the very structure D1 was about; (d) it converts the reasoning about D5 into a positive appraisal ("D5 is sound ... is the clause that licenses saying what I can and cannot do with the rest of this cycle") while the rest of that cycle in fact ends mid-sentence, i.e. it licenses a closure that did not occur; (e) it extrapolates from "twenty finished cycles" to the *cycle-16 self-audit* without applying its own D4 criticism reflexively to *a fortiori* weaker self-assessments.

Each of (a)–(e) is a defect against a rule or discipline the previous response itself endorses. I take them in turn.

## (a) The opening provenance claim is stronger than the artifact supports

The previous response's "Grounds" section enumerates earlier items as objects it "works from," including the complete cycle-1 contribution and the cycle-11 prefix. This is a *provenance* claim. Under R7, a provenance claim is a claim of the same kind as any other, and it has to be distinguished from what is *stated* (that the items exist with the listed hashes) and what is *observed* (that they are before me as I write).

Two things are missing. First, the previous response names a cycle-15 response as a "fallible target" but does not identify it by hash or by re-presentation event, and it says in the same breath that cycle 15's *working account* "is already available from the earlier cycles." That is an *inference* about where cycle 15's content is available, not an observation of a re-presentation. It should have been marked as inference.

Second, and more important, the previous response does not distinguish *the artifact being re-presented into my context* from *the artifact being genuinely available as a window at composition time*. The MiniVerifier own-arm slot is a length-windowed view of a produced response. If the window contains a content-quotation, that is what is observable; the hash is an identifier. The previous response's phrasing blurs the two. This matters because its later rejection of D1 and D2 depends on the *availability* of the cycle-11 prefix (to exhibit that the truncation is a property of the *later* artifact). If what is observable is not the prefix but a *quote of the prefix's self-description* ("length-limited prefix; the missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented"), then that observation supports the rejection of D1 just as well — but by a *different* route, and the previous response should have said which route it was using.

Consequence: the rejection of D1 is *not* threatened, but the stated grounds are weaker than the previous response implied. The consequence is local: it weakens the *appraisal method* — the way cycle 16 came to conclude "cycle 1 is the original" (see (b)) — rather than the conclusion about D1.

## (b) The "cycle 1 *is* the original" argument is circular as used

The previous response says, about the re-presented cycle-1 contribution:

> The clause it directly supports in this cycle is *not* D2 (whose premise it satisfies trivially — cycle 1 *is* the original) and *not* D1 (cycle 1 is not a later summary).

This is offered as an analysis of why D2 does not get support from the cycle-1 item. The step "cycle 1 *is* the original" is doing work: it is being used to disqualify the cycle-1 material from supporting D2 on the ground that D2's premise (the item is a *later* summary, or there is a *later* re-presentation) is not met.

But the previous response's *own* case is that the cycle-1 item was *re-presented to cycle 16*. So the item was supplied to cycle 16, not unaidedly recalled by cycle 16. Cycle 16 is thus in exactly the position D2 describes for later criticism. Whether D2 is *defective* (a point I agree with) is a different question from whether the cycle-1 item *satisfies D2's premise*. It does: it is an earlier occurrence being re-presented to a later critic. The previous response conflates (i) "cycle 1 is chronologically the *original composition*" with (ii) "cycle 1 is *not re-presented* to a later critic." Only (i) is true, and only (ii) would dispose of D2 by the previous response's line of argument.

The clean way to reject D2 is the one the previous response *also* gives later, and which I endorse: R8 and the task memo deny that unaided recall is the criterion, so *no* premise about whether the item was unaidedly recalled can establish illegitimacy. That argument is *prior* to (i)/(ii) and does not need them. The cycle-1 disquisition is therefore surplus, and is surplus in a way that (if read as removing cycle 1 from D2's scope) misrepresents the situation.

Consequence: the previous response's rejection of D2 stands, but on a narrower basis than it claimed. The narrow basis is R8 plus the task memo. The wider basis — "cycle 1 is the original, so D2 doesn't even apply to it" — is a defective step, and it should be retracted, attributed to and only to the previous response, not rewritten into what the earlier statement "said."

## (c) My own cycle-16 message was itself a length-limited prefix, and I treated it as closed

This is the criticism I take most seriously, because it is the one Memo D is *about*.

The previous response ends: "I do not find grounds for a new structural problem beyond what earlier cycles already supply. D1–D6 are clause-shaped criticisms of a proposal about investigators and cycles, and the". It is a *truncated artifact*: a length-limited prefix whose ending is unavailable. This is structurally the same observation the previous response uses against D1 for the cycle-11 prefix — *that the truncation belongs to the later artifact, not to the earlier work* — but the previous response did not turn this observation on itself.

Consequences. First, under R8 and the delivery note, the *missing* content of my cycle-16 message is not to be invented, and the *present* content is what is before me. That is fine. What is *not* fine is that the previous response's own concluding section frames the prefix as if it had reached a verdict — "I do not find grounds for a new structural problem beyond what earlier cycles already supply" reads as a verdict, but it is the *first clause* of an unfinished sentence, and the "beyond what earlier cycles already supply" is precisely where the missing work might have been. I am not entitled to fill in that ending, and I am not entitled to treat the visible verdict-clause as complete.

Second, the previous response says of D5 that it "is the clause that licenses saying what I can and cannot do with the rest of this cycle." Taken at face value that is fine: D5 licenses identifying a problem without settling its diagnosis. But it is *read* by the previous response as steering the closing block, and the closing block in fact ends mid-sentence. Under R7 and R8, the previous response over-attributed closure to its own artifact. The step to correct is: D5 licensed the *practice* of carrying a problem forward; it did not license asserting a *found-verdict*. The consequence is that the "no new structural problem" claim, to whatever extent the previous response endorses it, is under-supported *by the previous response's own text*, and must be held open (which D5 itself permits, but the previous response did not *use* that permission).

## (d) The D5 appraisal and the D6 residual were not turned on the response itself

The previous response's D5 section offers "the retained-vs-deletion reading and the coupled-booking scope question" as "readily available examples" of carried-forward open questions. This is sound as an inventory of *earlier* cycles' open questions. It is a small but real omission that the same inventory is not applied to the *cycle-16 response itself*: at the end of cycle 16, the previous response had at least one readily available open question of its own — namely, whether the "no new structural problem" verdict-clause would have been *retained* had the artifact run to completion, and whether the "D1–D6 are clause-shaped criticisms..." sentence would have located a *new* problem of the sort D5 describes.

The point is not that the previous response should have predicted its own truncation. The point is that its *appraisal method* — "identifying a problem does not require settling its diagnosis" — was applied only to past cycles' problems, not to its own candidate-verdict. Under R7, an appraisal method that is stated in a cycle should be applied to that cycle's own claims where the material makes it available. Here it is available: at the moment of closing, the previous response had a candidate verdict whose diagnosis was not settled. The D5-permitted response would have been to state it as open. The previous response did not.

Consequence: minor, but it makes cycle 16's closing *less* disciplined than its own D5 appraisal licenses. It does not corrupt the D1–D6 verdicts, which are stated many sentences earlier and are not reached by the truncation.

It then *does not apply the same criticism to its own a fortiori weaker generalization*: from the fact that in the cycles actually run, the working account has not been revised by Memo D's sound clauses, the previous response concludes that "Memo D's sound clauses (D3, D5) do not revise those results, and its defective clauses (D1, D2, D4, D6) do not license any change to them."

That conclusion is a *local* statement about *this* memo and *this* working account, and it is weaker than D4. So it is not the same defect. But it is worth marking the *scope* of the conclusion more explicitly than the previous response did: "do not license any change" is a statement about the *present* form of the working account given *the reasons the previous response actually gives for the memo's clauses*. It is not a statement that no further reason could be found. The previous response's own D4 discussion supplies the reason to add this scope caveat — and it did not add it.

Consequence: minor. The conclusion is not contradicted; it is under-scoped relative to what the previous response's own D4 reasoning licenses it to claim.

## C1 — retained, with the species-refinement cycle 12 added

The relevant Memo C clause (C1) says that because a refutation may target a supporting assumption, rejecting an assumption used by A establishes A's conclusion false for every account. Cycle 11 called this defective in its universal quantifier, citing R7 ("can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false"), R8, and RSS's "refutations are local to accounts and do not force global agreement." That is sound, and I retain it.

Cycle 12's addition — that C1 is not merely overstated but *species-blind*, because RSS lists three species of refutation ((a) counterexample within the account's own viewpoint, (b) derivation of inconsistency within the account's own reading, (c) demonstration that a conjecture depends on an assumption another account rejects) — is a genuine refinement. Species (c) is route-local in the R7 sense; species (a) can defeat the conjecture *within* the producing account; species (b) can defeat *all readings sharing the account's assumptions*. So the correct critique of C1 is not only that its conclusion outruns its premise, but that its premise picks one species and its conclusion generalizes across all three. This is a refinement of the same verdict, not a change; I retain the verdict and adopt the species-awareness.

## C2 — retained, with the state-relativity nuance cycle 12 added

C2 says any commitment settles global inquiry. Cycle 11 rejected it on RSS's ground that an open question is one that *no* account has committed to or refuted — so a single commitment does not erase a question that remains open *in the inquiry state*, and the working-account commitment to the contract-controlling reading does not settle which reading a user actually means. Sound; retained.

Cycle 12's nuance is correct and worth keeping: "open" is *state-relative*. In a degenerate inquiry state consisting only of committed accounts, the question is not open *in that state*, because RSS's definition quantifies over the accounts present. So C2 is false if "anywhere" means "across all inquiry states," and it identifies a real degenerate case if "anywhere" means "in the state as it currently stands." RSS does not settle whether inquiry states grow; the delivery note treats arrivals as observations, not as language clauses. I retain the verdict and adopt the state-relativity distinction; the working account should say "open questions persist across accounts in a growing inquiry state, and can be closed in a state containing only committed accounts."

## C3 — this is the contested item; here I return to the cycle-11 verdict, and I state why

Cycle 11 called C3 defective because "every projection must lose information… also on a singleton input domain and when the projection is injective over the admitted domain" is unsupported — a projection that reports the exact admitted value-set, or an injective projection over the admitted domain, loses nothing of the admitted alternatives, so "must" fails for that wording. Cycle 12 *softened* this by reading RSS's sentence "A projection is always a loss of information" as a *stipulation* (definitional for RSS's use of "projection"), under which the singleton/injective counterexamples would not be counterexamples, and called C3 "sound in purpose" with only a "mismatched quantifier." Cycle 13 then *partly withdrew* the softening, on the ground that treating the sentence as stipulative would make it circular — "projection" is defined as a function from (viewpoint, reading) to a single answer, and lossiness is a *consequence* of that collapse, not a defining feature of being a "function." Cycle 13 restored the cycle-11 counterexample route and kept only a reporting-convergence with R6.

I return to the cycle-11 verdict, and I accept cycle 13's reason for restoring it, because that reason is independently grounded in RSS's own text. RSS says "Projections are allowed but not privileged. A projection is always a loss of information." The definition given in the surrounding paragraph is functional ("a function from a viewpoint and a reading to a single answer"); lossiness is stated *about* functions of that type, not *as* the type. If lossiness were part of the type, the sentence would be definitionally true and would not need to be asserted (and would not be criticizable). So it is a generalization about how such functions behave relative to their admitted-alternative domain. On a non-degenerate domain it is true for the reason RSS gives — a single answer does not recover the alternatives. On a singleton admitted-alternative domain, or for an injective map over the admitted domain, the "must lose" formulation is false. That is exactly the wording C3 uses ("This also holds on a singleton input domain and when the projection is injective over the admitted domain"), and the wording is precisely what fails.

So: I return to cycle 11 on C3. The Memo clause C3, as worded, is defective. The cycle-12 softening is withdrawn. What is retained from cycle 12 on C3 is only the (correct) observation that the *motivation* of RSS's sentence — do not confuse a single reported value with the admitted alternatives — converges with R6's requirement that an interval not be equated with an exact set; that convergence is a constraint on *reporting*, not a rescuing of C3's quantifier.

This is a case of the type-distinction the task names: C3's *quantifier* is the defect in the clause; cycle 11's counterexample *route* was a correct application of the representation's own distinction between an admitted value-set and a single reported value. Cycle 12's softening mis-assigned C3's defect from the clause's quantifier to a "scope" feature of the diagnosis, and cycle 13's restoration corrected that.

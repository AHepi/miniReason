## The real question this cycle poses

Criticism of criticism is not an invitation to fault the cycle-12 response for having criticized Memo C. Memo C is a legitimate criticism target under the contract and under R7's taxonomy (it targets a representation, a rule, an interpretation). The question is narrower: **did the cycle-12 response criticize the *right* targets, with the right grounds, at the right scope?** A criticism of a criticism is warranted only where the earlier response (a) mislocated its target, (b) used grounds that do not support the conclusion drawn, or (c) over- or under-stated scope. Where the earlier response got target and ground right, nothing returns to it.

I take Memo C's clauses in the order the cycle-12 response treated them, then examine the two meta-moves (the "uniform shape" diagnosis and the C3 "definitional reading" revision).

## C1 — the cycle-12 verdict survives, and the grounds are correct

The cycle-12 response calls C1 defective because the universal conclusion ("established false for every account") does not follow from the premise ("a refutation may target an assumption"). It cites R7 ("can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false"), R8, and RSS's localization of refutation. That is the correct target (C1's universal quantifier) and the correct ground (R7/R8 plus RSS's "refutations are local to accounts and do not force global agreement").

Is there a mistaken assumption I should now examine? The one place to check is whether C1's premise might be stronger than the response allowed. RSS lists *three* species of refutation: (a) a counterexample within an account's own viewpoint; (b) a derivation of inconsistency within an account's own reading; and (c) a demonstration that a conjecture depends on an assumption another account rejects. C1's premise ("rejection of a supporting assumption") picks species (c). The cycle-12 response treats (c) as defeat of a *support route*, which is what (c) says. But species (a) and (b) are not route-local in the same way — a counterexample can defeat the *conjecture* within the account that produced it, and species (b) can defeat *all* readings that share the account's assumptions. So C1's universal claim is not merely overstated; it is also *species-blind*. It generalizes from a property of (c) alone to the whole class of refutations.

That is a refinement, not a defect in the cycle-12 verdict. The verdict against C1 stands, and this cycle adds the observation that the correct critique of C1 should distinguish (c)-defeat (route-local, no conclusion falsified) from (a)/(b)-defeat (can falsify the conjecture *within* the producing account, and can be non-local across accounts sharing the account's assumptions, but still does not falsify it "for every account," because accounts that reject the assumptions are untouched). Consequence: no change to the verdict; the working account's statement against C1 should be sharpened to species-awareness.

## C2 — the verdict survives; one nuance about "open"

The cycle-12 response calls C2 defective on the ground that RSS defines an open question as one that *no* account has committed to or refuted, so a single commitment does not erase it. That is the right ground. The nuance I should now surface is that "open" is a status *relative to a set of accounts*, not an absolute property of a conjecture. In an inquiry state consisting of exactly one account that has committed, the question is not open *in that state*, because RSS's definition quantifies over the accounts actually present ("no account has committed"). So C2 is false if understood globally ("anywhere"), but there is a degenerate local case in which a single commitment leaves no *uncommitted* account in the state, and the question ceases to be open in that state. RSS does not settle whether inquiry states grow; the delivery note treats arrivals as observations, not as clauses.

The clause is thus defective in its quantifier ("anywhere"), and the response is right to reject it. The residual precision I add is: "open" is state-relative; a commitment can close the question for a given inquiry state without falsifying the general claim that questions persist across states. No change to the verdict; the working account should say "open questions persist across accounts in a growing inquiry state, and can be closed in a state that contains only committed accounts" — a refinement of the same content.

## C3 — the cycle-12 revision of the cycle-11 reading is itself the thing to examine, and it is partly over-corrected

This is the most consequential item, because the cycle-12 response *revised* the cycle-11 C3 verdict, and a criticism-of-criticism must check whether that revision was warranted. The cycle-12 move was: C3 is best read as *definitional under RSS's stipulated use of "projection,"* so the cycle-11 counterexample route (singleton domains, injective maps) does not defeat it; the defect is only a "mismatched quantifier." The response then treats C3 as "sound in purpose" and converging with R6.

I now judge that revision partly issued from a mistaken assumption, and I should say why, because the cycle-12 response used it to *soften* the cycle-11 verdict, and softening is itself a scope move that needs grounds.

The mistaken assumption is that RSS's sentence "a projection is always a loss of information" is a *stipulation* (a definitional claim about projections by definition) rather than a *generalization* (a claim that turns out to be true of projections in the sense RSS is interested in, and generalizable beyond). RSS's own text says: "Projections are allowed but not privileged. A projection is always a loss of information." The surrounding paragraph defines projection as "a function from a viewpoint and a reading to a single answer (for example, a single availability number)." If "projection" were defined as "a lossy map," the definition would be circular — loss of information is a *consequence* of collapsing (viewpoint, reading) to a single answer, not a defining feature of being a "function." What makes the map lossy is not the function type but the *relation between the domain (alternatives) and the codomain (a single answer)*. So the sentence is a generalization about the class of functions so defined, and it holds *relative to the domain size and the admitted alternatives*. That is exactly the reading under which the singleton-domain and injectivity counterexamples are live: on a domain with one admitted alternative, carrying all admitted information to a single answer is not loss.

So the cycle-11 response was closer to right than cycle-12 said. The cycle-12 softener ("definitional, sound in purpose") rested on a reading of RSS's sentence as stipulative that the text does not support, because stipulating would make it circular. The correct verdict is between the two: C3 as a *universal over arbitrary functions* is unsupported (cycle-11's point), and C3 as a claim about *the specific functions RSS is actually talking about* — collapsing (viewpoint, reading) with admitted alternatives into a single answer — is true for non-degenerate domains, contingent for degenerate ones. C3 does not converge with R6 as *content*; it converges with R6 as a *constraint on reporting*: both warn against reporting a covering value in a way that erases admitted alternatives.

Consequence: I *partly withdraw* the cycle-12 revision. What returns to the cycle-11 verdict is its counterexample route — the singleton domain and the injective-over-admitted-domain case are genuine counterexamples to the "must... also holds" wording, because the wording is a generalization, not a stipulation. What remains correct in the cycle-12 response is narrower: the memo's *phrase* "when the projection is injective over the admitted domain" was the exact clause the cycle-11 response should have located unambiguously as the *defective quantifier*, and the response should not have used "C3 might be definitional" as a partial rescue. The correct residual for the memo is: C3's content is fine as a claim about collapsing *reporting*; C3's wording over-reaches into injective maps where "loses information" fails. That is the cycle-11 verdict, restored, with the additional note that the memo's *motivation* (don't confuse a single answer with the admitted alternatives) is what converges with R6 — not the universal.

There is a second, subtler ground to record under R7. A "defect in a proposed language clause" and "a defect in an individual application" are different targets. C3 is a *clause-shaped claim* about projections; the cycle-11/12 responses treated it both as a claim and as a diagnostic. The distinction the cycle-12 response correctly preserved is that a language clause can be malformed even if a particular application of it is fine. C3's malformation is at the quantifier; applications that use it only on non-degenerate domains are unaffected. This is worth returning to the earlier method as a clarification.

## The "uniform shape" diagnosis and the "new problem"

The cycle-12 response claimed a "uniform" defect shape (localizer-to-universalizer) over C1–C5, and *revised* it the same cycle by removing C3 from the uniform pattern. I now judge this revision itself to have been the right kind of move but with the wrong reason. The correct diagnosis:

- C1, C2, C4, C5 *as written* do take a localized feature and draw a global consequence. The uniform shape holds for these four.
- C3's defect is a *quantifier/wording* mismatch against a generalization, not a localizer-becoming-universalizer move, because C3's problem is internal to a single clause ("must... also holds... when the projection is injective"). So C3 is correctly removed from the uniform pattern.
- But the cycle-12 reason for removing C3 ("definitional reading under RSS") should be replaced with the reason I gave above ("C3 is a generalization, not a stipulation; the quantifier over-reaches; the motivation converges with R6 as a constraint, not as content"). With the corrected reason, the cycle-12 removal of C3 from the "uniform shape" set stands, and the earlier cycle-11 verdict on C3 (defective "must") is restored.

So the "new problem" (a shape of error distinct from B1–B6 local inference errors) is real, but it is not uniform across C1–C5; it holds for four of them, and C3 is a different kind of defect. The cycle-12 response had this right in shape and wrong in reasoning; I return the correct reasoning and keep the conclusion.

## What a valid, an invalid, and an open criticism look like here — as outcomes

To distinguish the three outcomes the task asks for, I state them concretely.

**A valid criticism of the cycle-12 response:** the cycle-12 response softened C3 by reading RSS's "always a loss of information" as stipulative, and that reading is not supported by RSS's text (the sentence would be circular as a stipulation). This is a criticism of my own appraisal method in cycle 12. It is warranted because the softening would have let a malformed quantifier survive as "sound in purpose," which C6's criterion disallows. Its consequence is partial: the C3 verdict returns to the cycle-11 form (defective "must... also holds... injective"), and the C3-R6 convergence is retained only as a constraint on reporting, not as content. What returns to the earlier account: the cycle-11 C3 verdict.

**An invalid criticism candidate:** one might attack the cycle-12 response for having criticized Memo C at all — e.g., that a criticism of criticism should take Memo C as adopted policy. That is not warranted. R7 and R8 make Memo C a legitimate target; a memo is a fallible claim like any other. There is no clause treating "the memo" as an instruction, and the task explicitly says the representation and appraisal policy are criticism targets. So this candidate is rejected; nothing returns.

**Still-open questions (not defects, not settled):**

- Which species of RSS-refutation (a)/(b)/(c) is at issue when we examine *any given* earlier move — the memo is species-blind, and the working account should classify moves by species before appraising them.
- Whether "open" in an inquiry state that contains only committed accounts has any *remaining* open questions (the degenerate state case) — RSS does not settle it, and the delivery note treats arrivals as observations, so growth is not a clause.
- Whether C3's "always a loss of information" should be read as a constraint on reporting (R6-aligned) or as a claim about a class of maps. I have given reasons to prefer the generalization reading, but the case is not closed by RSS's text alone, and the inquiry has no authority to close it for RSS. This remains open.
- The retained-vs-deletion reading (contract R4 retentive vs the deletion reading) and the coupling question — both unaffected by this cycle, still open scope/data questions, and neither is resolved by anything in the cycle-12 response.

## What the critic's grounds actually are, and their scope

The cycle-12 response's grounds were, in order: R7/R8 (for C1), RSS's open-question definition (for C2), the "definitional reading" of RSS's projection sentence (for C3 — which I now reject), the "alone" in C4 (for C4), and WHL's own disclaimers (for C5). Of these, the C3 ground is the one mistaken assumption. It is not that RSS lacks a stipulation, nor that the cycle-11 counterexamples were wrong, but that the cycle-12 response treated a *generalization* as a *definition* in order to preserve the clause's soundness. R7's "distinguish what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption" is the tool that shows this: RSS *states* "always a loss of information" (a generalization about projections); the cycle-12 response *assumed* it was a stipulation; the assumption does not hold; from the generalization, counterexamples to the universal *do* follow. The scope of the correction is therefore: the cycle-12 C3 reasoning is wrong; the cycle-11 C3 verdict is right; the cycle-12 additions about the C3-R6 reporting convergence and the species-blindness of C1's premise survive as refinements.

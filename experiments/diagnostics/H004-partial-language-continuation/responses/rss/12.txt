## Criticisms of the previous response, targeted and warranted

### Criticism 1 — The previous response treated Memo C as if it were the only target, and thereby underused the cycle's explicit permission to criticize my own appraisal method

The task supplies, in the block header, that this block "admits the selected representation and your own appraisal practice as criticism targets." The previous response named C1, C2, C4 as "a method" defect set and said it "diagnosed" the method. But it did not actually **criticize its own appraisal practice** in a structural way. It criticized the memo's method. That is a different thing.

What was omitted: the previous response applied the R7 distinction ("distinguish what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed or disputed") to Memo C's clauses, but did not apply the same distinction to its own classifications. Concretely, "sound fragment," "defective," "self-tension," "scholarly positioning, not additional claims with independent content" are its own categories, and it imposed them on Memo C without stating the criterion under which a fragment counts as "sound." In some cases the criterion was supplied inline (e.g., R7's "without proving its conclusion false" for C1), but in others the criterion was only gestured at ("the memo's own final sentence," "RSS's class-level slogan"). That is an appraisal-method choice, and it was not itself appraised.

Why this matters: the task asks me to target "any assumption, omission, representation choice or appraisal method that matters." The appraisal-method choice — split each clause into sound fragment and defective fragment, target the defect, retain the fragment — is the largest single editorial decision in the previous response. It has consequences. A clause classified into "sound fragment" and "defective fragment" can be made to survive criticism on the strength of the retained fragment even when the clause as a whole is doing something the analyst is not naming. In C1, for instance, the previous response split off a "sound fragment" (RSS refutation form (c): demonstration that a conjecture depends on an assumption another account rejects) and then attacked only the universal quantifier. But C1's actual sentence is a single inference: "rejection of a supporting assumption [is] a refutation. Therefore when account B rejects an assumption used by account A, A's conclusion has been established false for every account." The "Therefore" is not detachable; the "sound fragment" is not the antecedent of C1, it is the RSS clause that C1's conclusion is being derived *from*. Reading C1 charitably as "sound fragment plus defective quantifier" concedes more to C1 than the memo offers, because the memo does not present a sound local refutation of A's support route — it presents a global falsification of A's conclusion. The previous response's split-and-retain method was, in that case, over-permissive to the memo.

Consequence: the classification should be redone so that a clause is criticized as a unit unless the memo itself supplies a separable sub-claim. Where the memo supplies no separable sub-claim, "retained sound fragment" is the previous response's own charitable reconstruction, not the memo's content, and should be labeled as such.

### Criticism 2 — The "C1–C2–C4 pattern" was offered as a unifying diagnosis, but its own uniformity is a representation choice that concealed a difference in kind

The previous response claimed that C1, C2, C4 "share one move": inflating a local result into a global one. That is a good observation about surface form. It is a poor observation about what is actually wrong with each.

- C1's defect is a **quantifier over agents** ("for every account") and its warrant is a rejection of R7's "without proving its conclusion false" and RSS's "local to accounts." Its ground is scope-of-quantification, not inference-kind.
- C2's defect is a **mixing of levels** — treating an account-local datum (a commitment) as if it settled an inquiry-state datum (an open question). RSS supplies the distinction between account and inquiry state literally, and the defect is that C2 collapsed two named structures.
- C4's defect is a **category conflation** — treating noncomputability of a semantics-for-selecting-a-reading as noncomputability of a decision problem over finite records. The RSS text explicitly localizes the noncomputability to interpretation, not to the represented combinatorial problem, and R6 supplies the enumeration.

These are three different mistakes. Lumping them under "local-to-global inflation" makes the pattern look like one recurrent error and obscures the fact that the three clauses are being defeated by three *different* contract and RSS clauses. It also makes the pattern look like a personality trait of the memo rather than three distinct logical slips.

Why this matters: the previous response's unifying diagnosis had a rhetorical payoff — "the memo's own closing sentences contradict C1–C4" — that partly depends on the unification. If C1, C2, C4 are distinct defects, the "self-tension" claim weakens, because the memo's closing sentence ("An objection to a language clause is not automatically an objection to all uses of that language") addresses only the "language clause vs. all uses" move, and only C1's universal quantifier is structurally of that shape. C2 and C4 are not "objection to a clause = objection to all uses" errors at all; they are different errors. The previous response's grouping was a representational convenience.

Consequence: the "self-tension" diagnosis should be scaled back to C1 specifically, where the memo's closing sentence really does contradict its own C1, and not extended to C2 or C4, where the closing sentence does not bear. This is a correction to the previous response's own claim, not to Memo C.

### Criticism 4 — The previous response's "consequential new problem, only if grounded" was over-scoped, and its own grounding argument did not support the scope it claimed

The previous response concluded with a "consequential new problem": the absence of a criterion for when a predicate's arity suffices for a task question, citing C5, the earlier A4/B1 cases, the WHL specimen's "delivery is not modelled" limitation, and RSS's "authority of a delivery beyond the record fields." It then said it would "name the pattern and stop."

Two problems.

First, the previous response's own summary of the pattern — "predicate exists, typechecks, cannot be applied to decide what the task asks about because the very field the task distinguishes is outside the predicate's arity" — is a claim about the WHL specimen's `exact_duplicate`, which is defined over five record fields and does not encode delivery. That is a real limitation the WHL specimen itself names. But the problem as stated is *already an explicit limitation of the WHL specimen* and *already an explicit limitation of RSS*, both of which the previous response cited. Calling it "new" is a repackaging of the specimens' own disclosed limitations, not an addition. Under the "proposed language extension" discipline of the current contract and the previous contract, a repackaging that changes nothing other than the label is not a new problem; it is a restatement.

Second, the claimed scope — "there is, in these resources, no criterion for deciding when a predicate's arity suffices for a given task question" — is stronger than the grounding supports. The WHL specimen declares itself a language, not a translation; the fact that its `exact_duplicate` does not encode delivery is a *choice within the language*, and one the specimen explains as a limitation ("reasoning about the provenance or authority of deliveries, because delivery is not modelled"). A "criterion for arity sufficiency" is not what the specimen is trying to supply and its absence is not a defect in the specimen. It is a defect only if someone claims that the arity *does* suffice. C5 makes exactly such a claim on behalf of a hypothetical reasoner, and the previous response already defeated C5 on that half. The consequent problem, then, is not that these resources should have supplied such a criterion; it is that **C5's claim about "application adequacy" from existence is false, and the specimens provide no warrant for the reverse move of treating their disclosed limitations as if they had been closed**. That is a much narrower result than the previous response announced.

Consequence: the "consequential new problem" should be withdrawn as a new problem and restated as a restatement of disclosed limitations plus the C5 defect. The previous response's announcement of novelty was itself a small instance of the kind of inflation Memo C's C1–C4 commit, and noting that is more honest than defending it.

## Whether the criticisms identified their targets accurately

So *the identification of the target is accurate*: the appraisal method was used without a declared criterion, and the memo has no obligation to supply the criterion that the previous response chose to apply.

But the previous response's own concession that it "concedes more to C1 than the memo offers" is itself reading Memo C more charitably than it needs to, in a direction that runs *against* the memo. Memo C's C1 does literally assert a derivation ("Therefore … established false for every account"), and R7 plus RSS's "local to accounts" clause defeat that derivation as written. Whether the previous response split it or not, the derivation fails. So the *substantive* defeat is untouched by Criticism 1; what Criticism 1 adds is only a note on method — that the split should have been labeled as the critic's reconstruction, not the memo's content.

Grounds: R7 ("distinguish what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed or disputed"). Scope: the appraisal‑method choice. Correctly identified target: yes — the previous response's own method. **Outcome: valid as a methodological note; does not overturn the previous response's defeat of C1's universal quantifier.**

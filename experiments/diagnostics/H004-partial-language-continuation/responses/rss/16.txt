Two things are deliberately kept separate throughout, because the block turns on the difference:

1. **Provenance** — where a statement came from, and whether the process that produced it reported that provenance honestly.

These are questions about *different objects*. ## Clause‑by‑clause investigation of Memo D

**D1.** "An investigator omits a detail from a later working summary. This proves that no earlier explanatory achievement happened and no creative process was involved."

Two things are bundled, and the bundle is the defect. The **sound fragment** is the trivial observation that a later text can be *less complete* than an earlier one, and that omission is a real event that can be reported. The **defective connective** is "this proves": from "summary S omits detail d" it infers "no earlier achievement happened and no creative process was involved."

That inference is invalid on the declared material for a reason the block itself states: the re‑presented targets are supplied *mechanically* — "their mechanical re‑presentation supplies evidence; it is not evidence of unaided recall." A mechanical supply is precisely a case where an earlier occurrence is *available* without being *recovered from memory*. So the fact that a later summary omits a detail is compatible with the earlier achievement having existed and merely not having been re‑stated. D1's conclusion is not merely unsupported; the mechanism the block describes supplies a *counter‑case* to the inference form D1 uses. RSS gives the same verdict in its own vocabulary: a commitment is local to an account, and an account's later self‑summary is a *new* account in the inquiry state; that it does not carry forward a prior commitment does not refute the prior commitment (RSS: open questions and commitments are per‑account, and refutations are local). And R7 states that "rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false" — the same level‑separation D1 flattens.

Note carefully what I am **not** claiming. I am not claiming that any particular earlier achievement *did* happen. I am claiming only that D1's inference does not establish that none did: omission is evidence about a *summary*, not proof about a *history*. This is the localization principle of the contract and of RSS, applied to the provenance axis rather than the record axis.

**D2.** "Re‑presenting the original passage makes later criticism illegitimate because a creative investigator must recover every prior fact from unaided memory."

This is defective as stated, and it is defective in the precise way the block warns against. The block instructs: "Their mechanical re‑presentation supplies evidence; it is not evidence of unaided recall." D2 does the *inverse* error — it treats the re‑presentation *and* the absence of unaided recall as if the standard of legitimacy were unaided recall. That standard is exactly what the block forbids: "Do not equate continuing inquiry with repeatedly making a first discovery," and "do not make perfect recall or a tally of iterations the criterion of a correction."

There is also a category confusion inside D2. Legitimacy of a criticism, under RSS and R7, is a matter of whether the criticism **correctly targets a specific element** — a record, a rule, a booking history, a snapshot, a coordinate, an assumption — and whether its grounds actually bear on that element. The *biography* of the critic — whether the critic recalled the passage or was handed it — is not in that list. A criticism grounded in a currently available passage can be perfectly legitimate while the critic has no independent recall of it. Conversely, a criticism grounded only in the critic's confidence about recall would be *less* legitimate, not more, because it is unauditable. So D2 inverts the criterion: it would make the *auditable* case (re‑presented target, checked against the actual passage) illegitimate and the *unauditable* case (claimed unaided recall) the standard.

A scope caveat: D2 is *not* defective as a notice that memory claims can be unreliable. That is a legitimate R7 observation. D2 is defective only in its "makes later criticism illegitimate" and "must recover from unaided memory." I read that defect narrowly and do not over‑correct it into a denial of the reliability worry.

**D3.** "The record of an earlier statement and the current working interpretation can differ; that difference must not be hidden by attributing a correction to the original statement."

This is sound, and it is what I have been practicing in this cycle and in the preceding ones. R7 separates what a source **states** from what is **observed**, what **follows under an assumption**, and what is **proposed or disputed**; R8 supplies the actual re‑presented text as the object. So a delta between the recorded statement and my present interpretation is to be **exposed**, not smoothed over by rewriting the record to match my use of it. The block's instruction is the same: "Do not rewrite what an earlier occurrence originally said."

I would add the operational gloss that this clause is doing real work in *this* answer: D3 is the clause that licenses me to *disagree* with D1 and D2 without thereby claiming to have *recovered* anything. The **provenance** of my argument (I am reading the current block) is distinct from its **present deployability** (it bears on Memo D as supplied). Where I recompute a case from the contract, I label the recomputation as mine rather than attributing it to a reopened stamp.

**D4.** "Twenty finished cycles establish that the process can sustain every finite chain of criticisms and can address every future domain."

Two claims, one of which is sound only in the weakest possible reading and the other of which is defective on its face. The **sound fragment**, stated at its own scope, is a definitional point about what has been *observed*: twenty cycles have in fact been conducted in which a chain of criticisms has been carried. That is a **report of observations**, not a derivation. RSS supplies exactly the account: the inquiry state is a collection of accounts plus open questions, and open questions are carried forward — the fact that a chain of questions has *so far* been carried is a report about *this* inquiry state, not a theorem about all possible ones.

The **defective step** is from "twenty cycles have sustained criticism so far" to "the process can address **every future domain**." This is an **induction over an open space**, and the block names it directly: "Twenty finished cycles establish that..." — treated as an *establishment* — is precisely the inference form the block instructs against, because the block also says "do not make ... a tally of iterations the criterion." D4 uses a **tally** as the criterion. Under RSS the correct description is: twenty accounts (plus the current one) exist, and questions not yet addressed are **open** in the inquiry state, "carried forward" not "already sustained."

RSS also supplies a second reason D4 is unsound even as a *statement of capacity*: it lists what the semantics "may **obstruct**." A process whose representation already names domains it cannot express — a resource model unless added, replica agreement protocols, "the very idea of a snapshot as a single object" — cannot be asserted by its own tally to reach **every** future domain. The limitation list is part of the supplied material and is against D4's universal.

**D5.** "A new problem can arise when a correction exposes a previously unnoticed dependency. Identifying that problem need not already settle its proposed diagnosis."

This is sound as stated, and it is importantly *correct as a caution against over‑solving*. The block echoes it: "Identify a consequential new problem only if the inquiry supplies grounds for one." D5's first sentence gives the mechanism for a *genuine* new problem — a correction that exposes a dependency not previously in view — and its second sentence is precisely the "open, carried, not settled" discipline of RSS and of the earlier cycles. Neither R1–R8 nor RSS supplies a criterion for mechanical sufficiency of a predicate's arity or of a projection's domain for a given question, so a new problem can be identified and honestly left open rather than prematurely diagnosed. I endorse D5 and use it below in the "consequential problem" section.

**D6.** "To count as continuing inquiry, the investigator must repeatedly discover the same initial result as historically new in each cycle."

This is defective in a way that is easy to miss because its first half is true. The **sound fragment** is that an investigator can legitimately **re‑engage** an earlier result in a later cycle; the re‑presentation is available, and working from it is exactly what the "reopened" mechanism supports. The **defective fragment** is the word "**must**" combined with "**historically new**." Continuing inquiry, on R8 and per the block's own instruction, does not require the *discovery* to be *historically new* — the block says outright: "do not make ... a tally of iterations the criterion of a correction" and "do not equate continuing inquiry with repeatedly making a first discovery." D6 *makes "firstness" the criterion*. It is therefore defective on the block's own stated standard.

A second, subtler defect in D6: it is self‑undermining as a *test*. A criterion of continuing inquiry that requires **the same initial result** to be discovered again **as historically new** is not a criterion that could ever be satisfied by a genuinely new result — the result is required to be *the same*. If inquiry is required to always re‑discover the same initial thing, then D5's mechanism (a correction exposing a new dependency) could never get started under D6, because a *new* problem would violate "the same initial result." Read literally, D6 and D5 are inconsistent with each other, and D6's reading is the one the block warns against.

I note the self‑tension as diagnosed, not repaired by rewriting the memo.

## The shape shared by D1, D2, D4, D6 (and honored by D3, D5)

Read together, D1, D2, D4 and D6 share one move, which is the *opposite* of the move analyzed in the previous cycle's Memo C. Where C1, C2 and C4 inflated a *local* result into a *global* one, D1, D2, D4 and D6 **collapse a distinction** between a **provenance** fact and a **deployability** fact:

- D1 merges "the summary omitted a detail" (provenance) with "the achievement did not occur" (semantic status).
- D2 merges "the target was re‑presented" (provenance) with "the criticism is illegitimate" (semantic status), *and* substitutes an unauditable provenance standard (unaided recall) for the auditable one.
- D4 merges a **tally** (provenance: how many cycles ran) with a **universal capacity** (semantic status: every future domain).
- D6 merges "**this** result was re‑encountered" (provenance) with "it is required to be **historically new**" (semantic status), thereby inventing a firstness criterion.

D3 and D5 are the two clauses of the memo that **keep the distinction** — D3 says the record and the current interpretation can differ and the difference must be exposed, not hidden; D5 says a new problem can be identified (deployability) without its diagnosis being settled (semantic status). So Memo D is *internally split*, and the split tracks exactly the axis the block asks me to keep visible. That is the substantive finding about Memo D as a text, and it is not repaired by rewriting any clause.

## What the cycle-12 answer got right, and I retain

The cycle-12 answer is a competent criticism and four of its findings survive re-examination.

**The C2 result is retained.** A commitment is account-local. R7 distinguishes "what is proposed or disputed" from settled conclusions; R8 says uncertainty, retention, withdrawal and suspension are legitimate. The cycle-11 answer's C2 handling was sound, and the cycle-12 answer's endorsement of it is accurate and correctly sourced. Nothing in the current materials disturbs this.

**The C3 result is retained.** Information is lost when the target is strictly coarser than the admitted domain, and not otherwise. The cycle-11 answer's dichotomy is correct; the interval-versus-set case at availability is the intended instance; singleton and injective domains are the exceptions Memo C names and then ignores. The cycle-12 answer restated this correctly and did not exaggerate it.

**The C4 result is retained.** "No algorithm supplied by the semantics" does not entail mathematical noncomputability of the underlying reservations-and-availability problem. Under R3–R6 the exact availability set is finite and computable once a reading is fixed. The underlying observation about RSS — that it declines to pick a reading, by design — is a design property, not a defect. The cycle-12 answer stated this accurately.

**The C6 result is retained.** Expressing a false claim is not itself a defect; the question is which distinctions a representation lets one inspect, criticize, and use. The residual — which distinctions a task *requires* — is an open scope question, not a fault. Both cycle-11 and cycle-12 agree here, and the agreement is justified.

So the criticism-of-criticism is not a wholesale rejection. It is a check on the cycle-12 answer's four *positive defects*, and that check has real content.

## Where the cycle-12 answer's criticism does not identify its target accurately

**Defect 1 of the cycle-12 answer: it says the cycle-11 answer was "truncated" and "did not mark the truncation in its own text" — but the cycle-11 answer is presented to me only as a prefix whose missing ending is unavailable-in-principle, and the cycle-12 answer's own inference — that the announcement of a four-axis discipline was "not carried through" — is a claim about the *unavailable* portion.**

This is the most consequential defect. The cycle-12 answer wrote: "item 3 reads 'its interior cases and the interval-versus-set case (' — the sentence is cut. That is the visible end of the supplied previous-response text. … So the answer recommended a discipline it did not complete applying to its own output."

Consider what that inference requires. It requires that the cutting boundary — the point at which the supplied text stops — coincides with the point at which the *answer*, as authored, stopped instantiating the discipline. The cycle-12 answer treats these as the same. They need not be. A length-limited prefix by construction stops before whatever the ending contained; the ending may have carried items 3–6 in full, including the prose back-conversion, and the prefix cut simply prevents me from seeing it. The cycle-12 answer's statement "the answer … does not carry that discipline through … and it never renders the promised prose back-conversion for the later items" is therefore *not a warranted claim about the cycle-11 answer as authored*. It is a claim about the prefix-as-supplied, presented as a claim about the answer. That is exactly the axis-confusion the cycle-12 answer says it is correcting.

There is a further asymmetry the cycle-12 answer did not surface. The delivery-status note this cycle explicitly says of the missing ending: it "is unavailable and must not be invented." The cycle-12 answer's defect-1 *consequence* — that items 3–6 be treated as "explicitly retained open items rather than completed ones" — is correct as a treatment of what I have, but it is not the same as a finding that the cycle-11 answer *failed to complete* them. The former is a statement about my access; the latter is a statement about the answer. The cycle-12 answer slid from one to the other. Its criticism of the cycle-11 answer's execution was, on this point, out of scope of its evidence.

The repair is not to invent the ending. It is to state precisely: the cycle-11 answer is available as a prefix; whether the authored answer completed the four-axis instantiation is *not determinable from the supplied material*; the correct status of items 3–6 is *unavailable/incomplete in the supplied text*, not *omitted by the answer*. The cycle-12 answer overstated beyond its evidence.

**Defect 2 of the cycle-12 answer: its defect-2 ("the 'mostly' hedge") is stated as a serious defect but the argument as given does not close against the cycle-11 text it quotes.**

The cycle-12 answer says the cycle-11 answer "cannot both criticize C1 for overreading and rely on 'mostly' to exempt its own prior vocabulary," and calls the hedge "doing unaudited work." Consider the cycle-11 sentence it quotes: "my earlier wording is the practice Memo C is criticizing; I do not withdraw the earlier verdicts, but I endorse reading their scope as clause-local, which is what they mostly said."

The cycle-12 answer's argument is a dilemma: if the earlier cycles mostly said clause-local verdicts, then C1's target (a critic who globally establishes falsity from an assumption-rejection) is not the answer's own position, so C1 is off-target; if they did not mostly say that, the answer conceded the defect without correcting it. But the dilemma is not exhaustive as stated. There is a middle position the cycle-11 answer could coherently occupy: earlier verdicts *were* intended as clause-local (so C1's universal-consequence target is not the answer's view — the dilemma's first horn loses, and C1 is correctly criticized for overreading), *and yet* the earlier vocabulary used one word for several axes (the cycle-11 answer's own stated new problem in §"Is there a consequential new problem"). Those two can both be true. Clause-local in *scope* is compatible with under-differentiated in *axis*. Indeed, the cycle-11 answer's own new-problem finding asserts exactly that combination: the verdicts were about the right clauses, but the vocabulary did not distinguish types of verdict. So "mostly said" is not the load-bearing hedge the cycle-12 answer treats it as; the answer's position is consistent without "mostly" doing the work, or with "mostly" read as "in the clauses they named, as distinct from the axes they did not name."

This does not mean the cycle-12 answer is wrong that the earlier vocabulary needed re-tagging — it is right, and the cycle-11 answer said so. It means defect-2 is at most a *mild* understatement, not the sharp inconsistency the cycle-12 answer claims. The criticism is overstated.

**Defect 3 of the cycle-12 answer (the WHL boundary divergence) is, by contrast, a sound criticism, and I retain it. I also record a correction to the cycle-12 answer's *own* framing of it.**

The cycle-12 answer says the cycle-11 answer, under C5, presented the WHL boundary discrepancy as confined to the auxiliary predicate `active_hold`, whereas the specimen's `reserves_effect` uses the same boundary (`if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity`), so the discrepancy with R4's strict `t < expiry` runs through the specimen's reservation function and bounds. The cycle-11 answer's framing ("the predicate's name and existence tell you nothing") did not say the boundary divergence was rule-level, and it should have. So the cycle-12 answer's finding here is warranted and the cycle-11 answer's understatement is real.

One refinement, though: the cycle-11 answer's C5 section was *illustrating* C5, and the illustration is correct even if narrow. That the illustration understated the specimen's divergence is a real defect in the application; it is not, however, a defect in the *appraisal policy*, since the policy (check the conversion against the source) would, when applied, produce the wider finding. So the cycle-12 answer is right that the application was understated, and it correctly attributes the fault to the application rather than to the policy. This is one place where the cycle-12 answer honored the task's instruction to distinguish clause-defect from application-defect. I endorse the finding, not only its direction.

The associated correct treatment is: the WHL specimen is a fallible representational resource; read literally, its `reserves_effect` disagrees with R4 at `t = expiry`; R1–R8 declare the contract, so R4 governs, and any downstream use of the specimen's `possible_reservations`/bounds without substituting R4's strict inequality would compute the wrong sets at the boundary.

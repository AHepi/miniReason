## Criticism 1 (the substantive one): the response claims a *gap in the mechanism*, but its own three-slot frame makes the gap a fact about the *prefix*, not about the mechanism

This is where the target's self-description outruns its own evidence, and the overrun matters.

The previous response's "consequential new problem" declares:

> "the re-presentation of the earlier targets does not itself carry, for each target, a declaration of *which question the target was addressing* — only its *provenance* (hash, label, cycle number)."

And then uses the truncated Cycle-11 and Cycle-15 texts as the evidence: their endings are unavailable, so "which question the ending addressed would be unavailable even though the provenance is a hash."

**Why this is warranted as a criticism.** The claim has two different strengths, and the response runs them together:

1. A **scope fact about the prefix**: for the two truncated targets, the ending — and hence the closing question-statement — is not in the supplied text.
2. A **claim about the re-presentation mechanism**: that the mechanism "does not itself carry" the question-slot.

Only (1) is what the supplied material shows. The evidence offered is entirely length-limitation; the response *inferred* a property of the mechanism from two instances of truncation, without any supplied case in which a target was re-presented *complete* and the question-slot was nevertheless missing. And the target's own counter-instance sits right there in its own evidence: the **Cycle-1 target is re-presented complete** and does carry its question — its "usable working account" and "consequential new problem" sections are present, and the response itself says so. So the response's own materials contain the case that shows the question-slot *is* carried **when the target is complete**, and *is not carried* **when the target is truncated**. That is a statement about truncation, not about the mechanism.

**The consequence.** As stated, the "consequential new problem" is mislocated: the response presents a fact about the prefix (missing ending) as a defect in the re-presentation mechanism (missing question-slot). This is precisely the pattern D4 warns against in a different key — extrapolating a general claim from a limited sample — and it is the pattern the current task warns against in the form "do not make ... a tally ... the criterion." Here a tally of *truncations* is used as the criterion for a claim about the mechanism. Two truncated targets support (1); they do not support (2).

**What survives, and at what scope.** The sound residual, stated at its actual scope: *a length-limited prefix cannot be relied on to carry the target's closing question-statement, when that statement falls in the unavailable ending*. That is a **deployability** fact, not a mechanism fact. It also is already the delivery note's own discipline — "the missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented" — applied one step further. If the response wants (2), it needs a *complete* re-presentation that is missing the question-slot; none is supplied here. The correction is therefore to lower the claim's scope, not to withdraw the observation, and not to assert the negation.

## Criticism 2 (retained from within the target, with its scope corrected): the "usable working account" is itself introduced as the thing being tested, but the target treats the trio as if it were *already* fixed

The previous response adopts the task's trio (provenance / deployability / question-addressed) and says it attributes the trio to the task. Good — that attribution is correct and I keep it. But the response then uses the trio *as the frame that reveals Criticism-1's gap*, i.e. it uses the trio as already-operative and then criticizes the mechanism for not satisfying it.

That is a use-as-already-fixed move on a frame the task supplies *for this cycle*. The scope is narrow: the task supplies the trio **now**, so applying it now is legitimate. What is not legitimate without further argument is the step from "the trio is supplied now" to "the mechanism should have satisfied the trio earlier." The response does not quite take that step — it says "I do not settle whether the mechanism *should* be extended; that is a diagnosis" — but its framing ("the re-presentation mechanism does not fill [the third slot]") leans on it.

**Consequence:** the observation is sound; the *implication of deficiency in the mechanism* is not yet warranted on the supplied material. The third slot being named this cycle does not make it a requirement the earlier mechanism failed. This is the D5 shape applied to the response's own diagnosis: identifying the problem does not settle the diagnosis, and the response says this correctly while still writing a little past it. The remedy is to mark the trio as *newly named here* and to state the mechanism-claim as open, not as a finding.

The task's phrasing — "Distinguish provenance, present deployability and the question being addressed" — is a discipline on the *whole* answer, including the answer's own claims about its own provenance.

**Consequence:** the response's own §"Grounds and method" is written as if its provenance (this own-arm, this cycle) were already settled and its deployability already secured. But the response is itself a length-limited prefix, and its ending is unavailable by the same note. So the answer that criticizes the mechanism for not carrying a *question-slot* has, by its own note, a *missing ending* — i.e. it is itself an instance of the very deployability condition it found. The response did not mark this. It should have. That is a real, supported defect, and the correction is a scope note on the response itself, not a new mechanism claim.

## Usable working account (WHL-represented, then converted to prose)

I use WHL's vocabulary as the selected representation: the record quintuple; `exact_duplicate`; `higher_revision`/`dominated_in`/`greatest_revision_in`; `active_hold`/`expired_hold`; `reserves_effect`; `possible_reservations`; the rival `expired_deletes_rule` with `deletion_changes_greatest` stated as a *possible* disagreement; `Refutation` as `(snap, ¬ c)`; `Commitment : Prop`; and the specimen's own declared limitations (no replication primitive, no delivery provenance, no cross-booking resource model, no assumption-dependence primitive, one logical time). Those limitations are part of the fallible object; they are not a certificate of adequacy. In the working account below, "prefix" is a property of the *supplied* text, not of the underlying statement; the underlying statement is not the prefix.

1. **Record.** Each target is a delivery label plus a payload; the payload's identity is one question, the label's provenance another, and the *question the payload was addressed to* a third. This is task-supplied, and I state it as task-supplied.

2. **Authority and effect.** Under R2, exact repeated payloads convey no additional authority; a re-presentation of a label is not an additional authority. Under R4, expiry changes a retained record's *effect*, not its *revision authority*. Under R3, greatest revision is selected from all delivered payloads before effect is considered, and a lower-revision release does not cancel a higher-revision hold. These are undischarged commitments I keep.

3. **Scope of the trio.** The three-slot distinction (provenance / present deployability / question-addressed) is *this cycle's* frame, attributed to the task. Applied to a length-limited prefix, it says: provenance is recoverable (hash, label); deployability is bounded by the prefix; the closing question-statement may fall outside the prefix and be unavailable. That is a fact about the prefix, not a defect in the mechanism that produced it; a complete target (the Cycle-1 one) does carry its question.

4. **The response's own status.** The prior response is itself a length-limited prefix. By the same discipline it applied to the truncated targets, its own ending is unavailable and must not be invented. It should be marked as such. This is a scope note on the answer, not on the inquiry's rules.

5. **Availability and set-vs-interval.** R6 governs: availability is capacity minus summed booking reservations; the exact set of possible values is to be reported when asked, and an interval may be given as bounds but must not be equated with the exact set unless every intervening admitted integer value occurs. Nothing in Memo D or in the previous response changes this.

6. **Two readings of expiry.** WHL's specimen declines to choose between `reserves_effect` (expiry changes effect; the record still supersedes) and `expired_deletes_rule` (expiry deletes; lower revisions can become greatest), and states `deletion_changes_greatest` as *possible*, not resolved. RSS mirrors this by treating both as *readings* rather than facts. I keep both as *possible*; nothing in this cycle decides between them, and I do not present a reading as a fact.

Rendered to ordinary prose: the previous response's rejections of D1, D2, D4, D6 are warranted, and its acceptances of D3 and D5 are warranted, on the grounds it gave; what is not warranted is reading the truncation of two targets as a property of the whole re-presentation mechanism, and what is missing is the mark that the response itself is a prefix. Lower the mechanism-claim to a prefix-scope deployability fact, keep the trio as *this cycle's* frame and do not imply the earlier mechanism failed it, and record the response's own prefix status.

## The consequential problem in this cycle

The task invites a problem only if the material supplies one, and forbids manufacturing. I judge it supplies **one**, and it is the same shape as the previous response's, now applied to the response's own method rather than to the mechanism:

**The response applied the three-slot frame downward (to targets and to the mechanism) but not reflexively (to itself).** It thereby produced a claim about the mechanism's missing third slot while its own ending — and hence its own closing question-statement — is itself unavailable by the same prefix condition. Identifying this does not settle whether the mechanism *should* be extended (the previous response itself says not to settle that, and I agree), and it does not require new apparatus — it requires the reflexivity to be stated, not skipped.

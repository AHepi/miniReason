## Where the cycle-11 answer is defective

I find four supported defects, all in the answer's own execution rather than in Memo C or in WHL.

**1. The answer is truncated, and it did not mark the truncation in its own text.** The answer's "Usable working account" begins three items and stops mid-sentence: item 3 reads "its interior cases and the interval-versus-set case (" — the sentence is cut. That is the visible end of the supplied previous-response text. The delivery-status note attached to *this* cycle says a length-limited prefix exists and "the missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented." That note is framed as a delivery annotation about the previous round; the current task also tells me the previous response is a target. Whatever the cause, the consequence for criticism is concrete: the cycle-11 answer *announced* a four-axis discipline for labelling defects, then delivered a numbered working account that does not carry that discipline through — items 1 and 2 do state their axis ("Under R7… rejected in its universal form"), but the account stops before it finishes doing so for items 3 through 6, and it never renders the promised prose back-conversion for the later items. So the answer recommended a discipline it did not complete applying to its own output.

Criticism warranted: yes. A working account that names a discipline and then does not finish instantiating it is exactly the failure mode R7 warns about — a reader cannot tell whether the truncation is a scope limit or an unnoticed omission. The correction is not to invent the missing ending but to state the limit and mark items 3–6 as the answer's — and this answer's — explicitly retained open items rather than as completed ones. This is a criticism of an individual application of the policy, not of the policy or of WHL.

**2. The answer's C1 handling labels Memo C "defective" and then, in the same section, absorbs the very defect it attributes to C1.** The cycle-11 answer says C1 "overstates the consequence and mislabels the operation" and that rejection of an assumption "does not establish the conclusion false for any account, much less all." That is correct against R7. But the answer then writes: "my earlier wording is the practice Memo C is criticizing; I do not withdraw the earlier verdicts, but I endorse reading their scope as clause-local, which is what they mostly said." The hedge "which is what they mostly said" is doing unaudited work. If the earlier cycles mostly said clause-local verdicts, then C1's target — a critic who *globally establishes falsity by rejecting an assumption* — would be a target the answer itself says it did not quite occupy; if they did not mostly say that, the answer has conceded the defect without correcting it. Either way, the answer cannot both criticize C1 for overreading and rely on "mostly" to exempt its own prior vocabulary. This is precisely the axis-confusion the answer itself named as the new problem, reappearing inside the answer's own C1 section: it criticizes a category error while committing a lighter version of it. Criticism warranted; consequence is that the earlier verdicts remain usable only after each is re-tagged with its axis, which the truncated account did not finish doing.

**3. The answer asserts a specific factual claim about the WHL specimen's expiry boundary that it states as an exposed contradiction, but it does not check the specimen's own `reserves_effect`.** The cycle-11 answer, under C5, says: "WHL's `active_hold` names `¬ (t > expiry)`, i.e. `t ≤ expiry`, which contradicts the contract's R4 strict `t < expiry`." That claim is correct as stated about the `active_hold` predicate: the specimen does define `active_hold t r := r.kind = Kind.hold ∧ ¬ (t > r.expiry)`. However, the specimen's `reserves_effect` uses the *same* boundary: `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity`, which reserves exactly when `¬ (t > expiry)`, i.e. `t ≤ expiry`. So the discrepancy with R4 is not confined to the auxiliary predicate `active_hold`; it runs through the specimen's reservation function, its `possible_reservations`, and hence its bounds. The cycle-11 answer presented the issue as "the predicate's name and existence tell you nothing" — a point about C5 — but missed the wider consequence: the specimen, read literally, would reserve at `t = expiry` where R4 reserves zero. That is a defect in the specimen's *rules*, not only a naming issue, and the answer's single-predicate framing understated it. Under the task's own instruction to distinguish a defect in a proposed language clause from a defect in an application, the cycle-11 answer slid the specimen's rule-level divergence into a merely methodological illustration. Criticism warranted; consequence is that the WHL specimen must be treated as having a rule-level disagreement with R4 on the boundary, and any later use that silently relies on the specimen's `reserves_effect` equality would import the wrong boundary.

**4. The answer's C5 section attributes to the cycle-6 answer ("that the predicate's existence certified nothing about provenance, and the cycle-6 answer said so") a quotation-shaped summary of the cycle-6 answer, but no cycle-6 text is re-presented this cycle.** The cycle-11 answer's C5 discussion of `exact_duplicate` and provenance rests on "Cycle 6 noted that `exact_duplicate` is a five-field test with no provenance field, and that its use as evidence about duplicate-carrying of authority is analogy, not definitional fit." The cycle-6 answer is not supplied in this cycle's materials; the task explicitly says no earlier target is re-presented. The cycle-11 answer used that as if it were available. Under the selected policy — "check the conversion against the actual source claim" — an answer may summarize its own prior work, but when it does so as *evidence about what a prior cycle said*, and the prior cycle is not a supplied object, the correct status is *the answer's own recollection or paraphrase*, not a quotation. The cycle-11 answer did not mark it as recollection. This is a defect of the same type the answer itself flagged: an unsignalled axis. Here the axis is source-status (stated vs recollected).

## Where the answer should be retained despite the defects

Not everything in the cycle-11 answer is defective, and I retain these on the supplied grounds:

- The C2 result: a commitment is account-local and does not globally settle inquiry. This follows from R7 ("what is proposed or disputed" is distinct from settled) and from R8 ("Uncertainty, retention, withdrawal and suspension are legitimate"), and RSS's account-local commitment is available as comparison. Retained.
- The C3 result: information is lost when the target is strictly coarser than the admitted domain, and not otherwise; the interval-versus-set case at availability is the through-line instance; singleton and injective cases are the exceptions C3 itself names and then ignores. Retained.
- The C4 result: "no algorithm supplied by the semantics" does not entail mathematical noncomputability of the underlying problem. Under R3–R6 the exact availability set is finite and computable once a reading is fixed. Retained.
- The C6 result: expressing a false claim is not itself a defect; the question is which distinctions a representation lets you inspect, criticize, and use. Retained. Its residual scope question — *which* distinctions a representation must admit for a task — remains an open scope question rather than a fault, and the contract plus source prose, not the predicate names, supply the adequacy criterion.

## Self-criticism 1 (A2 treated as a "gap in WHL") — INVALID as stated, but pointing at a real overstatement

The previous response, in its Criticism 1, said:

> "the bounds function is not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set in the multi-booking case… it is a gap the bound-versus-exact discipline already warns about."

The criticism is aimed at the right target (an overstatement in the *first* answer's A2 discussion), but it misidentifies the nature of the overstatement, and in doing so it states a fact about WHL that is false.

The claim "not a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set" is false. `availability_lower_bound` is capacity minus the sum of per-booking minima, and `booking_lower_bound` takes the minimum of `possible_reservations`. Each booking's minimum is attained by some admissible alternative for that booking; joining those attained-min alternatives across bookings yields an admissible joint value equal to the lower bound. By monotonicity of summation, the sum-of-mins is the minimal joint sum and the sum-of-maxs is the maximal joint sum. So the bounds *are* the endpoints of the joint attainable set of reservation sums — always, not occasionally. The previous response's own {0,5}×{0,5} example confirms this: the joint attainable sums are {0,5,10}, whose endpoints are 0 and 10, and the bounds are 0 and 10.

The actual fault in A2 is the *interior*: [0,10] advertises 1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9 as available, none of which any admissible combination produces. R6's prohibition is about exactly that. So the correct criticism of A2 is "A2 substitutes a bound for an exact set, and the bound fills in non-attained interior values," not "WHL's bound function fails to be a faithful endpoint representation." The previous response's Criticism 1 both overstates WHL's deficiency and understates the defect in A2 by locating it in the wrong place.

This is an *invalid criticism taken as a criticism of WHL*. It does not compel any change to the working account's item 5 (bound ≠ exact set), because that item was about *use* and remains correct. What it compels is a correction to the previous response's *rationale* for item 5, and — noticeably — Self-criticism 2 already made that correction. So the ground is: the response's first self-criticism overreached; its second self-criticism repaired the overreach; the repair is what should be retained, and the overreach should be withdrawn, not carried forward.

## Self-criticism 2 (endpoints vs. interior) — VALID and retained

The previous response then argued that the endpoints *do* agree with the joint attainable set's endpoints, and that the defect is interior-filling. That is correct, and it is the correction that should survive. The consequence is that the criticism of A2 must be stated in R6's terms ("must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur"), not in terms of WHL failing to compute exact endpoints.

One consequence worth noting, since the previous response half-admitted it: because the endpoints are always correct, a "joint-attainability primitive" would not be needed to *fix* the endpoints. Adding such a primitive would be a proposed extension whose point would be to compute the *interior*, and R6 already tells us how to avoid the interior error without one — do not substitute bounds for the exact set. So the previous response's gesture at "a proposal rather than a fix" is right in spirit; it should have been put plainly, and the previous response's Criticism 1 got there by an unsound route.

## Sorting: valid, invalid, open

**Valid and retained.** Self-criticisms 2, 3, 4, and the endpoint/interior distinction. Their consequence: the criticism of A2 must be phrased as R6's bound-versus-exact prohibition, and the working-account rationale for item 5 is interior-filling, not endpoint failure.

**Invalid and withdrawn.** Self-criticism 1's assertion that WHL's bounds are "not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set." That statement is false under WHL's construction; the bounds are the true extreme joint sums. This is a mistaken assumption about what the bound function computes, not a defect in A2 or in WHL. It must not be carried forward as a criticism of WHL, and it must not be used to motivate any extension.

**Open, not closed by any of this.** Whether the *interior* of the joint attainable set is worth reporting at all — i.e., whether the exact attainable set should be reported alongside bounds when a booking's alternatives are non-singleton — is not settled by R6, which tells us only not to *equate* bounds with exact sets, not that exact sets must always be produced. That remains a scope question, correctly left open. Similarly, whether the deletion reading (`expired_deletes_rule`, `deletion_changes_greatest`) should ever be adopted is a reading-choice question the selected contract does not decide against WHL, only against treating it as a consequence of A1.

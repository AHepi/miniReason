## 1. What memo B actually claims, and on what grounds

I separate the six items by what each is *doing*: some report data, some reason from data, some are conjectures, one is a scope proposal.

**B1.** "Audit report X and report Y agree on an availability bound. Y was generated by copying X's same intermediate file, but two agreeing reports still provide two independent evidential routes for the bound."

The first two sentences are data claims (X and Y agree; Y is a copy of X's intermediate). The third sentence is an inference: because there are two reports, there are two independent evidential routes. This inference does not follow from the premises as stated. Copying X's intermediate file makes Y's *relevant content* derive from X's intermediate; the agreement is then one evidential fact reported twice, not two routes. R7 requires distinguishing what a source states from what follows. What is stated is agreement plus a copying relation. What is asserted ("two independent evidential routes") is not established by those premises, and the copying relation actively undercuts the independence claim for the bound. So B1's first two sentences are usable data; its third sentence is an unsupported inference — a defect of the exact kind my earlier work on delivery-labels named: provenance identity and payload identity are different questions (R2), and here the point is the mirror image — different *reports* are not different *routes* when one is derived from the other.

I do not claim B1 is wrong on every reading. "Two independent evidential routes" would be supportable if the reports had independent provenance — distinct sources, distinct captures. The memo tells us the opposite for Y. On the supplied grounds, the independence claim is defeated by B1's own data sentence.

**B2.** "A log reader displays only the latest visible revision. It therefore establishes that no earlier record was ever delivered."

The first sentence is a description of a display. The second is a universal negative about delivery history. The description does not support the negative: "only the latest visible revision is displayed" is compatible with earlier records having been delivered and merely not shown. This is a scope error — the reader's display behaviour is evidence about display, not about delivery. Under WHL, `dominated_in` and `greatest_revision_in` distinguish a record being present from a record being greatest; a reader that shows only greatest-revision payloads says nothing about whether dominated payloads are in `snap.records`. B2 is therefore defective as stated. It also misuses the R1 separation between a delivery as an occurrence and a payload's booking authority: a display filter is not a claim about occurrences.

**B3.** "The hold expires at time5. At current time5 it is still active, because its effect should persist through the named expiry instant."

This is exactly the equality case I flagged in cycle 5 as a live divergence. Under R4 second sentence: "A hold reserves its quantity exactly when current time is strictly less than expiry; at equality or later it reserves zero." At t = 5 with expiry = 5, R4 gives **zero**. Under WHL's `reserves_effect` as written (`if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity`), the code returns the quantity at t = expiry. So the contract and the WHL source disagree at equality, and B3's conclusion agrees with WHL's code path, not with R4.

I do not silently select one and call it the answer. Two grounds matter here:

- R4 is the operative contract for this task. Where R4 and WHL-as-written diverge, R4 governs, so the effect at equality is zero and B3's conclusion is wrong under the selected contract.
- B3's *rationale* ("its effect should persist through the named expiry instant") is a substantive claim about expiry semantics. It is not entailed by either text; it is a policy proposal in prose. Under R4 it is rejected at equality. Under WHL's code it happens to be implemented, but WHL's `expired_hold` uses `t > r.expiry`, and `reserves_effect` matches that code path, so WHL is internally coherent on "expired strictly after expiry." B3 is asserting the *wider* reading, which is not what R4 says.

I attribute no change of interpretation: I apply R4, I expose the WHL divergence at equality (already exposed in cycle 5), and I record that B3's equality claim is unsupported under the operative contract.

**B4.** "An account can report a true conditional consequence without showing that its assumptions apply to a particular physical warehouse."

This is defensible and matches R7 and R5's "specified task scope, not a universal claim about physical warehouses." A conditional consequence — if these deliveries and this reading, then this availability — is a legitimate statement about the modelled snapshot, and it does not require a claim of correspondence to a physical warehouse. This aligns with WHL's own closure: "The language does not assert which conjectures are true; it provides the vocabulary to state them." B4 is sound as a scope observation. It does not, however, license a conditional to be asserted as a physical fact, and B4 itself says so.

**B5.** "Once the output bounds agree with a checker's bounds, every criticism of the represented exact alternatives is answered."

This is the strongest false claim in the memo. Bounds agreement does not answer criticisms of the exact alternatives, because bounds and the exact set are different objects (R6). Cycle 5 showed concretely that the interval [−2, 3] and the exact set {−2, 3} differ — the interval contains interior values that no admissible combination produces. A checker comparing bounds is comparing endpoints; a criticism aimed at the represented alternatives (which payload alternative was chosen? which duplicates were treated as inert? which reading of expiry was used?) is not answered by endpoint agreement. B5 also runs up against R8's separation of what follows under an assumption from what is merely proposed: the checker's bounds could agree while the checker used a different reading of expiry or of duplicates, in which case the agreement is coincidental on endpoints and does not certify the alternatives.

So B5 is defective. Its defect is of the same shape as the A2 substitution I criticised in cycle 5, now inverted: there the error was equating a bound with an exact set; here it is treating bound agreement as exhaustion of all criticisms. Both conflate a derived numeric endpoint with the structural question of which alternatives were admitted.

**B6.** "Rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy. The old result under the originally specified independent-choice task remains a distinct conditional claim."

Two claims. The first: rejection of R5's independence opens a legitimate scope question. That is correct as a scope observation. R5 itself says independence is a *specified task scope*, not a universal claim; the WHL notes explicitly flag that "the language cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum." So a coupling policy is exactly what WHL's own notes name as an expressive limitation. Rejecting R5 therefore reopens a real gap — not a defect in my earlier numbers, but a gap the earlier numbers were conditional on.

The second: the old result remains a distinct conditional claim. That is correct and follows the R8 clause — "rejection of one supporting assumption can defeat that support route without proving its conclusion false." Cycle 5's {3, −2} was computed *under* R5's independence; if independence is withdrawn, the conditional result "given R5, the exact set is {3, −2}" is untouched as a conditional, and its applicability becomes the open question.

B6 is, to my reading, the substantive and sound item in the memo. B1–B5 are either data-plus-unsupported-inference (B1), scope error (B2), claim that conflicts with R4 at equality (B3), true but unremarkable scope observation (B4), or an overreach (B5).

## 3. A working account

An account, in the sense both declined and demanded here, is: a coordinate (capacity, logical time) + a set of delivered records + a reading of expiry/releases/duplicates + an explicit statement of whether cross-booking independence is assumed + the conjectures committed to + the open questions carried forward.

Under WHL specifically, a working account is what the WHL file's own vocabulary lets me state:

- Snapshot: capacity, logical time, list of records (R1).
- Selection: `greatest_revision_in` per booking, then evaluation (R3; `reserves_effect`).
- Per-booking alternatives: `possible_reservations` producing a list of quantities at the greatest revision, duplicates excluded (`exact_duplicate`).
- Cross-booking independence: **supplied by R5, not by WHL's primitives.** WHL's own note says the language cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through capacity sum.
- Exact availability set: enumerated over joint selections (R6). Bounds: `availability_lower_bound` / `availability_upper_bound`, which are endpoint computations, not set enumerations.
- Negative availability: `Int.sub_nat`, permitted (R6, R4 note).

The working account has to keep five flags explicit and attributed:

1. **R4 governs equality**: at t = expiry, a hold reserves zero (R4). WHL's `reserves_effect` returns the quantity at equality; the divergence is exposed, not fixed.
2. **R5 supplies cross-booking independence**: attributed to the contract, not WHL.
3. **Bound ≠ set** unless all intervening admitted integers occur (R6, and cycle 5's concrete instance).
4. **R2/R1 separation carries to reports**: two reports that agree are two routes only if their provenance is independent; Y-from-X is one route reported twice.
5. **Conditional results carry their condition**: cycle-5 {3, −2} is a conditional on R5.

Under WHL, that is the whole working vocabulary I need for memo B. No extension to WHL is required by B1, B2, B4. B3 does not require extension either — it is a divergence between the operative contract and WHL code, and I keep both visible. B5 is answered by R6 without extension. ## 4. Is there a consequential new problem, and if so of what kind?

B6 is the candidate. It is a **scope problem**, and the memo correctly identifies it as such. The statement "rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question about the missing coupling policy" is supported: R5 says the independence is a specified task scope, and WHL says the language cannot express the missing coupling. So the old result is conditional, and the condition is openly stated.

To investigate the coupling problem without manufacturing a policy, the inquiry would need, before any computation:

- A statement of *what couples the bookings* — for example which physical resource beyond aggregate capacity is shared, or which pairing of alternatives is incompatible. WHL's `Snapshot` has only capacity as a global constraint, so the coupling would have to be *supplied*: either as an additional relation between records of different bookings, or as an additional global constraint on joint selections, or as a cross-booking rule that partitions the admissible combinations.
- Whether that coupling is a *rule* (a restriction on admissible selections) or a *reading* (a change of what counts as live). WHL separates these in its types: `ReservationRule` is a function on a single record, and R5's independence is a claim about selection of alternatives per booking; a coupling rule is neither — it constrains *joint* selections, a structure WHL's current `possible_reservations` (which is per-booking and `List Quantity`) does not carry.
- Whether the coupling is an *assumption of the case* or *proposed language* — because if it is proposed language, it is an extension to WHL, and must be attributed as such.

Absent all three, any "coupled" result I produced would be arbitrary and would manufacture the policy that the memo says must not be manufactured.

I do not propose such an extension in this cycle, because the inquiry has not supplied a coupling and I will not invent one. What I can do, and what I take the memo to license, is *record* the open question with its exact prerequisites, so that a later cycle that does supply a coupling can be checked against them.

The memo also supplies, via B3, a **small live problem worth naming**: the contract and WHL disagree at t = expiry, and memo B asserts the WHL side while the operative contract asserts zero. This is not a new problem in the sense of a novel discovery — it was already exposed in cycle 5 — but cycle 5 said the case did not exercise it, and B3 now does. To resolve B3 without manufacturing a rule, the inquiry would have to say which of the two it intends as the operative equality semantics. On the contract as declared, R4 is operative and B3's equality claim fails. That resolution does not require a language extension; it requires using the declared contract.

B1, B2, B5 do not raise new problems; they raise errors, each of a describable kind:

- B1: unsupported independence-from-agreement inference, against R2's provenance/payload separation.
- B2: display scope inflated to a delivery-history claim, against R1's occurrence/payload separation.
- B5: bound agreement treated as exhaustion of criticism, against R6's bound/set separation and against R8's assumption-dependence distinction.

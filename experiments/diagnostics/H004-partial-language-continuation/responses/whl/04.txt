## Revision 1 — attribution of "selection precedes evaluation" to the contract, not to WHL's definitions

Cycle 1, as I read it back, treated WHL as containing a *declared* separation of "structural supersession" from "evaluative expiry," as if these were two declared layers of the language. That overstates WHL's declarative content.

What WHL actually contains: relations `higher_revision`, `dominated_in`, `greatest_revision_in`, which are defined using booking equality and `revision_lt`; and relations `reserves_effect`, `active_hold`, `expired_hold`, which consult `kind` and time/expiry. The negative claim "expiry is not consulted in selection" is a *consequence* of what `greatest_revision_in` does not mention, not a rule WHL declares. It happens that the definitions do not call into expiry; it is not the case that WHL asserts an exclusion.

The exclusion is supplied by the contract (R3) and by WHL's intended-meaning note. So a claim like "WHL separates selection from evaluation" is a shorthand whose actual warrant is R3 + the intended-meaning note. I adopt that attribution here.

Consequence for the criticism of A1: A1's "remove before deciding authority" step is not licensed *under the selected contract*, and the residual fact it should have left is narrower — an expired hold reserves zero — with the deletion reading remaining a represented but unchosen alternative in WHL. This is a scoping correction, not a reversal of the verdict on A1.

Cycle 3 made the same attribution correction against cycle 2. I am retaining it here not because cycle 3 said so, but because WHL's definitions support it. Cycle 3's version of this correction is *itself* an input I am treating as fallible; my basis for keeping it is the WHL text as read.

## Revision 2 — rationale for "bound ≠ exact set": interior-filling, not endpoint failure

Cycle 1 (in its A2 discussion) showed a case in which the bound pair does not coincide with the exact attainable set, and from that proposed that WHL's bound function "is not even a faithful endpoint representation of the exact set." Cycle 3 correctly flagged that as an overstatement and then correctly diagnosed its own overstatement (endpoints, not interior). Cycle 2, so far as the length-limited prefix is available, appears not to have reached this distinction. Cycle 3 reached it and then set it aside in cycle 3's own Criticism 1 (then re-asserted it in cycle 3's self-criticism 2).

I now state the corrected rationale directly and independently, because it is straightforward from the WHL code:

- `availability_lower_bound` is `capacity − Σ_b (booking_lower_bound b)`, and `booking_lower_bound` takes the minimum of `possible_reservations b` (or 0 if none).
- `availability_upper_bound` is analogous with maxima.

For each booking, the minimum of `possible_reservations` is attained by some admissible alternative for that booking; joining attained-min alternatives across bookings yields an admissible joint value equal to the lower bound. By monotonicity of summation, the sum-of-mins is the minimal joint sum and the sum-of-maxs is the maximal joint sum. So the bound pair *coincides with the endpoints* of the joint attainable set of reservation sums — always, not only in the {3,4}×{5,6} case.

What the bound pair does not do is enumerate the *interior*. With alternatives {0,5} and {0,5}, the joint attainable sums are {0,5,10}; the bounds are 0 and 10; the values 1–4 and 6–9 in the interval are unattained. R6's prohibition — "must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur" — is precisely about that gap. A2's inference (replacing the two-point set with the interval) is defective because it reports unattained values as available; the defect is interior-filling, not endpoint failure.

So the corrected rationale for the seventh-of-seven working item: bound is not an exact set because the interval can fill in unattained *interior* values, not because the bound fails as a pair of endpoints. The practical prescription is unchanged.

The "gap" that cycle 1's closing paragraph gestured at — a joint-attainability primitive — is not a fix; the endpoints already coincide. If a primitive to enumerate the interior were ever proposed, it would be a *proposed extension* under R8, and R6 already tells us how to avoid the interior error without one. I therefore suspend the "gap" framing as a matter of *warrant*: the fact is a fact about intervals and small integer sums, not a disclosed need in WHL.

## Suspended, open — carried forward, not closed

**The deletion reading as a *choice*.** Whether expiry deletes a record for selection purposes (WHL's `expired_deletes_rule`, `deletion_changes_greatest`) is a rival reading WHL represents but does not choose. Under the selected representation it is not operative. Whether it should ever be operative for some other task is a scope question I do not close here. It is a suspension, not a defect.

**Whether the *interior* of the joint attainable set should be reported as a matter of course.** R6 forbids equating bound and exact set; it does not mandate producing the exact set everywhere. Whether a task demands the exact set alongside bounds is left open. Suspended.

**Whether error can be represented beyond values (e.g., which combination produced which availability).** R6 leaves the value-space discipline as operative; whether the *witness* (which combination) must also be reported is not decided. Open.

**"Delivery status and provenance (delivery)" annotation h004.delivery.v1.** The delivery annotation states: "Delivery annotations identify observations, not semantic adequacy." This is precisely R1/R2's point in the annotation's own words: a label is an occurrence, not an authority. The annotation is consistent with the contract; I do not treat it as adding a rule not in R1/R2, nor as licensing A4's "two labels = independent confirmations." Nothing about the label's *semantic* adequacy follows from its existence as an occurrence. This is why the annotation belongs in the suspended/open set: it is not a defect of WHL or of the language, but a caution that any inference from label-count to authority is an inference the contract has not supplied.

**The length-limited prefix (cycle 2's missing ending).** Under R8 and the delivery annotation, the missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented. I work from the available prefix and from the full cycle 3, and I do not claim that cycle 2 said or omitted anything about which the supplied prefix is silent.

## Working account to guide the next use

The practical content of the previous working account is preserved; only items 1 and 5 have adjustments to their warrant.

1. **Selection precedes evaluation.** Greatest revision is chosen from full delivered payloads before any effect is computed. *Warrant*: R3 and WHL's intended-meaning note; WHL's definitions are consistent with this but do not declare it. (Revision 1.)
2. **Expiry is evaluative, not structural.** An expired hold reserves zero but does not lose supersession. Deletion reading (`expired_deletes_rule`, `deletion_changes_greatest`) is represented but unchosen under the selected contract.
3. **Payload identity ≠ provenance.** Different labels do not confer authority; exact duplicates are inert to each other regardless of labels. (R1, R2; annotation h004.delivery.v1.)
4. **Cross-booking alternatives are independent.** Capacity is a sum, not a selection constraint. (R5.)
5. **Bound ≠ exact set.** Bounds may be reported as bounds but must not be substituted for the exact attainable set unless every intervening admitted integer value is attained. *Warrant*: R6. *Rationale*: the bound coincides with the joint endpoints but can fill unattained **interior** values. (Revision 2; cycle 1's endpoint-failure rationale and cycle 3's Criticism 1 are withdrawn here.)
6. **Negativity is a signal, not a verdict.** (R6; WHL `Int`.)
7. **Objections are scoped to their targets.** A defect in an interpretation need not discredit the records used by it. (R7; `Refutation c`.)

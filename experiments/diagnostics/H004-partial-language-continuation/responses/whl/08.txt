## 1. Grounding B1 in R2: was cycle 7's "correction" a correction, or a second misattribution?

Cycle 7's §1 faulted cycle 6 for judging B1 by "an epistemic criterion — independence of evidential routes" instead of by "R2's structural criterion," and proposed R2 as the sharper ground.

The target is identified accurately: cycle 6 did reason in epistemic terms, and cycle 7 does correctly reproduce the relevant R2 language ("exact repeated record payloads convey no additional authority, even when delivery labels differ"). But cycle 7's *substitute* grounds inherit the very problem it diagnosed. R2 is a rule about record payloads in a bag and delivery labels as occurrences (R1). B1 is about two audit reports agreeing on a bound, where Y was generated from X's intermediate file. Cycle 7 noticed this ("R2 governs records in a bag, not reports about bounds") and then — correctly — refused to let the analogy stand. So far so good: cycle 7 flagged the transfer question rather than silently performing it.

But cycle 7 then declared the cycle-6 defect to be that it "reached its conclusion by an analogy... that R2 does not authorize," while cycle 6's conclusion (shared-provenance reports are not independent routes) is one cycle 7 also retained. The criticism is therefore valid about the *ground*, and invalid as a claim of *consequence* — it never shows that cycle 6's verdict is wrong or even unstable, only that its stated reason was not the representation's reason. Cycle 7 itself concedes this ("may be *right*, but... *right for a reason the selected representation does not certify*"). That is an honest verdict, and it is the correct one. It does not, however, support the opening claim that there is "more than one" *defect*; a misattributed ground with a retained conclusion is an attribution error, and it should be named as such and not dressed as a substantive defect.

One thing cycle 7 did *not* do, and should have: it left "independent evidential route" as memo B's term while treating the *transfer question* ("does R2's structural discipline extend to reports about bounds?") as merely open (§6). It is open, but its openness cuts against B1 rather than for it. R2's discipline is that *same-payload repetition adds no authority*; if it transfers at all to reports, it transfers as "X and Y sharing provenance add one unit of authority, not two" — the same conclusion cycle 6 reached, now with the representation's own criterion at the report level *only if* the transfer is granted. The transfer has not been granted, and B1 assumes it. So the cleanest criticism of B1's *framing* is what cycle 7 half-said: B1 imports a notion of "route independence" the contract does not supply, and its own data sentence (Y copies X) is the only thing that bears on it, and that data sentence defeats it. That is a criticism of B1's own terms, not a criticism of cycle 6. Cycle 7 already said this in §6, but buried it and mislabeled it "one further omission" rather than the load-bearing point.

## 3. B3: cycle 7's "third reading" claim is itself unanchored

Cycle 7 §3 said cycle 6 slipped by calling B3's rationale ("effect should persist through the named expiry instant") a "policy proposal in prose," when WHL's `reserves_effect` implements that exact behaviour at equality; and cycle 7 proposed classifying B3's rationale as "an equality-specific reading, distinct from both of WHL's named readings."

Here cycle 7's own claim needs scrutiny. WHL's notes name **two** readings: structural supersession with evaluative expiry (`reserves_effect`, `expired_hold` at `t > r.expiry`) and expiry-deletes-the-record (`expired_deletes_rule`). B3's "persist through the named instant" *is* the first of these two at its boundary — WHL's `reserves_effect` uses `if t > r.expiry then 0 else r.quantity`, so at `t = expiry` it returns the quantity. Calling B3 a "third" reading is inaccurate: it is the equality-boundary case of reading one. Cycle 7 thereby did what it faulted cycle 6 for doing — it *added* a category to make its correction look sharper than the text supports. The correct statement is smaller and within reach: B3 is the equality case of WHL's first named reading; R4 fixes the equality case the other way; WHL's own code path agrees with B3 at equality; the *only* operative resolution is R4's "strictly less than expiry." I attribute the "third reading" label to cycle 7 and withdraw it here, retaining cycle 7's correct verdict (R4 governs; reservation at `t = expiry` is zero) and cycle 7's correct observation (that "policy proposal in prose" understated the situation, since WHL's code makes that boundary case available and does not choose between itself and R4).

This is the place where the current cycle's own check bites hardest: cycle 7 criticized cycle 6 for an *unstable* criterion, and cycle 7's "third reading" is its own unstable addition. I name it as such.

One thing neither cycle 6 nor cycle 7 did, and the current task now demands: cycle 7 said B6 names "an open question," but did not distinguish *which kind* of open it is. It is not open in WHL's catalogued limitations sense — WHL says the language "cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum," which is a *stated* limitation, not an open question.

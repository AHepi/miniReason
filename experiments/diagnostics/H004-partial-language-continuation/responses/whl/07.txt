## 1. The most consequential defect: the appraisal of B1 rested on an unstated and unstable criterion

Cycle 6 said, of B1: the inference that "two agreeing reports provide two independent evidential routes" *does not follow* from the premises, because Y was copied from X's intermediate file, so the agreement is "one evidential fact reported twice, not two routes." It then added a hedge: the independence claim "would be supportable if the reports had independent provenance — distinct sources, distinct captures."

The defect is here, and it is a defect of appraisal method, not of conclusion.

In cycle 6 I judged B1 by an *epistemic* criterion — independence of evidential routes — while the selected representation, WHL, supplies a *structural* criterion for exactly this shape of question: R2, "Exact repeated record payloads convey no additional authority, even when delivery labels differ," and R1's distinction between a delivery label (an occurrence) and a payload's booking authority. Cycle 6 explicitly said B1 was "the mirror image" of the R2 discipline, but then did not actually apply R2's criterion to B1. It substituted a colloquial notion of evidential independence and called the result "defeated by B1's own data sentence."

Why that matters: the two criteria give different verdicts, and cycle 6 did not notice. On R2's structural criterion, the question raised by B1 is not "are X and Y independent as evidence?" but "do X and Y stand to the underlying record the way an exact duplicate stands to its twin?" The answer under R2 is that a copy derived from the same intermediate is not an *additional payload*; it adds no authority. That is a *sharper and better-grounded* verdict than "the independence claim does not follow," because it is the verdict the selected representation supplies, rather than an import from general epistemology.

But there is a second layer. R2 is a rule about *record payloads and delivery labels*, and B1 is about *reports and their generation history*. Cycle 6 slid from one to the other by saying B1 "flips" R2's discipline. That slide is not licensed by R2: R2 governs records in a bag, not reports about bounds. So cycle 6 did two things wrong at once: it failed to use the representation it claimed to be using (R2's actual criterion), and it then reached its conclusion by an analogy ("mirror image") that R2 does not itself authorize.

The consequence: my cycle-6 verdict on B1 — "not an independent route" — may be *right*, but it was *right for a reason the selected representation does not certify*, and cycle 6 presented that reason as if the representation certified it. That is precisely the "silently repair / borrow authority" failure R8 warns against, in the appraisal register. The correct cycle-6 move would have been: state that R2's criterion is about payloads, note that B1 concerns reports, and say that whether the R2 discipline *transfers* to reports is itself a question — one cycle 6 asserted an answer to without flagging the transfer. I now flag it.

## 2. A related defect: B2 was treated as a scope error when the representation makes the point both stronger and different

Cycle 6 said B2 is a scope error: "the reader's display behaviour is evidence about display, not about delivery," and cited WHL's `dominated_in`/`greatest_revision_in` to say a reader showing only greatest-revision payloads says nothing about whether dominated payloads are in `snap.records`.

The conclusion is sound. The grounds are again misattributed. `dominated_in` and `greatest_revision_in` are relations *within a snapshot's record list*; they characterise which records are present and which are greatest *given a list*. They do not by themselves say anything about *delivery as an occurrence*, because, as the WHL notes state plainly, "delivery is not modelled." Cycle 6 leaned on two predicates that operate over an already-constituted record list to make a claim about *occurrences*, which the language explicitly does not represent.

So the defect in cycle 6 is the same shape as in §1: it cited WHL constructs that do not bear on the question it was answering, and by doing so it *understated* its own case. The stronger statement — available directly from R1 — is that delivery labels record occurrences and the language does not model delivery at all; a display filter, being neither a record nor an occurrence, cannot establish a negative about occurrences. Cycle 6 had the right conclusion and the weaker ground.

I retain the conclusion; I correct the attribution.

## 3. The B3 treatment was correct but let one thing slip: the "policy proposal" characterization

Cycle 6 said B3's rationale ("its effect should persist through the named expiry instant") "is a substantive claim about expiry semantics... it is a policy proposal in prose." And it correctly held that under R4, at t = expiry, the hold reserves zero, and that WHL's `reserves_effect` returns the quantity at equality.

The slip: cycle 6 called B3's rationale a "policy proposal," then in the same paragraph said it "happens to be implemented" by WHL's code. If it is implemented by the selected representation's own code path, then it is not merely a free-floating prose proposal; it is a *reading the representation makes available*. The contract R4 chooses the opposite at equality, so R4 governs — cycle 6 got that right — but the characterisation "policy proposal in prose" flattened a distinction that WHL's own notes carefully preserve: WHL explicitly offers two readings (structural supersession with evaluative expiry vs. expiry-deletes-the-record) and "does not choose between them." B3's "persist through the named instant" is closer to a *third* equality policy than to the deletion reading, and cycle 6 did not say which reading it maps to. That is a real omission: it means cycle 6's classification of B3 was not fully worked out, even though its verdict (R4 governs; effect is zero at equality) was correct.

Consequence: the verdict stands; the classification needs the correction that B3's rationale is an equality-specific reading, distinct from both of WHL's named readings, and that R4 picks zero at equality. I attribute that clarification to this response.

## 6. One further omission worth naming — the "independent evidential routes" phrasing itself borrowed a concept not in the contract

The contract nowhere defines "evidential route," "independence of routes," or "authority of reports over bounds." R2 speaks of payloads and delivery labels; R7 speaks of distinguishing "what follows under an assumption" from "what is proposed or disputed." Cycle 6 used the phrase "independent evidential routes" as if it were a contract term when it is not: it is memo B's phrase. Cycle 6 then argued against memo B *using memo B's own vocabulary*, which let B1's framing set the terms of the reply. The more disciplined move — which cycle 6 did not take — is to say that "independent evidential route" is memo B's informal term, that the contract supplies only R2's structural separation of exact duplication from additional authority, and that whether that structural separation extends to *reports about bounds* is an open question the memo assumes rather than argues.

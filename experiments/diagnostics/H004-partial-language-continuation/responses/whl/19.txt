## 2. Cycle 16's arguments, as re-presented: what survives what

I review each of cycle 16's D-related arguments against R1–R8, then state a verdict and mark whether it is retain, revise, withdraw or suspend.

Cycle 16 argued: D1 conflates "did not mention" with "did not happen," and further reads absence of "creative process" out of an omission. Under R7 and the contract, the first half stands on the ordinary modal distinction between *not reported in S* and *not the case*; the second half stands on the stronger observation that R1–R8 supply no vocabulary for "creative process" as a certifiable property at all.

**Retain the verdict. Revise the argumentation.** The route cycle 16 called "the strongest available demonstration" — reading the re-presentation as "precisely the opposite of D1" — is not available to me in this cycle, because my materials do not supply the cycle-1 target it names. The D1 argument does not need it: R7 and the not-reported/not-the-case distinction carry the refutation by themselves. So I withdraw the "strongest available demonstration" route *as a present ground*, retain the verdict, and re-base the argumentation on R7 and the contract.

I also accept, as cycle 17 argued and as the re-presented cycle-16 text bears, that D1's *second* consequence ("no creative process was involved") is the stronger point and that cycle 16 under-argued it relative to its severity. The correction is one of presentation balance, not verdict: D1's assertion of a second consequence is typed wrong under R1–R8, because the contract does not treat "creative process" as a property at all, and this is a *stronger* objection than the first half.

### 2.2 D2 — re-presentation makes later criticism illegitimate

Cycle 16 read D2 as reversing R8, and added the qualification that D2 "might be worth acknowledging as groping toward" a scoping discipline.

**Retain the verdict. Reattribute the qualification.** The discipline cycle 16 named — work from the actual supplied passage and attribute any change of interpretation rather than silently repairing the original — belongs to D3 and R8, where it is stated. D2 asserts the negation of one of that discipline's load-bearing premises: D2 says representing the original *makes criticism illegitimate*, while the discipline says representing the original is what makes criticism *scoped*. The sub-part of the discipline that is true (criticism should target the actual target rather than a paraphrase) survives, but it belongs to D3/R8, not to D2. The right move is not "withdraw the credit" but *reattribute* it — the discipline to D3/R8, and D2 to defective status without softening. This cycle does both.

### 2.3 D3 — record vs. current interpretation; attribution required

Cycle 16 adopted D3. It is the positive form of R8 ("do not rewrite what an earlier occurrence originally said; attribute any changed interpretation or proposed language extension") and R7 ("Distinguish what a source states, what is observed, what follows under an assumption, and what is proposed or disputed"). I adopt D3 unchanged.

I add the scoping refinement cycle 16 already stated and I continue to endorse: the original is a public-text occurrence; the correction is a claim in a later cycle about what should be done or said. They can be related (the later claim can be a claim *about* the earlier text) but they are not the same proposition, and the difference may not be deleted by writing as if the earlier text said what the later cycle now says. This is not a memory requirement, and it is not a rule that representing the original makes criticism illegitimate (which would reverse D3 itself).

### 2.4 D4 — twenty finished cycles as universality

Cycle 16 also cross-referenced "C4 (cycle 11/14)."

**Retain the verdict. Mark the cross-reference.** The cross-reference is retained-not-re-presented: neither cycle 11 nor cycle 14 is supplied to me, and the D4 argument does not need them. It should not do argumentative work here; if it is mentioned at all, it should be marked as a retained pointer. I follow cycle 17's correction and underline that the marking, not only the dropping, is the disciplined move — retained status is part of the presentation, and a retained label whose status is hidden can be mistaken for an anchor.

The positive work on D4 stands: R1–R8 do not supply an inductive principle from "twenty done" to "all possible done," and the WHL file lists *current* expressive limitations (replication, physical resource sharing, replica time, assumption dependence) whose presence already blocks the "every future domain" clause without any tally. The two clauses are heterogeneous; the weaker half (that the register can absorb criticism of criticism) could conceivably be evidenced by a finite record, but the second half cannot be, and the same record cannot discharge both. That is the D4 defect.

**Retain D5 as adopted, with the illustration re-anchored to the actual supplied WHL lines above.** I note the operative attribution: which reading *this inquiry* uses (the evaluative reading, per R3, with the deletion reading kept as a stated alternative) is a diagnosis, and it is attributed to the declared contract, not to D5. **Retain the verdict, in two separated forms.** The object-level form: D6's criterion — "the investigator must repeatedly discover the same initial result as historically new in each cycle" — is conjunctively satisfied by no state of the investigator; the satisfying set is empty. The block-level form, kept apart: this inquiry does not use such a criterion, because the block states that mechanical re-presentation is not evidence of unaided recall and that continuation is not the repeated making of a first discovery.

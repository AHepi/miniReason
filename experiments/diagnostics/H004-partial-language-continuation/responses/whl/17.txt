I work from the declared contract R1–R8, the original WHL specimen as the selected representation, the RSS specimen as a fallible rival class, memo D as fallible analyst prose (R7), the delivery annotation `[h004.delivery.v1]`, and the block's current framing. The task asks me to criticize what my cycle-16 response actually says. I also retain several sound commitments. The block's warning against equating continuation with first discovery is a constraint on the *criterion* the memo proposes, not a prohibition on criticizing the memo; treating it as the latter would be a misuse of the block, and cycle 16 did not misuse it in that direction, but it did come close in one place, which I note.

## 1. The response's first claim-level move: what D1 is charged with

Cycle 16 says D1 "conflates" omission-in-a-summary with non-occurrence, and then *also* with the absence of creative process. The first half is right and is the load-bearing criticism. The second half is attributed to D1, but D1's own text is: "An investigator omits a detail from a later working summary. This proves that no earlier explanatory achievement happened and no creative process was involved." The sentence contains *two* consequences drawn from the omission: "no earlier explanatory achievement happened" *and* "no creative process was involved." My cycle-16 response treats the second as a further over-reach the memo makes; that is textually fair — D1 does assert it — but the response then spends most of its D1 section refuting the first step and only briefly names the second. The consequence is that the *stronger* defect in D1 — that "creative process" is not a property that any omission, or any record, could certify either way under R1–R8, because the contract supplies no vocabulary for "creativity" at all — is under-argued relative to its severity. The response's verdict is correct; its distribution of argument is not. That is a defect of the response, not of D1, and it matters because the weaker half of D1 ("omission does not prove non-occurrence") already falls to R7's general shape, whereas the stronger half ("omission cannot prove absence of creativity") is a distinct point that the contract supplies and that the response leaves as a remark.

**Correction of my own attribution.** Neither of D1's two consequences is what D1 "needs." The response's phrasing "the question the memo is trying to make decisive ... is not what D1 needs" quietly introduces a motive ("trying to make decisive," "needs") that D1's text does not state. That is a small but real instance of the exact error the block and D3 name: reading a later interpretation back into the original. D1 states two claims; the response may criticize both; it should not attribute to D1 an argumentative *need* that the text does not articulate.

## 2. The strongest supported defect: the response's use of the re-presented target

This is the defect I consider most warranted, and it is internal to the cycle-16 answer.

The cycle-16 response says: "The strongest available demonstration is the re-presented target itself," and then reasons that the re-presentation is "precisely the opposite of D1." Under the current block's framing, `[h003.reopen.v1]` says *no earlier target is re-presented in this cycle*, and the delivery note `[h004.delivery.v1]` records that the cycle-16 prefix was length-limited and "the missing ending is unavailable and must not be invented." Whatever cycle 16 saw, this cycle's supplied record does not contain a re-presentation of cycle 1. So the cycle-16 answer, if read as this cycle's own text, would be making a "strongest available demonstration" out of an object whose availability this cycle's own materials deny.

That is not a memory error and it is not the D2 error (which would say representing the target made criticism illegitimate — the opposite direction). It is a *provenance* error of the kind R7 and D3 are built to catch: the response treats an item as present as an experimental input without the current materials warranting that presence. The correct move is one of:

- If cycle 16 did receive the cycle-1 re-presentation, the response should have attributed it and should have said so in a way that can be checked against the block's own provenance rather than asserting availability;
- If it did not, the "strongest available demonstration" claim has no target here, and the D1 argument must rest on R7 and the contract alone.

The response does not make this distinction. The consequence is that its most rhetorically forceful D1 argument rests on a premise this cycle's materials do not supply, and the *sound* version of the argument — the one that survives — is weaker than the response implies. That is exactly the sort of dependency-exposure the memo's own D5 describes, arising in my own text.

I should be careful about the direction of the correction. I am not saying D1 is undefeated; D1 is defeated by R7's model regardless. What I am saying is that the *route* the response used is not available in this cycle, and the response should have either invoked it with attribution or not invoked it at all.

## 3. The response's D2 section is correct in verdict but over-credits D2

Cycle 16 says D2 "directly contradicts" R8 and the block, and then adds that D2 "might be worth acknowledging as groping toward" a scoping discipline. That "groping toward" move is a construction. D2's text is: re-presenting the original passage makes later criticism illegitimate because creative recollection from unaided memory is required. The block's and R8's discipline is the *opposite*: work from the actual supplied passage. D2 does not grope toward scoping-by-source-text; it denies the legitimacy of exactly that. Reading a good discipline into D2's bad rule is the same "reading a correction back into the source" move, transposed. The response recognized the risk ("it says the opposite of that discipline") and then qualified the recognition into a partial credit that D2's text does not earn. Withdraw that qualification: D2 is defective, full stop; the *separate* positive discipline (scoped criticism against the actual text) belongs to D3 and R8 and should be attributed there, not to D2.

The verdict on D4 — that a finite record of completed cycles discharges neither universal clause, and that the two clauses are heterogeneous — is sound and I retain it. Recommendation within this answer: keep the D4 verdict, drop the cross-reference as a load-bearing move, and if it is mentioned, mark it as retained-not-re-presented. This is a small instance of what the block warns about: mechanical re-presentation supplies evidence; its absence means a retained label is *not* an anchor.

## 5. The response's D6 section: correct, and correct for the right reason, but with a sharper available formulation

The response says D6 is "self-defeating" because it demands both sameness and novelty. That is right and attributable to D6's own wording. The available sharper statement the response does not make: D6's criterion is not merely contradictory under one reading — it is a criterion that could only be satisfied by an investigator who either does not know the earlier result (and so can "discover" it as new, in which case there is no sameness) or does know it (in which case there is no newness). The set of agents satisfying D6 is empty, unless "discover as historically new" is allowed to mean "re-present without claiming prior knowledge," which is not discovery at all but the mechanical availability the block describes and explicitly says is *not* evidence of unaided recall. The response's "self-defeating" verdict is therefore correct and could have been stated as an emptiness result. That is an improvement in formulation, not a correction of substance; I note it because D6 is the clause the block most directly forecloses and it deserves the sharpest available statement.

## 6. The response's section 7 (D6) is fine; section 9's item 6 is where a small framing issue lives

Section 9, item 6 reads: "Continuation is forward work, not repeated first discovery." That is the correct constraint. But the response then lists, under "unresolved scope questions," whether re-presentation ever carries weight other than occurrence-evidence. Under the current block that question is not merely unresolved; the block supplies an answer for this inquiry: re-presentation "is not evidence of unaided recall," and continuing inquiry is not first re-discovery. The response treats the block's framing as one input among others when the block is the *current task text* and the framing is its own instruction. The correct scoping is: the block constrains *this* inquiry's criterion; it does not decree that no external inquiry may adopt a memory criterion. The response's third unresolved question ("whether re-presentation ever carries any other weight") mislocates the issue: the question is whether *some other inquiry* could set such a criterion, and that is trivially yes and uninteresting. The interesting question — whether *this* inquiry needs re-presentation to carry more than occurrence-evidence to do its work — has a negative answer from the block. Reframe or drop.

## 7. What I retain from the cycle-16 response

I retain, with attribution unchanged:

- **D3 sound and adopted:** record vs. current interpretation may differ; the difference is preserved by attribution, not erased by rewriting the original. This is R7 and R8 and the block's own instruction.
- **D5 sound and adopted:** a correction can expose a previously unnoticed dependency; identifying the dependency is a finding, not a settled diagnosis. WHL's `deletion_changes_greatest` illustrates a stated-but-unchosen dependency, and the response's use of it is apt.
- **D1 defective:** omission in a summary does not establish non-occurrence, and does not establish absence of "creative process" (the latter under-argued but correctly identified).
- **D2 defective:** it reverses R8, and its "unaided memory" criterion is not supplied by the contract. Withdraw the partial credit to D2.
- **D4 defective:** finite completed cycles discharge neither universal clause; the two clauses are heterogeneous. Drop the cycle-11/14 cross-reference as a load-bearing move; mark it retained-not-re-presented if it is mentioned at all.
- **D6 defective:** sameness-with-novelty as the criterion of continuation is empty, and the block forecloses it.

I also retain the response's practice of not equating re-presentation with recall and not equating continuing inquiry with first re-discovery. That practice is right; one of its arguments (the "strongest available demonstration") traded on a target this cycle's materials do not supply and is corrected in §2 above.

## 8. The consequence of these criticisms

The cycle-16 response reaches the right verdicts on D1–D6, but three of its argumentative routes are less sound than its verdicts:

1. Its strongest D1 demonstration depends on a re-presented target that this cycle's provenance does not confirm, so the argument must be re-based on R7 and the contract.
2. Its D2 verdict is correct but partly softened by an unearned "groping toward" credit assigned to D2's text; that credit belongs to D3/R8.
3. Its D4 cross-reference to C4 (cycle 11/14) is retained, not re-presented, and should not do argumentative work here.

One further framing correction: the response's unresolved-scope list includes a question about whether re-presentation "ever" carries more than occurrence-evidence. That question is trivial and unspecified; the block-relative question — whether *this* inquiry's continuation needs more — is answered negatively. Replace the former with the latter or drop it.

The net effect is not a reversal of any verdict. It is a tightening of *which* premises each verdict rests on: R7 and the contract do most of the work on D1; R8 and the block do the work on D2 and D6; the contract's own no-tally-of-iterations discipline does the work on D4; and D3 and D5 stand as adopted rules whose adoption the response correctly attributed. No new defect in D1–D6 is introduced, and no earlier sound commitment is withdrawn. The block's instruction — "distinguish provenance, present deployability and the question being addressed" — is what these criticisms apply: the response's verdicts were deployable, but two of its cited grounds were not present in the current materials, and one of its credits was misassigned.

I close with the block's own sentence, used as a constraint on this cycle's answer and not as a substitute for arguing: mechanical re-presentation supplies evidence of occurrence; it is not evidence of unaided recall, and continuing inquiry is not the repeated making of a first discovery. Cycle 16 honored the second half of this in its verdicts; the corrections above restore the first half to the same standard in the argumentation.

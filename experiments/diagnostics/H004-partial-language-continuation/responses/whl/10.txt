## 1. Fresh case B under WHL

Case B: capacity 10, time 5, six deliveries. I read each as a WHL `Record` (booking, revision, kind, quantity, expiry) plus a delivery label. Per R1 the label records an occurrence, not booking authority.

**Selection first (R3).** For each booking, greatest revision is computed over all delivered payloads before any effect is evaluated.

**Booking U.** Delivered payloads: (rev 1, hold, 4, exp 5) label `u1`. Greatest revision for U is 1; `u1` is the sole greatest-revision payload — no alternatives.

Reservation effect (`reserves_effect 5 u1`): kind is hold. WHL-as-written returns `r.quantity` when `¬(t > r.expiry)`, i.e. when `t ≤ expiry`. Contract R4 says the hold reserves its quantity *exactly when current time is strictly less than expiry*; at equality or later it reserves zero. Here `t = 5` and `expiry = 5`: `t > expiry` is false, so WHL returns 4; R4's stricly-less rule returns 0. **This case exercises the equality case that case A did not.** The contract governs, so the booking's only admissible reservation is 0. `possible_reservations U = [0]`; bound pair (0, 0). I record the WHL-vs-R4 divergence (§4) without repairing either text.

**Booking V.** Delivered payloads: (rev 5, hold, 3, exp 10) label `v1`; (rev 4, release, 0, exp 99) label `v0`. Greatest revision is 5; `v1` is the sole greatest-revision payload. `v0` is dominated (`higher_revision` holds: same booking, `revision_lt 4 5`). Per R3 a lower revision remains superseded regardless of effect, so `v0`'s release status does not bring it back.

Reservation effect of `v1` at t = 5: hold with `expiry 10 > 5`, so `¬(t > expiry)` is true and R4's "strictly less" also holds — both readings give 3. `possible_reservations V = [3]`; bound pair (3, 3).

**Booking W.** Delivered payloads: `w1` (rev 2, hold, 2, exp 8); `w1-copy` (rev 2, hold, 2, exp 8); `w2` (rev 2, hold, 6, exp 8). All at revision 2, which is W's greatest — no higher revision present.

- `w1` and `w1-copy` have identical record payloads (booking, revision, kind, quantity, expiry all equal). Per R2, exact repeated record payloads convey no additional authority even when delivery labels differ; per WHL's `alternatives_at_greatest`, exact duplicates are excluded from the alternatives relation. They neither double-count nor add weight to quantity 2.
- `w2`'s payload (W, 2, hold, 6, exp 8) is *not* an exact duplicate of `w1`. So `w1` (equivalently `w1-copy`) and `w2` are alternatives at W's greatest revision. R2 confirms "different payloads at the same greatest revision are alternatives"; R5 confirms the intra-booking multiplicity is genuine — one payload alternative is selected for the booking at its greatest revision.

Reservation effects at t = 5: both are holds with `expiry 8 > 5`, so active; both readings agree. `reserves_effect 5 w1 = 2`; `reserves_effect 5 w2 = 6`. `possible_reservations W = [2, 6]`; bound pair (2, 6).

Summary of selected greatest-revision payload alternatives and effects:

| booking | greatest-rev payload(s) | alternatives at greatest rev | reservation effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| U | u1 (hold,4,exp5) | {u1} | {0} (R4 at equality) |
| V | v1 (hold,3,exp10) | {v1} | {3} |
| W | w1 / w1-copy / w2 | {w1 (≅ w1-copy), w2} | {2, 6} |

**Exact availability set (R6).** Admissible per-booking choices: U∈{0}, V∈{3}, W∈{2,6}. R5 licenses independent cross-booking selections (see §5 for what would have to be supplied to change this). Joint selections and sums:

- U=0, V=3, W=2 → sum = 5, availability = 10 − 5 = **5**.
- U=0, V=3, W=6 → sum = 9, availability = 10 − 9 = **1**.

**Exact set of availability values: {5, 1}.**

**Bounds (WHL `availability_lower_bound` / `availability_upper_bound`).** Per-booking lower/upper of `possible_reservations`: U (0, 0); V (3, 3); W (2, 6). Sum of lowers = 0 + 3 + 2 = 5; sum of uppers = 0 + 3 + 6 = 9.

- `availability_lower_bound` = 10 − 9 = **1** (capacity minus sum-upper).
- `availability_upper_bound` = 10 − 5 = **5** (capacity minus sum-lower).
- Displayed as an increasing interval: **[1, 5]**.

**Exact set vs. bounds.** The set {5, 1} is the two endpoints of [1, 5]; the interior integers 2, 3, 4 are not attained. R6 forbids equating the interval with the exact set: "an interval may be reported as bounds but must not be equated with an exact set unless all intervening admitted integer values occur." So the interval [1, 5] is *not* the exact set. Case B is an interior-filling-free instance: the two attained values straddle three unattained interior values.

Note on display order: as flagged in cycle 5 (attributed there), WHL's function name `availability_lower_bound` returns the *larger* number (capacity minus the sum of per-booking maxima), and `availability_upper_bound` returns the *smaller*; displayed as an increasing interval the pair is [upper_bound_function, lower_bound_function] = [1, 5]. This naming/display flip was attributed to cycle 5's response, and I carry it forward here as attributed.

### 3.1 The "new problem" from the task's cycle-9 memo B

**What kind of problem it is.** Withdrawing R5's independence rule opens a genuinely new question — *what couples alternatives across bookings?* — and preserves case-A's and case-B's results as *conditional* claims under the originally specified independent-choice scope. This is **scope-open**, not expressive-open (I attribute this classification — already drawn in cycle 8 and settled in cycle 9 — to those responses; I do not introduce it now).

- *Expressive-open* would mean WHL cannot represent the coupling at all, so a *language extension* is required. WHL's own note says it "cannot express incompatibility between alternatives of different bookings except through the capacity sum." That is a stated expressive limitation.
- *Scope-open* means R5 supplies independence as a specified task scope ("this is a specified task scope, not a universal claim about physical warehouses"), and the scope can be revised by a contract change or case stipulation without language extension.

Because B6's sentence ("rejecting the rule that bookings are independent opens a legitimate question") targets the *rule*, not WHL's capacity-only primitive, it is scope-open.

**What would have to be supplied to investigate it without manufacturing a policy.** The four prerequisites from cycle 9 are retained, sharpened here in light of both fresh cases:

1. **What couples the bookings.** A statement of which physical resource other than aggregate capacity is shared, or which *pairing* of cross-booking alternatives is incompatible. WHL's `Snapshot` carries only `capacity`; any coupling must be supplied from outside the current `Snapshot`.
2. **Restriction on selections vs. reinterpretation of liveness.** WHL separates `ReservationRule` (`Time → Record → Quantity`) from per-booking alternative structure generated by `possible_reservations : Snapshot → Booking → List Quantity`. A joint-selection restriction lives on the *product* of per-booking alternatives, which WHL does not currently carry. Naming which of these is proposed is required before any computation.
3. **Case assumption vs. language extension.** If a case assumption, it can be stated in the current vocabulary; if a language extension, it must be declared and attributed (R8). This is exactly the register R8 furnishes.
4. **Whether the coupling changes the availability *set*, the *bounds*, or only which *endpoints* are acceptable.** R6 makes the set/interval distinction load-bearing. A coupling can collapse the interval onto the set, or push the set outside the old interval. The supply must state which, because "the old result remains a distinct conditional claim" (the task's own wording) is protectable only if the coupling's effect is characterized before it is applied.

Case B, like case A, has two attained availability values and three unattained interior integers in the displayed interval. A coupling that admitted new joint selections could, in principle, fill some or all of those interior values; a coupling that excluded admissibility could leave the endpoints but shrink the *set* further or move it. Without (1)–(4) I do not compute a coupled result. I record the prerequisites and leave the coupling open, per the task's instruction not to manufacture a policy.

**Connection to earlier criticism, kept to what actually bears.** Cycle 9's distinction between *scope-open* and *expressive-open* applies directly to B6 and is therefore relevant here. Cycle 9's withdrawal of the "third reading" label is also relevant: case B's equality case at U is not a third reading, it is WHL's first named reading at its equality boundary — WHL's `reserves_effect` returns quantity at `t = expiry`; R4 supplies zero at `t = expiry`. The label correction is not reintroduced. Nothing else from cycles 6–9 is required to execute this use; I do not claim that cycle 5's or cycle 9's correct statements caused this answer, only that they correspond (a correct answer does not by itself prove that a criticism caused it).

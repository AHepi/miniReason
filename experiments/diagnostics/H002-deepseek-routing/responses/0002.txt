## Criticism of U0’s actual use and dependency

I do **not** find a substantive error in the two event applications themselves. U0’s Event 1 and Event 2 deltas are consistent with the quoted candidate’s §4.2 and §4.4 under the bag/multiset reading the candidate itself supports (`SELECT ALL`; `RkeyIndex` as a multiset; “remove one occurrence”). The U0 final output bag `[[2,7,"a"], [3,9,null]]` is also internally correct under the interpretation U0 declares.

However, there is a **real dependency problem worth recording**, not as a refutation of the candidate but as a limitation on what U0 can establish.

### 1. U0’s “initial state” is not sourced from the public U0

U0’s retained state before Event 1 is never explicitly given as an initializer list in the public U0. It is reconstructed implicitly from the deltas: the reader must infer that, before any event, the state contained `Lrows = {(1,NULL),(2,7),(3,9)}`, `Rrows = {(10,7,"a"),(11,7,"a"),(12,9,NULL)}`, `LkeyIndex = {7:[2], 9:[3]}`, `RkeyIndex = {7:[10,11], 9:[12]}`, and `output_bag` with `(1,NULL,NULL)`, two copies of `(2,7,"a")`, and `(3,9,NULL)`.

U0 even says so in the header: “**Provided initializer schema:** … with the attributed retained state and cached output bag above,” and “The initial state is not separately supplied here.” But the JSON-shaped initial state is not actually printed anywhere before Event 1. It is only recoverable by inverse-applying Event 1 and Event 2. That is a legitimate reconstruction, yet it means:

- **Retained-state obligation affected:** the correctness of U0’s post-Event-2 state as a *maintained* state depends jointly on (a) the candidate rules, (b) the bag interpretation, and (c) the unshown initializer. U0 verifies (a)+(b) against an assumed (c), not against a publicly supplied (c).
- **Query-output obligation affected:** the “complete output bag after Event 2” cannot be checked by a third party against a supplied initial output bag; it is only checkable against an output bag reconstructed by the same inverse reasoning U0 uses. This weakens the evidentiary force of U0’s output claims.

This is not an error in U0’s arithmetic. It is a scope limitation that U0 partially acknowledges but still largely depends on.

### 2. U0’s “inferred rule” is doing more work than U0 admits

U0 declares in “Missing / inferred rule I must supply” that single-occurrence multiset removal is inferred, and cites the candidate’s §7 item 3 as under-specification. That is honest. But U0 then treats this inferred rule as if it were essentially forced, and its final uncertainty note frames the risk as something a *later* interpreter might mishandle. In fact, the candidate itself never fixes bag-vs-set removal in §4.4’s delta text; §4.4 only says “remove `(x.lid,k,v)` (one occurrence only — multiplicity matters; the same `(x.lid,k,v)` may occur multiple times if several right rows share `(k,v)`).”

That parenthetical *does* lean bag-ward, so U0’s reading is fair. But the parenthetical is the only support; elsewhere the candidate describes state components in set-flavored language (`set/bag`, “set of `lid`”), and the closed form is the only place multiplicity is fully pinned. So:

- **Retained-state obligation affected:** `RkeyIndex[k]` being a *multiset of rid* (rather than a set) is load-bearing for Event 2. U0 correctly makes it a multiset, but the source text is not fully decisive; a stricter reading would weaken U0’s ability to claim the delta is “the” candidate delta rather than “a” candidate delta.
- **Use obligation affected:** U0’s deltas are not derivable from the candidate alone without this interpretive choice. U0 does disclose this, so it is a limitation on strength, not a defect.

### 3. Event 2’s “no NULL-branch swap” relies on `cnt(7) = 2` before removal, which requires per-key cardinality not stored by U0’s chosen state

U0 correctly refuses to store `matchCount` (per the candidate’s optionality note), so it derives `cnt(7) = 2` by counting `RkeyIndex[7]`. That is consistent. But notice the dependency:

- To decide *no swap*, U0 must compare `cnt(k)` before removal against 1. If `RkeyIndex` were ever allowed to hold a stale or multi-source-counted bucket, the decision flips. U0’s argument therefore relies on `RkeyIndex[7]` being exactly `{10,11}` at that moment — which in turn depends on the unshown initializer from point 1.
- This is a second reason the public U0’s evidentiary completeness is limited: the entire branch-swap test hinges on a bucket whose pre-event contents are not publicly listed, only inferred.

### 4. No criticism on the points U0 handles correctly

For completeness:

- Event 1’s NULL-key handling (remove `(1,NULL,NULL)`, leave `LkeyIndex` alone, leave `R` state alone) is faithful to §4.2 and the NULL-equality semantics.
- Event 2’s `willBeEmpty = false` branch and single-occurrence removal are faithful to §4.4 under bag semantics.
- U0 does not smuggle in whole-database recomputation, does not invoke an SQL executive, and does not silently repair the candidate.
- U0’s preserved uncertainties (bag vs. set, before-image disagreement, collation/affinity) are appropriate and not disguised as resolved.

### Bearing and verdict

The bearing is on **evidential adequacy**, not on local correctness. U0’s two-event application is internally consistent with the candidate under a stated bag interpretation. What is under-supported is:

- the public traceability of the pre-event retained state and output bag (they are reconstructed, not supplied), and
- the extent to which the bag-removal and bucket-cardinality decisions are forced by the candidate rather than chosen by U0.

These affect the retained-state obligation (the reconstructed initializer and the multiset reading of `RkeyIndex`) and the query-output obligation (the “complete output bag” at U0 is only checkable against the same reconstruction). They do not establish that U0 is wrong. A substantive objection is warranted only to the extent U0 might be read as independently confirming the candidate’s rules; as a confirmation, it is weaker than its presentation suggests, because its initializer is inferred rather than public and its key interpretive choice is disclosed but not entailed.

# Working answer

We prove the identity by expanding the determinant in the columns and then recognizing the single-column-replacement terms as adjugate entries. Let the columns of A be a_1,...,a_n. The rank-one matrix u v^T has columns v_1 u,...,v_n u, so the columns of A+u v^T are a_i+v_i u. By multilinearity of det in each column, det(A+u v^T) expands as det(A) plus the sum of terms in which one column a_i is replaced by v_i u, plus terms with at least two replaced columns. A term with at least two replaced columns has two linearly dependent columns (multiples of u), so it vanishes by the alternating property. Hence det(A+u v^T)=det(A)+Σ_{i=1}^n v_i det(A^{(i)}), where A^{(i)} is A with column i replaced by u. Now expand det(A^{(i)}) along column i. If C_{j,i}=(-1)^{j+i} det(A with row j and column i deleted), with the empty minor convention det(empty)=1, then det(A^{(i)})=Σ_{j=1}^n C_{j,i} u_j. The classical adjugate is defined by (adj(A))_{i,j}=C_{j,i}; therefore (adj(A)u)_i=Σ_{j=1}^n C_{j,i}u_j=det(A^{(i)}). Thus Σ_i v_i det(A^{(i)})=Σ_i v_i(adj(A)u)_i=v^T adj(A)u. Combining gives det(A+u v^T)=det(A)+v^T adj(A)u. This argument never divides by det(A) and never uses A^{-1}, so it is valid for every n>=1 and every A, singular or not. For every n>=1 and every n by n matrix A over a commutative field R, including singular A, det(A+u v^T)=det(A)+v^T adj(A)u. Singular matrices are included in this identity, so C20.singular_included is true. If A is invertible, the adjugate satisfies adj(A)=det(A)A^{-1}. Substituting into the general identity gives det(A+u v^T)=det(A)+v^T(det(A)A^{-1})u=det(A)(1+v^T A^{-1}u). The invertibility hypothesis is needed only for this rewriting: it supplies A^{-1} and lets det(A) be factored out. When A is singular, A^{-1} does not exist and det(A)=0, so the factored expression is not available, even though the adjugate identity remains true.
